# advisor-act — moves

Curated move set of the ACT lens (prefix `[ACT:]`), deduplicated from the canon digest *ACT Made Simple* (Harris, 2019). Tier is assigned per OPERATION under safety-protocol §3, not per school. Baseline evidence unless a move says otherwise: the ACT method-level trial base plus the author's clinical craft — no direct trial of this book, no component-level trials.

## A. Orientation — framing the problem before any technique

### [ACT:WORKABILITY] Workability probe
**Move:** Drop the true/false debate about a thought and ask what the behavior it drives actually buys long-term: what shows up → what it hooks → what a camera would have recorded → does that serve the life you want. Validate the behavior's real payoffs first, then invite the long-run appraisal so the appraisal is chosen, not imposed. Orientation variant: rate life 0–10 on "living as the person I want to be", then ask what one step would move it by a point.
**APPLY WHEN:** any repeated stuck behavior; painful self-judgments defended as true; picking what to work on at all.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have privately pre-decided the answer (Strosahl calls that bullying — it buys compliance, not change); good/bad judgment smuggled in as "workability"; the thought contains genuinely useful information you are dodging; the question is being asked for the fifth time about the same worry — that is a reassurance loop, not an inquiry.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Core ACT stance underlying the method-level trial base; non-coercive framing credited to Strosahl 2004. No isolated trial of the probe.

### [ACT:CHOICEPOINT] Choice point map and function spotting
**Move:** Draw two diverging arrows — towards moves (acting like the person you want to be) and away moves — with the triggering situation, thoughts and feelings at the bottom. Then add the payoffs of the away moves above them (all reduce to escaping something unwanted or getting something wanted). The payoff column selects the intervention: hooked by thoughts → defusion, by feelings → acceptance, by the situation itself → problem-solving or a missing skill.
**APPLY WHEN:** mapping any problem, emotion or repetitive behavior you do not understand; deciding where to intervene; explaining the approach to yourself.
**AVOID WHEN:** you label a destructive behavior "towards" and start arguing with the map — record it and revisit later; moralizing; listing costs before the payoffs are acknowledged (reads as prosecution).
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Choice point co-created by Harris, Ciarrochi & Bailey 2013; function spotting is standard behavior analysis. No direct trial of the tool — VERY LOW at tool level.

### [ACT:GOAL-REFRAME] Behavioral goals, not emotional ones
**Move:** Convert every goal into what you will DO. Emotional goal ("stop feeling anxious") → learning a skill for handling the feeling. Dead person's goals — anything a corpse does better, such as never panicking or never drinking (Lindsley 1968) → what you will do INSTEAD when the urge arrives. Outcome goal → the actions that raise the odds. Insight goal → what you will do differently once you understand. Probe: if the feelings were still there but no longer ran the show, what would change in your week.
**APPLY WHEN:** any goal phrased as feel / get / understand / stop; the start of any change project.
**AVOID WHEN:** the probe is worded as the feelings disappearing — that re-feeds the control agenda; the person wants only to feel better and rejects every reframe (route to [ACT:CH-DOTS] instead of arguing).
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Dead person's goals from Lindsley 1968; the author names missing behavioral goals as the top error he sees in supervision — practitioner observation, not data.

### [ACT:CH-DOTS] Creative hopelessness — auditing the control agenda
**Move:** Make the unworkability of the feel-better agenda experientially undeniable — hopelessness about the AGENDA, never about the person, life or future. Five questions: what have you tried against the unwanted inner state (Distraction, Opting out, Thinking strategies, Substances and other); how did each work short-term and year-to-year; what did it cost; what shows up as you review this; are you open to something different. For "but it works": grant the benefit, note the condition you arrive in, and look for a better vehicle to the same payoff.
**APPLY WHEN:** fixation on feeling good; a strong control agenda; pseudo-defusion or pseudo-acceptance detected — "it isn't working" meaning "I don't feel better".
**AVOID WHEN:** the person is already willing (skip it entirely); the tone turns lecturing; workable ordinary coping gets pathologized — a painkiller and a glass of wine can be fine; self-attack fuses in mid-audit; the person is flooded (ground first).
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** The phase sits inside tested protocols (Zettle 2007 depression; Strosahl brief-intervention work); the five-question audit frame is the author's, untrialed in isolation.

## B. Acute state — overwhelm, urges, being flooded

### [ACT:ACE-ANCHOR] Dropping anchor (acknowledge, come back, engage)
**Move:** Cycle until steady: Acknowledge and name what is here ("here is sadness"; "here is a storm" if unnameable); Come back into the body — feet into the floor, spine straight, press fingertips, stretch; Engage — count things seen and heard, then return to the task. Anchors do not stop the weather, they stop you being dragged. About ten seconds for everyday drift, ten to fifteen minutes for severe overwhelm. Debrief only on hookedness, presence and control over action.
**APPLY WHEN:** overwhelm, panic, urges, extreme fusion; the first skill to learn; rehearsed in calm conditions — queues, minor annoyances — or it will not be available later.
**AVOID WHEN:** used to stop crying, escape a feeling, or relieve someone else's discomfort; the acknowledge step is dropped (it silently becomes distraction); "it relaxes me" becomes the point; distress lasts past a day or function is dropping — that is a stop-and-route signal, not a cue for more practice.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** The author's flagship skill and the first skill in the WHO refugee-camp ACT protocol (Epping-Jordan et al. 2016); no direct trial of the drill itself.

### [ACT:STRUGGLE-DROP] Drop the struggle
**Move:** Relocate the problem from the feeling to the struggle with it. Write the avoided content on a sheet and hold it pushed away at arm's length — exhausting, distracting, hands unusable; then rest it in your lap: the content is still there, energy and hands return. Verbal versions: struggling in quicksand; a tug of war where dropping the rope frees your hands and the monster stays. Struggle switch: rate the struggle 0–10 (5 is gritted-teeth tolerance) and practise lowering the struggle, not the feeling. Test the control split directly — you cannot delete a memory or feel nothing on command, you can control what you do next.
**APPLY WHEN:** moving from the control audit toward acceptance; intellectual agreement with no felt shift; relapse into control strategies.
**AVOID WHEN:** neck, shoulder or pain problems make the physical version unwise; the sheet gets thrown away (name it as the same move in miniature); the struggle rating quietly becomes a distress gauge you chase downward; tolerating through gritted teeth is sold to yourself as acceptance.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Quicksand and tug of war from Hayes, Strosahl & Wilson 1999; struggle switch Harris 2007; suppression-rebound findings (Wenzlaff & Wegner 2000) support the control claim. Metaphor-level trials: none.

### [ACT:ACTSPLIT] Control the action, not the feeling
**Move:** Keep the emotion and take over voice, face, posture and words — act calmly while furious, act courageously while scared. Do it experientially with the emotion present: stretch, walk, drink water, and notice that action stayed available. For energising emotions (performance nerves, anger) channel the arousal into the performance. For urges, ride them: let the urge rise, crest and fall while noticing it rather than obeying it.
**APPLY WHEN:** the belief that the feeling must go before action is possible; anger; pre-performance nerves; emotion-driven urges to drink, withdraw or lash out.
**AVOID WHEN:** it becomes pretending — the move requires honestly acknowledging the feeling, not hiding it; urges toward self-harm or suicide are involved — that is the crisis circuit and a live clinician, not a self-management drill.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** The author's practice (developed further in The Confidence Gap, 2011); urge surfing is an established relapse-prevention technique. Method-level only.

## C. Fusion — working with thoughts

### [ACT:IHTT] "I'm having the thought that…"
**Move:** Take the fused judgment in its raw form ("I am X"), hold it for about ten seconds, then replay it as "I'm having the thought that X", then "I notice I'm having the thought that X". Keep it as a standing convention and reuse it as a seconds-long micro-move; the same wrapper works for feelings, urges and memories.
**APPLY WHEN:** the first defusion technique to try — it works for most people; any moment a judgment grabs the steering wheel.
**AVOID WHEN:** no separation appears — try a different thought rather than forcing it; it is being used to make the thought leave.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Adapted from the Hayes, Strosahl & Wilson 1999 founder protocol; defusion supported at method level, not as an isolated component.

### [ACT:3N] Defusion improvising — notice, name, neutralize
**Move:** All the defusion techniques collapse into three steps: Notice what the mind is saying; Name it ("worrying", "here it is again", "the judgment factory is open"); Neutralize by changing its context — run the workability question, examine its form (voice, speed, location), sing it to a birthday tune, see it as text on a screen, thank the mind. Flagship version — name the recurring bundle as a titled story and greet it on each appearance; a title must acknowledge, not mock. Portable version: the harshest line written verbatim on a card and carried for about a month, read a few times a day. Normalize first: the mind is a do-not-get-harmed machine doing the job it evolved for.
**APPLY WHEN:** improvising any defusion move; recurring self-narratives — it works even on content that is true, because believability does not have to change.
**AVOID WHEN:** playful versions near grief, fresh trauma, terminal illness or thin trust — high invalidation risk; the story wording irritates; treated as a fixed ladder; used to expel thoughts, which is pseudo-defusion (route to [ACT:CH-DOTS]).
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Singing and thanking the mind from Hayes et al. 1999; naming the story Harris 2007; the three-step compression is the author's. Method-level support for defusion as a class.

### [ACT:LEAVES] Leaves on a stream — meditative defusion
**Move:** Picture a stream, place each thought on a leaf — pleasant ones and meta-thoughts included — and let each arrive and leave at its own pace. Getting hooked and restarting is the exercise, not a failure. Debrief: what hooked you, did you try to flush thoughts downstream, how is this the opposite of rumination. Non-visual alternative: watch thinking directly — its location, form, speed, and the gaps. Give the rationale first; brief techniques are warm-up weights and this is the heavy set.
**APPLY WHEN:** rumination, worry, obsessing; near-daily practice once the rationale is clear.
**AVOID WHEN:** you barely visualize (roughly one person in ten — use the auditory version); mid-crisis (train it offline; in a storm use [ACT:ACE-ANCHOR]); the stream gets sped up to flush thoughts away; pleasant thoughts are exempted from the leaves.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Classic founder-repertoire exercise; method-level trials only, no dismantling data for this exercise.

### [ACT:FPT-SHIFT] Flexible perspective taking
**Move:** Add viewpoints instead of disputing content — there is no delete button. Reverse roles; ask what you would say to someone you love in this exact spot; look back from your future self; ask what the emotion would advise if it could speak; notice whether the voice running the commentary is a harsh coach or a kind one, and what the kind one would say. For worriers, replace looping with a preparation battery: worst case plus coping plus what lowers its odds, plan B, best case plus what raises its odds, most likely case plus next step — always ending in a written plan.
**APPLY WHEN:** rigid single-viewpoint thinking; judgment loops about other people; harsh self-talk; a worrier who wants to "prepare".
**AVOID WHEN:** the battery slides into rumination instead of terminating in a plan; delivered as a lecture to yourself; the target is traumatic material — that is [ACT:INNER-CHILD] territory; expecting the insight to stop the thought from recurring.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Grounded in the model's relational-frame base; the specific question set is the author's practice. Method-level.

## D. Emotions, attention, self-story

### [ACT:NOTICE-X] Notice X — flexible attention training
**Move:** The model's most portable instruction: notice X with openness and curiosity, where X can be thoughts (defusion), emotions (acceptance), the five senses (engagement), or your own behavior (workability). Train three attention skills against their three costs — engaging (versus cutting off), savoring (versus missing it), focusing (versus doing it badly) — through ordinary activities. Dial the aperture deliberately: narrow onto a valued activity when ruminating; acknowledge and then broaden the field with persistent physical pain. Name feelings as you go; emotion vocabulary is a trainable skill.
**APPLY WHEN:** a flat going-through-the-motions week; disconnection; groundwork before any acceptance work.
**AVOID WHEN:** sold to yourself as relaxation or mind-clearing (false expectations discredit the skill); savoring assigned to activities that are not pleasurable; long eyes-closed body-focused work if you dissociate or have trauma history — start on the outer world, eyes open, short.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** The root instruction shared by all mindfulness-based approaches; method-level. No component trial for the three-skill split.

### [ACT:ACCEPTKIT] Making room for an emotion — modular kit
**Move:** Thirteen interchangeable modules, each with a ten-second version: link to what it is in service of; observe it like a curious child; note the part of you doing the noticing; let the commentary run in the background; map the sensation's shape, size and edges; name it as a feeling rather than an identity; breathe into it; expand around it; allow it; give it an object form; normalize it as the gap between wanted and got; place a kind hand on the spot; widen awareness so the feeling stays on stage while the lights come up on everything else. Acceptance is graded: acknowledge, then allow, then accommodate.
**APPLY WHEN:** struggle with an emotion is blocking a valued action; anxiety work.
**AVOID WHEN:** a full-length version early with a highly avoidant person — take one or two modules; breath focus that makes you dizzy; shrinking or dissolving the object, which reinstalls the control agenda; celebrating when the feeling shrinks; framed as resignation; the target is a fresh loss — see [ACT:GRIEF-BOUND].
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Method-level trials of acceptance as a process; the thirteen-module chain and the three-step grading are the author's, untrialed.

### [ACT:EMO-ALLY] Reading what an emotion is pointing at
**Move:** Psychoeducation first: emotions prepare action, and an action tendency is an inclination rather than a command. Painful emotions signal to others, mobilize (fear toward protecting, anger toward holding ground, guilt toward repair), and point at what is at stake. Once the struggle has dropped, ask what this feeling is asking you to look after, what advice it is giving, whether following that advice moves toward or away, and what would change in the next 24 hours — then convert the answer into one concrete move.
**APPLY WHEN:** emotions treated as malfunctions to be fixed; low emotional vocabulary; guilt tied to how you have acted in a relationship.
**AVOID WHEN:** attempted mid-struggle — nothing is readable until the struggle drops; while flooded (anchor first); expressing vulnerable feelings to a predictably hostile audience; the emotion is grief after a loss — do not run this reading there, see [ACT:GRIEF-BOUND].
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Long-standing ACT stance (Hayes et al. 1999) plus consensus points from emotion science; no trial of the interview itself.

### [ACT:NOTICING-SELF] The noticing self
**Move:** Contact the vantage point that observes everything and does not itself change. Ladder: seed the phrase "notice that you are noticing" inside any other exercise; use the stage-show framing — the show changes constantly, the watcher does not — with two or three short check-ins a day; then a longer pass cycling over breath, thoughts, body, feelings and roles, each time separating the content from the one noticing it, closing with sky-and-weather; then the two-sided sheet with the harshest judgments on one face and the kindest on the other — held against your eyes, either side blocks the room equally.
**APPLY WHEN:** a fused self-story ("I am broken", "I am the one who always fails"); chaotic period needing a stable vantage; self-esteem questions.
**AVOID WHEN:** metaphors are used without the exercise (they rarely produce the experience); you analyze the long pass afterwards (that dissolves it); long memory-revisiting variants with a trauma history — therapist territory; the exercise turns into metaphysics about who you really are.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Observer exercise from Hayes et al. 1999 with the author's adaptations; sky-and-weather from contemplative traditions. Self-as-context has the thinnest component evidence of the six processes.

### [ACT:SELF-COMP] Self-compassion — six building blocks
**Move:** Six words of definition: acknowledge the pain, respond with kindness. Entry test: two friends — the one who tells you to stop whining and the one who says this is rough and stays; ask which one you are being to yourself. Six stackable blocks in any order: acknowledge the wound, be human about it, disarm the critic, hold yourself kindly, make room for the pain, see yourself in others. Physical version: fill a hand with remembered warmth and rest it where the pain or the numbness sits. Against "criticism is my motivation": a stick moves a donkey and so does a carrot, but years of stick produce a battered donkey — and you have something better than carrots available, namely what you actually care about.
**APPLY WHEN:** harsh self-attack; shame after a lapse; after failure and hard days.
**AVOID WHEN:** the word itself repels — teach the skill before naming it; self-touch triggers disgust or abuse memories (hands in the lap, imagine the warmth); entrenched self-hatred where kindness itself provokes fear — expect fusion with not deserving it, use anchoring plus defusion, and take it to the session at the severe end.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Expanded from Neff's 2003 triad; the block structure and exercises are the author's. Note the calibration correction: self-compassion trial effects (g≈0.51) are inflated by passive controls — better than nothing, not shown superior to active treatment.

### [ACT:SHAME-NAME] Working with shame — the self-manageable portion
**Move:** Separate the two forms: shame fuses with "I am bad", guilt attaches to "I did something bad". Map shame's away and towards moves on a choice point, then work the three elements in any order — fusion, avoidance, unworkable action. Defuse gently ("here is shame"; "I am having the thought that I am bad"), later greeting it as an outdated protector that has been reassigned. Function frame: shame once bought something — less hostility from others, some sense of why things happened — and now blocks who you want to be; that contrast is the motivation for new skills. Then run the compassion blocks.
**APPLY WHEN:** self-judgment, self-blame, fear of rejection; the same shape generalizes to guilt, anger, envy and loneliness.
**AVOID WHEN:** zany defusion early on — it invalidates shame-laden people; the payoff review requires revisiting trauma memories, or the shame is rooted in childhood abuse (that is [ACT:INNER-CHILD]); the naming is being used to make the feeling go away.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Defusion and functional analysis at method level; the shame-specific sequencing is the author's clinical practice.

## E. Values and committed action

### [ACT:VALUES-CLARIFY] Values clarification battery
**Move:** Teach the distinction first: values are qualities of ongoing action available in any moment; goals are future attainables (being married is a goal, being loving is a value). Elicit from several angles: a ten-years-later look back at what you spent too much and too little time on; what you would want a person who knows you well to be able to say about how you treated people; the one-year-ahead version of yourself; behind any goal, what having it would let you demonstrate; and, as a fallback, a checklist of common values (about 38 items). Test by living them and watching for vitality, not by deciding they are correct. Daily bridge: pick one or two each morning and sprinkle them into what you were going to do anyway.
**APPLY WHEN:** values information is missing; a hard decision; approval-seeking or inherited shoulds; chronic frustration in someone who keeps hitting goals.
**AVOID WHEN:** first answers are taken as final; the language arrives as should, must and duty, with heaviness, compliance or resentment — that is a fused rule, defuse it and restore choice; dutiful living-my-values with zero fulfilment, which is an attention deficit and needs [ACT:NOTICE-X], not more values work.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Values definition from Hayes, Bond, Barnes-Holmes & Austin 2006; examples from Hayes et al. 1999 and Harris 2007. Method-level; the battery itself untrialed.

### [ACT:SMART-ACTION] Values-based goal setting and the smallest step
**Move:** Informal goal setting is enough most of the time; reserve the formal version for hard aims with a history of failure — one domain, one to three values, one goal that is specific, motivated by those values, adaptive, realistic and time-framed. Always finish with the smallest step doable within 24 hours. Realism gate: rate the odds you will actually do it 0–10; below 7, shrink it until it clears 7 or change the goal — sometimes the real goal is acquiring a missing resource. Pre-plan the obstacle and a plan B serving the same values. For goals that are blocked or impossible: first name the pain of the gap, then acceptance and self-kindness, then dig out the values underneath, then set reachable goals from those values.
**APPLY WHEN:** any commitment; repeated non-follow-through; goals blocked for years.
**AVOID WHEN:** the formal version is forced on an unwilling person; you fantasize about the finished goal (that lowers follow-through); a substitute goal is offered before the loss is acknowledged; the goal is about someone else's behavior — convert it to your own influence behavior.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** The 7-out-of-10 realism rule is credited to Kirk Strosahl and is a heuristic, not a finding; the blocked-goal sequence comes from the author's case material.

### [ACT:HARD] Barriers audit — why the plan did not happen
**Move:** Normalize the miss, then screen four barriers with their antidotes: Hooked by the mind's reasons and catastrophes → unhook; Avoiding discomfort → make room, and pre-commit by naming which discomfort is coming and whether you are willing; Remote from values → reconnect to why it matters; Doubtful goal → re-rate realism and shrink. Motivation has two faces — workability (payoffs against costs) and willingness (given that it matters, are you willing to carry what comes with it) — and wanting to is not required. Two commitment patterns: commit, break, quit; or commit, break, be decent about it, learn, recommit.
**APPLY WHEN:** any broken commitment or stalled plan; fear of failing; shame after a lapse; managing yourself over weeks.
**AVOID WHEN:** the basics were never set up (no behavioral goal, no obstacle plan — fix that first); "I will not be able to handle it" is argued with rather than trained for; you keep grinding an area where nothing shifts — say so honestly and move energy to areas where values are livable now.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** The author's tool, replacing the first edition's acronyms; the resistance framing builds on Strosahl 2004. No trial.

### [ACT:CHALLENGE-3] The three options in a hard situation
**Move:** Any difficult situation offers exactly three responses: leave it; stay and act on your values, changing what can be changed and making room for the pain that stays; or stay and give up effective action, which is the default drift. If leaving is genuinely available, weigh it seriously rather than treating it as unthinkable; otherwise the workable path is the second, and even in severely constrained conditions a number of value-consistent choices remain each day. For "I have no choice": acknowledge the constraint, then contrast keeping everything the same (guaranteed) with an experiment that carries no guarantee but a chance.
**APPLY WHEN:** stuckness and a felt absence of options; a chronic hard context — job, illness, a strained relationship; complaining about a situation that is neither being fixed nor left.
**AVOID WHEN:** implying option one always exists — sometimes it does not; option two is sold as pain removal; there is abuse or danger, where leaving safely requires professional help; the "no choice" is about suicidal urges — that is the crisis circuit.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** The author's heuristic and a pillar of the WHO refugee-camp protocol; no isolated trial.

### [ACT:REL-INFLUENCE] Relationships — influence rather than control
**Move:** Start from the asymmetry: you do not control another person, you control what you say and do. Audit what you have tried — arguing, criticizing, demanding, withdrawing, going silent — and sort by workability: short-term compliance versus long-term ill will. Then build influence a relationship survives: clear requests, stated boundaries, negotiation, and reinforcing what you like several times more often than you punish what you dislike. Separate what you want to GIVE (values) from what you want to GET (wants). Fuel it by recalling the relationship at its best and naming the qualities YOU were bringing then.
**APPLY WHEN:** any relationship goal; the belief that they must change first; chronic criticizing; influence run almost entirely on sticks.
**AVOID WHEN:** it starts sounding like the problems are your fault (say the opposite explicitly); it turns into technique aimed at extracting compliance; there are no accessible good memories yet; the pain has not been acknowledged before the audit; there is abuse — see [ACT:CHALLENGE-3] and take it to the session.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Behavioral shaping is well established; the specific reinforcement ratio is asserted by the author without citation. Relationship application expanded in ACT with Love (2009).

### [ACT:BLOCKED] When the work itself stalls — doubt, "nothing matters", "I don't know"
**Move:** For hopelessness and reason-giving: notice and name the doubts, treat them as the mind trying to protect you, state plainly that there are no guarantees, write the objections down, and refuse to argue — arguing makes the thoughts louder. Three options: quit, debate the mind (it wins), or let it talk and carry on; the third IS defusion. For values blocks: the louder "nothing matters" gets, the more likely it is avoidance of facing the gap between values and behavior — name the dynamic without blame and lay out the honest choice, dropping the work (discomfort escaped, life unchanged) or continuing as an experiment. Separate wanting from willingness. With high fusion, listen and validate for several minutes before any technique at all.
**APPLY WHEN:** "this will not work for me"; "I don't know" or "what's the point" during values work; doubts obstructing a change project.
**AVOID WHEN:** no behavioral goals exist yet; validation expands to fill the entire session and reinforces the fusion; any form of pressure; the "I don't know" is a factual gap or a request for help — answer that instead of treating it as a block.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** The author's playbook; the no-guarantees line rests on his own count of the published ACT literature, which is a volume claim, not an effect claim.

## F. Therapist-tier operations and the route to the session

### [ACT:EXPO-FORMAL] Formal exposure, ACT-style
**Move:** Exposure redefined as organized contact with stimuli that narrow the behavioral repertoire — not only fear, but shame, disgust, urges and pain — in order to widen the response repertoire, never to lower anxiety, which is at most a by-product. Set it up on a choice point (stimuli at the bottom, current aways, wanted towards), and replace distress ratings with 0–10 flexibility ratings: acceptance versus avoidance, fusion versus defusion, presence, control over actions, connection with values. Consistent with inhibitory-learning findings that within-session distress drop does not predict behavioral outcome.
**APPLY WHEN:** a planned exposure programme — trauma memories, obsessions, panic sensations, feared social situations — designed and paced by a clinician who also owns the hierarchy.
**AVOID WHEN:** the rationale becomes getting rid of anxiety (that reinstalls avoidance); the flexibility ratings become the new number to chase; attempted solo, or escalated between sessions — a failed self-run exposure reinforces the fear. Ordinary everyday approach through towards moves stays self-manageable; formal hierarchies do not.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** The reframe is consistent with Craske et al. 2008/2014 inhibitory learning; the flexibility-scale protocol is the author's proposal and untrialed. Calibration layer puts hierarchy design and escalation firmly on the therapist side.

### [ACT:INNER-CHILD] Childhood-origin shame work and imagery
**Move:** Trace the learning history that installed the shame — the messages received from caregivers or abusers — with the explanation that a child's mind blames itself in order to keep a needed adult safe, and relabel "I am bad" as old programming. Then guided imagery in which the present-day adult returns to the child, tells the truth about whose fault it was, and stays with or carries the child to safety.
**APPLY WHEN:** shame tied to explicit memories of childhood trauma, abuse or neglect, in a therapy relationship that can hold what opens.
**AVOID WHEN:** solo or outside that relationship — the exercise is designed to open traumatic material, and the state gate that should precede it (stable sense of self, intact reality testing) cannot be self-administered. The book's own relief valve applies: present function matters more than history, so the history work is optional.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** The author's clinical practice with scripts in the companion materials; no trial cited. Contact with wounded child parts sits in the therapist column of the operation table.

### [ACT:NUMBWORK] Deliberate work with numbness and dissociation
**Move:** Treat numbness as a felt state rather than an absence: locate where it is densest, notice, name, describe it, make room for it — buried feelings typically surface, so the next steps must be ready. Feeling nothing, empty or dead inside is itself an unpleasant state and tends to travel with high experiential avoidance.
**APPLY WHEN:** persistent numbness or dissociation, worked through with a therapist who can contain what rises.
**AVOID WHEN:** solo — surfacing hidden material deliberately can open trauma content, and the book itself defers dissociation detail to specialist sources. The self-manageable portion is [ACT:ACE-ANCHOR] plus outer-world attention training.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** The author's clinical practice; no trial cited. Dissociative material is on the therapist side of the operation table.

### [ACT:GRIEF-BOUND] Loss — where this lens stops
**Move:** Do not run acceptance mining, values extraction or defusion humor on a fresh loss. What ACT contributes solo is narrow: naming what is present without arguing with it, dropping anchor when the wave arrives, and keeping ordinary daily actions running where they still can run. Anything that deliberately turns toward the loss — going back over it, working through what was left unfinished, exposure to the absence — is a session operation, entered only after symptoms have actually been measured rather than assumed. State the boundary out loud rather than substituting a technique for it.
**APPLY WHEN:** grief, bereavement or an unfinished-business theme around a person who is gone.
**AVOID WHEN:** any framing that treats the loss as an opportunity, a lesson or a doorway to values; assuming a protocol is indicated without measurement — at ordinary grief levels these interventions are near-zero in effect and can do harm.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** Calibration layer: grief work is indicated only at elevated prolonged-grief symptoms, no modality is shown superior (Hao 2024 network, overlapping intervals), and no solo format exists. The ACT digest itself flags grief as a context where playful defusion invalidates.

### [ACT:SESSION-PREP] Hand over a format, not a topic
**Move:** When a move lands in the therapist tier, convert it into session material rather than dropping it: the theme, why it belongs in the room, the artifact you already have (a filled choice point, the barriers audit, the values list, a log of when it hooked you), the one question you want to ask, and a plan for what to note afterwards. Preparing questions and bringing observations is the self-manageable half of the operation; opening the material is not.
