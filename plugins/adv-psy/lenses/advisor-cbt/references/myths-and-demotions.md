# advisor-cbt — myths, demotions, failure modes

What this tradition itself rejects, what did not survive verification, and how the method hurts people when it is run badly. When a claim here conflicts with something remembered from popular CBT material, this file wins.

## Myths the tradition rejects — but that circulate under its name

- **"Thoughts cause depression."** Explicitly rejected by the canon: causes are psychosocial, genetic and biological; construal shapes how the state is expressed and maintained. The negativity and the discounting are automatic products of the state, not laziness and not willful pessimism.
- **"Challenge your thoughts."** Prohibited three separate ways — the degree of distortion is unknowable before you look, most distressing thoughts hold a grain of truth, and disputing invalidates the person and breaks the joint-inquiry frame. Emotions are never disputed at all, and removing negative emotion is not a goal: it is as functional as pain.
- **"CBT is positive thinking."** Rejected repeatedly and in both sources: simplistic, rarely durable, throws away information, and manufactures a second failure when distress arrives anyway. The target is realism, which sometimes means correcting a belief DOWNWARD in an elevated state.
- **"Test every negative thought."** Some hot thoughts are accurate — including "I am being treated badly here". Then the record's job is confirmation and management, and rationalising the situation deepens the isolation — [CBT:PROBLEM-SOLVE].
- **"Wait until you feel like it."** Motivation follows starting, not the reverse; the stay-in-bed experiment has usually already run for months. Avoidance pays out in immediate relief and charges the disconfirming experience — [CBT:ACT-SCHED].
- **"Rumination is uncontrollable" / "if I think about it hard enough I'll solve it."** Meta-beliefs that feel functional and regenerate the same thoughts; disconfirmed live by a five-minute practice, not by argument. Thought suppression is both impossible and the wrong goal — [CBT:MINDFUL-PROCESS].
- **"Disputing a core belief will dislodge it."** Evidence-weighing fails at that level because the active belief filters the incoming data — affection gets read as pity. Build the companion belief instead — [CBT:CB-STRENGTHEN].
- **"Feeling better in the session means it worked."** Progress is cross-session change on measures; pleasant, flat sessions are a named failure state with its own checklist.
- **"Linear recovery."** Setbacks belong to the expected curve; a single week's score rise is not the absence of progress — judge multi-week trends — [CBT:RELAPSE-PLAN].
- **"Resistant / unmotivated / manipulative."** These labels mainly relieve the helper's sense of responsibility and stop the problem-solving; replace with what exactly was said, done, or not done by each side.
- **"The inventories say what is wrong with me."** They are tracking instruments and explicitly not diagnostic — and the workbook's are author-built and never independently validated — [CBT:MOOD-TRACK].
- **"Taking medication means something is deeply wrong with me."** Debunked in the workbook; separately, medication alone carries higher relapse than medication plus skills, and anxiety-dampening drugs both build tolerance and block the learning that the anxiety was survivable.
- **"That's just ageing."** Fatigue and withdrawal read as ordinary ageing is a documented route to never seeking help.
- **"Anger is the problem."** After a long period of helplessness the absent anger can be the pathology — the aim may be feeling it about mistreatment, not controlling it. And forgiveness is not offered while mistreatment is ongoing — [CBT:FORGIVE-LETTER].
- **"The child could have stopped it."** Responsibility for sexual abuse of a child sits entirely with the adult; not saying no is not consent, and having stopped it later proves nothing about the earlier ability.
- **"The CBT label guarantees the goods."** The canon's own foreword states that many self-described practitioners lack the basic conceptual skills, and that no book substitutes for supervision.

## Numbers that did not survive verification — use only the corrected form

- **Homework completion benchmarks: refuted.** The circulating "41%" traces to a 1998 non-CBT study and the "51% vs 72%, 2026 survey" figure does not exist. There is NO reliable completion benchmark. Cite completion FACTORS instead: setup quality, one step, fit with the person's goal, written form, obstacle plan.
- **Homework effect d = 0.48: demoted MODERATE → LOW.** Still directionally supported; no longer strong enough to justify treating non-completion as the person's failure. Quality outperforms quantity (g = 1.07 vs 0.51).
- **Alliance r = .278: the upgrade was invalid, LOW.** And the contrast that pitted an alliance "d = 0.57" against method "d ≤ 0.20" is retracted — a correlation and a randomised contrast are not commensurable numbers.
- **Monitoring payoff: bounded toward zero.** Pooled d ≈ 0.13–0.27; the off-track subgroup effect falls to d = 0.21 non-significant with an independent rater and to zero with an independent scale; feedback did not prevent deterioration (OR 1.16, p = 0.07). For the protective effect use NNT 9.7 (usual care) / 5.7 (waitlist), never the uncorrected RR 0.39 / NNT 20.
- **Grief superiority for this method: retracted, demoted to LOW.** A 55-study network (N = 10,330) shows five modalities with overlapping intervals. Say "works at elevated prolonged-grief symptoms; no winning brand" — and nothing else.
- **"Twice a week is better therapy": refuted long-term.** The acute advantage (d = 0.55, dropout roughly halved) is gone by 12–24 months and is not cost-effective. Frame density as an exit-the-acute-phase tool only.
- **"Behavioural assignments get completed more than written ones": refuted** (one small study plus an analogue). There are no strong format-completability data.
- **"Guided self-help is the main lever, OR 2.76": refuted as stated** — the figure was lifted from a different paper; the correct estimate is roughly half that and small. Guidance still matters, but through adherence and monitoring (g ≈ 0.26–0.29), not through symptom gain, which sits below the minimal important difference.
- **"Expressive writing is unsafe by default": bounded.** Newer synthesis finds a small positive effect. What remains contraindicated is unstructured venting during active rumination-driven distress.
- **Component-removal d ≈ 0.01: demoted.** Do not use dismantling numbers to argue that any single component is dispensable; the defensible statement is that parts perform about as well as the whole.
- **"Harm from therapy occurs in X%": the range spans 5–50.9% depending on definition.** Adverse events in roughly 5–20%, deterioration 4–5% against 12–13% in controls, and clinicians catch about 3 of 40 deteriorations. Judge a multi-point trajectory, never one bad session.
- **Allegiance inflation ≈ 30%** in developer-affiliated trials, and "several meta-analyses agree" is downweighted where the author groups overlap. Both apply to this tradition's own supportive literature.
- **Context figures are not findings.** The "50,000–70,000 thoughts a day" estimate, the million-plus readers and the count of therapists trained describe reach and rhetoric, not efficacy.

## Anti-recommendations — with the reason

- **Do not cite either source book as evidence for itself.** The canonical manual has no bibliotherapy trial, and the workbook's self-help seal and reading-list inclusion certify curation, not outcomes. Every claim they make rides on method-level protocol trials.
- **Do not transfer the older bibliotherapy trial base onto its successor.** The 1980 self-help title in this family does have direct trials; the 2020 successor is a different protocol without that base and must not inherit it.
- **Do not hand someone unguided self-help as an equivalent to guided work.** It is weaker than every guided format and not better than usual care; roughly one in five self-help books has been judged to carry harmful advice, and negative effects across self-help interventions are reported in a wide band. Above a PHQ-9 of 9, a human in the loop is not optional.
- **Do not present a filled-in formulation to someone as the answer**, and do not guess their automatic thought for them — both invite false agreement, and the canon discourages the first explicitly.
- **Do not stretch these moves across presentations they were never written for.** The manual excludes suicidality, self-harm, substance use, youth and older adults; the workbook adds that severe concentration and memory impairment make it unusable.
- **Do not treat a routing arrow as an assignment.** Roughly half the target→method arrows in the surrounding literature rest on LOW evidence, and personalised matching algorithms have no prospective validation.
- **Do not run this method's technique inventory as a technique pile.** The canon is explicit that assembling techniques without a formulation is not the method.

## Documented failure modes and iatrogenic patterns

- **Thought-challenging at high conviction backfires.** When belief strength is near the top, evaluation produces agreement without relief, and repeated failure teaches a second-order conclusion — that the person cannot even think properly — which then needs its own repair. Switch to usefulness rather than accuracy, to a behavioural test, or to a rule-level hypothesis.
- **Premature belief-level work reads as invalidation** and carries a real risk of intense emotion and of dropping out. Solo digging at negative core beliefs or their origins can trigger strong affect and a felt loss of safety even when nothing is being "processed" — [CBT:BELIEF-MAP], [CBT:CORE-BELIEF-MOD].
- **Cognitive work aimed at tolerating ongoing mistreatment is a named misuse.** The goal there is stopping it or leaving, with thought work used only to mobilise help-seeking.
- **Exposure run solo or run badly strengthens the fear.** A step producing zero anxiety means it was too easy or safety behaviours are running; regulation skills deployed to escape become safety behaviours; a helper who never fades becomes one too — [CBT:EXPOSURE], [CBT:SAFETY-ID].
- **Mindfulness practised only while calm does not transfer**, used as suppression it inverts the method, and pushed through worsening in someone with a trauma history it becomes harm — stop the practice, do not try harder — [CBT:MINDFUL-PROCESS].
- **The positive-data apparatus turns iatrogenic** when the chart becomes a performance audit, when replacement beliefs inflate into absolutes, or when gratitude is used to wave away a legitimate negative — [CBT:POSITIVE-DATA].
- **Comparing current enjoyment with the pre-onset baseline reliably backfires** and produces the conclusion that activity does not work. Compare with sitting at home instead — [CBT:ACT-SCHED].
- **Homework design failures land on the person.** Too-hard items, more than one step, no-lose framing kept past about two weeks of non-completion, or a plan never reviewed all teach that this does not matter; more than three actions at once reproduces a demanding-parent dynamic. Non-completion is a design signal — [CBT:ACTION-PLAN].
- **Score-watching becomes anxious self-monitoring**, and thresholds start substituting for judgement exactly when severe symptoms appear — [CBT:MOOD-TRACK].
- **The reassurance loop.** Repeated returns with the same anxious question are not information requests; answering them again reinforces the loop. Name the loop out loud instead of issuing a fresh round of reassurance.
- **Format harms specific to this delivery.** A session with no explicit end, availability at three in the morning without a closing ritual, unconditional agreement that consolidates avoidance, and the documented tendency of generative systems to affirm the user's framing far more readily than a person would — none of which the books anticipate, and all of which sit on top of a lens whose moves are designed for a second person in the room.
- **Grief.** No protocol without measurement first; no solo format for loss work at all; and no reframing of a loss into something positive, in any register, at any point.
