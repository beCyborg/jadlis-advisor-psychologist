# Compassion Focused Therapy: Distinctive Features — digest

## What this book is for the lens

Canon source for the lens's core distinction: shaming self-criticism runs on threat emotions (anger, contempt at self) and backward-looking condemnation; compassionate self-correction runs on care motivation — forward-looking, behaviour-specific, encouraging (Point 13, Table 2). Mechanism thesis: self-evaluation uses the same brain machinery as evaluating others, so an inner critic wounds like an external attacker. It beats other sources on two things: why rational reframes fail in high shame (the person follows the logic but feels nothing — the soothing system that would carry felt reassurance is offline), and the documented failure mode of naive application: in high self-critics, compassion practice itself can fire the threat system (Rockliff et al. 2008), so fear of compassion is often the work, not an obstacle to it.

## Core model

- **Three affect-regulation systems** (Point 5, "three circles"): threat/self-protection (fast, easily conditioned, dominant); drive/incentive (dopaminergic, achievement); soothing/contentment/safeness (endorphin–oxytocin, affiliative — the evolved regulator of threat). Pathology = organisation around threat with soothing starved. Safeness ≠ low arousal: isolation calms while cutting off the one system that regulates threat.
- **Social mentalities** (Point 3): whole-brain configurations (care-giving, care-seeking, competing, co-operating) that switch capacities on/off. Health = flexible switching; the therapeutic bet is that growing care-giving reorganises the rest.
- **Old brain / new brain loops** (Point 4): evolved motives plus new-brain rumination and self-identity produce distinctly human suffering; threat switches mentalizing off. The mind is multiple (bullying, perfectionist, frightened parts); the unified self is an achievement.
- **Shame model** (Points 10–11): external shame = a negative me in other minds; internal shame = my own judgement. Defences: internalizing (submissive self-blame) vs externalizing (humiliated aggression). Shame memories behave like trauma memories; aloneness at the moment of distress is the pathogenic core. Self-criticism is a threat-system safety strategy, in two forms: inadequacy-focused vs self-hating. The damage is done by the emotion in the criticism, not its content (Whelton & Greenberg 2005).
- **Compassion, operationalised** (Points 15–16): two circles — attributes (care motivation, sensitivity, sympathy, distress tolerance, empathy, non-judgement) wrapped by skills (compassionate attention, thinking, behaviour, imagery, feeling). Four imagery qualities: wisdom, strength, warmth, non-condemnation. Compassion = engagement with pain plus commitment to relieve it — includes boundaries and courage, never soothing pain away.
- **Four imagery orientations** (Points 17–26): developing the compassionate self; flowing out; flowing in; to oneself. Abuse histories start with the compassionate SELF; receiving from others comes later.
- **Change mechanisms** (Point 6): disengage from internal threat-stimulators; observe inner storms without fusing; deliberately switch on soothing; only then engage aversive material from the built base. Felt tone of an alternative matters more than its accuracy.
- **Practice rules** (Points 17–21): imagery needn't be vivid — fleeting impressions and intention count; practise when well, then grade into hard moments (crisis-only practice yields false "doesn't work"); noticing the wandering mind IS the practice; imagery bolted on outside the model underperforms.
- **Validation formula** (Point 15): given what happened, the feeling is an understandable human response — connect feeling to context before change work; never follow with "but you shouldn't feel it".
- **Stance**: not your fault (unchosen brain, genes, history) — still your responsibility to train. Formulation is functional, not diagnostic: history → external/internal threats + unmet needs → safety strategies → unintended consequences looping back (four domains, Point 9).

## Moves

### [CFT:MINDFUL] Mindful notice-and-return, extended to threat-mind monitoring
**Move:** Attend to present experience with curiosity and kindness; when the mind wanders, notice without criticism and gently return — frequent noticing means it's working. Extend to daily monitoring: track shifts into threat mind (attention, body, thought themes, action urges) and on noticing, refocus toward the compassionate pattern (soothing breathing + compassionate self) instead of arguing content.
**APPLY WHEN:** Baseline skill before imagery or behavioural work; rumination loops; waking irritable/anxious; post-stress touchiness.
**AVOID WHEN:** Framed as emptying the mind or a compliance test; used to suppress or shame the threat state — threat mind after stress is normal.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Mindfulness-based interventions have method-level trial support (Kabat-Zinn; Segal et al.); the threat-mind extension is Gilbert's clinical model.

### [CFT:FLOW-OUT] Compassion flowing out — well-wishing plus daily kind acts
**Move:** As the compassionate self, direct three wishes at a cared-for being — be well, be happy, be free of suffering — picturing them receiving it; warm up by recalling a moment of kindness toward someone NOT in acute distress (a minute each: body expansion, the wish, voice tone, pleasure in being able to be kind). Later extend to strangers and disliked people ("nobody chose their brain"). Behavioural arm: one kind act for self and one for another daily — boundary-setting and refusing wants that aren't needs count.
**APPLY WHEN:** Building caring motivation (often easier than self-compassion; a faint glimmer counts); translating imagery into between-session behaviour.
**AVOID WHEN:** Chosen memory centres on the other's distress; acts driven by appeasement or need to be liked — submissive niceness is a counterfeit of compassion.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Strongest trials in the book, but vs passive controls only: loving-kindness, Fredrickson et al. 2008 (67 vs 72 waitlist); Lutz et al. 2008; Pace et al. 2008.

### [CFT:COMP-LETTER] Compassionate letter writing
**Move:** Write to yourself from the compassionate position (dictation from the ideal image, compassionate-self mode, or as to a struggling friend); read aloud attending to tone; audit against the 8-quality checklist — genuine concern, sensitivity, being moved, helping face feelings, reflective understanding, non-judgement, pervasive warmth, orientation to the behaviour needed to move forward — and revise.
**APPLY WHEN:** Assimilating difficult experiences; making the compassionate voice durable and reviewable across states.
**AVOID WHEN:** Early letters uncoached — tone drifts critical, preachy or advice-listy; writing becomes pure problem-rumination.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Rides on expressive-writing literature (Pennebaker 1997), method-level, not CFT-specific; the tone-steering is the author's.

### [CFT:SELFCORR] Compassionate self-correction instead of shaming self-attack
**Move:** On catching self-attack after a mistake: name the threat emotions in the attack (anger, contempt, disappointment at self); then re-correct from care — "I actually want to do this well" — behaviour-specific, future-focused, in the tone you'd use with someone you're responsible for. Apply the same stance during tasks: pace, permission to stop, encouragement instead of bullying yourself through. Belief-test: contrast two teachers responding to a child's mistakes — critical vs encouraging — which would you choose for your own child; does your inner critic have your interests at heart?
**APPLY WHEN:** Mistakes, setbacks, self-performance review; tasks completed but followed by rising self-attack; belief that self-compassion lowers standards.
**AVOID WHEN:** Correction skipped entirely (not self-excuse); the "compassionate" voice is contempt in disguise — check the felt emotion, not wording; it becomes blaming one's original critics as new rumination.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Point 13 / Table 2 contrast, author's model; converges with evidence that the emotional tone of self-criticism does the damage (Whelton & Greenberg 2005); the switch untrialled.

### [CFT:SHAME-SORT] Sort the shame state before responding
**Move:** Two-column split of an episode: external shame (what I believe now exists about me in others' minds) vs internal shame (how I judge myself), naming the key fear under each. Then classify the emotion by attention focus (self as bad / other as attacker / harmed person), rumination content, and action urge (hide-appease / counterattack / repair) — distinguishing shame, humiliation, guilt. Where real harm was done, convert shame into guilt: behaviour-focused, repair-motivated, tolerated without self-condemnation.
**APPLY WHEN:** Post-rejection/failure rumination mixing "what they think" with "what I am"; choosing external- vs internal-focused coping; sorting turmoil after criticism or transgression.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used to argue oneself out of legitimate guilt; the childhood-root line reactivates trauma — stop at naming, take to therapy.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** External/internal shame distinction has psychometric support; shame/guilt/humiliation grounded in self-conscious-emotion research (Tangney & Dearing 2002); the worksheets are the author's.

### [CFT:CRITMAP] Map the critic's function — and let it stay for now
**Move:** Before replacing self-criticism, map it: when did it first appear and what threat was around; greatest fear in giving it up (typical: laziness, arrogance, losing control); what it claims to do (error vigilance, motivation). Externalize in imagery — if it stood outside your head: what form, expression, what emotions it directs at you — and notice these are threat emotions, not care. Then explicitly permit keeping it while building compassionate self-correction in parallel; let comparison decide.
**APPLY WHEN:** Start of any self-criticism work; demonstrating the critic is not a benevolent coach; high-shame cases where removing a safety strategy threatens.
**AVOID WHEN:** No real function exists (simple frustration) — don't over-interpret; the critic's image fuses with an abuser and floods — observation only, no solo dialogue; permission framed as endorsing self-attack.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Functional-analysis logic; forms/functions of self-criticism have questionnaire-level research (Gilbert et al. 2004a); the tools are clinical practice.

### [CFT:OLDNEW] Two-systems psychoeducation — why logic doesn't change feeling
**Move:** Teach the model: old-brain motives loop with new-brain thinking and self-identity to produce panic, rumination, catastrophizing; the threat system runs conditioned safety strategies and doesn't listen to logic — knowing you're not to blame while feeling blame is a systems mismatch, not failure. Walk the imagery chain: imagined meal → salivation; external bully → cortisol; therefore self-criticism = internal bully stimulating your own threat physiology, compassionate imagery = internal carer stimulating soothing. Trace one of the person's own symptom loops through it.
**APPLY WHEN:** Early in work; despair that insight doesn't shift feeling; reading one's reactions as proof of being broken; introducing imagery.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used to dismiss feelings rather than de-shame them; oversold as established neuroscience (it's a teaching simplification); closes inquiry instead of opening functional analysis.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Psychoeducation from evolutionary/affective neuroscience; premise indirectly supported by fMRI (Longe et al. 2010) and imagery-physiology studies; de-shaming effect is clinical-experience level.

### [CFT:3CIRCLES] Map your three systems and test what your calm is made of
**Move:** Draw the three circles — threat, drive, soothing — sized by dominance; annotate what feeds each and what history grew them; shift from diagnosis-as-identity to system balance. Include the safeness check: is calm produced by safeness or safety-seeking — does the strategy (isolation, withdrawal, appeasement) prevent a feared outcome while also blocking affiliative contact ("fine alone" while affiliative capacity shrinks)?
**APPLY WHEN:** Onboarding the model; assessment; spotting drive-as-threat-compensation; a diagnosis has become an identity.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used to prove one's brain is broken; used to bypass real environmental causes (poverty, bullying, abusive relationship); framed as demanding protective strategies be dropped before any soothing capacity exists.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Three-system model has partial affective-neuroscience grounding; the exercise and safeness check are clinical practice plus CBT safety-behaviour literature.

### [CFT:4DOM] Four-domain functional formulation of a stuck pattern
**Move:** Map a recurring difficulty: innate + historical influences → key external threats and internal threats (what may erupt inside) plus unmet needs → safety strategies → unintended consequences (symptoms, self-criticism, entrenchment) looping back. Ask what you had to get good at to stay safe. For threat specifically, analyse six dimensions: triggers/meanings, response form, speed/intensity, duration and what calms it, frequency and internal drivers (self-criticism, rumination, worry), and how coping amplifies the rest.
**APPLY WHEN:** A stuck pattern needs a non-pathologizing explanation; symptoms feel like defects; before choosing an intervention target.
**AVOID WHEN:** Mapping starts reliving abuse/trauma memories — to a therapist, as with any solo digging into attachment or abuse history; tick-box schema hunting; rumination about rumination.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Author's framework on CBT safety-behaviour work (Salkovskis) and attachment research; method-level at best; reframing safety strategies as evolved protection is itself de-shaming.

### [CFT:IDEAL-IMAGE] Build and receive from an ideal compassionate image
**Move:** Construct a personal, client-designed image with deliberately superhuman qualities: deep commitment to you, wisdom from lived struggle (understands unchosen brains and safety strategies from inside), strength not overwhelmed, warmth, acceptance plus commitment to help. Practise receiving its three wishes (well, happy, free of suffering) and unconditional valuing — it already knows everything you're ashamed of and stays committed; it backs genuine well-being (supports quitting drinking without condemning setbacks; won't endorse anorexic or suicidal goals). Worksheet if vague: visual qualities, voice tone, other senses, how it relates to you, you to it. Nonhuman first forms (tree, sea, sun) are common after neglect/abuse and humanize over time; a compassionate colour entering at the heart is the gentlest entry when personified images threaten. If the image turns critical or weak, rebuild the qualities.
**APPLY WHEN:** Training the capacity to receive compassion; high-shame clients (expect slow progress); warmth tolerable at least briefly.
**AVOID WHEN:** Framed as substitute for real relationships (parallel process, not either/or); untreated barriers dominate (fear it will reject or vanish, entrenched undeserving beliefs) — work those first; hostile images in complex cases need a therapist; forcing vividness — the felt sense of another mind wishing you well is the point.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Pilot (Gilbert & Irons 2004); case series in voice-hearers (Mayhew & Gilbert 2008); imagining being loved benefits even atheists (Newberg & Waldman 2007); no controlled trial.

### [CFT:COMPSELF] Become the compassionate self (method-acting), plus daily micro-doses
**Move:** After soothing breathing, imagine BEING a deeply compassionate person, dwelling on four qualities in turn: wisdom ("we all just find ourselves here" with unchosen brains), strength (calm inner authority), warmth, non-condemnation with desire to help; create the facial expression, tone, posture; imagine expanding into mature power. Variant: recall yourself at your best, calmly helping — focus on your wanting-to-help, not the other's distress. Feeling the qualities is not required; imagining having them is the practice. Micro-dose daily: minutes on waking under the covers, at the bus stop, before meetings.
**APPLY WHEN:** Cornerstone practice, built BEFORE compassion-for-critic, memory work or letters; first move for abuse histories that can't yet receive compassion from others; building the daily habit.
**AVOID WHEN:** Judged by whether feelings arrive ("doing it right" monitoring recruits threat); the recalled episode centres on someone's acute distress; micro-doses fully replace longer practice.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Acting/identity techniques plus correlational identity-goal research (Crocker & Canevello 2008); physiological rationale (Rockliff 2008; Longe 2010); no isolated trial.

### [CFT:SBR] Soothing breathing rhythm as universal preparation
**Move:** Sit straight, feet grounded; breathe slightly deeper and slower than usual, attending to the felt slowing (no counting); optionally contrast with deliberate fast breathing; frame bodily tension as protection to thank and release, not badness to purge. Precedes every imagery exercise.
**APPLY WHEN:** Preparation for all imagery/compassionate-self work; engaging distress or self-criticism in the moment.
**AVOID WHEN:** Breathing focus itself alarms (documented in chronic mental-health groups) — desensitize slowly or substitute another mindful anchor (the tennis-ball workaround, Gilbert & Procter 2006).
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Author's protocol (full version in The Compassionate Mind, pp. 224–228); arousal-regulation rationale; no standalone trial.

### [CFT:SAFEPLACE] Safe-place imagery where the place itself welcomes you
**Move:** Build a multisensory imagined place of safeness (sights, sounds, smells, textures); relax the body, allow a soft smile; crucially, imagine the place itself taking joy in your being there — targeting the feeling of being wanted, not just low arousal.
**APPLY WHEN:** First imagery exercise; establishing a soothing anchor to return to.
**AVOID WHEN:** High agitation (physical activity works better); used as escape/avoidance; depression blunts the feelings — then trying is the target, feelings may follow.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Standard therapeutic imagery adapted by the author; the welcoming-place element is theory-driven, untested separately.

### [CFT:COMP-FOCUS] Aim the compassionate self at a struggling part
**Move:** Name the parts present (bullying, perfectionist, frightened self — what each thinks, feels, wants) without identifying with any; then settle into the compassionate self and image the anxious or self-critical self in front of you: observe its face and rushing feelings, surround it with compassion, imagine it receiving all the support it needs and watch the image change; optionally run the film onward — coping, recovering, legitimate pride. For self-criticism, find the threat behind the critic (fear of rejection, of letting oneself down) and direct compassion at that fear. Recall in vivo when the state recurs.
**APPLY WHEN:** Everyday anxiety or self-criticism; feeling globally "bad" or flooded by contradictory reactions; some compassionate-self practice exists.
**AVOID WHEN:** Letting the critical part vent at length first (the goal is building the compassionate pattern, not exposure to the critic); getting pulled in without ability to re-anchor; abuser-voice critics (therapist protocol); part-naming slides into a new self-label.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Author's clinical method; its ~10 hypothesized mentalizing mechanisms explicitly await research; convergent with an 11th-century practice (Machig Labdron).

### [CFT:WARMREAD] Re-hear alternative thoughts in a warm tone — two-pass protocol
**Move:** Generate alternative/balanced thoughts (validation, common humanity, specific successes, help-seeking as compassionate behaviour), rate helpfulness 1–10; then one minute soothing breathing + one minute compassionate self; re-read the same alternatives slowly with maximum warmth, attending to felt tone over logic; re-rate and compare. Diagnostic: does the alternative currently sound cold, analytical or irritated?
**APPLY WHEN:** Alternative thoughts feel true but hollow — rational reframes with no emotional shift.
**AVOID WHEN:** Content-tinkering replaces the state shift — the engaged affect system is the active ingredient; warmth itself triggers dread, grief or contempt (then fear of compassion is the work).
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Author's demonstration exercise; premise (emotion system changes the impact of identical cognition) consistent with Brewin's retrieval-competition account; untested standalone.

### [CFT:SETBACK-SEQ] Eight-step setback protocol
**Move:** For an acute non-traumatic setback: 1) validate with "given X happened, it is understandable to feel Y" — never followed by a reversal; 2) tolerate the emotion without fighting it; 3) look BEHIND the self-criticism to the fears and sadnesses, direct compassion there; 4) soothing breathing; 5) refocus on helpful material (past successes, supporters); 6) imagine the compassionate self handling this; 7) counter black-and-white thinking while validating its pull, practising alternatives in a kind inner voice; 8) choose supportive short- and long-term actions.
**APPLY WHEN:** Acute setbacks with self-critical aftermath; distress being dismissed or minimized (by others or oneself).
**AVOID WHEN:** The setback tears open trauma material; chair-work and deep-letter extensions belong in therapy; validation slides into pathologizing or pressure to change the feeling.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Author's composite; components have method-level CBT support, the sequence untested; validation step shared with alliance literature (Leahy, Linehan).

### [CFT:NOTFAULT] Not chosen — still yours to train
**Move:** State plainly, about the specific strategy, that the passions and coping moves in a mind were built by evolution and unchosen circumstances ("drifting into drugs was your attempt to connect and feel better") — then immediately hand over responsibility for training the mind now (the garden: leave it and it still grows, just tangled). Anchor with the three reality checks (Point 6): evolved mind (brains are tricky, not chosen), tragic mind (illness, loss, death are structural, not personal failures), social mind (you are one of many possible versions — who would you be if born elsewhere?). Deliver slowly, until it lands emotionally.
**APPLY WHEN:** Hot self-blame for coping with clear survival logic; shame blocking engagement; a "something is wrong with me" diagnosis story.
**AVOID WHEN:** Only the first half delivered — no-fault without responsibility becomes a reason not to change; delivered as consolation over a live, changeable problem; said as a slogan without felt connection.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Author's clinical experience, including groups with severe borderline presentations; no component trial.

### [CFT:PLAYEXP] Behavioural experiments against threat-based striving
**Move:** Schedule non-achievement fun, playfulness, deliberately "pointless" pleasant activity; treat discomfort with contentment and affiliation as a desensitization target; distinguish enjoying-having from must-have, value-based from validation-seeking achievement. Rebalancing, not dropping goals — behavioural activation stays.
**APPLY WHEN:** Perfectionism where success gives only temporary relief; striving welded to identity; underlying aloneness themes — treat the connection deficit, not just the striving.
**AVOID WHEN:** Framed as abandoning ambition (discredits the work — CFT is not soothing-only); severe fear of positive emotion — grade it first.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Behavioural-experiment methodology (method-level CBT); the achievement-motivation distinctions are correlational (Dykman; Dunkley; Gilbert et al. 2007; Crocker & Canevello 2008).

### [CFT:KIND-IN] Train the receiving channel — kindness noticing and received-kindness memory
**Move:** Against threat-biased attention: notice and deliberately dwell on small kindnesses received daily (a shop assistant's smile counts), switching the attentional spotlight from threat rumination to kindness. Deepen with memory: breathing, compassionate posture and expression, then recall someone being kind to you NOT at high distress — a minute each on their face and movement toward you, words and tone, the emotion they felt for you, then the whole scene, letting gratitude grow. Principle: sensory-rich dwelling — merely thinking people can be kind doesn't stimulate the affect systems.
**APPLY WHEN:** Threat-biased rumination; zero-setup micro-practice needed; accessible positive relational memories exist.
**AVOID WHEN:** Becomes forced positivity invalidating real mistreatment (hostile rejection of the premise signals pacing work first); very few such memories or the exercise floods; distress-centred memories.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Rationale from negativity-bias research (Baumeister et al. 2001; Öhman et al. 2001 — hostile faces found faster than happy); interventions are clinical practice.

### [CFT:FEAR-OF-COMPASSION] Work the fear — and normalize the absence of feeling
**Move:** When compassion practice produces resistance, aversion or nothing, treat that as the work. Map it: greatest fears in becoming compassionate; key blocks; what goes wrong when visualizing a compassionate future self; think in avoided emotion systems, not single emotions. When no warm feeling comes, normalize: soothing may be underdeveloped by history, suppressed by threat, or blunted by depression; redirect the target from feelings to desires, motives, intentions — the fitness analogy: the body responds to training before you feel different, and nobody says they don't deserve to get fit. Proceed only with explicit collaboration.
**APPLY WHEN:** Resistance, flatness or aversive reactions to any compassion exercise — before pushing more practice; undeserving thoughts (expected in high shame).
**AVOID WHEN:** Used to override reluctance — collaboration is a precondition; numbness is actually dissociation from opened trauma, or conditioned trauma surfaces — escalate to therapist.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Author's framework; preliminary measure data — fears of giving, receiving and self-directed compassion all correlate with stress, anxiety, depression, self-directed strongest; qualitative support (Pauley & McPherson).

### [CFT:MEMWORK] Shame-memory work: unpack, re-engage, re-script
**Move:** After the compassionate self is developed: decompose the shame memory into conditioned components (trigger affect, the other's attack, injected verbal labels, others withdrawing, submissive body defence, aloneness afterwards, entrapment) and intervene per component rather than disputing a summary belief; staged re-engagement — then-vs-now body contrast, imagery re-scripting with new elements (helpers, support to the remembered self, new endings) with the compassionate self in control throughout, assertiveness toward the perpetrator in imagery/empty chair, unsent letters, desensitizing fear of one's own rage; pick target elements (powerlessness / aloneness / entrapment / betrayal) one at a time. Compassion toward the perpetrator comes much later, never first; the therapist titrates by felt safety.
**APPLY WHEN:** Trauma-linked shame memories with intrusion/hyperarousal maintain current threat responses; belief-level disputation has failed.
**AVOID WHEN:** Solo, always; pacing beyond client consent — never further than feels okay; no compassionate-self grounding.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** Imagery re-scripting has method-level trial support outside CFT (Brewin et al. 2009; Wheatley et al. 2007; Lee 2005); the conditioning account and CFT sequencing are the author's protocol.

### [CFT:COMP-CHAIR] Asymmetric compassionate chair work, with the abuser-voice gate
**Move:** Unlike balanced two-chair dialogue, the aim is strengthening the compassion chair. Client sits in a part's chair, recalls a mild instance (anger, anxiety, self-criticism), voices its feelings and body sensations; then physically moves to a facing chair, rebuilds soothing breathing and the compassionate self, and only from there engages the part: what threat drives it, what would the compassionate self say, how would the part feel if fully helped. If pulled into the part's affect, break contact and rebuild before re-engaging. Mandatory gate before befriending any critic: screen whether it is felt as the internalized voice of a past abuser — if so, mark it not-self and handle differently (assertive confrontation, inviting it to leave, empty-chair, trauma re-scripting, per client preference).
**APPLY WHEN:** Entrenched self-attacking where the felt relationship to the critic must change; client accepts the parts framing; start mild, scale to ability.
**AVOID WHEN:** Solo (vulnerable-parts dialogue can open trauma and dissociation); before any compassionate-self capacity exists — the critic simply wins; the part repeatedly overwhelms and cannot be re-anchored.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** Greenberg's emotion-focused chair work with process-experiential research (Whelton & Greenberg 2005, method-level); rationale from Brewin 2006; the CFT variant and gate untested separately.

### [CFT:POSEXP] Graded exposure to warmth and closeness
**Move:** Treat fear of warmth, care and closeness as an exposure target: approach affiliative feeling in graded steps, surface the attached beliefs ("if you really knew me you'd leave", "needing someone is weakness"), work through the resulting grief and resistance — positive affiliative affect needs desensitization just as threat does, because closeness historically predicted shame, rejection or dependency.
**APPLY WHEN:** The person is dismissive of, contemptuous toward, or frightened by kindness from self or others; compassion practice keeps triggering threat.
**AVOID WHEN:** Attempted solo — it opens attachment loss, abuse memory and dependency terror.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** Author's clinical experience (the model's originating case); physiologically consistent with Rockliff et al. 2008; not separately trialled.

### [CFT:BASEFIRST] Sequence: build the compassion base before aversive material
**Move:** Establish a working compassionate self and inner compassion base BEFORE engaging trauma memory, avoided emotions or feared material, and approach that material from the base as the stance — not as a preliminary relaxation trick.
**APPLY WHEN:** Aversive memories or emotions must eventually be faced and the person has some access to soothing.
**AVOID WHEN:** Solo; the compassion base itself still triggers threat — that fear comes first (see [CFT:FEAR-OF-COMPASSION]).
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** Author's clinical model; the sequencing claim is clinical, not trial-tested.

### [CFT:GRIEF-WORK] Grieving the care that never came
**Move:** Give space to grieve the childhood one wanted but did not have — the loss of what never happened (feeling loved, protected), not only of what did; tolerate sadness and yearning as emotions to work through, not around; gentle collaborative persistence over months, individual rather than group settings. The route to compassion commonly awakens this grief; Gilbert argues capacity to grieve it is often essential — explicitly against the view that grieving the past is unhelpful.
**APPLY WHEN:** Compassion work triggers deep sadness and yearning; trauma backgrounds including borderline presentations (linked to intolerance of this grief); recovery after major illness or psychosis.
**AVOID WHEN:** Solo; early-stage overwhelm with dissociation; tears that are perseverative frustrated self-pity rather than grief — named as when grieving doesn't help.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** Explicitly weak — the author states good evidence is lacking; clinical reports (Gilbert & Procter 2006 pilot) and anecdote.

## What the book debunks

- **"Self-compassion is weakness, self-indulgence, lowered standards"** — the near-universal objection (even among therapists); it sits on self-dislike, not reasoned argument. Compassionate self-correction keeps standards; only the emotion brought to improving changes.
- **Compassion ≠ submissive niceness** — it includes boundaries, honesty, refusing what someone wants in favour of what they need; kindness performed for approval is a documented counterfeit.
- **Compassion ≠ never feeling anger** — it begins with honesty about one's feelings; "I'm not an angry person" commonly flags feared, unprocessed rage; impotent rage maintains depression.
- **Self-compassionate behaviour ≠ pampering** — it is doing what flourishing requires, including hard confrontations (leaving a relationship and bearing the guilt).
- **Rational disputation fails in high shame** — evidence-for-and-against bounces off an angry-dominant critic; alternatives only help if they can be FELT as reassuring. The book's founding observation.
- **Pushing compassion exercises harder on high self-critics is the documented failure mode** — their physiology can answer compassion attempts with a threat signature (Rockliff et al. 2008); the fear of compassion is the target, not resistance to overcome.
- **Befriending an abuser-derived critic is a category error** — flagged explicitly; different protocol required.
- **"I can't visualize" / "my mind wanders too much"** — imagery is automatic and needn't be vivid; frequent noticing of wandering means the practice is working.
- **Crisis-only practice guarantees false negatives**; imagery fragments bolted on outside the model underperform — Gilbert warns therapists directly.
- **Soothing ≠ low arousal; "fine alone" can be safety-seeking** — isolation calms while starving the system that regulates threat; physical comforting can misfire as silencing when the person needs to be heard.
- **Problem-list assessment misses shame** — clients conceal central material through whole therapies (MacDonald & Morley 2001; Swan & Andrews 2003); idealized childhood reports often dissolve later.
- **Neff's self-compassion construct ≠ the CFT model**; many self-criticism scales actually measure social comparison or low self-esteem; shamelessness is not the goal — guilt must be learned and tolerated.

## Numbers that matter

- Rockliff et al. 2008: compassion imagery raised heart-rate variability and lowered cortisol in LOW self-critics; in HIGH self-critics it lowered HRV (threat signature), no cortisol change — the most application-relevant finding.
- Longe et al. 2010 (fMRI): distinct neural patterns for self-criticism vs self-reassurance; high self-critics show threat-like activation when attempting self-reassurance.
- Fredrickson et al. 2008: loving-kindness, 67 vs 72 waiting-list, six weekly 60-minute sessions — passive comparator only.
- Laithwaite et al. 2009: group CFT, 19 clients, high-security setting — large changes in depression and self-esteem, moderate in social comparison, small in shame; held at 6-week follow-up; uncontrolled.
- Gilbert & Procter 2006: small uncontrolled pilot — reduced shame, self-criticism, depression, anxiety. Pace et al. 2008: six weeks of compassion meditation improved immune/neuroendocrine stress responses.
- ~70% of voices in psychosis are hostile and critical (Gilbert & Irons 2005); two forms of self-criticism (inadequacy vs self-hatred; the latter rare outside clinical samples).
- Structure constants: 3 affect systems; 4 formulation domains; 4 compassionate-self qualities; 4 imagery orientations; 8-point letter checklist; 2 compassion circles; ~2 min/day micro-practice floor; SBR protocol at pp. 224–228 of The Compassionate Mind (Gilbert 2009a).
- Fear-of-compassion (preliminary): fears of giving, receiving and self-directed compassion all correlate with stress, anxiety, depression — self-directed strongest.
- As the book went to press (2010), RCTs were only being planned — the author says so himself.

## Blind spots of this book

- **No active-treatment comparisons anywhere.** Every quantitative result is uncontrolled, pilot, physiological, or vs waiting lists — consistent with later findings of CFT superiority only over passive controls. The book cannot say when CFT should be chosen over CBT, EFT or DBT, whose techniques it borrows; the incremental value of the compassion frame itself is asserted via mechanism, never measured.
- **Author-sourced evidence throughout.** Nearly every move is Gilbert's clinical practice; the key sequencing claims (base first, compassionate self before trauma work) and the mentalizing mechanisms are labelled hypotheses — credit for honesty, but a lens must not upgrade them to findings.
- **No dosage, duration or selection guidance** beyond "months" and "grade it"; grief-work evidence admitted lacking; guilt tolerance named as almost unresearched.
- **Societal claims are argument, not data** — competitive-culture-as-pathogen (via Wilkinson & Pickett) is cultural critique riding alongside the clinical model.
- **Genuinely threatening presents are under-served.** The model warns against bypassing real causes (poverty, abusive relationships) but offers little for when the threat system is responding accurately to current danger rather than to history.
- **The known harm case has no protocolized remedy.** High self-critics for whom compassion fires threat get a direction (work the fear, grade, collaborate) but no tested protocol — the failure mode is documented better than its fix.
- **Measurement is shaky under the edifice** — by the author's own account: self-criticism scales measuring adjacent constructs, no agreed cross-language definition of compassion, fear-of-compassion measures still in development at publication.
