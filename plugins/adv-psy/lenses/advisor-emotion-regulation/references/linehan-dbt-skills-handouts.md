# DBT Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets — digest

## What this book is for the lens
The operational workbook of the lens: exact protocols — numbered steps, worksheets, rating scales, decision trees — where other sources only explain. Mechanism-level thesis: an emotion is a full-system response (vulnerability → prompting event → interpretation → body → action urge → expression → aftereffects), and changing ANY component changes the whole response; every skill is an entry point into one component. Second load-bearing claim: above a personal "skills breakdown point" complicated skills are physiologically unusable, so skill choice is arousal-gated (body-chemistry first, cognition later). The author states outright there is NO research on this manual as self-help. Every skill works for someone; no skill works for everyone.

## Core model
- **Biosocial theory** (General Handouts 1–5): emotion dysregulation = biological vulnerability (higher sensitivity/intensity, slower return to baseline) × invalidating environment, maintained by transactions. Environments that give in only to escalation are training escalation.
- **Four options for any painful problem**: solve it, change the emotional response, radically accept it, or stay miserable — each with a matching skill set.
- **Three states of mind**: reasonable, emotion, Wise Mind (their synthesis) — skill choices are checked in Wise Mind throughout.
- **Module map** (anchors): General Skills (chain analysis); Mindfulness (Wise Mind; observe/describe/participate; nonjudgmentally/one-mindfully/effectively — Handouts 1–10); Interpersonal Effectiveness (DEAR MAN / GIVE / FAST, validation, Dime Game, behavior change — Handouts 1–22); Emotion Regulation (model of emotions, check the facts, opposite action, problem solving, ABC PLEASE — Handouts 1–25); Distress Tolerance (STOP, TIP, ACCEPTS, self-soothe, IMPROVE, radical acceptance, addiction D-C-B-A — Handouts 1–21).
- **Change routing** (ER Handouts 7–13): does the emotion fit the facts? is acting on it effective? → problem solving, opposite action, or mindful acceptance. Facts first; acceptance where change is impossible.
- **Arousal gating** (ER Handouts 23–25): moderate arousal → regulation skills; past breakdown point → crisis survival first, mindfulness of the emotion second, regulation third.
- **Assumptions**: people are doing the best they can AND need to do better; may not have caused their problems but must solve them anyway; new behavior must be practiced in every context where needed.

## Moves
Shared baseline for every move ("package-level"): Linehan 2015 component analysis + Neacsiu 2014 RCT (adults without BPD); facilitated groups, self-study untested. Lineage noted where it adds strength.

### [ER:NAME] Observe and name the emotion before changing it
**Move:** Walk one current emotion through the full model (ER Handouts 4–6, Worksheets 2–4a): prompting event in sensory facts, interpretation, body changes, action urge, expression, aftereffects; pick the exact label from the 10-emotion catalog; rate intensity 0–100. Record what the emotion is DOING for you — a reinforced emotion resists change.
**APPLY WHEN:** Emotion unclear, mislabeled, or blended; before any change skill; recurring emotion with unclear payoff.
**AVOID WHEN:** Past the skills breakdown point; mid-crisis — this is analysis, not regulation.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; catalog adapted from Shaver et al. 1987.

### [ER:ROUTE] Fits-facts × effectiveness decision tree
**Move:** Two questions route every strong emotion (ER Handout 7, Worksheet 6): fits facts + acting effective → act, problem-solve; fits + ineffective → don't act on the urge, consider opposite action; doesn't fit → change interpretation AND act opposite. Per-emotion fit criteria (fear fits only real threat to life/health; guilt only your OWN values violated; shame only real rejection risk by people you care about).
**APPLY WHEN:** Any strong emotion where the next move is unclear.
**AVOID WHEN:** Past breakdown point; before the emotion is named; unsure you want change (pros/cons first).
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; core routing logic of the tested module.

### [ER:CHECKFACTS] Check the facts
**Move:** Six steps (ER Handout 8, Worksheet 5): name the emotion; describe the prompting event stripped of judgments; list interpretations plus alternatives — if unverifiable, adopt a likely or useful one; label the assumed threat, estimate its real probability; imagine the worst plausible outcome AND yourself coping with it; rate 0–5 whether emotion fits the facts (2–4 = keep checking or run an experiment).
**APPLY WHEN:** Emotion seems disproportionate; before choosing opposite action vs problem solving; catastrophizing.
**AVOID WHEN:** Arguing yourself out of a justified emotion (fact-checking can't change an emotion that already fits); extreme arousal.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; cognitive-appraisal method shared with CBT's trial base.

### [ER:OPPACT] Opposite action, all the way
**Move:** Seven steps (ER Handout 10, Worksheet 7): name emotion → check justification incl. intensity → identify urge → ask Wise Mind if acting is effective → identify the opposite → do it ALL the way (behavior + posture + face + voice) → repeat until the emotion shifts. Opposites: fear → approach repeatedly; anger → gently avoid, be kind, imagine their perspective; envy → count ALL blessings; jealousy → stop surveillance; unjustified love → cut all contact and reminders; sadness → get active, bright posture; unjustified shame → disclose to accepting people; unjustified guilt → repeat the behavior openly. Guilt justified, shame not → apologize, repair, let go; shame justified, guilt not → hide the behavior, or change the group's values, or change groups.
**APPLY WHEN:** Emotion doesn't fit the facts, or acting on it would be ineffective.
**AVOID WHEN:** Emotion justified AND acting effective (problem-solve); real danger; trauma-cued fear — approach work on trauma belongs with the therapist; half-hearted application ("all the way" is a requirement).
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; strongest lineage in the book — exposure generalized (fear), behavioral activation (sadness).

### [ER:PROBSLV] Problem solving
**Move:** Seven steps (ER Handout 12, Worksheet 8): describe the problem; check the facts; set a simple achievable goal; brainstorm without critique; pick 1–2 solutions via pros/cons; execute as listed steps; evaluate — if failed, loop back.
**APPLY WHEN:** Emotion fits the facts and the prompting situation is changeable.
**AVOID WHEN:** Facts are wrong (fix appraisal first); event unchangeable — acceptance skills instead.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; classic problem-solving-therapy structure.

### [ER:WAVE] Mindfulness of current emotion — ride the wave, surf the urge
**Move:** Observe the emotion as a wave (ER Handout 22, Worksheet 15): don't block or push away, don't hold or amplify; locate the body sensations; time how long it lasts; notice the action urge without acting (urge surfing — same move for cravings); you are not your emotion and need not act on it. Claim: suppression INCREASES suffering and blocks nearly every other skill — load-bearing for the whole system.
**APPLY WHEN:** Emotion painful but arousal workable; no change route applies; impulse where acting would cost.
**AVOID WHEN:** Past breakdown point (crisis survival first); mistaken for suppression; crisis-level self-harm urges — escalate to crisis skills and the therapist.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; urge surfing shared with relapse-prevention literature.

### [ER:BRKPOINT] Skills breakdown point and the extreme-emotion sequence
**Move:** Learn your markers (ER Handout 23): extreme distress, overwhelm, attention locked on the emotion, mind shutting down. First check the facts ("am I really falling apart?"); if no → regulation skills; if yes → fixed order: crisis survival (TIP, distract, soothe, IMPROVE) → mindfulness of current emotion → other ER skills. Keep a written skills list for when recall fails.
**APPLY WHEN:** Repeated "skills don't work when I need them most"; too aroused to think.
**AVOID WHEN:** Arousal actually moderate; "past breakdown" as blanket excuse — the fact-check exists for that; never a substitute for solving the underlying problem.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; author's arousal-gating protocol.

### [ER:TIP] TIP — change body chemistry directly
**Move:** (DT Handouts 6–6b) Temperature: holding breath, cold water/pack on face 30 s, water above 50°F — dive response (onset 15–30 s) drops heart rate; Intense exercise: brief burst to discharge arousal; Paced breathing: ~5–6 breaths/min, exhale longer than inhale (5 s in / 7 s out) — most portable; Paired muscle relaxation: tense on inhale 5–6 s, exhale "Relax", train 3–4×/day until exhale alone triggers release.
**APPLY WHEN:** Emotion too high for any cognitive skill; arousal must drop in minutes; dangerous urges.
**AVOID WHEN:** Cold water/exercise: heart or medical conditions, beta-blockers, cold allergy, eating disorder — consult a provider first (book's explicit caution); PMR untrained in crisis; cold water loses effect amid activity.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Dive-response/slow-breathing physiology independently established; PMR from Smith 1980; package-level as protocol.

### [ER:STOP] STOP — insert a gap between urge and action
**Move:** Stop (freeze); Take a step back (break, breathe); Observe (situation, thoughts, feelings, others' actions); Proceed mindfully (weigh consequences in Wise Mind). (DT Handout 4.)
**APPLY WHEN:** Impulse to act on strong emotion in a way likely to worsen the situation.
**AVOID WHEN:** No stated contraindication; crisis skills are not for everyday problem solving.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; adapted from Perepletchikova and Axelrod.

### [ER:PROSCONS] Pre-written pros and cons, consumed at urge time
**Move:** While calm, fill all four quadrants — pros/cons of acting on the urge vs resisting (same format for crisis urges, practice motivation, power-vs-passivity; DT Handout 5, ER Worksheet 1, General Worksheet 1); carry the sheet; at urge time REREAD it and recall past consequences of giving in. Framing: a good day or a good life.
**APPLY WHEN:** Recurrent predictable dangerous/addictive/give-up urges; wavering motivation; willfulness.
**AVOID WHEN:** Filled only on one side (the contrast is the mechanism); written once and lost (findability at urge time is the point); rationalizing a choice already made in emotion mind.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; standard motivational component.

### [ER:CRISISMENU] Distract, self-soothe, improve the moment
**Move:** Three menus for surviving acute distress in place (DT Handouts 7–9a): Wise Mind ACCEPTS — Activities, Contributing, Comparisons, opposite Emotions, Pushing away (time-limited), Thoughts, Sensations; Self-soothe via five senses; IMPROVE — Imagery, Meaning, Prayer, Relaxing actions, One thing in the moment, brief Vacation (an hour to a day), self-Encouragement with pre-rehearsed lines. Log distress 0–100 before/after and effectiveness 1–5 to learn which channels work for you.
**APPLY WHEN:** Crisis-level distress unsolvable now; distress endured for hours or days; self-punishing mode blocking comfort.
**AVOID WHEN:** Distraction/soothing becomes chronic avoidance of a solvable problem; "vacation" stretches into escape; the soothing channel is itself the addiction; guilt about "deserving" soothing is a target, not a stop sign.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; sensory items adapted from Goldfried & Davison 1976.

### [ER:COPEAHEAD] Cope ahead — imaginal rehearsal of coping
**Move:** Five steps (ER Handout 19): describe the upcoming hard situation factually, naming likely interfering emotions/urges; script specific coping skills; imagine being IN the situation (not watching yourself); rehearse coping incl. complications and your feared catastrophe; finish with relaxation. Standard rehearsal vehicle for DEAR MAN scripts and relationship endings.
**APPLY WHEN:** Predictable emotional situations; anticipatory anxiety; planned high-stakes interactions.
**AVOID WHEN:** Rehearsal target is traumatic-memory material — therapist-guided; skipping the closing relaxation.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; imaginal-rehearsal method.

### [ER:ABCPLEASE] Reduce vulnerability: ABC PLEASE
**Move:** Prevention layer, scheduled and tracked (ER Handouts 14–20, Worksheets 9–14): Accumulate positives short-term (≥1 daily item from the 225-item Pleasant Events List, attended mindfully, deliberately UNmindful of worries) and long-term (values ladder: ONE value from the 58-item list → 2–3 goals → one goal → small steps → first step today; check values are truly yours); Build mastery (daily ≥1 difficult-but-possible task); Cope ahead; PLEASE — treat PhysicaL illness, balanced Eating, Avoid mood-altering substances, Sleep 7–9 h, Exercise to 20 min/day.
**APPLY WHEN:** Always-on baseline; chronically low positive emotion; helplessness; emptiness between crises.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used as crisis response; pleasant events as avoidance of a solvable problem; mastery overshooting into engineered failure; too-big value steps — shrink, don't abandon.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; behavioral-activation lineage; events list from Linehan, Sharp & Ivanoff 1980; values from Schwartz 1992.

### [ER:SLEEP] Sleep hygiene and nightmare rescripting
**Move:** Hygiene ladder (ER Handout 20b): fixed schedule incl. weekends, naps ≤10 min, bed not for daytime use, no late stimulants/alcohol/heavy meals, cool dark room; 30–60 min to fall asleep, then triage — calm-but-awake → get up, return when sleepy; ruminating → cold-water TIP + paced breathing, 9–0 countdown, reframe as "middle-of-the-night thinking"; unstoppable → solve if solvable, else cope-ahead the catastrophe. Nightmare protocol (20a): stabilize first; write the recurring dream in sensory detail; write a changed version altered BEFORE anything bad happens; rehearse changed dream + relaxation nightly until recurrence stops.
**APPLY WHEN:** Sleep-onset trouble, worry-driven waking, recurring manageable nightmares.
**AVOID WHEN:** Cold-water TIP without medical clearance; catastrophizing sleeplessness (never start the "day" at night); trauma nightmares solo — the book defers them; detailed write-out of trauma content belongs in therapy.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; stimulus-control and imagery-rehearsal adaptations (independent method-level support).

### [ER:RADACC] Radical acceptance, sustained by turning the mind, willingness, and the body
**Move:** Accept unchangeable facts with the whole self (DT Handouts 10–15a, Worksheets 8–12): notice fighting reality → facts are facts → acknowledge the causal history → practice via self-talk, breath, imagery, opposite action, cope ahead → allow disappointment/sadness/grief → life can be worth living with pain. Targets must be verified FACTS (Worksheet 9: strip interpretations and judgments first). Sustainers: Turning the mind — acceptance is a repeated fork-choice, recommit at every drift; Willingness — label willfulness, radically accept feeling willful, ask what the threat is, do one willing behavior; Half-smiling + willing hands — relaxed face, lips slightly up, open palms (face and hands feed back to the brain). Rated 0–5 (5 = at peace) — the outcome is peace, not lower arousal; sadness often comes first, then calm.
**APPLY WHEN:** Unchangeable facts keep producing bitterness, "why me"; acceptance keeps collapsing into resentment; body-first entry when cognitive routes fail.
**AVOID WHEN:** Situation changeable (problem-solve); "acceptance" tolerating ongoing abuse or fixable harm; willingness mislabeling compliance with harmful demands; traumatic loss flooding beyond capacity — pace with therapist; forced tense grin (masking).
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; core DBT component; half-smile adapted from Thich Nhat Hanh.

### [ER:MOT] Mindfulness of current thoughts — thoughts as brain events, not facts
**Move:** Catch a thought, label it ("the thought [X] went through my mind"; DT Handout 15, Worksheets 12–12a), then defuse: repeat it fast/slow/sung until it loses grip; imagine mind as conveyor belt, river, clouds; act as if you didn't believe every thought; ask what body sensation the thought avoids and turn toward it. Practice on pleasant and neutral thoughts too.
**APPLY WHEN:** Fusion with catastrophic thoughts; worry loops; rumination (framed as escape from difficult bodily sensations).
**AVOID WHEN:** Turned into thought suppression; dismissing thoughts that need fact-checking and action; repetition of self-attack content that escalates shame.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; defusion-style techniques shared with ACT.

### [ER:MINDCORE] Mindfulness core: Wise Mind + what/how skills
**Move:** Access Wise Mind (Mindfulness Handouts 1–5c): breathe in "Wise", out "Mind"; ask a question on the in-breath and LISTEN for (not generate) an answer; 3-minute reset — name mental events, narrow to breath, expand to whole body. "What" skills, one at a time: Observe (wordless attention), Describe (words only on what came through the senses — others' motives are inference), Participate (full entry until self-consciousness dissolves). "How" qualities, combined: Nonjudgmentally (drop good/bad verdicts, keep harmful/helpful discrimination; don't judge your judging), One-mindfully ("just this one moment, just this one task"), Effectively (ask "is this effective?" at the first sign of needing to be "right"). Start in easy situations so the skill is automatic when needed.
**APPLY WHEN:** Foundation for every other skill; rumination and autopilot; decisions needing a check that isn't pure logic or pure mood.
**AVOID WHEN:** Expecting Wise Mind to calm (success = accessing wisdom, not feeling better); observing used to suppress experience; treating the "answer" as an oracle overriding fact-checking.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; contemplative sources (Thich Nhat Hanh) and MBCT adaptations (Segal et al. 2013).

### [ER:CHAIN] Chain analysis and missing-links analysis
**Move:** For a recurring problem behavior, 8 steps (General Handouts 7–8): describe it re-enactably; identify the environmental prompting event; list vulnerability factors; write every link; describe maintaining consequences; circle key links and write skillful replacements; build a prevention plan; repair the actual harm (restore trust by being trustworthy, not self-berating). For effective behavior that DIDN'T happen, 4 questions, stop at the first "no": knew what was needed? → willing? → did it enter my mind? → what got in the way?
**APPLY WHEN:** Recurring problem behavior with unclear triggers and maintainers; skipped commitments.
**AVOID WHEN:** Turned into rumination or proof of defectiveness instead of link-hunting; suicidal/self-injurious behavior — chain work on that belongs inside therapy.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; functional analysis — backbone of behavior therapies.

### [ER:TROUBLESHOOT] Skill-failure differential — failure is diagnostic, not proof of defect
**Move:** When a skill fails, run the ordered checklist instead of self-blame. ER version (Handout 24, Worksheet 16): biology → PLEASE, consider medication; executed exactly as instructed? → reread, get coaching; emotion being reinforced? → pros/cons, honesty; low effort? → willingness; overloaded? → TIP first; emotion myths? → challenge them. Interpersonal version (IE Handout 9): skill deficit / unclear objectives / urges beating goals / over breakdown point / blocking myths / environment more powerful than skills — each with a matched remedy.
**APPLY WHEN:** A plausible, correctly chosen skill produced no change; repeated interpersonal failures; you keep not trying.
**AVOID WHEN:** Skill never executed to the letter (checkpoint 2, not system failure); self-attack ammunition; environment genuinely holds the power — shift to acceptance and allies.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; explicitly rejects "skills always work if done right".

### [ER:DEARMAN] DEAR MAN as a pre-written script, calibrated by the Dime Game
**Move:** Before a consequential ask/refusal: rank three goals (objective / relationship / self-respect) per situation; set intensity with the Dime Game (IE Handout 8): 10 yes/no factors → 10-level ladder from "don't ask, don't hint" to "don't take no". Then WRITE the script (Worksheets 4–5): Describe facts; Express with "I want/don't want"; Assert explicitly (no mind-reading expected); Reinforce by naming their payoff; stay Mindful (broken record — circle the line; ignore attacks); Appear confident (no self-undercutting hedges); Negotiate ("what do you think we should do?"). Rehearse aloud + cope ahead; against a skilled persistent other, refocus on the interaction itself ("you keep asking though I've said no"). Log components used afterward.
**APPLY WHEN:** Asking, saying no, resisting pressure — any planned high-stakes interaction.
**AVOID WHEN:** Improvising the acronym live without rehearsal — the book's own workflow is script-first (matches lens evidence context); attack/threat/ultimatum delivery; imputing motives; using skills to manufacture reciprocity (explicitly rejected); Dime arithmetic overriding Wise Mind when factors are unequal.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level (Neacsiu 2014 closest population); assertiveness-training lineage (Linehan & Egan 1985).

### [ER:GIVEFAST] GIVE and FAST — protect the relationship and self-respect while asking
**Move:** GIVE: Gentle — no attacks, threats (suicide-threat leverage named verbatim as banned), moralizing, sneering; tolerate a no; act Interested; Validate; Easy manner. FAST: Fair to both sides (validate your own feelings too); no over-Apologizing (not for existing, requesting, or disagreeing — over-apologizing is self-invalidation); Stick to your values; be Truthful (no acting helpless when you're not).
**APPLY WHEN:** Relationship matters as much as the objective; leaving interactions ashamed or self-betrayed; chronic appeasement.
**AVOID WHEN:** "Effectiveness" or gentleness rationalizing a values violation — FAST is that boundary; Gentle's bans hold even when your objective is legitimate.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level.

### [ER:VALIDATE] Validation — of others, and of yourself after invalidation
**Move:** Six levels outward (IE Handouts 18–19): pay full attention; reflect back, open to correction; name the unspoken, stay correctable; show how their response makes sense given causes and history — even if you disapprove; acknowledge the valid IN ACTION (fair complaint → fix it); show equality. Inward after invalidation (19a): nondefensively check the facts; change what really was invalid; stand up for yourself when reasonable; drop judgmental self-talk; admit it hurts even if the critic is right; validate yourself exactly as you would someone else.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any charged interaction; before problem solving with someone who feels unheard; habitual self-invalidation; de-escalation.
**AVOID WHEN:** Validating the invalid (only what fits the facts); solving the problem when the person just wants to be heard; collapsing into "my experience must be wrong" or its mirror — blasting the other while possibly wrong.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; validation levels from Linehan 1997; validation ≠ agreement ≠ approval.

### [ER:DIALECT] Dialectics — walk the middle path
**Move:** Daily both-and practice (IE Handouts 15–16b): ask Wise Mind "what am I missing?"; find the kernel of truth in the other side; swap always/never for sometimes; play devil's advocate against your own position; let go of blame via multi-causation; make small deliberate changes to build change tolerance. Middle-path self-analysis: mark your pole on four dilemmas (reasonable/emotion mind, doing/being, change/acceptance, self-denial/self-indulgence), pick ONE, commit to 1–2 rebalancing actions per week.
**APPLY WHEN:** Either/or conflict, blame loops, rigid "I'm right"; living at one extreme or oscillating.
**AVOID WHEN:** Both-sidesing the genuinely invalid; dodging a needed stand; working someone else's middle path instead of your own values.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; adapted from Miller, Rathus & Linehan 2007.

### [ER:BEHAVIOR] Operant toolkit for changing your own and others' behavior
**Move:** (IE Handouts 20–22a) Reinforce: pre-specify behavior and a consequence the person would actually work for; deliver immediately, every instance at first, then thin to intermittent; shape small steps. Extinguish: remove the maintaining reinforcer, or satiate before the behavior; expect an extinction burst and hold steady; reinforce a replacement simultaneously. Punish last: specific, time-limited, proportional, natural consequences, no punitive tone; stop when behavior stops — punishment teaches nothing new, works only while the punisher is present, drives avoidance of the punisher.
**APPLY WHEN:** Building habits; a behavior that demonstrably "works" for someone (incl. your own escalation being rewarded).
**AVOID WHEN:** Giving in intermittently during a burst (makes behavior extremely hard to ever stop); delayed/wrong-sized rewards; punishment first-line or continued "to send a message"; assuming universal reinforcers — consequences are idiosyncratic.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Operant conditioning — strongest independent method-level science in the book.

### [ER:ADDICT] Dialectical abstinence + environment engineering for any addiction
**Move:** For any behavior you can't stop despite consequences — substances, food, screens, gambling, work (DT Handouts 16–21, Worksheets 13–18): commit to 100% abstinence AND pre-write a harm-reduction slip plan simultaneously ("a slip must not turn into a slide"): who to call, opposite action against guilt/shame, chain-analyze the lapse, repair, recommit. Monitor "clean mind" traps weekly (19-item list: "I've learned my lesson", "I can control it", keeping paraphernalia). Engineer the environment, not willpower: burn bridges (delete contacts, discard cues, announce publicly — the tiniest loophole lets the whole problem back in), build new ones (competing imagery/smells against cravings, urge surfing); community reinforcement (non-using companions, replacement pleasures, abstinence sampling); alternate rebellion when defiance is the real function; adaptive denial (reframe the urge as an urge for something else; postpone in 5-minute increments).
**APPLY WHEN:** Recurring urge/craving-structured behavior at a moment of genuine commitment; early-abstinence overconfidence; cue-saturated environments.
**AVOID WHEN:** Physiological dependence (alcohol, opioids) — withdrawal needs medical oversight; either pole alone (pure commitment lengthens post-slip falls; pure harm-reduction relapses quicker); burning bridges that sever needed supports rather than enablers; misdiagnosed function (rebellion tools leave a soothing-driven urge intact).
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Community Reinforcement Approach from Meyers & Squires 2001 (independent trials); dialectical abstinence from DBT-SUD work; NOT covered by the Neacsiu 2014 population.

### [ER:SAFEEXIT] Exiting an abusive relationship goes through DV professionals, not skills
**Move:** Before leaving a highly abusive or life-threatening relationship, call a domestic violence hotline first (US National DV Hotline 1-800-799-7233; international directory hotpeachpages.net) for safety planning and referral. Ordinary destructive-but-not-abusive relationships get the skills route: Wise Mind pros/cons → cope-ahead rehearsal → pre-written DEAR MAN GIVE FAST ending script — never ended mid-argument in emotion mind; repairable important relationships get problem solving first.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any physical abuse or threat to life/safety in a relationship being ended.
**AVOID WHEN:** Substituting interpersonal skills for professional exit help — the book's quiz marks "use your skills to tell an abuser you're leaving" as the wrong answer.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** Author's safety protocol aligned with standard DV practice.

### [ER:GRIEFINVAL] Trauma-adjacent work: grieving traumatic invalidation, exposure to trauma material
**Move:** Grieve traumatic invalidation and its harm; describe those experiences in a supportive environment; radically accept the invalidating person (IE Handout 19a extension). The same boundary covers every trauma-target variant in the book: opposite-action approach work on trauma-cued fear, half-smile contemplation of an abuser's image, detailed write-out of trauma nightmares, focused acceptance of traumatic loss — each flagged by the book itself as deferred or therapist-paced.
**APPLY WHEN:** A history of severe/traumatic invalidation drives present reactions; trauma material surfaces inside otherwise-SELF skills.
**AVOID WHEN:** Attempted solo while dysregulated — these steps deliberately open traumatic material; the SELF versions on ordinary targets remain available.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** Author's clinical extension; the carve-outs are the book's own stated deferrals.

## What the book debunks
- **Its own self-help framing**: no research on this manual as self-study — all evidence from facilitated groups; skills videos have no outcome data. Not every skill must be learned.
- **Emotion myths (20, ER Worksheet 3)**: there's a right way to feel; showing feelings is weakness; being emotional = out of control; extreme emotions get you further; changing emotions is inauthentic; emotions should always be trusted (correction: sometimes); emotions happen for no reason (all are caused). Intensity is not evidence about the world — signals, not facts.
- **Interpersonal myths (24)**: asking proves weakness; they should know without my asking; saying no is always selfish; revenge will be worth it. Maximal assertiveness is not always right (Dime Game); skills cannot manufacture reciprocity or make someone love you.
- **"Skills always work if done right"**: a more powerful environment can defeat correct skill use — remedy is allies and acceptance, not more drilling. Many "skill failures" are motivational: the emotion is being reinforced.
- **Radical acceptance ≠ approval, compassion, passivity, or opposition to change** — "accepting means condoning" is itself listed as the main interference factor.
- **Suppression**: avoiding/suppressing emotion increases suffering and blocks nearly every other skill; criticizing your emotions does not regulate them.
- **Punishment folklore**: teaches nothing new, works only while the punisher is present; intermittent giving-in trains escalation.
- **Addiction traps**: "I've learned my lesson", "I can control it", "willpower is enough", "I can do this alone" — documented relapse precursors; one slip does not doom you (abstinence-violation spiral explicitly countered).
- **Crisis-skill overuse**: crisis survival skills are for short-term crises, not everyday problems or life building — over-reliance is a named misuse.
- **Sleep catastrophizing**: staying awake is uncomfortable, not a catastrophe; the fix is stimulus control, not forcing sleep.

## Numbers that matter
- **Ratings**: emotion/distress 0–100 before/after every practice; effectiveness 1–5 (anchor: 5 = "tolerated distress, resisted problem urges" — urge-resistance, not feeling good); acceptance 0–5 (5 = at peace); fit-the-facts certainty 0–5 (2–4 = keep checking).
- **TIP physiology**: cold water ≥50°F, 30 s on face, dive-response onset 15–30 s; paced breathing ~5–6 breaths/min, exhale > inhale; PMR 5–6 s tension, 3–4 practices/day.
- **PLEASE targets**: sleep 7–9 h consistent; exercise to 20 min/day; naps ≤10 min; 30–60 min fall-asleep window before triage.
- **Dosage**: each crisis skill drilled ≥2×/week; half-smile and thought-watching 2×/day; ≥1 pleasant event and ≥1 mastery task daily; 1–2 rebalancing actions per week (not more).
- **Catalogs**: 10 emotions × 6 layers; 225 pleasant events; 58 values; 20 + 24 myths; 6 validation levels; 10 Dime-Game factors → 10 intensity levels; 8 chain steps; 7 opposite-action and 7 problem-solving steps; 6 troubleshooting checkpoints; 19 clean-mind relapse precursors; 5 breakdown-point markers.
- **Retrieval anchors (2nd-ed. pages)**: DEAR MAN 125–127; opposite action 231–240; TIP 329; radical acceptance 342–344; addiction D-C-B-A 355–363.

## Blind spots of this book
- **Self-study is exactly the untested delta.** The entire trial base used facilitated groups with homework review and coaching; the book assumes a therapist/sponsor to call and a group for accountability. Solo workbook use — our lens's actual mode — is acknowledged by the author as having no evidence.
- **Population gap**: closest evidence is adults without BPD with anxiety + emotion dysregulation (Neacsiu 2014); the manual's origin population is suicidal/BPD clients. Generalization to a functioning adult doing psychoeducation is plausible but assumed, not shown.
- **No effect sizes, no dosing, no ranking**: hundreds of techniques presented flat ("every skill works for someone"), no data on which skills carry the outcome variance or minimal effective subsets. Selection burden falls entirely on the user — a real overwhelm risk.
- **Volume as a failure mode**: 225 pleasant events, 65 observing drills, dozens of worksheets — tracking presumes group accountability; solo, worksheet perfectionism and abandonment are both likely and only briefly warned against.
- **Weak on origins and meaning**: emotions handled at the response-system level; history appears only as "vulnerability factors" and the biosocial sketch. Trauma processing and attachment work are outsourced to therapy — correctly, but without guidance on what to do with what surfaces.
- **US-centric practicalities**: hotline numbers, group norms, public self-disclosure framing don't transfer directly.
- **2015 snapshot**: predates the last decade of evidence (app-delivered DBT skills, dismantling studies, online delivery) — the Revised 2025 printing was the intended source but the obtained file is the 2nd edition.
