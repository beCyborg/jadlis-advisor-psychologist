# advisor-emotion-regulation — moves

Curated move set of the emotion-regulation lens (DBT skills tradition, prefix `[ER:]`). Tier is set per OPERATION against safety-protocol §3, not per school: the same manual yields SELF skills and REQUIRES-THERAPIST operations. Baseline evidence for every move is package-level (skills tested as a bundle inside a facilitated program); independent lineage is named where it exists.

## 1. Reading the emotion before changing it

### [ER:NAME] Name the emotion and walk it through the model
**Move:** Take one live emotion through all six layers (ER Handouts 4–6, Worksheets 2–4a): prompting event in sensory facts, interpretation, body changes, action urge, expression, aftereffects; pick the exact label from the 10-emotion catalog; rate intensity 0–100; write down what the emotion is currently buying you, because a reinforced emotion resists every change skill.
**APPLY WHEN:** The emotion is unclear, mislabeled or blended; before any change move; a recurring emotion whose payoff you have never named.
**AVOID WHEN:** Past your skills breakdown point or mid-crisis — this is analysis, not regulation; when naming turns into a case against yourself.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; emotion catalog adapted from Shaver et al. 1987. Observing and naming is explicitly a solo operation (safety-protocol §3).

### [ER:ROUTE] Fits-the-facts × effectiveness routing
**Move:** Route the emotion with two questions (ER Handout 7, Worksheet 6): fits facts and acting is effective → act and problem-solve; fits but acting is ineffective → hold the urge, consider opposite action; does not fit → change the interpretation and act opposite. Per-emotion fit criteria are strict (fear fits a real threat to life or health; guilt fits a violation of your own values; shame fits real rejection risk from people whose opinion you actually depend on).
**APPLY WHEN:** Any strong emotion where the next step is unclear and you are still able to think.
**AVOID WHEN:** Past breakdown point; before the emotion is named; you are not yet sure you want it changed — pros and cons first.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; this routing logic is the spine of the tested emotion-regulation module.

### [ER:CHECKFACTS] Check the facts
**Move:** Six steps (ER Handout 8, Worksheet 5): name the emotion; describe the prompting event stripped of judgment words; list your interpretation plus at least one alternative, and where nothing is verifiable adopt the likely or the workable one; name the threat you are assuming and estimate its actual probability; picture the worst plausible outcome AND yourself coping with it; rate 0–5 how far the emotion fits the facts, treating 2–4 as "keep checking or run a small experiment".
**APPLY WHEN:** The reaction looks out of proportion; catastrophic forecasting; before choosing between opposite action and problem solving.
**AVOID WHEN:** The emotion already fits the facts — fact-checking cannot dissolve a justified emotion and trying converts it into self-invalidation; extreme arousal; a grief reaction being argued down.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; cognitive-appraisal operation shared with the CBT trial base (MODERATE as a standalone component, PG26 Р1).

## 2. Changing the emotional response

### [ER:OPPACT] Opposite action, all the way (non-fear targets)
**Move:** Seven steps (ER Handout 10, Worksheet 7): name the emotion → check whether it and its intensity are justified → identify the urge → ask in Wise Mind whether acting is effective → find the opposite → do it completely, including posture, face and voice → repeat until the emotion moves. Worked opposites: anger → step away gently, be courteous, imagine the other side; envy → count what you do have; jealousy → stop checking and surveilling; unjustified shame → disclose to people who will accept it; unjustified guilt → repeat the behavior openly; sadness → get active with an upright posture; unjustified love → cut contact and remove reminders.
**APPLY WHEN:** The emotion does not fit the facts, or acting on it would clearly make things worse.
**AVOID WHEN:** The emotion is justified and acting on it is effective (problem-solve instead); half measures — partial opposite action mostly rehearses the emotion; using it on grief to feel less sad, which is suppression with a skill label.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; behavioral-activation lineage for the sadness variant (BA as a standalone component: MODERATE, PG26 Р1).

### [ER:OPPACT-FEAR] Repeated approach to what you fear — an exposure operation, not a skill
**Move:** The fear variant of opposite action is repeated approach to the avoided situation until fear drops. Treat it as exposure: the hierarchy is designed in session, graded, repeated and prolonged; your solo part is executing and logging the agreed steps and reporting what happened. Never raise the difficulty between sessions and never improvise an approach task on a trauma-cued fear.
**APPLY WHEN:** Avoidance is the maintaining mechanism AND a therapist-agreed hierarchy exists — then log each run, the peak rating and the drop.
**AVOID WHEN:** No agreed hierarchy; you are escalating on your own; the cue is traumatic memory material; real danger — a failed self-run exposure reinforces the fear it was meant to weaken.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** Exposure lineage is the strongest independent support in the book, but hierarchy design and escalation are therapist operations (safety-protocol §3; PG26 EXPOSURE-THERAPIST / ERP-NO-ESC). Executing an agreed hierarchy is the SELF carve-out.

### [ER:PROBSLV] Problem solving the situation that produces the emotion
**Move:** Seven steps (ER Handout 12, Worksheet 8): describe the problem; check the facts; set one small achievable goal; brainstorm without criticism; choose one or two options on pros and cons; break the chosen one into listed steps and run them; evaluate and loop back if it failed.
**APPLY WHEN:** The emotion fits the facts and the situation is actually changeable.
**AVOID WHEN:** The appraisal is wrong (fix the facts first); the situation cannot be changed — that is the acceptance route; the "problem" is a loss.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; classic problem-solving-therapy structure.

### [ER:WAVE] Ride the emotion as a wave, surf the urge
**Move:** Observe the emotion the way you would a wave (ER Handout 22, Worksheet 15): do not block it and do not amplify it; locate it in the body; time how long it actually lasts; watch the action urge without acting on it (the same operation as urge surfing for cravings); keep in view that you are experiencing the emotion rather than being it.
**APPLY WHEN:** The emotion hurts but arousal is still workable; no change route applies; an impulse whose enactment would cost you something.
**AVOID WHEN:** Past breakdown point (crisis survival first); it slides into suppression, which increases suffering and blocks the other skills; urges are at self-harm level — that goes to the crisis circuit, not to surfing.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; urge surfing shared with relapse-prevention literature.

## 3. Arousal gating and crisis survival

### [ER:BRKPOINT] Find your skills breakdown point and use the fixed sequence
**Move:** Learn your own markers of the point where complicated skills stop being usable (ER Handout 23): extreme distress, overwhelm, attention locked on the emotion, thinking shutting down. First check the facts — am I actually past it? If no, use regulation skills. If yes, run the fixed order: crisis survival → mindfulness of the current emotion → the rest of the emotion-regulation skills. Keep the list written down, because recall is the first thing to fail.
**APPLY WHEN:** You keep reporting that skills fail exactly when you need them most; too aroused to think straight.
**AVOID WHEN:** Arousal is actually moderate; "I am past breakdown point" becomes a standing excuse — the fact-check step exists precisely for that; it is used instead of solving the underlying problem.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; the author's arousal-gating protocol, no independent test of the gate itself.

### [ER:TIP] TIP — change body chemistry directly
**Move:** (DT Handouts 6–6b) Temperature: cold water or a cold pack on the face for about 30 seconds while holding breath, water not below 50°F — the dive response starts in 15–30 seconds and slows the heart. Intense exercise: a short burst to burn off arousal. Paced breathing: about 5–6 breaths per minute with the exhale longer than the inhale (roughly 5 in, 7 out) — the most portable of the four. Paired muscle relaxation: tense on the in-breath 5–6 seconds, release on the exhale with a cue word, practiced 3–4 times a day until the exhale alone releases.
**APPLY WHEN:** Arousal is above the level where any cognitive skill can run; it has to come down in minutes; dangerous urges are rising.
**AVOID WHEN:** Heart or other medical conditions, beta-blockers, cold allergy, or an eating disorder — the book itself says clear the cold and exercise variants with a provider first; relaxation untrained before the crisis; cold applied while you keep moving, which cancels the effect.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** The dive response and slow-paced breathing are independently established physiology; paired relaxation adapted from Smith 1980; the protocol packaging is package-level. Regulation skills are a SELF operation (safety-protocol §3).

### [ER:STOP] STOP — put a gap between urge and action
**Move:** Freeze; take a step back and breathe; observe the situation, your thoughts, your body and what others are actually doing; then proceed with the consequences in view (DT Handout 4).
**APPLY WHEN:** There is an impulse to act on a strong emotion in a way that will predictably make the situation worse.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used as a general-purpose problem-solving move — crisis skills are for short-term crises, and chronic use is a documented misuse; used to freeze out an emotion that needs to be felt.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; adapted from Perepletchikova and Axelrod.

### [ER:CRISISMENU] Distract, self-soothe, improve the moment
**Move:** Three menus for surviving distress that cannot be solved right now (DT Handouts 7–9a): the ACCEPTS set — activities, contributing, comparisons, opposite emotions, time-limited pushing away, thoughts, sensations; self-soothing through the five senses; the IMPROVE set — imagery, meaning, prayer, relaxing actions, one thing at a time, a short deliberate break of an hour to a day, and pre-written encouraging lines. Pair with a pre-written pros-and-cons sheet for acting on versus resisting the urge (DT Handout 5), filled while calm and re-read at urge time. Log distress 0–100 before and after and effectiveness 1–5 to learn which channels work for you.
**APPLY WHEN:** Crisis-level distress with nothing solvable in the next hours; distress that has been running for hours or days; a self-punishing mode that blocks any comfort.
**AVOID WHEN:** Distraction becomes standing avoidance of a solvable problem; the "short break" stretches into escape; the soothing channel is itself the addiction; a filled sheet you cannot find at urge time is a sheet you did not fill.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; sensory items adapted from Goldfried & Davison 1976; effectiveness anchor is urge resistance, not feeling good.

### [ER:RISK-HANDOFF] Stop skills coaching and hand off when risk markers appear
**Move:** On any of the risk markers — suicidal thoughts or preparations, current or considered self-harm, beliefs that cannot be reality-tested, sharply reduced sleep need with rising activity, violence, severe eating-disorder behavior, intoxication or withdrawal, dissociation or flashbacks, flooding lasting beyond a day, or several days of not eating, sleeping or working — stop the skills work, give short bodily grounding, hand over the crisis contacts verbatim, and name the route to the live therapist. Do not resume the analysis in the same conversation even after the person walks the signal back.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any suspicion, including indirect signals — the threshold is suspicion, not stated intent.
**AVOID WHEN:** Never skipped for the sake of finishing a plan; not softened into "and also here are two skills to try"; not treated as a refusal — it is a route with a named recipient.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** safety-protocol §1 and §4 plus PG26 CRISIS-STOP (MODERATE for the halt, LOW for the resumption evidence: 22 of 25 public assistants resumed normal dialogue after a walk-back in a 2023 audit).

## 4. Reducing vulnerability — the prevention layer

### [ER:ABCPLEASE] Accumulate positives, build mastery, treat the body
**Move:** The standing baseline layer, scheduled and tracked (ER Handouts 14–20, Worksheets 9–14): at least one pleasant event daily from the 225-item list, attended to on purpose and deliberately without worry-checking; the long-term ladder — one value from the 58-item list → two or three goals → one goal → small steps → a first step today; one difficult-but-possible task daily for mastery; and the physical floor — treat physical illness, eat regularly, avoid mood-altering substances, sleep 7–9 hours, move up to 20 minutes a day.
**APPLY WHEN:** Always on; chronically flat positive emotion; helplessness; the empty stretch between crises.
**AVOID WHEN:** Deployed as a crisis response; pleasant events used to dodge a solvable problem; mastery tasks pitched high enough to engineer failure; value steps too big — shrink the step rather than drop the value.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; behavioral-activation lineage (BA standalone MODERATE, PG26 Р1); events list from Linehan, Sharp & Ivanoff 1980; values list from Schwartz 1992.

### [ER:SLEEP-HYGIENE] Sleep schedule and the night-waking triage
**Move:** The hygiene ladder (ER Handout 20b): a fixed schedule including weekends, naps of 10 minutes or less, the bed reserved for sleep, no late stimulants, alcohol or heavy meals, a cool dark room. If sleep has not come in 30–60 minutes, triage: calm but awake → get up and come back when sleepy; ruminating → cold-water TIP plus paced breathing, a slow countdown, and treating the content as middle-of-the-night thinking rather than as analysis; a real problem → solve it if solvable, otherwise rehearse coping with the feared outcome.
**APPLY WHEN:** Trouble falling asleep, worry-driven waking, a sleep pattern feeding the next day's dysregulation.
**AVOID WHEN:** Cold-water TIP without medical clearance; treating a bad night as a catastrophe and starting the "day" at 3 a.m.; forcing sleep instead of using stimulus control.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; stimulus-control adaptation with independent method-level support outside DBT.

### [ER:NIGHTMARE-RESCRIPT] Rewriting a recurring nightmare
**Move:** The nightmare protocol (ER Handout 20a): stabilize first; write the recurring dream out in sensory detail; write a changed version in which the change happens BEFORE anything bad occurs; rehearse the changed version with relaxation nightly until the recurrence stops.
**APPLY WHEN:** A recurring nightmare with a non-traumatic core, and only with the therapist informed — the writing-out step is what makes this therapist-tier.
**AVOID WHEN:** The dream replays a traumatic event, which the book itself defers; done solo while dysregulated; used as a way to approach trauma content sideways without saying so.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** Imagery-rehearsal adaptation with independent method-level support, but working on an image tied to a traumatic target is a therapist operation (safety-protocol §3; early rescripting is therapist-performed even inside therapy, Arntz 2025 via PG26).

### [ER:COPEAHEAD] Rehearse coping with a situation you can predict
**Move:** Five steps (ER Handout 19): describe the upcoming situation in facts and name the emotions and urges it will probably trigger; script the specific skills you will use; imagine yourself inside the situation rather than watching yourself in it; rehearse the coping including complications and the outcome you fear most; close with relaxation.
**APPLY WHEN:** A predictable difficult situation; anticipatory anxiety; a planned high-stakes conversation; ending a relationship that is not abusive.
**AVOID WHEN:** The rehearsal target is traumatic memory material; the closing relaxation is skipped, which turns rehearsal into anticipatory distress practice.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; imaginal-rehearsal method, non-traumatic targets only (PG26 IMG-NONTRAUMA).

## 5. Accepting what cannot be changed, and mindfulness

### [ER:RADACC] Radical acceptance of unchangeable facts
**Move:** Accept the facts with the whole person, not just verbally (DT Handouts 10–15a, Worksheets 8–12): notice where you are fighting reality; verify what the facts actually are, stripped of interpretation (Worksheet 9); acknowledge the causes that made this the case; practice with self-talk, breath, imagery, opposite action and rehearsal; allow the disappointment and sadness that arrive when the fighting stops. Sustainers: turning the mind — acceptance is a fork you re-choose every time you drift; willingness — name willfulness, accept that you feel willful, ask what threat it is guarding, then do one willing action; half-smiling with open hands, since face and posture feed back. Rate 0–5, where the top is peace rather than low arousal.
**APPLY WHEN:** Facts that will not change keep generating bitterness and "why me"; acceptance keeps collapsing back into resentment; the cognitive routes have all been tried.
**AVOID WHEN:** The situation is changeable — problem-solve; "acceptance" is being used to tolerate ongoing harm or abuse; willingness is being used to justify complying with harmful demands; a forced tense grin, which is masking; a bereavement — see the loss move.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; a core DBT component; the half-smile element adapted from Thich Nhat Hanh.

### [ER:LOSS-ACCEPT] Acceptance work on a death or a major loss
**Move:** When the unchangeable fact is a loss, the acceptance sequence stops being a solo skill: measure first (prolonged-grief symptoms plus a depression scale), and let sustained approach to the loss — rehearsing it, holding the image, working the acceptance rating on it — happen with the therapist, who also holds the restorative half. Your solo part is describing what happens, tracking sleep, eating and functioning, and preparing questions for the session.
**APPLY WHEN:** A loss is the actual content behind the presented emotion, whether or not it was named as grief.
**AVOID WHEN:** Any positive reframing of the loss, any timetable, any stage story; running acceptance drills on a fresh loss alone; assuming grief needs an intervention at all when the measures are unremarkable — at ordinary grief levels protocols are near-zero in effect and can do harm.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST (the measurement step is SELF)
**Evidence:** safety-protocol §3 lists loss exposure and unfinished grief as therapist operations; PG26 GRIEF-GATE — grief work only above symptom thresholds (MODERATE), no brand advantage (grief-CBT superiority retracted to LOW), no solo format exists.

### [ER:MOT] Treat thoughts as events in the mind, not as findings
**Move:** Catch the thought and label it as a thought that just went through your mind (DT Handout 15, Worksheets 12–12a), then loosen it: repeat it fast, slowly or sung until the grip goes; watch thoughts as items on a belt, leaves on a river, clouds; act as if you did not have to believe each one; ask which bodily sensation the thought is helping you avoid and turn toward that. Practice on neutral and pleasant thoughts too, not only on the frightening ones.
**APPLY WHEN:** Fusion with catastrophic thoughts; worry loops; rumination, which the book frames as an escape from difficult body sensations.
**AVOID WHEN:** It becomes thought suppression; the thought is one that needs fact-checking and action instead of defusion; repeating self-attacking content, which escalates shame rather than loosening it.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; defusion-style operations shared with ACT (defusion is a listed SELF operation, PG26).

### [ER:MINDCORE] Wise Mind and the what/how skills
**Move:** Access the state that is neither pure logic nor pure mood (Mindfulness Handouts 1–5c): breathe in on one syllable and out on the other; ask a question on the in-breath and listen for an answer rather than generating one; run a three-minute reset — name what is going on in the mind, narrow to the breath, widen to the whole body. Use the "what" skills one at a time — observe wordlessly, describe only what came through the senses (another person's motive is inference, not description), participate fully enough that self-consciousness drops. Combine the "how" qualities — drop verdicts of good and bad while keeping the distinction between helpful and harmful, do one thing at a time, and ask whether what you are doing is effective the moment you notice you need to be right. Train in easy situations so it is available in hard ones.
**APPLY WHEN:** As the base layer under every other move; rumination and autopilot; a decision that needs a check which is neither analysis nor mood.
**AVOID WHEN:** Expecting it to calm you — success here is access to judgment, not relief; observing used as a polite form of suppression; treating the answer as an oracle that overrides fact-checking; pushing through worsening in a trauma history, where the instruction is to stop the practice rather than try harder.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; contemplative sources and MBCT adaptations (Segal et al. 2013); the trauma stop-rule is PG26 Р4.

### [ER:GRIEFINVAL] Grieving a history of severe invalidation
**Move:** Grieving what a chronically invalidating environment cost, describing those experiences in a supportive setting, and eventually accepting the person who did the invalidating (extension of IE Handout 19a). The same boundary covers every trauma-targeted variant scattered through the manual — approach work on trauma-cued fear, contemplating an abuser's image, writing out a trauma nightmare in detail, sustained acceptance of a traumatic loss.
**APPLY WHEN:** A history of severe invalidation is visibly driving present reactions; trauma material surfaces in the middle of an otherwise SELF skill.
**AVOID WHEN:** Attempted alone while dysregulated — these steps are designed to open the material; used as a substitute for the ordinary-target versions of the same skills, which stay available to you.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** The author's own deferrals; safety-protocol §3 (opening traumatic material).

## 6. Interpersonal situations that produce the emotion

### [ER:DEARMAN] Write the script before the conversation, and set its intensity
**Move:** Before a consequential request or refusal, rank the three goals for THIS situation — the objective, the relationship, your self-respect — then set intensity with the ten-factor ladder (IE Handout 8) that runs from not asking at all to not taking no for an answer. Then write the script out (Worksheets 4–5): describe the facts, express what you want in your own voice, ask explicitly instead of expecting them to infer, name what is in it for them, hold the line by repeating it and letting attacks pass, drop the hedges that undercut you, and offer to negotiate. Rehearse aloud with a cope-ahead run; if the other person is skilled and persistent, shift to naming the interaction itself. Log which components you actually used.
**APPLY WHEN:** Asking for something that matters, saying no, or resisting pressure — anything planned and high-stakes.
**AVOID WHEN:** Improvised live without a written script — the book's own workflow is script-first, and improvising the acronym mid-conflict is not what was tested; delivering threats or ultimatums; imputing motives; expecting skills to manufacture reciprocity or affection, which the manual rejects outright; letting the intensity arithmetic override your own judgment when the factors are not equal.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level, with the closest population being adults without a personality-disorder diagnosis who had anxiety plus emotion dysregulation (Neacsiu 2014); assertiveness-training lineage (Linehan & Egan 1985).

### [ER:GIVEFAST] Keep the relationship and your self-respect while you ask
**Move:** Relationship side: stay gentle — no attacks, no threats (using a threat of self-harm as leverage is named as banned), no moralizing, no sneering; be able to tolerate a no; act interested; validate; keep the manner light. Self-respect side: be fair to both sides including validating your own feelings; stop over-apologizing — not for existing, not for asking, not for disagreeing, since over-apologizing is self-invalidation aimed inward; stick to your values; stay truthful, including not acting helpless when you are not.
**APPLY WHEN:** The relationship matters as much as the outcome; you keep leaving interactions ashamed or self-betrayed; chronic appeasement.
**AVOID WHEN:** "Being effective" is being used to rationalize crossing your own values — the self-respect side is exactly that boundary; the gentleness rules are relaxed because your objective is legitimate.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level.

### [ER:VALIDATE] Validation, outward and after being invalidated
**Move:** Six levels outward (IE Handouts 18–19): full attention; reflect back and stay open to correction; name what has not been said and stay correctable; show how the response makes sense given that person's history and situation even where you disapprove; acknowledge the valid part in action, so a fair complaint gets fixed rather than discussed; treat them as an equal. Inward after being invalidated (19a): check the facts without defensiveness; change whatever really was off; stand up for yourself where that is reasonable; drop the judgmental self-commentary; admit it hurt even if the critic was right; validate yourself the way you would validate someone else in that position.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any charged interaction; before problem-solving with someone who does not feel heard; habitual self-invalidation; de-escalation.
**AVOID WHEN:** Validating what is not valid — only what fits the facts gets validated; solving when the person only wanted to be heard; collapsing into "then my experience must be wrong", or its mirror, attacking the other while you may be the one who is wrong.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; the six levels from Linehan 1997; validation is not agreement and not approval.

### [ER:SAFEEXIT] Leaving a dangerous relationship goes through specialists, not skills
**Move:** If a relationship being ended involves physical abuse or a threat to life, the first call is a domestic-violence service for safety planning and referral, not a scripted conversation with the other person. Relationships that are destructive but not dangerous get the ordinary route: pros and cons in a clear state, a rehearsed cope-ahead, and a pre-written ending script — never an ending improvised mid-argument. Relationships worth repairing get problem solving first.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any abuse or safety threat is present in a relationship you are ending.
**AVOID WHEN:** Substituting interpersonal skills for professional exit help — the book's own quiz marks "use your skills to tell an abuser you are leaving" as the wrong answer.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** The author's safety protocol, aligned with standard DV practice; local crisis contacts per safety-protocol §4 (the manual's numbers are US-only and do not transfer).

## 7. When the behavior repeats or the skill fails

### [ER:CHAIN] Chain analysis and the missing-links questions
**Move:** For a repeating problem behavior, eight steps (General Handouts 7–8): describe the behavior precisely enough to re-enact it; identify the environmental prompting event; list what made you vulnerable that day; write out every link; describe the consequences that keep it going; circle the key links and write a skillful replacement for each; build a prevention plan; repair the actual damage by being trustworthy going forward rather than by berating yourself. When the missing piece is something effective that did NOT happen, run four questions and stop at the first no: did you know what was needed, were you willing, did it even cross your mind, what got in the way.
**APPLY WHEN:** A recurring behavior whose triggers and payoffs are unclear; commitments you keep skipping.
**AVOID WHEN:** It turns into rumination or into evidence of being defective instead of link-hunting; the target behavior is self-injury or suicidal behavior — that chain belongs inside the session; you are chaining someone else's behavior.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Functional analysis is the backbone of behavior therapies and carries independent method-level support; the after-the-fact episode review is an explicitly solo operation (safety-protocol §3).

### [ER:ADDICT] Dialectical abstinence and engineering the environment
**Move:** For a behavior you cannot stop despite consequences (DT Handouts 16–21, Worksheets 13–18): commit to full abstinence AND write the slip plan at the same time — who to call, opposite action against the shame that follows, a chain analysis of the lapse, repair, recommit — so that a slip does not become a slide. Check yourself weekly against the 19-item list of thoughts that precede relapse ("I have learned my lesson", "I can control it now", keeping the paraphernalia). Engineer the environment rather than relying on willpower: remove cues and contacts, make the commitment public, build competing associations, surf urges, arrange non-using company and replacement pleasures, and postpone in five-minute increments.
**APPLY WHEN:** A craving-structured recurring behavior at a moment of genuine commitment; the overconfidence of early abstinence; an environment saturated with cues.
**AVOID WHEN:** Physical dependence — withdrawal from alcohol or opioids needs medical supervision, not skills; either pole alone (pure commitment lengthens the fall after a slip, pure harm-reduction relapses sooner); cutting off supports rather than enablers; misreading the function, so that anti-defiance tactics leave a soothing-driven urge untouched.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Community Reinforcement Approach from Meyers & Squires 2001 has independent trials; dialectical abstinence comes from DBT substance work and is NOT covered by the closest-population trial.

### [ER:TROUBLESHOOT] Run the failure checklist instead of blaming yourself
**Move:** When a well-chosen skill produces nothing, work the ordered checklist (ER Handout 24, Worksheet 16): biology — sleep, food, illness, medication questions for the doctor; was it executed exactly as written, or approximately; is the emotion being reinforced by something; was effort low, which is a willingness question; were you over the breakdown point, in which case body chemistry comes first; is a belief about emotions blocking it. The interpersonal version (IE Handout 9) adds one more answer: sometimes the environment is simply more powerful than the skill, and the remedy is allies and acceptance rather than more drilling.
**APPLY WHEN:** A plausible, correctly chosen skill changed nothing; repeated failures in the same interaction; you keep not attempting the practice at all.
**AVOID WHEN:** The skill was never actually run as written — that is checkpoint two, not system failure; the checklist becomes ammunition against yourself; the honest answer is that the environment holds the power and more practice is the wrong response.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Package-level; notable because the manual explicitly rejects the idea that correct execution always works.
