# advisor-grief — core principles, ranked by evidence

Prefix `[GR:]`. The ranking below is by what is actually shown, not by how central the idea is to the tradition. The tradition's most famous parts sit in the weakest tier: the four-task model, the chair, the linking object are canon and untrialled. Numbers follow the PG26 Verification corrections; where a number was demoted there, the demoted version is the only one used here.

Vocabulary this lens keeps separate: bereavement is the loss, grief is the reaction, mourning is the process of adapting. Adaptation, not recovery — there is no return to the pre-loss state, and no version of this lens offers one.

---

## Tier 1 — MODERATE: shown, with magnitudes

### Targeting is the load-bearing principle — indicated d≈0.53, universal ≈ 0
The single best-supported claim in the whole tradition is about **whom not to treat**. Grief interventions produce a usable effect only where grief symptoms are already elevated (d≈0.53); offered to the bereaved generally, they produce approximately nothing, and at uncomplicated grief a protocol can do harm. Routine referral of everyone who has lost someone shows no benefit (Parkes 1998). Around 10–15% of the bereaved develop something needing more than facilitation — that minority is the lens's entire population. Operationally: [GR:MEASURE-FIRST] as the gate, [GR:RISK-SCREEN] as the filter. Everything else in this file is downstream of the gate.

### No brand wins at elevated symptoms — Hao 2024, 55 studies, N=10,330
Grief-CBT superiority was demoted MODERATE→LOW and retracted in PG26: the network meta-analysis shows five modalities significant with overlapping confidence intervals. What is shown is that *something structured, at elevated symptoms* helps. Which school delivers it is not shown. This forbids the lens from claiming its own procedures beat the alternatives; [GR:CONTRACT] carries the correction in its Evidence field.

### The surviving parent is the lever for a bereaved child
Harvard Child Bereavement Study — 125 children, 70 families, two-year controlled follow-up: about 80% cope, and the surviving parent's functioning is the strongest predictor of the child's outcome; ages 5–7 are the most vulnerable. Parent-targeted programmes have been trialled separately (Sandler 2010/2016). This is the strongest outcome evidence anywhere in the lens, and it points away from child-focused intervention: [GR:CHILD-NEEDS].

### Family intervention works on a screened subset, not on families in general
Kissane 2006 found benefit for at-risk families identified by screening — the same targeting shape as the individual finding, arrived at independently. It supports [GR:FAMILY-WORK] only in its screened form; a bereavement programme rolled out to all families is the universal-prevention error again.

### Deterioration is a real rate, not a theoretical risk
Psychotherapy adverse events run 5–20%; 5–10% leave worse; clinicians catch roughly 3 of 40 deteriorations. Applied here: the tier boundary is not caution theatre, and "the person has a therapist" does not by itself make an operation safe. PG26 base rates, imported unchanged.

## Tier 2 — LOW: consistent observational signal, no causal test

### Self-blame, not regret, is the strongest determinant of outcome — Stroebe 2014
Longitudinal, and grief-specific: it is self-blame that tracks the outcome, which is why [GR:GUILT-TEST] is the highest-yield self-manageable move in the set. What is *not* shown is that reality-testing the blame changes the trajectory — the target selection is evidenced, the intervention on it is Worden's technique.

### Oscillation beats immersion — Dual Process Model
Stroebe & Schut 1999/2010: alternating between loss-oriented and restoration-oriented activity is the adaptive pattern; continuous confrontation is not. Descriptive model, externally developed, and the best-supported regulation principle the lens holds — but it has not been trialled as a protocol. Carried by [GR:DOSE].

### Rumination costs meaning rather than producing it — Eisma 2015
Passive looping on grief symptoms predicts worse outcomes, and ruminators find *less* meaning, not more (Nolen-Hoeksema line of work). Solid as an observation. The counter-moves in [GR:RUMINATE-REDIRECT] are recommendations with no outcome data behind them.

### Avoidant coping predicts the bad outcomes — Stahl & Schulz 2014; Schnider 2007
Blame, permanent distraction, denial, withdrawal and substance use cluster with the worst courses, including posttraumatic and complicated-grief outcomes. Observational: the coping style may be a marker of severity rather than its cause. [GR:COPING-AUDIT] is justified as monitoring, not as a proven mechanism.

### Venting is not progress — Parkes 2001
Unfocused vehement emotion predicts worse outcomes a year on. Feelings need a target and a dose. PG26 bounds the neighbouring claim: expressive writing is not unsafe by default (a newer meta-analysis finds a small positive effect); only unstructured venting during active rumination-driven distress is contraindicated. Both halves live in [GR:JOURNAL-NARRATIVE].

### The mediators predict the course
Kinship, attachment quality, mode of death, prior losses, personality, satisfaction with support (satisfaction, not availability), concurrent stressors — each carries predictive signal in cohort data (Harvard; Cleiren 1993; Bonanno 2002). What is not established is the seven-factor grid as a structure; see Tier 3. [GR:MEDIATOR-MAP].

### Writing procedures have method-level support
O'Connor 2003, Ihrmark 2011 — trials of writing therapy conducted with clinician structure around them. The procedures transfer; the effect sizes do not transfer to a clinician-free format, which is why [GR:LETTER-UNFINISHED] sits on the therapist side while [GR:JOURNAL-NARRATIVE] does not.

### Continuing bonds, not detachment
Field 1999: the shift from being sustained by the dead person's possessions to being sustained by memories of them marks a healthier bond. Correlational, and the direction is unestablished — improvement may produce the shift rather than the reverse. [GR:RELOCATE-BOND], [GR:LINK-OBJECT].

## Tier 3 — VERY LOW: canon, face-valid, never tested

### The four tasks of mourning
The organising frame of the whole tradition — accept the reality; go through the pain; adjust to the world without them; find an enduring place for them while living the rest of the life. It replaces stages with something actionable, which is its real contribution, and it has no direct trial of any kind. Its value is as a locator of the blocked task, not as a treatment. [GR:TASK-MAP].

### Chair work as the high-leverage mechanism
Worden's clinical judgement, the Gestalt tradition behind it, one paradigm-support paper (Field & Horowitz 1998), extensive case material, and no randomised trial. The tradition's most distinctive procedure is also its least evidenced. It is also the one with an author-issued contraindication attached, which is why [GR:CHAIR] is REQUIRES-THERAPIST in every context.

### The linking object, the twelve clues, the multi-loss ordering
Volkan's concept, Lazare's taxonomy (never validated as an instrument — a prompt list, not a screen with properties), Kastenbaum's overload concept with Worden's sequencing rule on top. All clinically useful, all case-report level: [GR:LINK-OBJECT], [GR:UNRESOLVED-CLUES], [GR:MULTI-LOSS].

### Attachment-style tailoring
Preoccupied toward internalised proximity, dismissing with an eye on bodily expression, fearful with protection against isolation — theory-derived from Mikulincer & Shaver and Field 2006, tested as a matching rule nowhere. PG26 is blunter: personalised matching algorithms have no prospective validation at all. Treat as a question generator inside [GR:AMBIV-GRADE].

### Ritual, viewing, and making a loss real
Author experience plus a thin ritual literature (Hoy 2013; Kristensen 2012); perinatal practices reach method level with one RCT (Pruitt Johnson & Langford 2015). [GR:ACTUALIZE-RITUAL] is defensible mainly because its cost is low and its alternative — an information vacuum — has documented harms.

## Tier 4 — extrapolation, flagged as such

### Every move here was written for a counsellor, and inverted for solo use
The book addresses the helper. This lens is read by the bereaved person. That inversion has never been tested for a single move in the set, and it is the reason the REQUIRES-THERAPIST tier here is stricter than the digest's own tiering — two moves ([GR:RETELL-DEATH], [GR:LETTER-UNFINISHED]) were split out and re-tiered precisely because the counsellor-side version obscured that they are exposure operations.

### Delivery-mode indirectness caps everything above
PG26's standing correction: no study has tested a clinician-free format. Procedures transfer; effect sizes do not. The d≈0.53 in Tier 1 describes live human delivery to screened patients, and nothing in this file inherits it.

### Timelines are descriptive, never deadlines
About three months before a widow accepts the death as real; outside support collapses around three months with a further cliff at 6–12; fewer than half of widows feel like themselves at one year; roughly two years to a modicum of stability (Shuchter & Zisook), three to four for some (Parkes); anniversary reactions remain ordinary past ten years. These are distributions from Western cohorts. Used as targets, they generate self-blame — which Tier 2 identifies as the strongest determinant of a bad outcome. That is the sharpest internal contradiction the lens has to police.

### Live controversies the lens does not resolve
Delayed grief exists at 8% (Worden, two years) or 4% (Bonanno, 18 months) on the same instruments. Forewarning helps (Parkes) or does nothing (Nielsen 2016 systematic review). Suicide grief is inherently more intense (Farberow) or is not (Cleiren). The lens reports the split; it does not pick a side.
