# advisor-perfectionism — core

The method: cognitive-behavioural work on **clinical perfectionism**, in the workbook form trialled by Shafran, Egan and Wade. Principles below are ranked by what the evidence actually supports, not by how central the book treats them. Effect sizes appear in the subheadings where they exist. Everything here is calibrated against the psy-guidelines-2026 corrections; where that layer demotes a number, the demoted version is the one used.

## The model in one paragraph

The target is not high standards. It is **self-worth made contingent on striving and achievement**, kept running by rigid rules, all-or-nothing appraisal, counterproductive behaviours that look like quality control, and a rigged scoring system: missing a standard proves personal failure, meeting one gets discounted as too easy and the bar moves up. Three defining components: demanding standards with self-criticism when missed; continued striving despite documented costs; self-evaluation contingent on achievement. Healthy striving differs on exactly two axes — worth contingency and persistence despite cost — so someone with very high standards, intact self-worth and normal recovery from setbacks is not the target and is not treated [PERF:SCREEN9], [PERF:CYCLE-MAP].

## Tier 1 — Directly trialled in this exact format

The strongest claim this lens can make: **the package as a whole**, in the format a person would actually use it, has been tested repeatedly.

### The programme as a package (≥4 direct RCTs of this book; 8 sessions each; 6-month maintenance in both Australian trials)
Egan 2014, Handley 2015, Rozental 2017, Shafran 2017, plus the Steele & Wade 2008 pilot. Every trial format ran eight sessions, and the two Australian trials measured maintenance at six months. This is unusual: most self-help books borrow their evidence from clinic-delivered therapy, whereas this one was trialled as a book [PERF:AREA-MONITOR], [PERF:BEHEXP], [PERF:SURVEY].

### Self-help ≈ face-to-face, on average (Galloway meta-analysis: 15 RCTs, N = 912)
The meta-analytic base for perfectionism interventions is 15 trials and 912 participants — not the two-paper base an earlier reading suggested. "Approximately equal on average" is a population statement and says nothing about whether an individual will land near the mean [PERF:ESCALATE].

### Guided beats unguided, and this is the highest-confidence statement in the whole lens (HIGH)
Unguided self-help is weaker than every guided format and no better than usual care. Inside this book's own trials the split is sharper still: unguided use moved perfectionism but not anxiety or mood, while guided formats moved anxiety, mood and self-criticism as well. Solo use should expect the perfectionism gain and plan for the mood gain to require a person in the loop [PERF:ESCALATE].

### Secondary outcomes of this programme (Kothari 2019: intolerance of uncertainty d = −0.9; self-esteem d = +0.7; fear of self-compassion d = −0.8)
These are secondary outcomes of the package, not effects of any named exercise. The uncertainty result in particular is a side effect: the programme moves tolerance of uncertainty **without ever teaching it**, which is a reason to run the programme and not evidence that it contains an uncertainty skill [PERF:BROADBASE], [PERF:CRITIC-COMPASSION-DIARY].

### Prevention in classrooms, children aged 9+ (benefits held at 1 year; Nehmy & Wade 2015)
Reduced perfectionism versus control with benefit maintained at one year. Independent of the adult protocol, and the only long-horizon result in the lens.

## Tier 2 — Method-level support, never isolated inside this programme

These techniques have their own trial base in CBT generally. What is NOT known is how much of this programme's effect each contributes: no dismantling study exists here. A lens that keeps half the protocol cannot claim the trial results.

### Behavioural experiments (method-level trial support across CBT)
The book's central change engine, and the operation everything else feeds [PERF:BEHEXP], [PERF:PROC-AUDIT], [PERF:RULE-LIST]. Note the boundary that the calibration layer enforces: an experiment on an ordinary task is SELF, an exposure hierarchy is a different operation and belongs to a clinician.

### Behavioural activation and activity scheduling (standalone component: MODERATE)
The best-evidenced independent component in the toolkit — and the one most likely to be dismissed as too simple [PERF:BALANCE-SCHEDULE].

### Cognitive restructuring as a standalone component (MODERATE)
Supports the thought-diary machinery [PERF:THOUGHT-DIARY]. Written, not mental — the book insists the writing is the mechanism, though that specific claim is clinical reasoning rather than a tested contrast.

### Self-monitoring (method-level support across anxiety, mood and eating problems)
Recording a behaviour reduces it. Live recording; retrospective logs are markedly weaker [PERF:AREA-MONITOR], [PERF:HOTSPOT-DIARY].

### Graded task assignment and structured problem-solving (method-level CBT support)
[PERF:CHUNK], [PERF:PROBSOLVE]. Neither trialled inside this programme.

### Surveys, case formulation and relapse blueprints (method-level standard practice)
Standard CBT operations imported into the protocol [PERF:SURVEY], [PERF:CYCLE-MAP], [PERF:RELAPSE-BLUEPRINT].

### Motivational rulers and decisional balance (method-level support outside this book)
Part of the trial-tested package but not trialled standalone here [PERF:MOTIV-AUDIT].

### Acceptance and mindfulness elements (method-level: fewer depressive episodes in recurrent low mood; reduced body dissatisfaction)
Cited by the authors from other literatures, not measured inside this programme [PERF:OBSERVE-NOT-REACT].

## Tier 3 — Correlational and mechanistic, i.e. the model, not the treatment

### Dichotomous thinking as a maintainer (correlational)
Identified as a factor separating healthy striving from the unhelpful form in correlational work — a reason to target it [PERF:CONTINUUM], not proof that targeting it produces the change.

### Self-criticism and less progress toward valued goals (correlational)
The claim that criticism damages motivation and progress is an association. What is directly contradicted is only the reverse claim that criticism is a performance tool [PERF:VALUES-MIRROR].

### Repeated checking lowers confidence in one's own memory (method-level experimental)
The strongest mechanism claim in the lens, and the one that makes checking rituals self-defeating rather than merely costly [PERF:CONTRAST].

### The inverted-U effort curve (Yerkes–Dodson, 1908) and lower efficiency at high perfectionism
An old and general finding used as a frame for the personal effort log; the log itself is untested [PERF:FACTCHECK].

### Imagery carries the perfectionism-to-distress link better than repetitive verbal thinking does (one Australian study)
A single-study claim, used to justify working in imagery at all [PERF:IMG-FUTURE], [PERF:IMG-RESCRIPT-MEM].

### Heritability 24–49% (twin studies)
Enough to make "it's just how I'm built" partly true and entirely beside the point: the model is built on present-tense maintainers, and origins are deliberately deprioritised.

## Tier 4 — Author clinical practice and worked cases only

Everything here is the weakest tier in the toolkit and should be presented as such: it is what experienced clinicians do, backed by case material.

- Portable cards, cue lists and coping images [PERF:PORTABLE-COUNTERS] — the book's own anecdote-grade material.
- The absent-criticism column in the positive-evidence diary [PERF:POS-DIARY] — an authors' addition.
- Fact-checking a rule's empirical claim, and personal effort curves [PERF:FACTCHECK].
- Risk-mapping when the standard is moral rather than comparative [PERF:RISK-BOTH-WAYS] — one case.
- Cost-benefit review of a long-pursued goal [PERF:COST-BENEFIT-DREAM] — one case.
- The integration step that assembles scattered exercise outputs into one written reality statement [PERF:REALITY-INTEGRATE].
- Worked numbers are cases, not effect sizes: Suzie's four hours to one with zero complaints; Aimee's anxiety 95% → 60%; Gemma's 90% → 45%; Simon's predicted 90% embarrassment against an actual 40%.

## Tier 5 — Extrapolation, or explicitly not claimed

State these as gaps whenever a query touches them.

- **Component attribution.** Nothing tells you which of the roughly 40 worksheets did the work.
- **Performance improvement.** Deliberately not promised. The honest position is flexibility without deterioration, sometimes improvement — so this lens has nothing for someone whose actual question is how to produce more.
- **Interpersonal uncertainty.** No worksheet exists for a pause in contact, an unanswered message or ambiguity inside a relationship. The nearest instruments are the reassurance-seeking entries and the alternating-days checking contrast [PERF:CONTRAST] — an oblique fit, and the book refers this out.
- **Grief and identity loss.** Outside the model entirely; there is no protocol for the mourning that follows giving up a life goal, and no solo grief format exists anywhere in the calibration layer.
- **Dosing and stopping rules.** The book gives no criterion for when a technique has had a fair trial, and no measure of change beyond re-reading your own worksheets. Nor does any completion benchmark for between-session practice exist — the figures once quoted for that were refuted.
- **Routing itself is a hypothesis.** "Perfectionism → this protocol" is MODERATE but was never tested against generic CBT. It is a working hypothesis for lens choice, not a treatment assignment [PG26:ROUTING-AS-HYPOTHESES].

## Operating principles that follow from the tiering

1. **Target maintainers, not causes.** Origins do not drive change; the single aetiological study in the book is retrospective and memory-biased.
2. **Volume down, never off.** The goal for the critical voice is a knob turned down. "Silence the critic" manufactures one more standard to fail [PERF:OBSERVE-NOT-REACT].
3. **Falling anxiety is the wrong success criterion.** Flexibility and workable choice are; more tolerated uncertainty is the expected price, and short-term anxiety often rises [PERF:RISK-BOTH-WAYS], [PERF:CRITIC-COMPASSION-DIARY].
4. **Action generates motivation, not the reverse.** Waiting to feel ready guarantees delay [PERF:CHUNK].
5. **Data before argument.** Facts open the door; surveys calibrate; only experiments move belief [PERF:FACTCHECK] → [PERF:SURVEY] → [PERF:BEHEXP].
6. **One micro-practice at a time.** More than a couple of assigned actions reproduces the demanding-parent pattern the method is trying to loosen; non-completion means the task was wrongly designed, not that the person failed [PG26:MICRO-TASK].
7. **The tier boundary runs by operation.** Ordinary behavioural targets are self-work; anything that reactivates memory or loss material is session work, from the same school and the same book.
