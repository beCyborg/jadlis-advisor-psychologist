# advisor-schema — curated move set

## Screening and sequencing

### [ST:GATE] Screen for what must be stabilised before pattern work
**Move:** Before opening any pattern, check five postponement conditions — an active life crisis, a breakdown in reality testing, an acute condition being handled elsewhere, moderate-to-severe substance use, and a problem that is genuinely situational rather than lifelong. Stabilise that layer first and only then take up the chronic pattern; re-run the check whenever intensity re-escalates mid-work.
**APPLY WHEN:** at the start of any schema or mode work; whenever intensity jumps partway through.
**AVOID WHEN:** never skipped, and never used to delay urgent clinical contact. Pattern work under active substance use is close to futile — the substance blunts the very affect the work needs.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** clinical judgement plus general CBT outcome literature; the screen itself is untested [young-schema-therapy].

### [ST:SAFETY-FIRST] Protection replaces pattern work where there is current violence
**Move:** Where a relationship carries current physical danger, intimidation, or a partner with an assault history, the goal switches from expressing needs to physical protection — specialist and protective services, not a worksheet. Asserting needs toward a violent partner is named as likely to escalate the danger.
**APPLY WHEN:** any current danger, intimidation, or history of assault by the other party.
**AVOID WHEN:** never skipped — this overrides every other move in this file, including the ones marked SELF.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** explicit clinical warning in the workbook, routed to services; no outcome data attaches to it [jacob-breaking-patterns].

### [ST:CRITERIA] Define progress as three measurable shifts, not disappearance
**Move:** Track how often the pattern fires, how high it peaks, and how long recovery takes — three separately falling curves — instead of waiting for the pattern to be gone. Most of these patterns never fully clear; the working target is regulation of a circuit, not its deletion.
**APPLY WHEN:** setting expectations at the outset; when any re-activation reads as relapse; when "I understood it and nothing changed" reads as failure.
**AVOID WHEN:** used to redefine away a genuine absence of progress over months.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** the authors' stated realistic outcome, resting on an indelible-memory hypothesis they explicitly flag as untested for schemas [young-schema-therapy].

## Mapping schemas, modes and origins

### [ST:MAP18] Map the eighteen themes and split conditional from unconditional
**Move:** Go through the 18 named themes across 5 domains and mark which describe a lifelong theme; rate severity by how many situations trigger it, how intensely, and for how long; group by domain to see which need is missing. Then sort each into unconditional (no escape clause) or conditional — the "if I do X, then Y will not happen" bargains such as submitting, self-sacrificing, seeking approval, holding feelings in, holding impossible standards — and name the core theme each bargain holds off. Carry over the manual's channel weighting: the disconnection group is worked mainly through relationships, dependence/vulnerability/failure/self-control/negativity/standards mainly through thinking and behaviour, isolation and enmeshment mainly through present life rather than childhood.
**APPLY WHEN:** diffuse dissatisfaction across love, work or play; the same pattern repeating across different relationships; pleasing or perfectionism that cannot say what it protects.
**AVOID WHEN:** acute crisis; labelling another person; treating the output as a diagnosis; or forcing a bargain-to-core pairing that does not fit. Questionnaire scoring is not part of this move and belongs elsewhere.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** factor-analytic support for the item list via the schema questionnaire (Schmidt 1995; Lee 1999); the conditional/unconditional split and the channel weighting are clinical observation only [young-schema-therapy].

### [ST:NEEDS5] Audit the five core needs, then read the moment on two poles
**Move:** For each of five needs — secure attachment; autonomy, competence and identity; freedom to voice needs and feelings; spontaneity and play; realistic limits — ask which were reliably met in childhood, which are met now, and by whom. For a fast in-the-moment read, collapse them to two poles and ask what is under threat right now: connection to people, or the capacity to act on your own. Fear and sadness point to the first, anger and disgust to the second.
**APPLY WHEN:** setting change goals; when "what am I actually missing?" is unanswerable in ordinary terms; mid-conflict when the feeling will not name itself.
**AVOID WHEN:** it turns into cataloguing parental blame rather than naming a present unmet need; or the two-pole shortcut substitutes for the fuller map on a decision that matters.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** the five-need list is footnoted by its own authors as theory-derived and empirically untested; the two-pole compression is a later contextual reformulation with no validating measure [young-schema-therapy; RU contextual donor].

### [ST:ORIGINS4] Type each origin and separate temperament from pattern
**Move:** Classify each pattern among four origin types — too little of what was needed, being harmed or used, too much of a good thing with no limits, and absorbing a significant adult's stance — keeping "I was the target" separate from "I took this in and now do it myself." Then place yourself on the temperament dimensions (calm–anxious, introverted–extraverted, passive–aggressive, optimistic–dysthymic) and split the constitutional part from the pattern-driven part, so effort goes where change is possible. Note that a demanding or guilt-loading voice can also come from grandparents, siblings, teachers or classmates, and that a parent may have been loving in most respects and damaging in one narrow area.
**APPLY WHEN:** a pattern fits no obvious story; repeated failed attempts to change a trait wholesale.
**AVOID WHEN:** the typing starts opening abuse or loss memories — it has stopped being mapping; or temperament is used to declare a pattern unchangeable.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Kagan, Reznick & Snidman (1988) for temperamental stability; the four-type taxonomy and the dimension list are hypotheses with no measure of innate temperament [young-schema-therapy; jacob-breaking-patterns].

### [ST:MODE-MAP] Keep one running master sheet of your states, personally named
**Move:** Maintain a single sheet listing your child, parent, coping and steady-adult states, built from several episodes rather than one memory, and fold every later observation back into it. Give each a personal nickname and use it out loud when the state shows up — the label creates the distance the state itself dissolves. Read states by affective tone: lost, enraged, harsh, flat and mechanical, steady and caring. For the steady state specifically, record which activities, situations and people reliably bring it on, since change is scaled from where access is already easy.
**APPLY WHEN:** states feel like different people; moods arrive causeless; rapid swings; a rigid part has deadlocked a decision.
**AVOID WHEN:** it degenerates into collecting labels instead of observations; the nickname turns contemptuous, which recruits the punishing voice instead of creating distance; it is applied to another person; or marked dissociation is present.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** the taxonomy is the tradition's canon with its ten states conceded as provisional; the master sheet and the naming device are workbook structure and clinical experience [young-schema-therapy; jacob-breaking-patterns].

## Telling a real pattern from ordinary life

### [ST:SIX-PROBE] Probe an activated state along six channels
**Move:** After the state has cooled, write six channels for one episode: the trigger situation, the feelings, the thoughts, the body sensation, the associated memories or images, and the behaviour toward other people. Then add what preceded and followed the state, what you actually needed there, and whether your behaviour got that need met — the last column is the working lever.
**APPLY WHEN:** mapping a state you can already notice, on ordinary non-traumatic triggers, once it has cooled.
**AVOID WHEN:** the memory channel opens abuse or loss material — leave that probe closed and take it to the therapist; or the state is at peak intensity.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** workbook exercise (Worksheets 2 and 3); no outcome trial [jacob-breaking-patterns].

### [ST:DISPROPORTION] Require disproportion plus uncontrollability before calling it a pattern
**Move:** Test any candidate against two criteria together: the emotion is too big for the trigger, and it is hard to steer. For the states that feel good rather than bad, substitute frequency plus serious life damage. Use the look-alike pairs to keep ordinary life out — disciplined-with-limits versus a demanding voice, self-criticism versus self-hatred, enjoyment versus impulsive overdoing, socially adequate anger versus an outburst, a situational switch-off versus blanket avoidance, taking charge flexibly versus rigid control. Ambition still counts as healthy while sleep, rest and friendships survive.
**APPLY WHEN:** you cannot tell whether something is a pattern or just a bad week; high standards you are reluctant to call a problem.
**AVOID WHEN:** used to pathologise a temporary genuine crunch, ordinary irritation, ordinary procrastination, or genuine rest and pleasure — that misreading feeds the demanding voice directly.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** conceptual rule and contrast table from the workbook, offered without cut-offs or validated thresholds [jacob-breaking-patterns].

### [ST:CM-DETECT] Treat outside feedback as the primary channel for the fight-back styles
**Move:** Yielding and avoiding are relatively self-detectable; fighting back is not, so outside data comes first. Ask people directly which coping style they associate with you; check whether the same feedback arrives from unrelated life contexts; and run the three-part proportionality test on any fight-back behaviour — is it proportionate, does it weigh the other person's feelings, could it plausibly produce the outcome you want. Failing any one marks overcompensation rather than assertion. Because people withdraw rather than confront, feedback that reaches you is under-reported: the same comment from more than two people probably means something.
**APPLY WHEN:** your own distress is low but other people's is high; confident-looking behaviour keeps costing relationships; success settles nothing.
**AVOID WHEN:** the informants are adversarial, dependent on you, inside the conflict, or a single hostile voice; or a compensation has just collapsed, when this becomes one more self-attack.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** the proportionality criterion is the authors' clinical test with no trial; the ">2 people" figure is a stated impression, not a measured threshold [young-schema-therapy; jacob-breaking-patterns].

## In the moment: regulation and distance

### [ST:BODY-FIRST] Log the sensation before the thought
**Move:** When a reaction spikes, write the bodily sensation and the emotion first and the thought second, treating the sensation as the primary signal that an old pattern fired. When the feeling is unreadable, ask what a younger version of you would be scared or angry about — as a question, without entering a childhood scene.
**APPLY WHEN:** reactions arrive faster than any identifiable thought; disputing the thought changes nothing; "I don't know what I'm feeling."
**AVOID WHEN:** the somatic signal may be medical and has not been assessed; or the likely content is trauma with no support available.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** a two-track fear-memory model (LeDoux 1996) is cited as rationale, but the authors state it has not been established for schemas; the exercise itself is untested [young-schema-therapy].

### [ST:SAFE-PLACE] Build a safe anchor and a containment kit, then carry them
**Move:** Build a detailed safe-place image with full sensory detail, phrased positively rather than by negation, and return to it deliberately at the opening and closing of any loaded work. Pair it with a containment kit you actually like — slow breathing into the belly, a physical object, naming five things you see, four you hear, three you feel on the skin. Then extend it: in imagery enter a situation where you need no coping at all, saturate that feeling, and carry it into the difficult situation, registering even a slight shift as a result.
**APPLY WHEN:** flooding, impulsive urges, the need to self-soothe; before and after any emotionally loaded exercise.
**AVOID WHEN:** no safe image comes — do not force one; ground in the present room and read the failure as a signal. Also when the skills harden into a new way of switching off.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** standard affect regulation; the mindfulness and distress-tolerance components carry their own trial base (Linehan 1993), while placement inside this method is clinical judgement [young-schema-therapy; jacob-breaking-patterns].

### [ST:PAUSE] Put a named gap between impulse and action
**Move:** Three moves inside one gap. Name what is running in the third person — "the harsh voice is active" rather than "I am worthless" — since first and second person amplify affect and third person creates distance. Stand up; a posture change shifts the state. Then delay: state the impulse aloud, wait ten minutes without forbidding it, ask what the impulse is actually for, re-decide, and extend the delay over weeks. Support it with a written card of reasons and methods and a reward after restraint, then run graded practice on the boring end of life — organisation, punctuality, structure — starting slightly difficult, holding a fixed short duration, then extending.
**APPLY WHEN:** impulsivity, outbursts, chronic lateness, disorganisation, non-persistence; or a thought has fused with you so completely it reads as fact.
**AVOID WHEN:** started at a difficulty that guarantees failure; the pattern is long passivity punctuated by sudden aggression, which points to something else underneath; or the driver looks biological, where medical assessment comes first.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** operant self-control practice with broad CBT support; the defusion and posture components are borrowed from acceptance-based work and are untested inside this method [young-schema-therapy; RU contextual donor].

### [ST:CARD-DIARY] Write the card first, graduate to the diary later
**Move:** Build a four-section card: the feeling and the trigger; the pattern and where it was learned; what it exaggerates plus the strongest counter-evidence; and the exact alternative behaviour. Carry it and read it before and during the trigger, not afterwards. To fill the counter-evidence section, list lifespan evidence for and against the belief, generate the refuting side with deliberately extreme prompts, write it down because it gets dismissed within seconds, then list how you discount each item and reclaim it. Once cards work reliably, graduate to a longer diary where you generate the healthy response unaided.
**APPLY WHEN:** the counterargument is available when calm but unreachable when triggered; the belief is stated as plain fact.
**AVOID WHEN:** the first cards are written before you can produce a healthy side at all — a weak card teaches the wrong thing; reading the card replaces the action; the evidence genuinely supports the content, where the branch is skill-building instead; or "realistic concerns" smuggle the pattern back in.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** the card and diary templates are the authors' own (Young 1993; Young et al. 1996), reported as the most helpful coping tool by many patients but never trialled in isolation; the evidence ledger is standard cognitive validity testing applied lifespan-wide [young-schema-therapy].

## The inner critic and the parent voices

### [ST:PM-COGID] Identify the voice cognitively and log its origin and current weight
**Move:** Recall a situation of disproportionate pressure or rejection at a cognitive level only: what you felt obliged to do, how you would have felt acting otherwise, what the voice sounds like and whose it is. Classify by the dominant feeling — a sense of failure points to a demanding voice, guilt to a guilt-loading one, shame or self-hatred to a punishing one. Then write each recurring message with its biographical origin, a 0–100 rating of how much it still weighs today, and what activates it.
**APPLY WHEN:** a recurring self-critical or guilt-loading inner voice; the first mapping of that voice.
**AVOID WHEN:** you start sliding into the felt scene rather than describing it — getting inside the punishing voice is explicitly ruled out for solo work; the voice is anchored in abuse memories or tells you to hurt yourself; or you are already flooded.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** workbook exercise with an explicit in-chapter safety carve-out; the 0–100 rating has no psychometrics behind it [jacob-breaking-patterns].

### [ST:PM-SORT] Sort each message into keep, moderate or erase — with counter-evidence
**Move:** For every logged message decide one of three fates. Discipline and care messages belong to the steady adult and stay. Demanding ones get rewritten into a proportionate rule. Self-devaluing punitive ones are erased rather than softened. For each message you are unsure about, write personal counter-evidence and treat it as a truth test against selective perception — half-suspecting the harsh voice might be right is described as the normal starting state, not as confirmation.
**APPLY WHEN:** the state vocabulary is solid and the message inventory is already written.
**AVOID WHEN:** attempted before the definitions are solid, where the sort collapses; or the punishing voice strengthens under challenge — that is the stated indication for professional support, not for more effort.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** cognitive restructuring has method-level support; the three-way sort and its worked tables are clinical experience only [jacob-breaking-patterns].

### [ST:EXTERNALIZE] Address the punishing voice as someone else's, and interrogate the urge to punish
**Move:** Name the voice and speak to it as a third party, relabelling it as the original speaker's voice rather than your own — that makes it something you can ally against instead of something you are. At each urge to punish yourself or another, run the chain: were the intentions good; if so does that not warrant forgiveness; how exactly would punishment help; will the behaviour not simply recur unobserved; is the cost worth it. Replace it with honest appraisal, remorse, restitution, better future behaviour, self-forgiveness.
**APPLY WHEN:** self-attack is continuous and treated as simple truth; moralistic intolerance that makes no allowance for circumstances.
**AVOID WHEN:** the naming turns into one more slur on yourself; gross negligence or genuine wrongdoing is what is actually at issue; or self-harm is present, where fighting this voice belongs in a live session.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** externalising is clinical experience; the punishment chain cites operant work on punishment (Baron 1988; Rachlin 1976) [young-schema-therapy].

## Changing behaviour and life structure

### [ST:CM-PROCON] Weigh the coping style honestly, including where it genuinely works
**Move:** For one coping style, first sort where you yield to it, avoid through it and fight back with it, separately per life area. Then build a two-by-two of advantages and disadvantages split short-term against long-term, deliberately listing the situations where that coping is genuinely useful, and keep the disadvantage column physically visible. Read the advantage column as the real obstacle list, not as something to argue away. The stated goal is weakening, never erasure — every coping style has a function.
**APPLY WHEN:** before any attempt to reduce a coping style; when the state feels good in the moment and motivation is wobbly.
**AVOID WHEN:** used as self-prosecution — a grid without genuine short-term gains will not be believed; used to justify erasing the style outright; or the style is aggressive protection and no substitute protection has been built yet, which is explicitly warned against.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** decisional balance is a standard CBT component; the coping-triad sort and its worked lists are clinical taxonomy, illustrative rather than data-derived [young-schema-therapy; jacob-breaking-patterns].

### [ST:BREAK] One behaviour at a time — define the replacement, rehearse, do, debrief, name the block
**Move:** Pick the most costly behaviour you believe you can actually attempt, not the easiest, and define the positive replacement rather than just the stopping. Write how the old behaviour once made sense, which converts shame into understanding. Rehearse it vividly and pre-specify the situation, the exact intended behaviour, which states have interfered before, an in-situ reminder, and the reward. Run it first in a low-demand situation, then generalise. If it goes undone, run the block interview — what was I afraid of, angry about, unable to tolerate, what did I stop believing possible — then retry smaller.
**APPLY WHEN:** the pattern is named, the mapping is done, and a real non-traumatic occasion exists this week.
**AVOID WHEN:** the assignment is vague; rehearsal expands as postponement; the target is trauma exposure or a structured exposure hierarchy; the reward is the maladaptive behaviour itself; partial success gets judged as failure; or the block resolves into an abusive figure.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** rehearsal and contingency management have broad CBT support; "start with the most problematic behaviour" is asserted rather than trialled, and the worksheet structure is the workbook authors' [young-schema-therapy; jacob-breaking-patterns].

### [ST:HC-RESTORE] Reopen access to play before prescribing it, then dose it
**Move:** Prescribing fun fails where the playful side is suppressed, because it no longer knows what it likes and asking in a low mood yields nothing. So first relax and re-live clearly good times in full sensory detail — smells, tastes, sounds, skin — until the affect reactivates; only then generate today's analogues from a prompt list, trying at least one new thing. Then dose it: about an hour rather than ten minutes, small steps, fortnightly beats never, shared with people rather than long and solo, and swapped for something steadier on a genuinely bad day. Check the ratio periodically — a thin playful side under chronic load is named as a burnout risk, and living there permanently is not the goal either.
**APPLY WHEN:** you cannot name what you enjoy; chronic joylessness under high load; or persistent escapist play.
**AVOID WHEN:** the "happy" memory sits inside a loss — that is grief, this exercise is not grief work, and it must never be used to brighten one; it becomes a duty list policed by the demanding voice; or it is dodging a problem that needs facing.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** the affective bridge is a described phenomenon, untested as an intervention here; the dosing rules and prompt list are clinical experience with no trial [jacob-breaking-patterns].

### [ST:BIG-CHANGE] Screen a major life change for pattern drive before making it
**Move:** Before leaving a job, a relationship or a city, ask what you would do if you did not carry these patterns, then list the alternatives with advantages, disadvantages and practical feasibility. Try changing behaviour inside the current situation first. A sudden uncharacteristic improvement — "everything is fine now, I don't need any of this" — reads as probable overcompensation rather than recovery. Unpack any trait-shaped goal into named behaviour in named situations, and cap intended changes at three, rating each for importance, your actual influence over it, motivation, and willingness to spend effort.
**APPLY WHEN:** a big change is contemplated and it is unclear whether it is growth or escape; goals phrased as traits; too many change ambitions at once.
**AVOID WHEN:** the situation is dangerous or intolerable — leaving is then not screened as pattern-driven; or concreteness hardens into one more perfection standard.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** clinical experience with no trial; the goal-specificity claim is stated as an observed predictor, not a measured effect [young-schema-therapy; jacob-breaking-patterns].

## Relationships and interpersonal cycles

### [ST:PARTNER] Audit partner selection for familiarity-driven attraction
**Move:** Review past partners for a repeating type: did the strongest attraction go to people who trigger the core pattern, and did people who actually met the need feel flat or suffocating? On this model the felt "chemistry" is the pattern recognising something familiar, and a fluent rationalisation for why the kind partner did not work is data rather than explanation. Calibrate rather than eliminate — enough of the familiar quality for attraction to exist, plus a demonstrated willingness to weigh your needs.
**APPLY WHEN:** a selection pattern repeats; healthy candidates feel boring; "everyone in my life treated me badly."
**AVOID WHEN:** used to pathologise a good current relationship or as sole grounds for ending one; or it slides into blaming yourself for having been mistreated.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** the "schema chemistry" construct is the authors' clinical term with no trial; the pairing table is illustrative [young-schema-therapy; RU origins donor].

### [ST:CYCLE-CLASH] Map the two-sided cycle and agree a pause signal
**Move:** Write the loop as a sequence rather than a grievance: I do X, you do Y, I do more X. Then write what sits behind each move — what is visible is coping, what drives it is the vulnerable or angry state underneath, on both sides. Name the shape (both attacking, one pressing while the other withdraws, both withdrawing, one dominating while the other yields). Agree a neutral code word meaning "we are in the loop, pause", break contact physically for a few minutes, and return naming your need rather than their behaviour. Raise the pattern with them only while they are in a steady state, never mid-activation. Afterwards run the reflection separately: what triggered me, what I felt in the body, what my intention was, what actually happened, what I needed — then what they probably felt, what I expected, and how I would have felt in their place.
**APPLY WHEN:** the same argument repeats with minor variations; reasonable arguments change nothing; you act in ways you know better than.
**AVOID WHEN:** safety is at issue; the other person has no interest in the frame; it becomes a scoring device against them mid-conflict; or reassurance keeps failing and you read that as licence to withdraw, which completes their loop rather than exiting yours.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** a contextual reformulation of the mode model plus a mentalization-derived question set; conceptual, with no outcome data [RU contextual donor; jacob-breaking-patterns].

### [ST:ASK-NEED] State the need as a prediction test, and run the resentment ledger
**Move:** First test whether the behaviour is chosen: for each burdensome activity ask whether you want to do it and whether you have to — neither makes it a candidate — and imagine concretely declining, reading what comes back (sharp anxiety points one way, guilt another). Then write what you predict will happen if you state the need or show the emotion, state it to a specific appropriate person, and compare prediction against outcome. For ongoing accommodation, track what you give against what you get across the relationship as a whole, agree only to what you will not later resent, and start nothing you cannot continue indefinitely unless you time-box it out loud. Expect overshoot — too little first, sometimes too aggressive next.
**APPLY WHEN:** chronic hinting, over-compliance, accumulating resentment; guilt at any reduction of care; vague loneliness with no named cause.
**AVOID WHEN:** the other person genuinely and consistently retaliates, where the target is selection rather than expression; saying no would be dangerous; the relationship is inherently non-equal, such as a parent and a small child; or you expect no friction — the fear of criticism when you give less is initially realistic, and the loop only breaks if that phase is expected.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** behavioural-experiment logic plus clinical experience; the claim that this class of pattern responds especially well is author-reported, not measured [young-schema-therapy; jacob-breaking-patterns].

## Operations that need a live therapist

### [ST:VC-BRIDGE] Affective bridge from today's feeling to its origin
**Move:** Relax, re-enter a recent situation carrying the vulnerable feeling, intensify the emotion, erase the current scene while keeping the feeling in the body, and let earlier images surface on their own; then compare the two scripts. Run in reverse it starts from today's unexplained affect. The claimed mechanism is that imagery run from a feeling reaches the biographical origin of that feeling — which is exactly why it is not a solo operation: what surfaces is not chosen in advance.
**APPLY WHEN:** a recurring disproportionate reaction whose origin is unclear, with a therapist present and recovery time afterwards.
**AVOID WHEN:** solo; the likely origin is abuse, violence or loss; dissociation or self-harm risk; late in a session with no recovery time; or content is suggested rather than allowed to arrive, which contaminates the image.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** clinical method framed on hot cognitions shifting more readily than cold ones; no isolated trial, and no data at all for a self-help version [young-schema-therapy; jacob-breaking-patterns].

### [ST:VC-RESCRIPT] Imagery rescripting of the childhood scene
**Move:** Bring the adult self, or another caring figure, into the childhood scene to comfort and protect the younger self, and let the encounter run until that need is actually met rather than merely addressed; then fix the result as a durable image, drawing or object. Where the person's own adult self takes the role, it comes after the model has been provided, never first — an internal adult recruited cold tends to arrive critical.
**APPLY WHEN:** contact with the vulnerable state is established and tolerable, inside a live therapy frame.
**AVOID WHEN:** solo; the scene is an unprocessed traumatic memory; fragility, dissociation-proneness, or decompensation risk. Early rescripting is performed by the therapist even inside therapy, so there is no version of this that is a self-exercise.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** method-level trial support within schema therapy; no data for a solo version, and the operation-level boundary places it with a clinician regardless [young-schema-therapy; jacob-breaking-patterns; psy-guidelines-2026].

### [ST:DP-BYPASS] Get past the switched-off state before anything else
**Move:** Run the ladder: name the state, map its cues, tell its origin story, credit what it once protected, list its current costs and benefits, then negotiate with it from the steady adult position and ask it to step aside for a bounded stretch, letting it keep something concrete in exchange. Probe for hidden detachment by asking what you feel now, what you would like now, what you feel toward this person — numb or "nothing" is the state answering, not health.
**APPLY WHEN:** affect is flat, answers are compliant, no real material is reachable, and the work is stuck because the avoidance cannot be penetrated — with a clinician present.
**AVOID WHEN:** solo; no working alliance yet; no safer coping available for whatever emerges; or it opens traumatic material with no containment. Cognitive argument beyond the educational minimum is counterproductive here — reasoning is what this state already does.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** clinical experience; the framing of the refuge as a prison is theory rather than measurement [young-schema-therapy].

### [ST:PP-FIGHT] Fight the punishing voice as the original speaker, never as yourself — chair work included
**Move:** Identify whose voice the state carries and rename it after that person; in imagery the punishing voice is always embodied by that figure and never by the person themselves. Speak against it firmly rather than reasoning with it — this state is granted no goodwill and no empathy in the model, so debate fails — and transfer the speaking role to the person over time. Two-chair dialogue between the steady adult and the punishing voice runs with the clinician coaching from outside rather than occupying a chair. Pre-agree replacement actions for punishment urges and watch for post-session flips.
**APPLY WHEN:** self-attack is chronic and feels like plain truth, inside an established treatment.
**AVOID WHEN:** solo — chair work with vulnerable material is clinician-led without exception, and the workbook's endorsement of solo chair dialogue is overridden here; the person pictures themselves as the punishing figure, which is corrected first; or risk is high, where substitute actions must not replace a safety assessment.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** clinical experience, commonly a year or more of work; granting this state no redeeming function is a model commitment rather than a finding [young-schema-therapy; jacob-breaking-patterns].
