# Schema Therapy: A Practitioner's Guide — digest

## What this book is for the lens

Mechanism thesis: a childhood-formed schema (memories + emotions + cognitions + bodily sensations; behavior excluded) recruits three coping styles — surrender, avoidance, overcompensation — that keep manufacturing confirming evidence. Argument alone does not move it, because the affective and somatic components sit in a fast, coarse, unconscious fear-memory system; the realistic target is regulation over an indelible circuit, not deletion. Where it beats other sources: a finite enumerable set (18 schemas, 5 domains, 3 coping styles, 10 modes) with per-schema channel weighting and per-mode strategy, turning "something is wrong with my life" into an addressable target list — plus unusual explicitness about which of its own material is untested and about how the helper fails.

## Core model

- **Early Maladaptive Schema** — a broad pervasive theme about self and relationships, formed in childhood/adolescence, dysfunctional to a significant degree. Dimensional: severity sets how many situations trigger it, how intense, how long. Schemas fight for survival — the familiar feels true. The emotional reading of childhood is usually accurate; the causal attributions are not.
- **18 schemas / 5 domains.** *Disconnection and Rejection*: Abandonment/Instability, Mistrust/Abuse, Emotional Deprivation (nurturance/empathy/protection), Defectiveness/Shame, Social Isolation. *Impaired Autonomy and Performance*: Dependence/Incompetence, Vulnerability to Harm or Illness, Enmeshment/Undeveloped Self, Failure. *Impaired Limits*: Entitlement/Grandiosity, Insufficient Self-Control. *Other-Directedness*: Subjugation, Self-Sacrifice, Approval/Recognition-Seeking. *Overvigilance and Inhibition*: Negativity/Pessimism, Emotional Inhibition, Unrelenting Standards, Punitiveness.
- **Unconditional vs conditional.** Core schemas have no escape clause; conditional ones (Subjugation, Self-Sacrifice, Approval-Seeking, Emotional Inhibition, Unrelenting Standards) are "if I do X, Y won't happen" bargains that eventually fail back onto the core.
- **Origins.** Five core needs (secure attachment; autonomy/competence/identity; freedom to express valid needs and emotions; spontaneity and play; realistic limits) meet four experience types: toxic frustration, victimization, too much of a good thing, selective internalization. Plus seven bipolar temperament dimensions (labile–nonreactive, dysthymic–optimistic, anxious–calm, obsessive–distractible, passive–aggressive, irritable–cheerful, shy–sociable), treated as largely fixed.
- **Coping styles** = freeze/flight/fight → surrender, avoidance, overcompensation. Style is a trait, response a state; the same schema yields opposite behaviors across people and decades, so behavior never identifies the schema.
- **Modes** = whichever schemas/operations are active now, on a dissociation spectrum. Ten in four families: Child (Vulnerable, Angry, Impulsive/Undisciplined, Happy); Coping (Compliant Surrenderer, Detached Protector, Overcompensator); Parent (Punitive, Demanding); Healthy Adult. BPD set: Abandoned Child, Angry and Impulsive Child, Punitive Parent, Detached Protector, Healthy Adult. NPD: Lonely Child, Self-Aggrandizer, Detached Self-Soother.
- **Architecture.** Assessment & Education (life history, YSQ, YPI, Young–Rygh Avoidance Inventory, Young Compensation Inventory, imagery, Case Conceptualization Form) → Change Phase with four co-equal blended components: cognitive, experiential, behavioral pattern-breaking, therapy relationship. Two relationship stances: empathic confrontation, limited reparenting.
- **Anchors.** Ch. 1 model; Ch. 2 assessment; Ch. 3 cognitive (Flash Card = Fig. 3.1, Schema Diary = Fig. 3.2); Ch. 4 experiential; Ch. 5 behavioral (Table 5.1 = 18 schemas × 3 styles); Ch. 6 relationship; Ch. 7 per-schema strategies; Ch. 8 mode work; Ch. 9 BPD; Ch. 10 NPD. Reader: *Reinventing Your Life* (Young & Klosko, 1993).
- **Channel weighting (Ch. 7).** Relationship-led: Abandonment, Mistrust/Abuse, Emotional Deprivation, Defectiveness. Cognitive-behavioral-led: Dependence, Vulnerability, Failure, Insufficient Self-Control, Negativity, Unrelenting Standards. Behavioral+experiential-led: Emotional Inhibition. Current-life- not childhood-led: Social Isolation, Enmeshment.

## Moves

### [ST:GATE] Sequencing and suitability screen
**Move:** Screen five postponement conditions — major life crisis, psychosis, acute untreated Axis I disorder, moderate-to-severe substance abuse, a genuinely situational problem. Stabilize that layer first, then take up the chronic pattern.
**APPLY WHEN:** at the start; whenever acute symptoms re-escalate mid-work.
**AVOID WHEN:** never skipped, never a reason to delay urgent clinical contact; schema work under active substance use is near-futile — the substance numbs the emotions the work needs.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** clinical judgment plus general CBT outcome literature; screen itself untested.

### [ST:MAP18] Map the 18 schemas; split conditional from unconditional
**Move:** Mark which of the 18 name a lifelong theme; rate severity (how many triggers, how intense, how long); group by domain to see the missing need. Formal version: YSQ read item by item, three or more 5s/6s in one schema counts as live. Then sort each into unconditional or conditional, writing the conditional ones as explicit "if… then…" bargains and naming the core schema each holds off.
**APPLY WHEN:** diffuse dissatisfaction in love/work/play; patterns repeating across relationships; pleasing or perfectionism that cannot say what it protects.
**AVOID WHEN:** acute crisis; labeling another person; reading totals instead of item patterns; forcing a bargain pairing.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** factor-analytic support for the list via YSQ (Schmidt 1995; Lee 1999); the 3-item rule and the conditional split are clinical observation.

### [ST:NEEDS5] Audit the five core emotional needs
**Move:** For each of the five needs, ask which were reliably met in childhood, which are met now, and by whom. Where one is chronically unmet, note the matching schema domain.
**APPLY WHEN:** setting change goals; when "what am I actually missing?" is unanswerable in ordinary terms.
**AVOID WHEN:** it becomes blame-cataloguing of parents rather than mapping present unmet need.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** authors footnote this list as theory-derived and empirically untested.

### [ST:ORIGINS4] Type each origin; place your temperament
**Move:** Classify each schema among the four origin types, separating "I was the target" from "I absorbed this and now do it." Then locate yourself on the seven temperament dimensions and split constitutional from schema-driven, so effort goes to the modifiable part.
**APPLY WHEN:** a schema fits no obvious trauma story; repeated failed attempts to change a trait wholesale ("stop being shy").
**AVOID WHEN:** the typing opens abuse or loss memories — it stops being mapping; or temperament is used to declare a pattern unchangeable.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Kagan, Reznick & Snidman (1988) for temperamental stability; the taxonomy and dimensions are hypotheses, with no measure of innate temperament.

### [ST:PERP-AUDIT] Coping-triad sort plus perpetuation log
**Move:** For one schema, write where you surrender, avoid and overcompensate, separately per life area, using Table 5.1 as prompts not answers. Then log how the schema is fed: what got distorted cognitively, what feeling was blocked or exaggerated, what behavior chose the confirming situation, what interpersonal move pulled the confirming response.
**APPLY WHEN:** one schema produces contradictory behavior; a pattern recurs across people; its evidence is believed objective.
**AVOID WHEN:** the label is still guesswork; the style is treated as fixed identity — it drifts, the schema does not; or real harm is ongoing, where "your distortion" is the misuse case.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** clinical taxonomy; Tables 1.1/5.1 illustrative, not data-derived.

### [ST:COMPTEST] Overcompensation proportionality test, with an outside informant
**Move:** Three-part test on any fight-back behavior: proportionate, weighs others' feelings, could reasonably produce the wanted outcome. Failing any one marks overcompensation, not assertion. Self-observation is systematically poor here, so ask a partner or friend for concrete examples and treat that as data.
**APPLY WHEN:** confident-looking behavior keeps costing relationships; success does not settle anything; "too much" warmth producing no felt closeness.
**AVOID WHEN:** a compensation has just collapsed — the schema is at full intensity and this becomes another self-attack; or the informant is unsafe or hostile.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** authors' clinical criterion; no trial.

### [ST:LEDGER] Lifetime evidence ledger, then reclaim the discounts
**Move:** List all lifespan evidence for and against one schema and weigh both columns; the near-impossibility of the refuting list is itself the demonstration. Generate that side with deliberately extreme prompts and write it down, since it gets dismissed fast. Then list how you discount each item ("they don't know the real me") and reclaim it.
**APPLY WHEN:** the schema is stated as plain fact; or the refuting list exists but produces no felt shift.
**AVOID WHEN:** the evidence genuinely supports the content — switch to skill-building, the book's own branch; where life truly holds little disconfirming evidence, name the absence rather than fabricating.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** standard cognitive validity testing applied lifespan-wide; one component of a package tested only at method level.

### [ST:CARD-DIARY] Flash card first, Schema Diary later
**Move:** Card on the four-section template (Fig. 3.1): feeling and trigger → schema and where learned → what it exaggerates plus strongest counter-evidence → the exact alternative behavior. Carry it; read it *before* and *during* the trigger. Once cards work, graduate to the nine-field Schema Diary (Fig. 3.2), which requires generating the healthy response yourself.
**APPLY WHEN:** the counterargument is available when calm but unreachable when triggered; the diary once you can respond unaided.
**AVOID WHEN:** first cards written alone too early — the healthy side writes a weak card; reading replaces the action; the diary precedes card proficiency; or "realistic concerns" smuggle the schema back in.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** authors' templates (Young et al. 1996; Young 1993); reported as the most helpful coping strategy for many BPD patients — no isolated trial.

### [ST:CRITERIA] Define progress as three measurable shifts
**Move:** Track activation frequency down, peak intensity down, recovery time down — rather than waiting for the schema to be gone. Most schemas never fully heal; the affective and somatic memory is regulable, not erasable.
**APPLY WHEN:** setting expectations at the start; when any re-activation reads as relapse, or "I understood it and nothing changed" reads as failure.
**AVOID WHEN:** used to redefine away a genuine absence of progress.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** from the authors' stated realistic outcome plus the LeDoux-based indelible-memory hypothesis, flagged by them as untested for schemas.

### [ST:BODY-FIRST] Log sensation before thought; ask the child part
**Move:** When a reaction spikes, log the bodily sensation and emotion first and the thought second, treating the sensation as the primary signal that a schema fired. When feelings are unreadable, close the eyes, picture your child self, and ask what she is scared or angry about.
**APPLY WHEN:** reactions arrive faster than any identifiable thought; disputing the thought changes nothing; "I don't know what I'm feeling."
**AVOID WHEN:** the somatic signal may be medical and unassessed; or the likely content is trauma with no support available.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** LeDoux (1996) supports the two-track model; the exercise is the authors' untested application.

### [ST:SAFE-PLACE] Safe place, grounding, self-soothing kit
**Move:** Build a detailed safe-place image with full sensory detail, phrased positively ("you are safe") rather than by negation, and return to it deliberately. Pair with a containment kit you actually like: relaxation, transitional objects, mindful observation of emotion without acting until you can think.
**APPLY WHEN:** opening and closing any emotionally loaded work; flooding, impulsive urges, need for self-soothing.
**AVOID WHEN:** no safe image comes — do not force one; ground in the present room and treat the failure as a signal. Also when the skills become a new Detached Protector.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** standard affect regulation; mindfulness and distress tolerance from Linehan (1993), which has its own trial base — placement inside ST is clinical judgment.

### [ST:MODE-MAP] Map and personally name your modes
**Move:** Map recurring states onto the inventory from several episodes rather than one memory, then give each a personal name ("The Wall", "the Status-Seeker"); keep a name only if recognized intuitively. Read modes by affective tone: lost, enraged, harsh, flat and mechanical, strong and loving. Probe for hidden detachment — what am I feeling now, what would I like now, what do I feel toward this person; numb or "nothing" signals the coping mode, not health.
**APPLY WHEN:** states feel like different people; moods arrive causeless; rapid affect swings; a rigid part has deadlocked a decision.
**AVOID WHEN:** diagnosing someone else; pejorative names; persuading against denial; naming parts instead of acting; or marked dissociation, where reifying parts is a clinician's call.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** clinical practice with BPD and NPD; the taxonomy is ISST canon, and the 10 modes are stated as provisional.

### [ST:EXTERNALIZE] Externalize the punitive voice; interrogate the punishment urge
**Move:** Name the punishing voice and address it as a third party, relabelling it as the parent's voice rather than your own — that makes it ego-dystonic and lets you ally against it. At each urge to punish yourself or another, run the chain: were the intentions good; if so, does that not warrant forgiveness; how would punishment help; will the behavior not recur unobserved; is the cost worth it? Replace it with honest appraisal, remorse, restitution, better future behavior, self-forgiveness.
**APPLY WHEN:** self-attack is continuous and treated as simple truth; moralistic intolerance with no allowance for circumstances.
**AVOID WHEN:** the naming becomes a slur on the self; the voice is a genuine hallucination; gross negligence or immoral intent is at issue; or self-harm is present, where fighting the mode belongs in session.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** externalizing is clinical experience; the chain cites operant research on punishment (Baron 1988; Rachlin 1976).

### [ST:HA-FUNCTIONS] Practise the Healthy Adult's three jobs separately
**Move:** Instead of vague "being rational", practise one function at a time: comfort and protect the vulnerable part; set a limit on the angry and impulsive part; contradict and moderate the punishing and coping modes.
**APPLY WHEN:** you can already name at least two of your modes; anger or panic takes over the whole personality with no counterweight.
**AVOID WHEN:** the Healthy Adult is near-absent — self-generated "limits" arrive as the Punitive Parent renamed; or the practice suppresses the vulnerable part instead of caring for it.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** named as the central goal of schema therapy; method-level evidence only.

### [ST:BREAK] One behavior at a time — weigh, rehearse, do, debrief, name the block
**Move:** Pick the most distressing or damaging behavior you believe you can actually attempt, not the easiest, and define its healthy replacement. List advantages and disadvantages of *continuing* it and weigh rather than count. Write how it once made sense, converting shame into understanding. Rehearse in imagery, prepare a flash card for the predicted obstacle, write the assignment, execute, debrief. If undone, run the block interview — what was I afraid of, angry about, unable to tolerate, what did I stop believing possible — then retry smaller or attach a reward you value.
**APPLY WHEN:** the schema is named, groundwork done, and a real occasion exists this week.
**AVOID WHEN:** the assignment is vague; rehearsal expands as postponement; the target is trauma exposure; the block resolves into an abusive figure; the reward is the maladaptive behavior; or the interview becomes self-attack.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** rehearsal and contingency management have broad CBT support; "start with the most problematic behavior" is asserted, not trialed.

### [ST:BIG-CHANGE] Screen a major life change for schema drive
**Move:** Before leaving a job, relationship or city, ask "if I did not have my schemas, what would I do?", then list alternatives with advantages, disadvantages and practical feasibility. Change behavior inside the current situation first; a sudden uncharacteristic improvement ("flight to health") reads as probable overcompensation.
**APPLY WHEN:** a big change is contemplated and it is unclear whether it is growth or escape.
**AVOID WHEN:** the situation is dangerous or intolerable (abuse) — leaving is then not screened as schema-driven.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** clinical experience; no trial.

### [ST:PARTNER] Audit partner selection for schema chemistry
**Move:** Review past partners for a repeating type: did the strongest attraction go to people who trigger a core schema, and did people who met the need feel suffocating or flat? Schema triggering is what the model says generates "chemistry", and a rationalization for why the kind partner "didn't work" is data, not explanation. Calibrate rather than eliminate — for Subjugation, dominant enough for chemistry but willing to weigh your needs; for Self-Sacrifice, strong and giving rather than weak and needy.
**APPLY WHEN:** a partner-selection pattern repeats; healthy candidates feel flat; "everyone in my life treated me badly."
**AVOID WHEN:** used to pathologize a good current relationship or as sole grounds for a breakup; or it slides into blaming yourself for having been abused.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** "schema chemistry" is the authors' clinical term; no trial.

### [ST:ASK-NEED] Express a need or feeling as a prediction test
**Move:** Write what you predict will happen if you state the need or show the emotion, state it to a specific appropriate person, then compare prediction with outcome. Sort felt deprivation into nurturance / empathy / protection and map which is missing where. Ask directly instead of hinting; present as vulnerable rather than strong.
**APPLY WHEN:** compliance, feeling trapped, chronic hinting; flat affect and withheld warmth; vague loneliness with no named cause.
**AVOID WHEN:** the other person genuinely and consistently retaliates — the target is then relationship selection; or it swings to the opposite extreme. Expect overshoot: too little first, sometimes too aggressive next.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** clinical experience plus behavioral-experiment logic; the claim that Subjugation is easier to treat is author-reported.

### [ST:GIVE-GET] Give/get ledger and the resentment test for limits
**Move:** Per significant relationship, track what you give against what you get; where skewed, give less and ask for more, judging balance over the relationship as a whole. Test the belief that others are fragile by giving less once and observing that they are fine. For any ongoing accommodation apply the resentment test: agree only to what you will not later resent, and start nothing you cannot continue indefinitely unless you state a time box.
**APPLY WHEN:** chronic over-giving with accumulating resentment; guilt at any reduction of care; negotiating availability with a demanding person.
**AVOID WHEN:** inherently non-equal relationships (parent–child); the dependent party genuinely cannot cope; safety, which overrides the resentment test; or schemas other than Self-Sacrifice — score-keeping is flagged as helpful *only* here.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** clinical experience; Kohlberg (1963) cited to place Self-Sacrifice above Subjugation in moral development.

### [ST:TRUST-SPECTRUM] Spectrum trust; answer criticism by rule
**Move:** Rate people on a continuum with "trustworthy enough" as the working threshold; stop score-keeping and loyalty tests. For criticism use a two-branch rule: if valid, accept and change; if invalid, state your view once and internally affirm it is false — in neither branch attack back. Before reacting to a perceived slight, list alternative explanations.
**APPLY WHEN:** chronic guardedness, preemptive betrayal, paranoid testing; criticism triggers collapse or counterattack; ordinary delays read as abandonment.
**AVOID WHEN:** the person is demonstrably abusive — there the instruction is distance and limits; or it talks you out of a legitimately unsafe read.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** clinical experience; standard cognitive technique inside ST.

### [ST:PAUSE] Insert thought between impulse and action; grade the discipline
**Move:** Make the consequence check explicit before acting, supported by time-outs, distraction, a flash card of reasons and methods for self-control, and self-reward after restraint. Then run graded homework on organisation, boring tasks, punctuality, structure and frustration tolerance: start slightly difficult, hold a fixed short duration, then extend.
**APPLY WHEN:** impulsivity, outbursts, chronic lateness, unreliability, disorganisation, non-persistence.
**AVOID WHEN:** started at a difficulty that guarantees failure; or the pattern is long passivity punctuated by sudden aggression, which points to Subjugation underneath. Where the driver looks biological (e.g. ADHD), medication should be considered.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** clinical experience; this schema is unusual in being ego-dystonic, and addictions do not define it.

### [ST:WORRY-LEDGER] Prediction log, worry budget, controllability split
**Move:** Write concrete negative predictions and record how often they come true; schedule a fixed worry time and postpone worry into it; schedule pleasure against a survival-organised life. Challenge the belief that worrying prevents bad outcomes and compare mood under both stances. Classify each past negative event as controllable (fix the mechanism) or uncontrollable (no predictive weight). Separately re-estimate the probability of a feared catastrophe *and* your capacity to cope, and drop safety signals in graded steps.
**APPLY WHEN:** chronic worry, indecision, complaining; dread-to-panic range with a life restricted by prevention.
**AVOID WHEN:** used to dismiss grief not yet done; complaining is deprivation-driven, where the move is asking for care; or the danger is real.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** cites Borkovec et al. (1983) for the "magic of worrying"; the healthy benchmark is a mild positive bias (Taylor & Brown 1994), not accuracy.

### [ST:PREFERENCE-LOG] Find your own preferences and act on them
**Move:** As a behavioral experiment, list what you inherently enjoy — using bodily pleasure as the reference signal — plus what you like and dislike in significant others, then act on those preferences even where they diverge from the person you are fused with. List how you are similar to and differ from that figure, keeping the similarities. Cost-benefit your standards and deliberately do some tasks imperfectly.
**APPLY WHEN:** emptiness, "no direction", unknown preferences; unclear identity boundaries with a parent, partner or boss; perfectionism with no pleasure from success.
**AVOID WHEN:** attempted in crisis or before stabilization; overcompensation denies all similarity to the fused figure; or moved too fast, since slow steps with close review is the stated method here.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** clinical experience and case illustration; no trial.

### [ST:OBSERVER] Drop aggrandizement for one occasion; run the reciprocity check
**Move:** For one social evening refuse to switch into self-aggrandizement: observe, listen, withhold every remark designed to elicit admiration, and record what happened. Alongside it apply the reciprocity test — what you expect to receive against what you actually give — and stop expecting anything you are unwilling to supply. Track the devaluation arc: log each moment of contempt with what had just happened to your own sense of adequacy.
**APPLY WHEN:** the aggrandizing pattern is visible to you; a relationship has cooled into chronic fault-finding; a partner reports feeling used.
**AVOID WHEN:** the setting is high-stakes professionally; the experiment becomes a new performance to be graded; the log becomes a dossier of the partner's failings.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** authors' behavioral homework; method-level trials only.

### [ST:CASE-CONCEP] Build and keep revising the Case Conceptualization Form
**Move:** One form holding relevant schemas, current problems and their schema links, triggers, severity and decompensation risk, temperament, origins, core childhood memories, cognitive distortions, coping behaviors per style, modes, and effects on the therapy relationship. Note which schemas fire together as a unit and map the chain. Living draft.
**APPLY WHEN:** after life history plus inventories plus at least one experiential probe, before change work.
**AVOID WHEN:** built off one data source, diagnosis alone, or a simplistic reading of childhood — misidentification flips the intervention (Emotional Deprivation read as Dependence turns reparenting into withheld guidance).
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** clinical framework; method-level trials evaluate the package, never this form.

### [ST:IMG-ASSESS] Assessment imagery and the affect bridge
**Move:** Give a three-point rationale, then elicit with the least instruction that yields a usable image — let it float up, described aloud in present tense, first person, with sensory detail and the bodily location of the emotion. Fixed order, one per session: free upsetting childhood image → one with each parent separately → other formative figures. Then intensify the emotion, wipe the image while keeping the feeling in the body, and let a present-day scene with the same feeling arrive; compare the scripts. Runs in reverse from today's unexplained affect. Always debrief eyes-open in schema language.
**APPLY WHEN:** questionnaires give a flat picture; the person cannot report the relevant thoughts; affect is disproportionate and feels older than the situation.
**AVOID WHEN:** solo; late in a session with no recovery time; when content is suggested, which contaminates the image and risks manufactured memory; with active abuse memories, severe fragility or decompensation risk.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** clinical method framed on "hot cognitions change more readily than cold"; no isolated trial. Budget ~5/25/20 min.

### [ST:ANGER-GRIEF] Imagery dialogue: anger at the parent, entitlement, then grief
**Move:** In the image, name what the parent did and the unmet need, then speak anger and rights directly to them. Teach which universal childhood entitlement the schema violated — respect for Defectiveness, affection and protection for Emotional Deprivation, expression of needs for Subjugation. Then make room for grief: the loss, the parent who will not change, the childhood that cannot be redone. Claimed mechanism: re-externalization returns the criticism to the parent and turns the schema ego-dystonic.
**APPLY WHEN:** the cognitive case is built and the report is "I know it, I don't feel it."
**AVOID WHEN:** fragility or dissociation-proneness; a traumatic target not yet processed; grief attempted first in place of anger; escalation beyond what can be come down from; or a violence history. Anger to real parents is separately discouraged without a cost-benefit review.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** clinical experience; the claim that "I've already forgiven them" is usually inaccurate rests on impression.

### [ST:REPARENT-IMG] Reparent inside the image, then hand the role over
**Move:** Ask the adult for permission to speak to the child part; if refused, ask what the child fears rather than overriding — the refusal is a Detached Protector operation and forcing it repeats the original violation. Once granted, enter the image, ask the child what she feels and wants, and provide it within an appropriate parental register. Later bring the person's own adult self into the image to do it, copying what was modeled; the endpoint is self-reparenting. Unsent letters consolidate the work and are read aloud in session.
**APPLY WHEN:** Disconnection and Rejection schemas with detachment; after anger and grief work, for the letter.
**AVOID WHEN:** the scene is an unprocessed trauma memory; the handoff comes first with no model to copy, since the internal adult arrives critical; or the letter is sent without a repercussion review.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** clinical experience; reparenting is method-level canon and "limited" by design — bounded, not a substitute parent.

### [ST:TRAUMA-IMG] Trauma imagery: pace it, never suggest, work the theme
**Move:** Invert the usual pacing rule — no pushing. The person sets the pace, in short bracketed doses with an agreed stop signal, safe-place bookends, and weeks between related sessions; the practitioner stays near-silent early. Later passes slow the action, query every sensory channel, assemble fragments into a narrative, and have her do and say what she could not then, including fighting back with the practitioner in the image. Never state or imply what really happened; if she later says "I made it up", work the theme without adjudicating accuracy either way.
**APPLY WHEN:** three preconditions hold — sufficient stability, the trauma already discussed cognitively, advanced trauma training on the practitioner's side.
**AVOID WHEN:** solo; as flooding; with a history of violent behavior; when dissociation or decompensation is a live risk, flagged explicitly for BPD; or without prior rationale, since reliving without it retraumatizes.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** clinical method plus an ethical rule from the era's false-memory literature; method-level support only. ~15–20 min, 20 min recovery.

### [ST:DP-BYPASS] Bypass the Detached Protector before anything else
**Move:** Run the ladder — name the mode, map its cues, tell its origin story, credit its past protective function, list current costs and benefits, then as Healthy Adult negotiate and insist it step aside. Give it a face in imagery and interview it ("what are you afraid will happen if these feelings come out"), letting it keep something concrete in exchange. After bypass, imagery of the vulnerable child reaches the feeling fastest.
**APPLY WHEN:** affect is flat, answers compliant, no real material reachable; therapy stuck because avoidance cannot be penetrated.
**AVOID WHEN:** no bond yet; no safer coping available for what emerges; or it opens traumatic material without containment. Cognitive work past the educational minimum is counterproductive — rationality is what the mode already does.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** clinical experience; the refuge-as-prison framing is theory, not measurement.

### [ST:PP-FIGHT] Fight the Punitive Parent as the parent, never as the self
**Move:** Identify whose voice the mode carries and rename it after that person; in imagery the punitive voice is always embodied by the parent, never by the patient. Enter the image and speak against it aggressively, then transfer the role over time. Two-chair between Healthy Adult and Punitive Parent, practitioner coaching from outside rather than occupying a chair. Stance is limit-setting, not debate: this mode has neither goodwill nor empathy, so reasoning fails. Pre-agree replacement actions for punishment urges and monitor for post-session flips.
**APPLY WHEN:** self-attack is chronic and ego-syntonic; in BPD, yelling at oneself, cutting, starving.
**AVOID WHEN:** she pictures herself as the punitive figure — correct that first; the fighting is too passive, or continued so long she never learns to do it; or risk is high and substitution replaces a safety assessment.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** clinical experience; granting the mode no redeeming function is a model commitment. Commonly takes a year or more.

### [ST:EMPATHIC-CONFRONT] Empathic confrontation as a stance
**Move:** Hold both poles in one breath — validate why the belief and the coping made sense, then name the present cost and the healthier alternative — shifting rather than settling. Fixed order when a schema fires in a relationship: let the person state their truth fully; ask what they thought, felt, wanted to do, what triggered it, who else evokes the same; empathize and apologize where warranted; only then reality-test the distorted part, often disclosing its actual effect. Typical yield: a kernel of truth plus schema distortion.
**APPLY WHEN:** Socratic questioning leaves the schema intact; over-reaction or hostile-defensive behavior inside the relationship.
**AVOID WHEN:** confrontation precedes feeling understood — reversing the order reads as attack; only empathy or only confrontation, both predicting no change; the other person is unsafe or you hold less power; or outside a therapy frame, where it is just criticism.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** clinical stance offered as the ST alternative to collaborative empiricism, never as a tested comparison.

### [ST:REPARENT-BY-SCHEMA] Match reparenting to the schema, not the behavior
**Move:** Pick the posture from the 18-item list: transitional stability for Abandonment; honesty and slowed trauma work for Mistrust/Abuse; nurturance plus permission to have needs for Emotional Deprivation; acceptance and admitted imperfection for Defectiveness; boundary-setting for Enmeshment; structure for Insufficient Self-Control; non-directiveness for Subjugation; blocked caretaking for Self-Sacrifice; forgiveness for Punitiveness. The same act — chronic lateness — gets a different response depending on the schema producing it.
**APPLY WHEN:** the core schema is identified and the relationship is stable enough to be the corrective.
**AVOID WHEN:** applied by the wrong schema — what heals one perpetuates another (guidance for Emotional Deprivation vs for Dependence); or attempted by friends or self-help. Hard boundaries: no meetings outside the office, no touch, no dual relationships, no cultivated dependence.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** from Bowlby/Ainsworth secure-base research and the corrective-emotional-experience tradition; no per-strategy trial.

### [ST:ANGER-4STEP] Ventilate, empathize, reality-test, rehearse — in that order
**Move:** For non-abusive anger aimed at you: full ventilation in a flat neutral tone, then schema-level empathy naming the underlying need and shifting toward the vulnerable mode, then reality-testing that concedes and apologizes for any true part before confronting the exaggeration, then rehearsal of an assertive version. Abusiveness is stopped with a stated consequence plus the twin message that the anger is wanted and the delivery is not.
**APPLY WHEN:** anger at you or a close other, not abusive; passivity-to-aggression swings.
**AVOID WHEN:** empathy arrives during step 1, which kills the venting; she leaves believing the anger was wholly justified; rehearsal starts before venting; or the responder defends, counterattacks or withdraws.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** clinical protocol; low dropout when venting is allowed within limits is a clinical claim.

### [ST:SUICIDE-MODE] Triage suicidality by mode, then escalate on a fixed ladder
**Move:** Identify which mode generates the urge — punishment (Punitive Parent), ending unbearable loneliness (Abandoned Child), piercing numbness (Detached Protector), revenge (Angry Child) — and answer that mode's logic. Escalate in order: more frequent contact with brief daily check-ins; risk assessed high/medium/low each time; if high, involve a significant other or arrange hospitalization; cotherapist and psychiatric consultation; adjuncts; voluntary admission, involuntary if imminent. The standing agreement is not a no-suicide contract but that she may voice suicidal thoughts freely, on the single condition of reaching the therapist before any attempt.
**APPLY WHEN:** any suicidal or parasuicidal crisis inside an established treatment.
**AVOID WHEN:** used instead of safety planning or acute risk management; as a solo arrangement with no clinician on the other end; or with the family of origin as supervising contact when that family formed the schemas.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** clinical protocol, no outcome trial; the no-suicide contract is explicitly rejected for this population.

### [ST:HELPER-AUDIT] Audit the helper's own schemas against the failure list
**Move:** Self-check per person: do I genuinely care; which of my schemas is this triggering; could I tolerate their panic, rage or grief; can I provide the reparenting they need. Match against the documented failure modes — schema-clash loops; mismatch between their need and your schemas; overidentification and collusion; withdrawal under intense affect, especially dangerous with BPD where it triggers Abandonment and escalates suicidality; overcompensation as retaliation; scolding from a Dysfunctional Parent mode; meeting your own needs through them; blame when progress stalls; envy.
**APPLY WHEN:** early in any helping relationship, and whenever your reactions feel disproportionate.
**AVOID WHEN:** substituted for actual supervision in a deteriorating case. Self-Sacrifice is named the most common schema among therapists; an emotionally inhibited helper may harm BPD-range people.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** clinical taxonomy, observational; the prevalence claim about therapists is self-report.

### [ST:PURE-VS-FRAGILE] Separate pure from fragile entitlement before mode work
**Move:** Ask whether there is any history of feeling unloved, ignored or devalued underneath the demandingness. Pure entitlement — spoiled, too few limits, no reciprocity learned — has an impulsive undisciplined child at the core and no underlying schema, so limits and reciprocity are the whole treatment. Fragile entitlement (the authors' term for narcissism) compensates for Emotional Deprivation and Defectiveness in a Lonely Child, where the schemas are the target and limits are secondary.
**APPLY WHEN:** demanding, superior behavior is the complaint; choosing between limit-setting and vulnerability-oriented mode work.
**AVOID WHEN:** used as a one-question screen — the two look alike from outside and most cases are mixed; vulnerability-oriented work has nothing to reach in genuinely pure cases.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** clinical experience; no trial of the differential.

### [ST:UNDER-COMPENSATION] Get beneath the compensation: vertical arrow, unsoothed hour
**Move:** Run the vertical arrow on a status attribute — "if I were not brilliant/rich/successful, what would that mean, what would happen, what would my life be like?" — descending until an image of a loveless life appears, and write down what surfaces. Pair with scheduled time alone with no stimulation and no soothing activity, recording what arrives.
**APPLY WHEN:** self-worth is fused to achievement or appearance; solitude is reflexively filled with games, screens, food, work or risk-taking.
**AVOID WHEN:** done alone during a collapse of the compensation — it lands straight on deprivation and defectiveness material with nothing holding it; substance dependence, suicidality or acute crisis; or the emptiness has no one to bring it to.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** vertical arrow attributed to Burns (1980); the application is clinical judgment.

## What the book debunks

- **"Non-compliance means laziness."** Avoidance here is learned negative reinforcement; a refused questionnaire, forgotten homework or blank imagery is schema data, and forcing it backfires. Persistent inability to image is a severity signal, not defiance.
- **"Vanquish the schema."** Named an unrealizable ideal — most schemas never fully heal. Resistance is self-preservation: giving one up means giving up a stable account of who one is.
- **"Insight is change."** Necessary but not sufficient, and the more severe the pathology the less it carries. In narcissism the authors flag *belle indifférence* — clear self-knowledge with no distress — so awareness must not be read as progress. "I can say the healthy side but don't believe it" is the expected intermediate state.
- **"Fix the flaw and the feeling goes."** Falsified in the book's own material: Defectiveness persisted after the weight loss it was blamed on.
- **"Coping behavior identifies the schema."** Opposite behaviors serve the same schema. Parallel critique of DSM-IV Axis II: many criteria list coping responses, less stable than the schemas driving them, so the apparent diagnosis shifts under you.
- **"Overcompensation looks like health."** Socially rewarded, common among high performers; its failure mode is decompensation into depression when a big enough setback breaks it. "Flight to health" gets confronted, not celebrated.
- **"Calm detachment is progress."** A punctual, compliant, homework-doing "good patient" may be in Detached Protector throughout; the test is whether needs and feelings are experienced and expressed, not whether behavior is appropriate. Likewise a subjugated person reads as a good patient, and an Approval-Seeker's polished false self is the presentation most often mistaken for health.
- **"BPD means visible rage."** Contradicted: typical outpatients live mostly in Detached Protector, and Angry Child is the least frequent mode. Pejorative framings ("manipulative") make the holder into the Punitive Parent. Authors' reframe: needy, not greedy.
- **"Cognitive techniques are a safe opening move."** A documented failure mode with BPD when mistimed — rejected as mechanical, felt as abandonment, or absorbed into detachment. Also counterproductive for Emotional Inhibition and the Detached Protector, since it reinforces the over-reliance on rationality that is the problem.
- **"Argue the pessimist out of it."** Turns sessions into debate and the relationship adversarial; where complaining is deprivation-driven, counter-evidence does not land ("help-rejecting complainers", Frank et al. 1952).
- **"Try harder to please a devaluing partner."** Documented to accelerate devaluation; standing up for oneself raises one's value in the devaluer's eyes.
- **"Recovered memories are historical fact."** Images carry emotional truth, which is not a claim about what happened; suggesting content is prohibited outright.
- **The helper is a documented failure source.** Ten enumerated therapist-schema failure scenarios; treating trauma is itself potentially traumatizing (Pearlman & MacIan, 1995); and the helping relationship reproduces the schema — permanent dependence for Abandonment, new fusion for Enmeshment, rewarded compliance for Subjugation, rewarded false self for Approval-Seeking, mirrored self-denial for Self-Sacrifice, mirrored perfectionism for Unrelenting Standards. Any self-help substitute should assume the same trap.

## Numbers that matter

- 18 schemas in 5 domains; 3 coping styles; 10 modes in 4 families (4 Child, 3 Coping, 2 Parent, 1 Healthy Adult); 5 core needs; 4 origin types; 7 temperament dimensions.
- Standard CBT for depression: >60% success immediately post-treatment, ~30% relapse within a year; course ~20 sessions. Straightforward schema assessment may need as few as 5 sessions.
- YSQ-L2 = 205 items over the 16 schemas identified at construction; short form 75 items; 6-point Likert. Alphas .83 (Enmeshment) to .96 (Defectiveness); test–retest .50–.82 nonclinical. Patient sample: 15 factors, 54% of variance. Social Undesirability failed to emerge (Schmidt 1995; Lee 1999) and was merged into Defectiveness. Carine (1997): schema profile classified Axis II group membership correctly 83% of the time. Strongest PD-symptom links: Insufficient Self-Control, Defectiveness.
- Young Parenting Inventory = 72 items, items 1–5 reverse-scored, given ~session 5–6. Young–Rygh Avoidance = 41 items. Young Compensation = 48 items. *Reinventing Your Life* = 11 lifetrap chapters; recommended dose is the 5 introductory chapters plus 1–2 schema chapters.
- Flash card = 4 sections; Schema Diary = 9 fields; 6 cognitive techniques in fixed order; 7 steps of mode work; 18 schema-specific reparenting strategies; 4-step anger sequence.
- BPD: patients typically carry nearly all 18 schemas; 5 working modes, 3 treatment stages, 4 predisposing family factors. Vulnerable Child runs at ~age 4+ in healthier patients, under 3 in BPD. Minimum effective treatment at least 2 years; self-destructive behavior expected to lose significant frequency within ~6 months; individuation and intimacy 2–3 years. Dutch pilot: ~year one against the Detached Protector, much of year two against the Punitive Parent — an author observation, not a measured endpoint. Three-fourths of diagnosed BPD patients female (Gunderson et al. 1991).
- Limit escalation: 1st violation = state the limit, 2nd = firm disapproval plus the promised consequence, 3rd = defined break or transfer, 4th = possible termination with referral.
- NPD: 3 primary modes plus Healthy Adult; 9 DSM-IV criteria all describing the Self-Aggrandizer only; 4 childhood origin factors.
- Authors' three most common schemas in practice: Emotional Deprivation, Subjugation, Defectiveness. Longest treatments: Mistrust/Abuse from early trauma, and Abandonment. Emotional Inhibition plus Unrelenting Standards together cover nearly all OCPD criteria.

## Blind spots of this book

- **No outcome evidence for its own protocol.** A practitioner manual, not a trial report: every technique is a structured clinical hypothesis backed by ISST canon and the authors' experience. Zhang's meta-analytic estimates have CIs crossing zero for both group and individual ST, and no RCT tests this book. The one directly trialed ST title is the companion self-help book (Yasar 2024, n=110) — as a standalone course, not as the paced adjunct described here.
- **The biological chapter is self-declared speculation.** The authors state research has not established whether the LeDoux two-track model applies to schemas — yet it justifies why cognitive methods underperform and why healing is regulation rather than erasure.
- **Untested core constructs.** The five core needs are footnoted as theory-derived. The conditional/unconditional split, four origin types and seven temperament dimensions have no validating instrument, and the 10 modes are conceded to be provisional.
- **Case vignettes stand in for data.** The strongest claims — that "I've already forgiven them" is usually inaccurate, that "I can't visualize" is almost always avoidance, that Subjugation has a high success rate — rest on impression and can be misused as pressure.
- **The comparison chapter is advocacy.** Written by the model's own authors; its verdicts that rival models lack detailed change strategies are assessments, not independent review.
- **Built for a dyad, silent on going it alone.** Nearly every high-leverage technique — imagery, mode dialogue, reparenting, trauma work, empathic confrontation — presupposes a trained person in the room. There is no protocol for the self-help residue and no criteria for when solo work becomes unsafe; the boundary must be derived from scattered cautions.
- **Culture, class, gender and material constraint absent.** No framework for schemas that remain adaptive in a hostile *present* environment; "the world no longer matches the reading" is assumed rather than checked.
- **A 2003 snapshot.** Written before the trial base it anticipated, before DSM-5, and inside the recovered-memory controversy — which sharpens its trauma cautions but dates its framing.
