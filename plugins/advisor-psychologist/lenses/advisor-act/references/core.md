# advisor-act — core principles, ranked by evidence

How to read this file: the ranking is by what is actually shown, not by how central the idea is to the model. ACT's evidence lives almost entirely at package level — whole protocols against waitlist or treatment-as-usual. Below that level, almost everything is craft: the six processes are a teaching architecture, not six separately validated mechanisms, and the specific tools this lens ships (choice point, the anchor drill, the control audit, the barriers audit, the acceptance kit) have no isolated trials at all. Two indirectness layers apply to every number here: findings come from clinical populations, and from delivery by a live clinician — no study has tested a clinician-free format.

---

## Tier 1 — MODERATE: the package, and the boundary conditions on it

### Bona fide psychotherapy versus waitlist, depression: d ≈ −0.62 to −0.92
This is the band any competent method occupies, ACT included. It is what "it works" means here — not a claim about ACT specifically. Response rates put the honest expectation on the table: roughly 41% respond versus 17% in controls, so most people do not respond within about two months, and the lens should say so before proposing anything [ACT:GOAL-REFRAME].

### Between-method differences in depression ≈ zero — but ACT is NOT inside that finding
The equivalence result (Barth 2013, 198 RCTs) is MODERATE for depression only, and ACT was absent from that network. So the lens may not claim "as good as CBT" on those grounds, and may not extend interchangeability to PTSD-, GAD- or ritual-shaped targets, where real differences exist. Where the target is compulsion-like or a traumatic memory, protocol-specific treatment wins on evidence and the ACT lens should hand over [ACT:SESSION-PREP].

### Within-session distress reduction does not predict behavioral outcome
The inhibitory-learning literature (Craske et al. 2008, 2014) is the strongest external support ACT has for a genuinely distinctive claim: dropping the habituation criterion. It licenses [ACT:EXPO-FORMAL]'s swap of distress ratings for flexibility ratings — and it is also the one place the digest's own phrasing outruns its citation, stating a flat non-correlation without giving a magnitude.

### Thought and mood suppression rebound
Suppression research (Wenzlaff & Wegner 2000) supports the control-agenda argument behind [ACT:STRUGGLE-DROP] and [ACT:CH-DOTS]: pushing content out raises its frequency and intensity. Note the scope — this supports "control as a strategy backfires", not "acceptance therefore works".

### Structure, not school, drives who stays
Dropout runs about 19.7% overall, but 17.8% in time-limited versus 29.0% in open-ended work, and 18.3% manualized versus 28.3% not. For a self-run ACT programme this means the format decisions in [ACT:SMART-ACTION] — one domain, a defined step, a time frame — carry more weight than which of the six processes you pick.

### There is no fixed dose, and count is not the lever
Routine practice runs 4–26 sessions with a naturalistic mean below five, the improvement curve is logarithmic, and the share of people who improve does not grow with the number of sessions. ACT's own dosing claim ("three sessions to years") is consistent with this, but it means the lens must never promise a timeline. Steer by whether the behavior in the episode changed, not by how many rounds have been done.

## Tier 2 — LOW: single trials, small samples, or the wrong book

### The six processes are a teaching architecture, not six validated mechanisms
No component-level trial separates defusion from acceptance from values work inside ACT; the hexaflex and its three-unit compression are how the model is taught, and the pathological mirror-processes are descriptions rather than measured entities. The broader dismantling literature has itself been demoted — the honest summary is that parts of packages tend to perform about as well as the whole, which cuts against any claim that a specific ACT process is doing the work. Practical consequence: when this lens says "defusion is what you need here", that is a routing hypothesis from [ACT:CHOICEPOINT], not a mechanism finding.

### Brief ACT in severe presentations: Bach & Hayes 2002, rehospitalization roughly halved after 3–4 hours
The most-cited ACT number and the weakest kind of evidence for a self-help lens: one small trial in a clinical inpatient population, nothing like the context this council operates in. Quote it as history, never as a dose promise.

### Higher experiential avoidance tracks worse symptomatology (Hayes, Masuda et al. 2004)
Correlational and cross-sectional. It supports treating avoidance as a worthwhile target [ACT:CHOICEPOINT], not the claim that reducing avoidance causes recovery.

### Self-help ACT in book form has trials — but not this book
The book-format randomized evidence in the ACT family belongs to *Get Out of Your Mind and Into Your Life* (Jeffcoat & Hayes 2012, N≈236; Muto & Hayes 2011, N=70). *ACT Made Simple* is a practitioner manual with no outcome study of its own, so every tool this lens ships inherits method-level support at best.

### Self-compassion effects are passive-control-inflated (g≈0.51)
Applies directly to [ACT:SELF-COMP]: better than nothing, not shown superior to active treatment. The correction is part of the calibration layer, and the lens must not sell compassion work as a stronger component than defusion or acceptance.

### Homework presence d=0.48 — demoted, and no completion benchmark exists
Practice between sessions is the mechanism ACT stakes everything on, and its evidence is LOW: quality beats quantity (g=1.07 versus 0.51), compliance correlations sit around r=.22–.27, and the commonly quoted completion percentages have been traced to the wrong studies or to nothing at all. Practical consequence for [ACT:HARD]: review the quality of one micro-practice and treat a miss as a design fault, never as a compliance figure.

## Tier 3 — VERY LOW: the author's heuristics, useful and unmeasured

Everything in this tier should be delivered as "a rule of thumb that helps people follow through", never as a finding.

- **The 7-out-of-10 realism gate** for shrinking a step until you will actually do it — credited to Strosahl, no supporting study [ACT:SMART-ACTION].
- **Reinforce at least five times as often as you punish** in relationships — asserted without citation [ACT:REL-INFLUENCE].
- **The 0–10 struggle scale**, with 5 as gritted-teeth tolerance [ACT:STRUGGLE-DROP], and the 0–10 life-workability rating [ACT:WORKABILITY]: teaching devices that quietly invite number-chasing.
- **The tool-count heuristics** — about three techniques per process as a starting kit, more than a hundred defusion techniques in circulation, roughly a year of practice to run the model fluidly — which concede how much judgment the model requires.
- **Roughly one person in ten barely visualizes**, which is why [ACT:LEAVES] needs a non-visual variant and why failed imagery is not resistance.
- **The named tools themselves** — choice point, the ACE anchor, the control audit, the barriers audit, the thirteen-module acceptance kit — are structured clinical experience with no direct trials.

## Tier 4 — extrapolation: what happens when this is applied alone

This tier is not weak evidence, it is absent evidence, and it governs how the lens speaks.

- No study has tested a clinician-free delivery of any of this. Procedures transfer; effect sizes do not.
- Unguided self-help is weaker than every guided format and not clearly better than usual care, though IPD data do not show it to be harmful on average (deterioration 5.8% versus 9.1%). Guidance — even non-expert — closes most of the gap.
- Negative effects have been reported across a wide and poorly bounded range of self-help interventions, and a meaningful minority of self-help books contain advice that is actively unhelpful. Absence of harm reports in the ACT literature is not evidence of safety: adverse events were actively collected in only 3 of 22 relevant trials.
- The manual assumes a therapist titrating in real time — checking permission, catching invalidation, repairing after a rupture. Self-application removes that layer exactly where the book itself locates its highest risks, which is why [ACT:EXPO-FORMAL], [ACT:INNER-CHILD], [ACT:NUMBWORK] and [ACT:GRIEF-BOUND] are therapist-tier operations rather than advanced techniques.

## Operating principles — load-bearing, low evidence, stated as commitments

These are the lens's decision rules. They are not findings; they are how this method reasons, and they are why it disagrees with other lenses.

1. **Workability replaces truth as the criterion.** A thought is not evaluated for accuracy but for what acting on it produces over time [ACT:WORKABILITY]. This is the single most consequential difference from cognitive restructuring — and its own weakness, since almost any behavior can be argued into or out of "workable" after the fact.
2. **Function over form.** Two identical-looking behaviors get different verdicts depending on what they are in service of; two opposite behaviors can serve the same avoidance [ACT:CHOICEPOINT]. Judging by form is the error the whole model is built to catch.
3. **Relief as the goal is the failure detector.** Every skill here degrades into a control technique the moment feeling better becomes the aim. "It isn't working" almost always decodes to "I don't feel better", and the response is the control audit rather than another technique [ACT:CH-DOTS].
4. **Acceptance is graded and paired with change.** Acknowledge, allow, accommodate — and always alongside changing what can be changed. It is not resignation, and the model will support leaving a bad situation [ACT:CHALLENGE-3].
5. **Sequencing is free, which is a cost for solo users.** Any process, any order, switch when stuck — this demands the clinical judgment a book cannot supply. The practical fallback ordering is: ground first, then unhook, then attention, then compassion, then acceptance, then the noticing self, with values and action running throughout.
6. **Change happens between sessions.** Insight is not the deliverable; a repeated small action is. Any output that ends without one concrete step within 24 hours has not finished [ACT:SMART-ACTION].
7. **Willingness is not wanting.** The question is never whether you feel ready but whether you will carry what comes with it, given that it matters [ACT:HARD]. This is where the method most easily curdles into obligation — see the bias note in role-bias.md.
8. **No delete button.** The model does not remove thoughts, feelings or memories; it adds response options [ACT:FPT-SHIFT]. Any promise of removal from this lens is a misuse of it.
