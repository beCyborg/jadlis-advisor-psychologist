# ACT Made Simple — digest

## What this book is for the lens
The canonical how-to of ACT: psychological flexibility (mindful, values-guided action) built through six interdependent, nonlinear processes, judged by one metric — workability ("does acting on this build the life you want long-term?"), never truth or symptom relief. It beats other sources on delivery craft: exact wording, sequencing, failure modes, and repair scripts for every technique. Its unique trap-detector: any skill silently degrades into an emotional-control technique the moment relief becomes the goal — the book teaches how to catch and reverse exactly the error self-help users make most. Written for practitioners; the lens translates therapist craft into self-application and flags where translation must stop.

## Core model
- **Target = psychological flexibility**: six processes (hexaflex: present-moment contact, defusion, acceptance, self-as-context, values, committed action), compressible to the **triflex** — be present / open up / do what matters (ch. 1). Six mirror pathological processes: fusion, experiential avoidance, inflexible attention (3 Ds), remoteness from values, unworkable action, fusion with self-concept (ch. 2).
- **Getting hooked** = fusion (cognitions dominating action/awareness) + experiential avoidance (struggling to escape private experience). Fusion — not avoidance — is the umbrella clinical problem (ch. 11). Thoughts and feelings are never the problem per se; the rigid response context makes them toxic (ch. 2, 28).
- **Choice point** (Harris, Ciarrochi & Bailey, 2013): the running map — difficult situation/thoughts/feelings at the bottom; hooked → "away moves," unhooked → "towards moves." Optional but organizes the whole book (ch. 1, 6).
- **Workability** = functional analysis in lay clothes: judge behavior by function in context, not form or morality (ch. 4). The mind is a problem-solving machine that fails when turned inward on feelings (ch. 2).
- **Sequencing is free**: any process, any order, switch when stuck (ch. 7). Routing: overwhelmed/dissociated → drop anchor; stuck/paralyzed → open up; ready → values + committed action; fixated on feeling good → creative hopelessness (ch. 8). Change happens between sessions — practice is mandatory (guitar analogy, ch. 5).
- **Navigation anchors**: worksheets Dissecting the Problem, Bull's Eye, Join the DOTS, Struggling vs. Opening Up, Engaging Savoring and Focusing, Checklist of Common Values (~38 items), What's Stopping You (HARD); free "Extra Bits" companion eBook holds all scripts.

## Moves
Deduplicated across all six parts; ordered by tier, then evidence strength. Baseline evidence unless noted: author's clinical practice on ACT's method-level trial base; no direct RCT of this book.

### [ACT:WORKABILITY] Workability probe
**Move:** Refuse every true/false debate. Trace: what shows up → what it hooks → "if a film crew recorded you hooked, what would the footage show?" → does that behavior serve the life you want long-term? For "but it's true!": "if you let this thought dictate your actions, does it help you be who you want to be?" Validate the behavior's real payoffs FIRST, then invite the long-run appraisal so the person chooses. Orientation variant: rate life 0–10 on "living like the person I want to be" — 3–4 → "what would make it a 5?"; 8–9 → the work is wrapping up.
**APPLY WHEN:** any repeated stuck behavior; painful self-judgments held as true; choosing what to work on; lost and needing direction.
**AVOID WHEN:** you've privately pre-decided the answer (Strosahl: that's "bullying"; produces empty compliance); smuggling in good/bad judgments; dodging genuinely useful information inside the thought.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Core ACT stance underlying the trial base; Strosahl 2004 on the non-coercive frame.

### [ACT:IHTT] "I'm having the thought that…"
**Move:** Take the fused judgment as "I am X," sit with it ~10 s, then replay as "I'm having the thought that X," then "I notice I'm having the thought that X." Establish as a standing convention; reuse as a seconds-long micro-intervention. Variants for feelings, urges, memories.
**APPLY WHEN:** first formal defusion technique — works for almost everyone; any moment a judgment hooks.
**AVOID WHEN:** no separation appears — retry with a different thought, don't force; used to make the thought go away.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Adapted from Hayes, Strosahl & Wilson 1999 founder protocol; method-level trials.

### [ACT:STRUGGLE-DROP] Drop the struggle (paper, quicksand, tug-of-war, switch)
**Move:** Reframe the problem as struggle, not the feelings. (a) Pushing Away Paper: write avoided content on a sheet, push it away at arm's length — exhausting, distracting, hands unusable for living; drop it in the lap: content still present, energy and action return. (b) Verbal: Struggling in Quicksand; Tug of War with a Monster (drop the rope — monster remains, hands free). (c) Struggle Switch: ON turns pain into anxiety-about-anxiety amplification; rate struggle 0–10 (5 = gritted-teeth tolerance), practice lowering it. (d) Control-illusion mini-experiments: delete a memory on command; don't think of ice cream; feel zero anxiety under polygraph-or-electrocution; fall in love for a billion dollars — little control over inner life, high control over actions. Teach the control split: uncontrollable — most thoughts, emotions, memories, other people, outcomes; controllable — responses, focus, what you say and do.
**APPLY WHEN:** transitioning from creative hopelessness to acceptance; intellectual agreement without conviction; relapse into control strategies.
**AVOID WHEN:** neck/shoulder/pain issues for the physical version; person throws the paper away — name it as the same avoidance; the struggle scale drifting into a covert distress gauge; tolerance sold as acceptance.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Quicksand and Tug of War from Hayes et al. 1999; Struggle Switch Harris 2007; Pushing Away Paper author's signature; suppression-rebound research (Wenzlaff & Wegner 2000) backs the claim.

### [ACT:LEAVES] Leaves on a Stream (meditative defusion)
**Move:** Visualize a stream; place each thought — positive AND negative, meta-thoughts included — on a leaf, let it "come and stay and go in its own good time"; getting hooked and restarting is the expected loop. Debrief: what hooked you; did you try to wash thoughts away; how is this the opposite of rumination? Non-visual alternative — Watch Your Thinking: observe thoughts' location, form, speed, gaps, as a curious child. Rationale first, gym analogy: brief techniques are warm-up weights, this is the heavy lifting.
**APPLY WHEN:** rumination, worry, obsessing; daily/near-daily practice once the rationale is clear.
**AVOID WHEN:** poor visualizers (~1 in 10 — auditory variant); mid-crisis (train offline, deploy as unhook-ground-refocus); speeding the stream to expel thoughts — avoidance; refusing to put pleasant thoughts on leaves.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Classic founder-repertoire exercise; method-level trials.

### [ACT:NOTICING-SELF] The noticing self (self-as-context)
**Move:** Contact the vantage point that notices everything and never changes. Ladder: (1) seed lines in any exercise — "notice you're noticing"; (2) Stage Show metaphor — life as an ever-changing show, a part of you watches unchanged; 2–3 daily check-ins; (3) Continuous You (~15 min): cycle over breath/thoughts/body/feelings/roles — "there's X, and there's you noticing X; if you can notice X you can't BE X; X changes, the noticer doesn't"; pre-warn the mind will debate — let it; close with Sky and Weather (self as sky, inner events as weather — no storm harms the sky); (4) Good Self/Bad Self — harshest judgments on one side of a sheet, kindest on the other: either side at the face blocks life equally; on the lap, engagement returns. Explicit work is optional — only a transcendent/stable self strictly requires it.
**APPLY WHEN:** fused self-story ("I'm broken / an addict / superior"); chaotic life needing a stable vantage; self-esteem requests.
**AVOID WHEN:** metaphors without exercises (they almost never produce the experience alone); analyzing Continuous You afterwards (kills the impact); long memory-revisiting versions with trauma histories — therapist; guru talk on "then who am I?" — give the everyday answer (body, mind, noticing self) and stop.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Observer exercise Hayes et al. 1999, author's adaptations; Sky and Weather from contemplative traditions.

### [ACT:CH-DOTS] Creative hopelessness — Join the DOTS
**Move:** Make the emotional-control agenda's unworkability experientially undeniable — hopelessness in the AGENDA, never in self/life/future. Five questions: (1) what have you tried against unwanted inner states — Distraction, Opting out, Thinking strategies (positive thinking, debating, self-criticism), Substances & other (incl. therapy, self-help, overwork); (2) how did each work — validate short-term relief, establish the long-term return ("year to year: better or worse?"); (3) what has it cost; (4) what shows up now — normalize ("you're not stupid; everyone does this"); (5) open to something radically new? For "but it works!": validate the benefit, then Rickety Bicycle — an old bike gets you from New York to Mexico, but in what condition? — and build a better vehicle to the same payoff. Revisit over time; it gets faster.
**APPLY WHEN:** fixation on feeling good; strong control agenda; pseudo-defusion or pseudo-acceptance detected ("it's not working" = "I don't feel better").
**AVOID WHEN:** already willing (skip CH entirely); judgmental or lecturing tone (backfires); workable strategies pathologized — aspirin and a glass of wine stay ("we're not mindfulness fascists"); self-attack fuses in — defuse; overwhelm — ground first.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** CH phase inside tested protocols (Zettle 2007 depression ~20 min; Strosahl 2005 brief); DOTS frame author's; no isolated trial.

### [ACT:GOAL-REFRAME] Behavioral goals, not emotional ones
**Move:** Convert every goal into "what I will DO": emotional goal ("stop feeling anxious") → "learn skills to handle these feelings" (learning a skill IS a behavioral goal); dead person's goals (anything a corpse does better — no panic, no drinking; Lindsley 1968) → "what will you do INSTEAD when the urge shows up?"; outcome goal → "do things that raise the odds"; insight goal → "once you understand, what will you do differently?" Probes: magic wand — feelings still exist but are "no longer a problem" (deliberately NOT "gone") — what would you do?; seven-day documentary — what would a film crew record after success?
**APPLY WHEN:** any goal phrased as feel/get/understand/stop; addiction-flavored "I just want to quit"; start of any change project.
**AVOID WHEN:** wording the magic wand as feelings disappearing (re-feeds avoidance); the person wants ONLY to feel good and rejects all reframes — route to creative hopelessness, don't argue.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Dead person's goals Lindsley 1968; missing behavioral goals = the top error Harris sees in supervision.

### [ACT:NOTICE-X] Notice X — flexible attention
**Move:** The model's most flexible instruction: "notice X with openness and curiosity" — X = thoughts (→ defusion), emotions (→ acceptance), five senses (→ engagement), own behavior (→ workability). Train three skill classes against three costs: engaging (vs. cutting off), savoring (vs. missing out), focusing (vs. doing poorly) — via mindful ordinary activities and drills like 5 minutes noticing your own hand as a curious child (elicit the "boring" prediction, test it, extrapolate to attending to loved ones). Dial focus: rumination → NARROW onto a valued activity; chronic pain → acknowledge, then BROADEN to the whole field. Name feelings continuously — emotion vocabulary is a learnable skill.
**APPLY WHEN:** flat going-through-motions life; disconnection; groundwork before acceptance work; any process, any moment.
**AVOID WHEN:** sold as relaxation or thought-clearing (false expectations discredit the skill); savoring assigned to non-pleasurable activities; long eyes-closed interoceptive work with trauma-prone/dissociating people — start on the OUTER world, eyes open, short.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Universal root instruction of all mindfulness-based approaches; method-level.

### [ACT:CHOICEPOINT] Choice point map + function spotting
**Move:** Two diverging arrows: towards moves (acting like the person you want to be) vs. away moves; triggering situations/thoughts/feelings at the bottom. Extend to functional analysis: PAYOFFS of away moves at the top (all reduce to escaping the unwanted or accessing the wanted) — answers "why do I keep doing this?" and selects the intervention: hooked by thoughts → defusion; feelings → acceptance; situations → problem solving/skills. Make the link explicit before offering any skill: "when hooked, what do you start/stop doing — what would the footage show?" Without the fusion→behavior link, nobody wants unhooking skills.
**APPLY WHEN:** mapping any problem or emotion; explaining the approach; repetitive behavior you don't understand; choosing where to intervene.
**AVOID WHEN:** the person labels a destructive behavior "towards" — record without debate, revisit later (the map is their worldview, not a verdict); moralizing; eliciting costs before validating payoffs (reads as judgment).
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Choice point co-created 2013; function spotting is standard behavior analysis; no direct trial of the tool.

### [ACT:ACE-ANCHOR] Dropping anchor (ACE)
**Move:** Cycle until grounded: **A**cknowledge inner experience and name it ("here's sadness"; "storm" if unnameable); **C**ome back into the body — feet into floor, straighten spine, press fingertips, stretch; **E**ngage — 5 things seen, 3–4 heard, back to the task. Frame: anchors don't control the weather, they hold you steady. Micro ~10 s for everyday drift; 10–15 min for severe overwhelm. Debrief ONLY on hookedness, presence, control over action — never "do you feel better?" Non-negotiable difference from generic grounding: keep re-acknowledging the pain — omit that and it silently becomes distraction.
**APPLY WHEN:** overwhelm, dissociation, flashbacks, panic, urges, extreme fusion; first skill to learn; practiced in fine weather (queues, small annoyances) or it won't be available in storms.
**AVOID WHEN:** used to stop crying, escape feelings, or relieve someone else's discomfort (named misuses); the acknowledge step dropped (covert avoidance); "it relaxes me" becomes the point — re-clarify.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Author's flagship; first skill in the WHO refugee-camp ACT protocol (Epping-Jordan et al. 2016); no direct trial.

### [ACT:HANDS] Hands as Thoughts and Feelings (+ index card)
**Move:** Everything that matters imagined out in the room; hands = thoughts/feelings; over the eyes = hooked (missing out, can't focus, can't act); lowered to the lap = unhooked — thoughts still present, usable if useful, world back (~4 min). Portable variant: harsh thoughts verbatim on an index card ("I'm…"), used in the metaphor, then carried in a wallet ~1 month, read 3–4×/day — a pocket metaphor for defusion AND acceptance. Explicitly separates real problems from thoughts ABOUT them.
**APPLY WHEN:** first experiential intro of fusion/defusion; relapse into the thought-control agenda; pre-empting "but I have real problems!"
**AVOID WHEN:** framed as getting rid of thoughts (relief is a by-product); substituting for actual practice; card refused — that's fusion + avoidance in action: don't push.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Evolved from Harris 2009a; defusion backed at method level.

### [ACT:3N] Defusion smorgasbord — Notice, Name, Neutralize
**Move:** All 100+ defusion techniques reduce to: **Notice** ("notice what your mind is telling you") → **Name** ("worrying"; "aha, here it is again"; "there goes the judgment factory") → **Neutralize** by recontextualizing: workability question, examine its form (voice, speed, location), sing it to Happy Birthday, see it as text on a screen, thank the mind, a nonchalant "nice one!" Flagship: Naming the Story — bundle recurring content into a titled story ("the not-good-enough story"); check the title acknowledges rather than trivializes; then "aha, there's the story" on each appearance; late catches → drop anchor, re-engage. Normalize first via the caveman spiel: the mind is a don't-get-harmed machine doing its evolved job.
**APPLY WHEN:** improvising any defusion intervention; recurring self-narratives (works even on true, heavy content — believability need not change).
**AVOID WHEN:** playful techniques near grief, fresh trauma, terminal illness, or thin rapport — high invalidation risk; "story" language if the person bristles; treated as a fixed ladder; used to expel thoughts — pseudo-defusion, route to CH-DOTS.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Singing and Thank Your Mind from Hayes et al. 1999; Naming the Story Harris 2007; 3N frame author's.

### [ACT:VALUES-CLARIFY] Values clarification battery
**Move:** Teach the split first: values = desired global qualities of ongoing action, available in any moment; goals = future attainables (getting married vs. being loving; Two Kids Driving to Disneyland — same arrival, one rich journey). Elicit from several angles: ten-years-later look-back ("too much time worrying about…; not enough doing…"); funeral-video scenario — what would you LOVE a loved one to say about who you were; one-year-ahead best self; goals-to-values ("suppose you got it — what would it let you demonstrate?"); fallback — Checklist of Common Values (~38 items). Authenticity: the proof of the pudding is in the eating — live them, track vitality and sense of being yourself. Daily bridge: pick 1–2 values each morning, sprinkle into what you do (flavor), notice the difference (savor).
**APPLY WHEN:** values info missing; hard decision or live crisis; approval-seeking or inherited shoulds; goal-chasers with chronic frustration.
**AVOID WHEN:** treating first answers as final; should/must/duty language with heaviness, compliance, resentment — that's a fused rule, not a value: defuse, restore choice; dutiful "living my values" with zero fulfillment = presence deficit — treat with attention skills, not more values work.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Values definition Hayes, Bond, Barnes-Holmes & Austin 2006; married/loving example Hayes et al. 1999; Two Kids Harris 2007.

### [ACT:EMO-ALLY] Emotions as allies — mine the pain for values
**Move:** Psychoeducation: emotions prepare action; action TENDENCY means inclination, not compulsion. Painful emotions communicate (signal to others), motivate (fear→protect, anger→stand ground, sadness→rest, guilt→repair), illuminate (spotlight what matters). Once accepted, mine: what does it remind you to care for; what advice does it give, and does that lead towards or away; what to face or do differently — then translate into towards moves. Deep version for guilt/grief around a relationship: ground, name, open around it, then "what does this pain tell you about who matters? — if you didn't care, there'd be no guilt"; land on chosen qualities, check willingness to feel what acting on them costs.
**APPLY WHEN:** emotions treated as malfunctions; low emotional literacy; intense guilt or grief tied to a relationship; self-condemnation over dark thoughts.
**AVOID WHEN:** attempted mid-struggle — the information is unreachable until the struggle drops; while flooded — anchor first; expressing vulnerable feelings to predictably hostile audiences.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** "Your pain is your ally" lineage Hayes et al. 1999; consensus emotion-science points only.

### [ACT:ACCEPTKIT] Acceptance of emotions — modular kit
**Move:** 13 mix-and-match techniques, each with a ~10-second version: link to values first; observe like a curious child; note the noticing part; let the mind chatter in the background; map the sensation's geometry; name it ("I'm noticing a feeling of X" — counters "I AM the emotion"); breathe into it; expand around it; allow it; physicalize as an object (shape, temperature, texture); normalize (pain = the gap between want and got); compassionate hand on the spot; expand awareness — stage-lights up on everything else while the feeling stays on stage. Acceptance is graded: acknowledge → allow → accommodate. Homework: recordings, a 2–3-word cue (observe–breathe–expand), or a week of noticing struggling vs. opening up.
**APPLY WHEN:** struggle with an emotion blocks valued action; anxiety and grief work.
**AVOID WHEN:** full-length early with a highly avoidant person (use 1–2 modules); breath-focus with those it makes dizzy — pull it; shrinking/dissolving the object (feeds control agenda); celebrating when feelings shrink — reduction is a bonus; framed as resignation (ACT pairs making room WITH changing the changeable).
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Method-level trials; the 13-module chain and "three As" grading are the author's.

### [ACT:SELF-COMP] Self-compassion — six building blocks
**Move:** Six words: acknowledge pain and respond with kindness. Entry test — Two Friends: the dismisser ("stop whining") vs. the supporter ("this is rough, I've got your back"); everyone picks the second; "which friend are you being to yourself?" Six stackable blocks, any subset, any order: acknowledge the wound; be human (normalize); disarm the critic (defuse); hold yourself kindly; make room for the pain; see yourself in others. Physical: Kind Hand — fill a hand with remembered kindness, place it where the pain (or numbness) sits, hold the feeling like a crying baby (5 s–5 min; spirit counts, not duration). Micro: "this is a moment of suffering — go easy on yourself." Against "self-criticism is my motivation": the donkey — stick and carrots both move it, but years of stick yield a battered donkey; humans have better than carrots: values.
**APPLY WHEN:** harsh self-attack; post-lapse shame; after failures and hard days.
**AVOID WHEN:** the word repels (teach the skill before naming it); self-touch triggers disgust or abuse memories (hands in lap, imagine warmth); entrenched self-hatred, where kindness itself elicits fear — expect fusion ("I don't deserve it"), treat with anchor + defusion, professional support at the severe end.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Expanded from Neff's 2003 triad; block structure and exercises the author's.

### [ACT:ACTSPLIT] Control the action, not the feeling
**Move:** Make room for the emotion while controlling voice, face, posture, words — act calmly while furious, courageously while scared. Experiential beats didactic: with the emotion present, mindfully move — stretch, walk, drink water — to directly experience that action stays controllable (lectures on this fail). Energizing emotions (performance anxiety, anger): defuse, ground, channel the arousal into the performance. Urges to drink, self-harm, withdraw: urge surfing — let the urge rise, crest, and fall like a wave, noticed without being obeyed.
**APPLY WHEN:** "the feeling must go before I can act"; anger management; pre-performance nerves; emotion-driven urges.
**AVOID WHEN:** framed as pretending — the move is honestly acknowledging the feeling AND acting like the person you want to be; acute suicidal or self-harm crisis handled solo — needs professional risk management.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Author's practice (extended in The Confidence Gap, 2011); urge surfing an established relapse-prevention technique.

### [ACT:SMART-ACTION] Values-based goal setting and tiny steps
**Move:** Informal goal setting (small towards moves, skills practice) suffices most of the time; reserve formal setting for hard aims with repeated failure: ONE domain → 1–3 values → a goal Specific / Motivated by values / Adaptive / Realistic / Time-framed (Harris's SMART redefinition). Always end with the smallest step within 24 hours. Realism gate: rate follow-through 0–10; below 7 → shrink until ≥7 or change the goal (sometimes the real goal = acquiring the missing resource). Pre-plan obstacles and a plan B on the same values. Impossible/blocked goals: (1) validate the pain of the reality gap — pain signals something matters; (2) acceptance + self-compassion; (3) dig out the values UNDER the goal; (4) set reachable goals from those values.
**APPLY WHEN:** any commitment; repeated non-follow-through; goals unachievable or years-blocked ("it's not the same" protests included).
**AVOID WHEN:** forcing formal SMART on the unwilling; fantasizing about the achieved goal (reduces follow-through); substitute goals before validating the loss; goals about other people's behavior — convert to your own influence behavior.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** ≥7/10 realism rule credited to Kirk Strosahl; reality-gap protocol from author's case material.

### [ACT:HARD] Barriers audit — why the plan didn't happen
**Move:** Normalize non-follow-through, then screen four barriers with antidotes: **H**ooked — the mind's reasons/catastrophes → unhook; **A**voiding discomfort — what you're unwilling to feel → expansion + pre-commitment ("what discomfort is coming, am I willing?"); **R**emote from values → reconnect with why it matters; **D**oubtful goals — realism 0–10 → shrink to ≥7. Motivation has two faces: workability (link the old behavior's validated payoffs to its costs) and willingness ("given it matters, willing to make room for all that?" — wanting not required). Commitment patterns: pattern 1 = commit → break → give up; pattern 2 = commit → break → self-compassion → learn → recommit; if beating yourself up changed behavior, you'd be perfect already.
**APPLY WHEN:** any broken commitment or stalled plan; fear of failure; post-lapse shame; on yourself as self-manager.
**AVOID WHEN:** the basics were never done (no behavioral goal, no obstacle plan — fix first); "I won't be able to handle it" argued with instead of believed — train the skills first; grinding a stuck area when nothing helps — acknowledge honestly, shift energy to areas where values ARE livable.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Author's tool (replaces 1st-ed. FEAR/DARE); resistance frame builds on Strosahl 2004.

### [ACT:CHALLENGE-3] The challenge formula
**Move:** Any challenging situation offers exactly three options: (1) leave; (2) stay and live your values — change what can be changed AND make room for the pain that remains; (3) stay and give up effective action — the default gravity. If leaving is available, weigh it seriously; otherwise the path is option 2 — even in a refugee camp, dozens of daily value-consistent choices remain. For "I have no choice / I'm powerless": validate the suffering, then two choices — keep doing the same (100% guaranteed to keep life as is) or run a no-guarantee experiment that at least offers a chance.
**APPLY WHEN:** stuckness and felt choicelessness; chronic hard contexts (bad job, conflictual relationship, illness); endless complaining about a situation neither fixed nor left.
**AVOID WHEN:** implying option 1 always exists (prison, camps, dependency); selling option 2 as pain removal; abuse — leaving safely needs professional support; "no choice" about suicidal urges without risk management in place.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Author's heuristic; central pillar of the WHO refugee-camp protocol.

### [ACT:BLOCKED] When the work itself gets blocked — doubt, "nothing matters," "I don't know"
**Move:** For hopelessness and reason-giving: notice and name the doubts; validate ("mind trying to protect you"); declare no guarantees (distrust anyone promising results); write objections down; refuse to convince ("the harder I argue, the louder the thoughts"); offer three choices — give up / debate the mind (it wins anyway) / let it talk and carry on — option 3 IS defusion. For values blocks: the louder the "nothing matters," the more likely it's avoidance of facing the values–behavior gap; press pause, name the dynamic without blame, lay out the honest choice (drop the work: escape discomfort, life stays as is / continue as an experiment: something new becomes possible) and let the choice be real. Also: "and yet you're here — what matters enough that you came?"; 1–2 min of silent reflection; split wanting from willingness ("are you WILLING, given it matters?"). Preface for high fusion: dance in the dark first — listen, validate authentically for a few minutes before any technique.
**APPLY WHEN:** "this won't work for me"; "I don't know / nothing matters / what's the point" during values work; doubts obstructing any change project.
**AVOID WHEN:** no behavioral goals exist yet (set them first); validation stretching to the whole session (reinforces fusion); coercion of any kind; "I don't know" that is a genuine fact or help request — respond by function, not content.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Author's playbook ("dance in the dark" coined in Harris 2018); no-guarantees spiel cites 1,000+ published ACT papers.

### [ACT:FPT-SHIFT] Flexible perspective taking and flexible thinking
**Move:** ACT does change thinking — by adding flexible repertoires, never by disputing or deleting (no delete button in the brain). Shifts: role reversal; loved-one lens ("what would you say to someone you love here?"); future self looking back; "if this emotion could speak, what would it advise?" Mind-as-character: harsh coach or kind coach talking — and what would the kind one say (kind coaches get better results)? Preparation battery for worriers: worst case + coping + cutting its odds; plan B; best case + raising its odds; most likely case + what next — always ending in a concrete plan.
**APPLY WHEN:** rigid single-viewpoint thinking; judgment loops about others; harsh self-talk; worriers who want to "prepare."
**AVOID WHEN:** sliding into rumination instead of a plan; delivered as a lecture; targeting traumatic material (inner-child territory — therapist); promising insight will stop the thoughts recurring (it won't).
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** FPT grounded in the model's RFT base; questions from author's practice.

### [ACT:REL-INFLUENCE] Relationships — influence, not control
**Move:** Nobody controls another person; you control your own words and actions. Audit what you've tried (fighting, criticizing, demanding, withdrawing, silent treatment), sort by workability: short-term compliance vs. long-term ill will. Build relationship-healthy influence: clear requests, boundaries, negotiation; reinforce liked behaviors at least 5× as often as punishing disliked ones. Diagnostic split: what you want to GIVE (→ values) vs. GET (→ wants). Values fuel — Connect and Reflect: vividly recall the relationship at its best, name the qualities YOU were bringing; those are your partner values. Rainbow-vs-roadblock check: appreciating the person or seeing an obstacle? — run the judgments through workability.
**APPLY WHEN:** any relationship goal; "they must change first"; chronic criticizing; influence dominated by sticks.
**AVOID WHEN:** sounding like the problems are your fault (pre-empt explicitly); mistaken for manipulation (carrots must be genuinely healthy); no accessible positive memories yet — build safety first; run without validating the pain (reads as blame); abuse — see ACT:CHALLENGE-3.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Behavioral shaping; ≥5:1 ratio author-asserted (uncited); expanded in ACT with Love (2009); Connect and Reflect Harris 2018.

### [ACT:SHAME-NAME] Working with shame (self-help portion)
**Move:** Shame = fusion with "I AM bad" (guilt = "I DID something bad"). Map its aways and towards (choice point with shame at the bottom), then work the three context elements — fusion, avoidance, unworkable action — in any order. Defusion, gently: "here's shame"; "I'm having the thought that I'm bad"; later, greet it as an outdated protector ("I know you're trying to help; I've got my values now"). Function frame: shame once paid off (reduced hostility, elicited kindness, made events make sense) and now blocks who you want to be — that contrast motivates new skills. Run the six self-compassion blocks; once unhooking works, mine it for values.
**APPLY WHEN:** self-judgment, self-blame, rejection fears; the same protocol generalizes to guilt, anger, envy, loneliness.
**AVOID WHEN:** zany defusion early (invalidates shame-laden people); the payoff review requires revisiting trauma memories, or shame roots in childhood abuse — that's ACT:INNER-CHILD, therapist territory; used to suppress the feeling rather than hold it.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Defusion and functional analysis at method level; shame-specific adaptation author's.

### [ACT:EXPO-FORMAL] Formal exposure, ACT-style
**Move:** Exposure redefined: organized contact with repertoire-NARROWING stimuli (not just fear — shame, disgust, urges, pain all narrow behavior) to INCREASE response flexibility, never to reduce anxiety (drops are a by-product). Set up via choice point: stimuli at the bottom, current aways, desired towards; frame as learning a new response so the stimulus stops running your life. Replace SUDS with 0–10 flexibility scales: acceptance vs. avoidance, fusion vs. defusion, presence, control over actions, connection with values. Aligned with inhibitory learning theory (Craske et al. 2008, 2014): within-session distress reduction does not predict behavioral outcomes.
**APPLY WHEN:** planned exposure programs — trauma memories, obsessions, panic sensations, social situations; combining ACT with EMDR/PE (legitimate only with the scale swap).
**AVOID WHEN:** rationale framed as "getting rid of anxiety" (re-installs avoidance); scales chased as the new number; attempted solo — formal exposure and any exposure to trauma memories or major loss needs a therapist. Informal everyday exposure via towards moves remains SELF territory.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** ACT reframe consistent with the cited inhibitory-learning literature; the flexibility-scales protocol is the author's proposal, untrialed.

### [ACT:INNER-CHILD] Inner-child imagery and shame's learning history
**Move:** Explore the history that built the shame — shame-fueling messages from caregivers or abusers; psychoeducation that an abused child's mind blames itself to keep the needed attachment figure safe; relabel "I am bad" as old programming. Then guided imagery: today's adult self travels back to comfort the child self — kindness, the truth that the adults were at fault, ending by holding the child or carrying them to safety.
**APPLY WHEN:** shame tied to explicit memories of childhood trauma, abuse, or neglect — with a therapist who can contain what opens.
**AVOID WHEN:** solo or outside a therapy relationship — the exercise deliberately opens traumatic material; note the book's relief valve: past functions are optional, present functions are what matter.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** Author's clinical practice (script in Extra Bits); no trial cited.

### [ACT:NUMBWORK] Deliberate work with numbness and dissociation
**Move:** Treat numbness as a feeling: locate the area of greatest numbness, notice, name, describe, make room — buried feelings typically rise; be ready to segue to anchoring and defusion. "I feel nothing / empty / dead inside" is itself an unpleasant felt state tied to high experiential avoidance.
**APPLY WHEN:** persistent emotional numbness or dissociation, worked with a therapist.
**AVOID WHEN:** solo — deliberately surfacing hidden material can open trauma content; the book itself offloads dissociation details beyond its core text. The SELF-safe portion is ACT:ACE-ANCHOR and external-world attention training.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** Author's clinical practice; no trial cited.

## What the book debunks
- **Mindfulness ≠ meditation, relaxation, positive thinking, distraction, or thought-removal.** The most common misunderstanding is expecting anxiety relief; ACT favors brief informal skills over formal sitting (compliance is the point). The word itself carries so much baggage Harris recommends skill-specific language instead.
- **Every skill degrades into a control technique if relief becomes the goal.** "It's not working" = "I don't feel better" reliably exposes a hidden avoidance agenda — answer with creative hopelessness, not more technique. Distress reduction is always a bonus, never the aim (pseudo-defusion, pseudo-acceptance, anchoring-as-distraction are named failure modes).
- **Thought suppression rebounds** in frequency and intensity; mood suppression intensifies the mood — the control agenda is empirically self-defeating.
- **Defusion ≠ believability change and ≠ dismissal.** You can fuse with thoughts you don't believe and defuse from 100%-true ones; painful thoughts often carry real information — extract the wisdom.
- **"ACT doesn't change thinking" and "ACT ignores emotions" are both wrong.** It changes thinking by adding repertoires, never disputing; emotions are named, mined, and become allies. No delete button in the brain — insight will not stop a thought recurring.
- **Acceptance ≠ resignation, tolerating, liking, approving; and it is graded, not binary** (contra some ACT texts). ACT pairs making room for feelings with action to change situations, and will support leaving a bad one.
- **Avoidance is not inherently pathological** ("we're not mindfulness fascists") — targeted only when rigid, excessive, costly; aspirin and a glass of wine can be workable.
- **Old-school exposure's habituation criterion is outdated**: within-session SUDS drops do not predict behavioral outcomes (inhibitory learning research).
- **Self-esteem programs create the fusion problem in reverse** — a tightly gripped positive self-image blocks life exactly like a negative one.
- **Self-criticism is not a behavior-change technology**; "guilt motivates, shame demotivates" is a gross oversimplification — either can do both.
- **"Depression" is not an emotion** — the book treats it as a pattern of away moves triggered by thoughts and feelings.
- **Resistance is contextual, not client pathology**; distrust anyone guaranteeing results. Judging behavior by form instead of function is the central error the whole model targets.

## Numbers that matter
- **6** core processes (hexaflex) ↔ **3** triflex units; **6** fusion categories (past, future, self-concept, reasons, rules, judgments).
- **ACT trial base**: effectiveness across ~15 conditions, ~20 trials cited; Bach & Hayes 2002 — 3–4 one-hour sessions in chronic schizophrenia cut rehospitalization ~50%; 1,000+ published ACT papers (author's claim). Higher experiential avoidance tracks anxiety, depression, substance abuse, PTSD severity (Hayes, Masuda et al. 2004).
- **Dosing**: no fixed dose — 3–4 sessions to years; Harris contracts 6-session blocks; ~50% of supervision problems trace to poor setup; trauma histories need 2–3 slower rapport sessions before active work.
- **Skill durations**: dropping anchor 1–3 min (10–15 min severe; ~10 s micro); Leaves on a Stream 2–30 min; Continuous You ~15 min; Kind Hand 5 s–5 min; acceptance-kit modules ~10 s–10 min.
- **Thresholds and ratios**: homework realism ≥7/10 or shrink the task; reinforcement:punishment ≥5:1; struggle scale 0–10 (5 = tolerance); life-workability 0–10 (8–9 → wrap up, 3–4 → probe); tiniest step within 24 hours; index card ~1 month, read 3–4×/day.
- **Craft**: toolkit = 3 techniques × 6 processes = 18, then extend singly (Strosahl); 100+ defusion techniques; ~1 in 10 people can barely visualize; ≈1 year of practice to run ACT fluidly; skill-teaching order: anchor → simple defusion → meditative defusion → attention → savoring → self-compassion → acceptance → noticing self.

## Blind spots of this book
- **No outcome evidence of its own.** A practitioner manual: every specific tool (choice point, ACE, DOTS, HARD, the acceptance kit) rests on the author's clinical authority plus the ACT method-level base; the only direct book-form RCTs in the family belong to Get Out of Your Mind. Component-level effects are untested; the flat "no correlation between SUDS drop and behavior change" is stated without a cited effect size.
- **Author-brand ecosystem.** Harris cites his own products throughout (The Happiness Trap, The Confidence Gap, ACT with Love, apps, courses); memorable numbers (≥5:1 carrots, ≥7/10 realism) are asserted heuristics, not findings.
- **Therapist-in-the-room assumptions.** Nearly all craft presumes real-time titration — permission checks, catching invalidation and overwhelm, repair after ruptures. Self-application loses that safety layer precisely where the book locates the biggest risks (shame, trauma material, dissociation, exposure); its own REQUIRES-THERAPIST boundaries confirm the limit.
- **Weak on severity boundaries and differential indication.** Suicide risk management, psychiatric comorbidity, medication, and when ACT is the wrong fit get passing mention; mandated clients are explicitly out of scope. No guidance on choosing ACT versus other first-line treatments for a given diagnosis — the book assumes ACT was already chosen.
- **Nonlinearity taxes self-learners.** "Any process, any order" plus 100+ techniques demands exactly the clinical judgment a book cannot supply; Harris's own antidotes (read the whole book first, 18-tool kit, one-year fluency estimate) concede the curve.
- **Culture and context largely unexamined.** Aside from the WHO refugee-protocol adaptations, cultural fit of metaphors, values language, and the individual-choice framing goes mostly unaddressed.
