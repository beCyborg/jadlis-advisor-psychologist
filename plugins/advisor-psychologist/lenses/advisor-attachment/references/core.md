# advisor-attachment — core principles, ranked by evidence

The claim of this lens is narrow and testable: distress lives in the CURRENT interpersonal field, and solving one bounded current problem — a death, a dispute, a transition, an isolation — moves symptoms. Everything below is sorted by how much that claim is actually backed for the component in question, not by how central the component feels.

Rating scale used by the source manual (Ch. 10): **4★** = at least two RCTs superior to control; **3★** = one RCT, or equivalence to an established treatment; **2★** = open or pilot data; **1★** = untested; **0★** = negative findings. PG26 supplies the GRADE-style weights where the two systems overlap.

## Tier 1 — MODERATE: shown, with magnitudes

### The package beats no-treatment for depression (d = −0.62…−0.92 vs waitlist)
IPT belongs to the bona fide psychotherapies whose advantage over waitlist and usual care in depression is MODERATE evidence. PG26 rates IPT for depression MODERATE specifically. Response rates in that literature run 41% versus 17% — meaning most people do NOT respond inside roughly two months, which is the honest baseline any application of [ATT:FORMULATE] must state up front.

### Between-method differences are approximately zero — for depression only (d = 0.01…−0.30)
Barth 2013 (198 RCTs, N=15,118) found no reliable ranking among bona fide methods for depression; the one significant contrast disappeared in large trials, and Egger p<0.001 marks small-study inflation. Consequence for this lens: it may not claim superiority over cognitive, behavioural or compassion-focused work on a depressive target. It earns its place by FIT — a nameable current interpersonal problem — not by outperforming. PG26 forbids extending this interchangeability to ritual-shaped or trauma-memory targets, or to acceptance- and schema-based methods absent from that network.

### Continuation dosing for recurrent episodes (4★)
[ATT:MAINT-CONTRACT] is the best-evidenced single element here: Frank 1990/1991 and Reynolds 1999, with tested doses of weekly for six months and monthly for up to three years. It is also the element most clearly outside solo reach — it is a treatment-plan decision, REQUIRES-THERAPIST by operation.

### Adaptations that carry 4★
Perinatal-adjacent and adolescent populations, older adults, medically ill patients, the binge-purge eating pattern, and cross-cultural depression (the Uganda group trial, Bolton 2003: local prevalence ≈21%, sex-segregated groups of 8–10, two individual plus sixteen weekly 90-minute sessions, dropout 7.8%). Note what the Uganda trial did to this lens's own map: "interpersonal deficits" was dropped as culturally meaningless, which is a finding about the framework, not about Uganda.

### Structure, not school, is the retention lever
Time-limited formats drop out at 17.8% against 29.0%, manualized at 18.3% against 28.3%; overall weighted dropout is 19.7%, and the strongest early predictor is a missed session inside the first four. This supports [ATT:FORMULATE]'s bounded shape while explicitly NOT supporting its specific session numbers.

### The four focal areas are not equally supported
The manual insists on choosing exactly one focus, but the four are not peers:

- **Disputes** — the most common focus in depressed outpatients, and the one where the session engine ([ATT:COMM-ANALYSIS], [ATT:DISPUTE-STAGE]) was built. Best-fitting.
- **Role transitions** — well covered by the package trials, and the frame most often available when nothing else fits cleanly; also the recommended relabelling for isolation cases, because it is more palatable and works the same way.
- **Grief after a death** — carried by the package, but gated externally: PG26 admits grief protocols only above measured elevated prolonged-grief symptoms, and no modality wins (Hao 2024, 55 studies, N=10,330, overlapping intervals). Below that threshold, effects are near zero and the intervention is potentially harmful.
- **Isolation** — the residual category, chosen only when the other three do not apply. The manual concedes it has no controlled data of its own and that its suggestion to refer such people elsewhere is itself unsupported. Weakest, and precisely the population an attachment lens most often meets.

## Tier 2 — one trial or equivalence (3★ / LOW-to-MODERATE)

### Social rhythm regularization as an adjunct (3★)
[ATT:SRM] rests on Frank 2005 (n=175). Regularization during the acute phase predicted fewer recurrences, and the payoff appeared as longer stable stretches in maintenance rather than faster acute stabilization. Adjunct to mood-stabilizing medication throughout — never tested without it, so the rhythm grid is a self-manageable operation attached to a plan that is not.

### Perinatal and postpartum work (3★; miscarriage 2★)
Enough for a working hypothesis, not enough for a claim of specificity. Pregnancy is not a protected state: roughly 10% of pregnant women meet criteria for major depression, and it does not spontaneously remit.

### Loneliness targets (MODERATE for interventions, LOW for mechanism)
PG26's anchor is Hickin 2021 (31 RCTs, N=3,959). Note the asymmetry with the source manual: the interpersonal lens's own isolation category is its weakest, while the general loneliness-intervention literature is decent. That gap means [ATT:DEFICIT-REBUILD] borrows credibility it did not earn inside its own tradition.

## Tier 3 — preliminary (2★ / LOW)

- **Non-exposure trauma track** ([ATT:PTSD-SOCIAL-REBUILD]): one open trial plus one group RCT against usual care. Direction only.
- **Anxiety presentations**: 2★. Interchangeability of methods explicitly does NOT extend here per PG26 — real differences exist for anxiety-spectrum and trauma targets.
- **Conjoint and telephone formats**: 2★ each.
- **Simplified brief counselling variants**: never tested head-to-head against the full protocol, so "a shorter version works too" is unmeasured, not established.

## Tier 4 — untested or negative (1★ / 0★)

- **Emotion-dysregulation and self-harm-risk presentations**: 1★, an unfinished open trial. [ATT:DYAD-FEEDBACK] and [ATT:ANGER-GRADED] both live here; the source manual itself says other approaches have stronger evidence for this population.
- **Prepubertal children**: 1★.
- **Chronic low mood as monotherapy: 0★.** Medication is first-line; combination rates only 2★. Browne 2002 (n=707): response 47% for IPT against 60% for sertraline and 57.5% for combination — with IPT lowering health and social-service costs, making combination most cost-effective, but on a dose-unbalanced design (12 sessions against 2+ years of medication). Markowitz 2005 (n=94): sertraline beat both IPT and brief supportive therapy. The one encouraging signal is mechanistic — change in the focal interpersonal problem tracked symptom improvement.
- **Substance problems: 0★.** Two negative RCTs (Rounsaville 1983, Carroll 1991). The authors say plainly this is not a treatment for that target.
- **Restriction-type eating patterns: 0★** (McIntosh 2005).

## The extrapolation layer — where this lens stops being evidence

### Every move-level claim is package-level
No dismantling trial of this method exists. Each "Evidence: method-level" line in moves.md means the whole protocol worked in trials, not that the isolated technique did. PG26's own removal-dismantling estimate was demoted to LOW as circular, so the honest phrasing everywhere is "parts as good as the whole" (Bell 2013) rather than "this component carries the effect".

### Two indirectness layers cap the entire file
Clinical trial populations to subclinical everyday use; and live clinician to a text interface. No study has tested a clinician-free format. Procedures transfer; effect sizes do not. Every SELF-tier move in this lens is therefore an operation the manual describes for a clinician to run and the council runs unaccompanied — reasonable, unmeasured.

### The time limit itself is untested
The manual presents the fixed endpoint as therapeutic in its own right. It was never compared against open-ended delivery of the same method, and PG26 rejects fixed session counts in favour of trajectory-steered length. Keep the boundedness, drop the specific number.

### The lens has no attachment measurement at all
Despite its position in the council, the canonical manual never measures attachment — no dimensions, no instruments, no styles. It avoids categorical typing by omission rather than by design. Anything this lens says about attachment PATTERNS is a framing borrowed from elsewhere; what it can actually deliver is a procedure for one current relational problem. See myths-and-demotions.md before letting this lens speak in typology language.

### Where the mechanism claim is honest
The method claims a CONNECTION between mood and interpersonal events, never a cause. [ATT:MOODLINK] is a linking operation, not an explanation. The one place directional evidence exists is the chronic-low-mood trials, where movement in the focal interpersonal problem correlated with symptom change — correlation, in a treatment the same trials found inferior to medication.

## Timing and course-correction numbers this lens inherits

- Acute course as trialed: 12–16 weekly sessions of about 50 minutes; assessment ≤3 meetings; the literature spans 6 weekly to 36 monthly sessions.
- Severity bands: ≤8 remission, 9–12 mild, 13–19 moderate, ≥20 moderate-to-severe, ≥30 clearly severe; re-measure every 3–4 weeks. Response is not remission — a drop from 30 to 13 is a response sitting near relapse threshold.
- Under 50% symptom reduction by ~12 weeks means switch or augment ([ATT:NONRESPONSE-REFRAME]). PG26 tightens the front end: review at 2–4 weeks, zero-response checkpoint at 4–6 weeks, and run the checklist (circumstances, comorbidity, attendance, adherence) BEFORE changing school.
- Roughly two thirds respond to a first course of medication; about 50% remit within 4–6 weeks of adequate treatment; 30–40% of people with one episode never have another (Judd 1998).
- Base rates: point prevalence 3–4% of adults; children of depressed parents carry 2–3× the risk (6–9% against ~3%) and most never become depressed; a parent's remission measurably improves the children's symptoms (Weissman, Pilowsky 2006).
- Deterioration is not rare: 5–10% of people in psychotherapy leave worse, adverse events run 5–20%, and clinicians catch roughly 3 of 40 deteriorations. This is why [ATT:SCALE-TRACK] runs even though its own payoff is small.
