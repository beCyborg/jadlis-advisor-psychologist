# advisor-attachment — moves

Citation prefix `[ATT:]`. Source: Weissman, Markowitz & Klerman, *Clinician's Quick Guide to Interpersonal Psychotherapy* (2007), calibrated against PG26. This lens reads distress as a problem in the CURRENT interpersonal field: one of grief after a death, a dispute over unmatched expectations, a role transition, or isolation. Tier is set per OPERATION (safety-protocol §3), not per school; several moves below sit on the therapist side of that line even though the book presents them as routine practice, because the book assumes a live clinician and the council does not.
## A. Framing and assessment

### [ATT:MOODLINK] Mood–event linking
**Move:** Open each review of a week with two questions in either direction — how the week went, and what happened around any mood that is reported; or how it felt, if an event is reported instead. Anchor everything to one concrete, affect-laden incident from the last seven days, and ask for a specific example whenever the account turns abstract. A daily mood-and-event diary carries the same link between reviews.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any recurring review of a period; a mid-week mood swing with no obvious cause; theory-talk, jargon or generalities replacing lived material.
**AVOID WHEN:** The link is treated as proof of causation — the method claims connection between mood and interpersonal events, not cause; or when the review drifts into unstructured talk, since the link itself is the intervention.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Method-level — the engine of every IPT session across its RCTs; no dismantling trial isolates it.

### [ATT:SCALE-TRACK] Serial symptom scale
**Move:** Choose one depression scale (Beck Depression Inventory as a self-report; Ham-D where a clinician rates), take a baseline, repeat every 3–4 weeks, and state aloud what the number means — the bands run ≤8 remission, 9–12 mild, 13–19 moderate, ≥20 moderate-to-severe, ≥30 clearly severe. Itemizing symptoms reframes them as an illness state rather than personal failings.
**APPLY WHEN:** The start of any structured effort against low mood, and at fixed intervals thereafter; deciding whether a course is moving at all.
**AVOID WHEN:** The scale is read as a diagnosis (it measures severity only); score-checking becomes a reassurance ritual; or a suicide item is endorsed — that ends self-tracking and starts [ATT:SUICIDE-INQUIRY].
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Standard practice across IPT trials (method-level). PG26 bounds the payoff: routine outcome monitoring runs d≈0.13–0.27, and the off-track-alert subgroup claim falls toward zero with an independent scale — measure because it is cheap and targets the tail, not because it is powerful.

### [ATT:SICKROLE] The sick role and the two-illness frame
**Move:** State that a depressive state is a treatable condition rather than a failure of will: what it blocks is excused the way influenza excuses missed work, while the person takes on the active duty of working at recovery. Where a physical illness is also present, hold them as two independent treatable conditions — both need care, neither is the person's fault, and neither explains the other away.
**APPLY WHEN:** Self-blame over reduced functioning; hopelessness read as an accurate forecast; low mood dismissed as the understandable consequence of a physical diagnosis.
**AVOID WHEN:** It slides into passivity — the role explicitly includes working as a patient; guilt is delusional in quality; or onset is late-life or somatically suspicious, where a medical workup comes first.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Core IPT component; medically ill populations rated 4 stars (HIV adaptation, Markowitz 1998, where treating depression relieved fatigue and insomnia both parties had attributed to the illness). Method-level.

### [ATT:INVENTORY] Onset timeline plus interpersonal inventory
**Move:** Lay symptom onset against interpersonal events — what was changing (a death, a fight, a move, a job, an illness), who was involved, who supports, who is estranged, what strengths remain. Then run the inventory (Handout 2.3) person by person: quality of contact, what each side expects and whether it is met, what satisfies and what does not, what change is wanted, who counts as a confidant, what repeats. A graphic variant places the self at the centre of a closeness circle with markers set at felt distance.
**APPLY WHEN:** Before choosing any focus; at the start of reviewing an episode; when the stated complaint looks like the visible tip of something larger.
**AVOID WHEN:** Done in a rush — a shallow inventory produces a wrong focus and mid-course surprises; or when the absence of an obvious precipitant is treated as failure, since consequences of the low mood (strained ties, a lost job) qualify as a focus in their own right.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Standard IPT intake; independent raters agree reliably on the focus it yields (Markowitz et al., 2000). Method-level.

### [ATT:FORMULATE] One focus, one stated endpoint
**Move:** Compress the case into a single plain sentence linking the current state to exactly ONE focal area, get explicit agreement to it, and commit to a bounded stretch of work with a named endpoint rather than an open horizon. Tie each week's material back to the agreed focus, tolerate digressions briefly, return, and change focus only by explicit renegotiation.
**APPLY WHEN:** Closing an assessment (the initial phase runs ≤3 meetings); starting any bounded change effort where deadline pressure helps.
**AVOID WHEN:** Two or more foci are chosen — dilution leaves no focus at all; a formulation is pushed over the person's own alternative; the label "interpersonal deficits" is said aloud (say isolation or loneliness); or the endpoint is defended against genuinely new material such as a fresh mid-course loss.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Formulation method of Markowitz & Swartz (1997, 2006); method-level. Two corrections: the fixed time limit was never tested against open-ended IPT, and PG26 rejects planning a fixed session count outright, steering by trajectory instead. What survives both is structure per se — time-limited formats drop out at 17.8% versus 29.0% for open-ended ones.

### [ATT:STATE-NOT-TRAIT] Illness state, not character
**Move:** Attribute the apparent character faults visible during an episode — passivity, irritability, avoidance, lateness, a sense of being defective — to the state itself, and postpone any verdict about "real personality" until mood has lifted. For years-long low mood fused with identity, treat the start of any change effort as a transition INTO health: separate what the condition is from who the person is, and practise handling situations the way a well version would.
**APPLY WHEN:** Self-condemnation during a low period; the pull to self-diagnose a character problem while depressed; "I have always been like this"; success feeling fraudulent and closeness avoided out of fear of exposure.
**AVOID WHEN:** Stable patterns clearly visible outside episodes are waved away; real external problems are reclassified as "just the illness"; or the reframe is sold as a stand-alone cure for chronic low mood — IPT alone scored 0 stars there, medication is first-line and combination rates only 2.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Personality measures did not predict short-term IPT outcome (Zuckerman 1980); personality-disordered patients improved comparably (Markowitz 1998); apparent traits receded over maintenance (Cyranowski 2004); social-anxiety work rests on an open trial (Lipsitz 1999) and one group RCT showing parity with CBT (Hoffart 2005).
## B. Disputes over unmatched expectations

### [ATT:DISPUTE-STAGE] Stage the dispute, map the expectations
**Move:** Define the dispute as nonreciprocal expectations with a significant other, then let its STAGE choose the strategy: renegotiation while the parties are still talking (work the communication), impasse where talk has stopped into silence or smouldering resignation (deliberately reopen the conflict), dissolution where the tie is unrecoverable (treat the ending as a transition). Map it with Handout 4.1 — what each side wants, how each has disappointed the other, what has been tried, what options remain for both, how likely change realistically is, what resources exist, how similar past disputes went.
**APPLY WHEN:** A dispute is the chosen focus; the situation is presented as impossible and immovable; nothing shifts and the reason is unclear.
**AVOID WHEN:** Reopening an impasse is expected to feel better immediately — open disagreement often increases first, by design; or fault is assigned wholly to one side before the map is drawn.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Method-level; disputes are the most frequent focus among depressed outpatients. Even a failed renegotiation yields communication skill and a more realistic location of blame — sometimes the obstacle is the other party's unwillingness to change.

### [ATT:COMM-ANALYSIS] Communication analysis
**Move:** Reconstruct one important conversation utterance by utterance — what was said, what was felt, what was intended at each step. Flag the disjunctions: anger felt and nothing said, sulking or going mute standing in for a stated grievance, assumptions that the other should simply know, readings of the other distorted by low mood, and words that do not match affect (a betrayal recounted flatly, an argument narrated with a smile). Let the person draw the conclusion before any alternative is offered.
**APPLY WHEN:** Fights that repeat; messages that land wrong; any disappointing exchange inside the chosen focus; a silence-and-sulk style substituting for voiced complaint.
**AVOID WHEN:** Validation is skipped — anger and sadness must first be normalized as legitimate signals; contradictions are used as gotchas; or one culture's communication norms are imposed as the standard.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Signature IPT technique, method-level, never dismantled.

### [ATT:ASSERT-VALID] Needs into words, channel chosen to fit
**Move:** Validate needs and anger as ordinary interpersonal signals, then convert the feeling into a deliverable sentence — unvoiced needs go unmet, others expect self-advocacy, and unexpressed annoyance guarantees the behaviour continues. Draft the actual words, tune the tone, and start where stakes are low (defining working hours) before moving to riskier ground. Choose a channel that the context accepts: a direct statement, an indirect signal, a trusted intermediary such as a relative or a doctor, or a reframing of the request around what the other party values.
**APPLY WHEN:** Everyone else's needs come first; anger is read as bad or selfish; conflict is answered with silence; self-erasing accommodation has produced helplessness; or a hierarchy or cultural setting makes direct confrontation read as disrespect.
**AVOID WHEN:** It overcorrects into constant self-assertion (which spoils relationships too); retaliation is a realistic risk and safety planning must come first; or indirection has become chronic avoidance of a dispute that could actually be resolved.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Method-level skills training. The channel tactics are field adaptations from the Uganda group trial (Bolton 2003), authored by local trainees and not separately tested.

### [ATT:TRANSGRESSION] Name the transgression
**Move:** When a significant other has broken a clear social code — an affair, deliberate cruelty, physical harm — name it as a transgression that entitles the injured party to redress, at minimum an apology. Then run the sequence: validate the feeling, scan the options for expressing it, rehearse the encounter with the right tone.
**APPLY WHEN:** A guilt-prone, passive person cannot mobilize legitimate anger inside a dispute they are clearly on the receiving end of.
**AVOID WHEN:** Danger is ongoing or the other party is abusive — confrontation escalates and needs professional safety planning; or naming feeds grievance rumination instead of one bounded action.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Authors' clinical heuristic only; support amounts to reported feelings of vindication in case material, no controlled data.

### [ATT:ROLEPLAY] Rehearse, attempt, debrief
**Move:** Rehearse the encounter aloud including tone; take the other party's part to hear how one comes across; iterate until the words match the intent. Rehearse contingencies separately — what to say to oneself if anxiety symptoms arrive mid-attempt, labelling them symptoms rather than defects. Then run it for real and debrief in four parts: acknowledge the risk taken, link the mood shift to how it went, reinforce whatever worked, and if it went badly, commiserate and look for the next option.
**APPLY WHEN:** Before assertions, confrontations, social or dating approaches, and any unfamiliar situation; after every real-life attempt, without exception.
**AVOID WHEN:** Rehearsal loops replace the actual conversation; a polished script is mistaken for control over the other person's response; or the debrief steals the credit — the out-of-session attempt is what builds mastery.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Used throughout IPT trials, method-level. The ban on role-play in the bulimia trials was a design artifact keeping IPT distinct from CBT; the authors state it should not be dropped in practice.

### [ATT:DECISION-ANALYSIS] Options, with the person in the lead
**Move:** Name the wanted outcome, generate the full list of options, and walk the consequences of each before acting — by questioning until the person names feasible alternatives themselves, so the competence is theirs. Teach it explicitly as a transferable skill.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any interpersonal problem with real choices; passivity defaulting into non-decision; a claim that everything has been tried and nothing works.
**AVOID WHEN:** The answers are supplied from outside — it flatters the helper and leaves the person feeling incompetent; or the analysis itself becomes a substitute for acting.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Generic technique inside the method-level package; authors' clinical guidance. The "tried everything" claim is usually inaccurate — attempts were half-hearted or options were overlooked through discouragement.
## C. Role transitions

### [ATT:TRANS-NAME] Name the transition
**Move:** State which role ended and which began. The label itself does work: a transition, unlike chaos or free fall, is a thing one adjusts to over a known arc.
**APPLY WHEN:** Low mood follows any life change, including a wanted one — a promotion, a move, retirement, a new baby, a diagnosis, a change in finances; or an apparent "dispute" is really a shift, such as a conflict with a boss inside a company that was itself changing.
**AVOID WHEN:** A death caused the loss (that is the grief focus) or a live struggle with one specific person dominates (that is a dispute) — choose by the key issue, change versus struggle, and where both fit, take the frame less threatening to the person.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Method-level.

### [ATT:TRANS-WORK] Relinquish the old role, build the new
**Move:** Work the five tasks: give up the old role; let the feelings about losing it be spoken, sadness and anger and fear included; acquire the skills the new role needs; build new attachments and supports; register what the new role actually offers. Correct the memory split that low mood produces — an idyllic before, a traumatic change, a dreadful now — with a balanced audit of what was both good and bad about the old role and both hard and promising about the new. Sequence confidence-building through the structured arena first (work, with its built-in job description) before the uncharted social one.
**APPLY WHEN:** Nostalgia framing an earlier period as uniformly fine; guilt about grieving a change others call positive; gratitude used to silence complaint; feeling incompetent in the new role; new rewards seeming worthless at first.
**AVOID WHEN:** The transition itself is traumatic — violence, a life-threatening diagnosis — and detailed revisiting floods the person, which is therapist territory; the balanced audit is aimed at a death loss, where it becomes a positive reframe and is prohibited; or the work drifts into vocational counselling instead of the feelings blocking adjustment.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Method-level within transition-focus IPT; the five-task map is the authors' framework, not separately trialed.
## D. Grief after a death

### [ATT:GRIEF-SCREEN] Screen before any grief work
**Move:** Screen with Handout 3.1 before deciding anything: timing of the death against symptom onset, ability to speak about the person who died, avoidance of the funeral or the grave, possessions kept untouched, fear of dying the same way, self-blame about the death, what support existed at the time. Telltales include never mentioning the deceased or the circumstances, worsening around anniversaries, mourning that was delayed or never arrived, somatic presentation, and taking on the deceased's illness.
**APPLY WHEN:** A significant death sits in the history alongside a current low state, and someone is about to propose grief work.
**AVOID WHEN:** Normal mourning is being pathologized — sadness, broken sleep and reduced functioning that resolve over some months are adaptive and must not be discouraged; the markers of complication are a severe phase past two months or an inability to function at work or family two to three months after the death.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Authors' clinical heuristics inside method-level grief-focus trials. PG26 makes measurement the mandatory entry gate: grief protocols only above elevated prolonged-grief symptoms, since for uncomplicated grief the effects are near zero and potentially harmful, and no brand wins (Hao 2024, 55 studies, N=10,330, five modalities with overlapping intervals).

### [ATT:GRIEF-REENGAGE] Staged reengagement after affect has moved
**Move:** Once mood has begun to shift, rebuild contact in graded steps — resume an activity that used to give pleasure, contact one existing friend, then test one new relationship — and debrief each attempt concretely: what was done, what was bearable, what was hard, whether it would be repeated.
**APPLY WHEN:** The later stretch of grief-focused work, after feeling has already moved rather than as a way of skipping past it.
**AVOID WHEN:** Guilt about improving is unaddressed ("recovering would mean I did not love them") or fear that a new relationship betrays the old one is unspoken — the fear is discussed before the attempt, never after; or when excessive guilt and thoughts of dying are present, which are not features of normal grief and route to §A of the safety protocol.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Method-level component of the grief focus; the staging is authors' clinical guidance with no isolated trial.

### [ATT:GRIEF-RECON] Facilitated mourning
**Move:** Reconstruct the relationship and the death in detail — how the news arrived, the circumstances, life before and during and after — deliberately inviting the disliked sides of the person alongside the loved ones, allowing catharsis to run without interruption, and staging an imagined conversation to voice what was never said. Photographs, meaningful places and conversations with others who knew the dead person serve as aids.
**APPLY WHEN:** Complicated bereavement has been confirmed by measurement and the work is being carried by a clinician.
**AVOID WHEN:** Attempted alone or with an AI layer — this operation opens loss material by design, and no solo format for it exists; also expect irritation at ambivalence probes when the deceased was the person's only secure attachment, and in cultures that forbid speaking ill of the dead, ask about times of disappointment instead.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** Grief-focus component of IPT trials (method-level); the prolonged-grief adaptation adding trauma-treatment elements was trialed by Shear, Frank, Houck & Reynolds (2005). PG26 classes exposure to loss as a therapist operation without exception.
## E. Isolation, affect, and daily rhythm

### [ATT:DEFICIT-REBUILD] Pattern review plus graduated contact
**Move:** Review past significant relationships, good and bad, and extract both the recurring strengths and the recurring breakdown patterns; work through Handouts 6.1–6.2 — counts of actual contact, what makes closeness hard, and the thought experiment of a room full of strangers with the question of what exactly one would do. Then graduate the contact: re-contact one old friend or one trusted person, enter low-stakes settings, rehearse heavily beforehand, and debrief every encounter step by step.
**APPLY WHEN:** Isolation is the focus — which the method permits only when no death, no dispute and no transition fits, since this is the residual category.
**AVOID WHEN:** A personality problem is read off an episode snapshot (apparent traits recede as mood lifts); the practice is framed as formal homework, since non-completion makes people feel like bad patients and predicts dropout — keep it an invitation; or the phrase "interpersonal deficits" is used with the person, because it insults.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** The book's own weakest category — no controlled data specific to this focus, and its suggestion to refer such patients to CBT or long-term treatment is flagged by the authors as unsupported. Often recastable as a role transition, which is more palatable and equally workable. PG26's loneliness anchor (Hickin 2021, 31 RCTs, N=3,959) is MODERATE for interventions overall and LOW for mechanism.

### [ATT:AFFECT-ELICIT] Elicit, normalize, then decide about expression
**Move:** Draw out the feelings attached to key events, normalize them explicitly, and stay with strong emotion rather than hurrying past it until it becomes tolerable and informative. Hold feelings as data about interpersonal events — powerful but only feelings, and steerable. Only then weigh whether, how and to whom to express them outside, and rehearse first. Practise expressing warmth and gratitude too, not only anger.
**APPLY WHEN:** A decision is stuck over an unacknowledged feeling layer; guilt attaches to having negative feelings at all; affect is habitually avoided around current relationships.
**AVOID WHEN:** It becomes raw venting into a fragile relationship — what is said in a review is not a template for outside use; the same angry or sad outburst repeats without change; or trauma-linked affect or dissociation appears, where grounding comes first and the work moves to a live session.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Core across IPT protocols, method-level. PG26 bounds the adjacent practice: unstructured venting during active rumination-driven distress is the contraindicated form, not expressive writing in general.
### [ATT:BINGE-CONTEXT] Interpersonal context of the urge
**Move:** When the urge or the symptom itself becomes the topic, redirect to what preceded it interpersonally — what was felt just before, what had just happened or was about to happen with someone. Normalize the feeling that surfaces, usually disappointment or anger, then look for the repeating feeling-to-context pattern rather than for the symptom's own logic.
**APPLY WHEN:** A repetitive habit or symptom obscures its own interpersonal triggers, or the person calls their own feelings weird or abnormal.
**AVOID WHEN:** Restriction rather than bingeing is the primary pattern — no benefit was found there (McIntosh 2005); medical risk from eating needs direct management, which is a crisis route under the safety protocol; or the person already over-identifies with being ill, where more illness psychoeducation is the wrong move.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** The binge-purge adaptation rates 4 stars across individual and group trials (Fairburn); IPT worked more slowly than CBT but caught up in two studies and was inferior in a third (Agras 2000).

### [ATT:SRM] Social rhythm regularity
**Move:** Keep a daily grid of the social anchors — waking time, first contact with another person, meals, bedtime, and how stimulating each contact was — then review weekly for regularity and name the disruptors. Protect sleep above everything: fixed sleep and wake times, an evening wind-down, no late caffeine or alcohol, no all-nighters.
**APPLY WHEN:** Mood instability tracks the schedule; a mood-stabilizing medication regimen is already in place and this runs alongside it.
**AVOID WHEN:** Offered as a substitute for medication — it was never tested that way; or after an activated episode has already begun, since the rhythm work prevents rather than treats.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** IPSRT RCT (Frank 2005, n=175; 3 stars, adjunct only): greater rhythm regularization during the acute phase predicted fewer recurrences, with the benefit appearing as longer stable periods in maintenance rather than faster stabilization.
## G. Closing, non-response, continuation

### [ATT:WHY-BETTER] Name the gain, hand over the credit
**Move:** At the agreed endpoint, re-take the symptom scale, state the gain in its terms, then ask the person to explain why THEY improved — steering the credit toward their own out-of-session work — and rehearse applying the new skills to situations already on the horizon. Label the sadness of an ending as the ordinary bittersweet feeling of a transition, which is categorically different from symptoms returning.
**APPLY WHEN:** Closing a bounded effort where the measure actually moved; wobbly weeks immediately after improvement.
**AVOID WHEN:** There was no real improvement, where forced credit rings hollow and [ATT:NONRESPONSE-REFRAME] applies instead; the scale shows genuine symptom return, which is relapse risk rather than normal sadness; or reluctance to end converts automatically into continuing.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Termination phase of IPT trial protocols, method-level.

### [ATT:NONRESPONSE-REFRAME] The treatment failed, not the person
**Move:** When an honest attempt has not worked, apply the medical frame to the failure itself: it is the treatment that failed. Note that roughly two thirds respond to a first course of medication and most non-responders respond to a later one, then pivot to a concrete alternative — a different agent, a different approach, a different practitioner, a renegotiated plan.
**APPLY WHEN:** Under 50% symptom reduction after about 12 weeks of honest effort; a person labelling themselves a treatment failure.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used to console instead of actually changing course, or to justify drifting on past the agreed endpoint with something that is not working.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Authors' clinical guidance plus the pharmacotherapy response literature. It targets a documented failure mode: non-responders blame themselves and abandon all treatment. PG26's normative timing is compatible — review at 2–4 weeks, zero-response checkpoint at 4–6 weeks, and run the NICE checklist before any change of school.

### [ATT:MAINT-CONTRACT] Continuation contract for high-risk responders
**Move:** After acute response in someone at high risk — several prior episodes, meaningful residual symptoms — negotiate a NEW explicit and still time-limited continuation agreement, with the focal area free to move as life events arrive and the terms renegotiated at each interval's end. The goal shifts from reducing symptoms to preventing return.
**APPLY WHEN:** Recurrent episodes; a responder left with residual symptoms; pregnancy or lactation; intolerance of or refusal of medication.
**AVOID WHEN:** A single episode with few residual symptoms, where advice and discharge may be enough; or above age 70, where monthly IPT alone was inferior to medication (Reynolds 2006). Post-response stability feels fragile for weeks or months (Markowitz 1993) — fragility is not failure.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** 4 stars (Frank 1990/1991; Reynolds 1999); tested doses are weekly for six months of continuation and monthly for up to three years. Social skills continue growing through the year after acute treatment (Weissman 1981). It is a treatment-plan decision, which the council never makes.
## H. Operations that stay with a clinician

### [ATT:SUICIDE-INQUIRY] Graded inquiry about wanting to die
**Move:** Ask directly, in a graded sequence — life not feeling worth living, wishes to be dead, thoughts of self-harm, then plan, means, past attempts, and the ability to stay safe and to tell someone. Weigh lethality, available support and the strength of the relationship, and escalate to a second opinion, a parent where a minor is involved, or emergency care when a plan is present or safety cannot be assured.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any evaluation of a depressed state, and immediately whenever a suicide item on a scale is endorsed.
**AVOID WHEN:** Attempted as solo self-assessment or run by an AI layer — risk triage is clinical work, and under the council's safety protocol any hint of this content halts the frame and routes to crisis contacts rather than continuing the analysis.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** Standard protocol across IPT trials; primary-care IPT for people declining medication reduced suicidal ideation across a year, significant by four months (Bruce 2004).

### [ATT:MED-EVAL] Medication decision
**Move:** Weigh whether medication belongs in the plan by symptom severity, the person's own preference, prior treatment history and medical contraindications; combine modalities for severe presentations; add the interpersonal work to ongoing medication as augmentation where that is the shape of the case.
**APPLY WHEN:** Severe vegetative symptoms, scores in the high 20s to 30s, high suicide risk where medication acts faster, beliefs not testable against reality (where no psychotherapy alone suffices), non-response to therapy alone, or chronic low mood where medication is first-line.
**AVOID WHEN:** Anywhere near the council — this lens never suggests starting, stopping or changing a psychiatric medication, and a stress trigger never rules medication out. Pregnancy and lactation are relative contraindications on both sides: severely recurrent cases have been maintained through pregnancy on monthly IPT without medication (Frank 1990), while stopping medication in pregnancy carries substantial relapse risk (Cohen 2006).
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** Method-level combination literature; per-patient superiority of one modality over another remains empirically unresolved. PG26 makes medication advice a hard output ban regardless of evidence.

### [ATT:PTSD-SOCIAL-REBUILD] Non-exposure trauma track
**Move:** Treat the traumatic event as a role transition and work exclusively on current interactions — voicing feelings, setting limits, reclaiming closeness with a partner and with colleagues — without any exposure to the memory or its reminders. Patients sometimes expose themselves spontaneously as they improve; that is an observation, not a promise, and it is not to be blocked when it happens.
**APPLY WHEN:** Exposure-based treatment is refused or not tolerated, and low mood accompanies the trauma picture.
**AVOID WHEN:** Attempted alone — this is a formal protocol for a trauma condition with suicide-relevant comorbidity, and PG26 prohibits prompted retelling of recent trauma in any solo or AI format (the debriefing harm signal runs at an odds ratio around 2.5).
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** Open trial (Bleiberg & Markowitz 2005) plus a group RCT against usual care in low-income women reported positive (Krupnick); 2 stars, preliminary.

### [ATT:DYAD-FEEDBACK] The working relationship as a live sample
**Move:** Use the treatment relationship itself as a here-and-now sample of the person's interpersonal style: invite complaints about the clinician and the treatment, correct distortions, apologize for real mistakes. When the alliance ruptures, name the rupture as an instance of the presenting problem, work the expression options in the moment, then transfer the same skill to outside relationships. Never framed as transference or as a re-enactment of childhood.
**APPLY WHEN:** Friction inside an ongoing treatment; a person whose focus is isolation and who needs one modelled relationship; a fragile alliance where endings need discussing early and often.
**AVOID WHEN:** Applied outside a therapy dyad — an AI layer is not the dyad and cannot host this; when it displaces the outside-life focus; or when it drifts into interpretation, which the model explicitly avoids.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** Authors' clinical guidance; the variant for emotion-dysregulation presentations comes from an unfinished Columbia open trial (Markowitz, Skodol & Bleiberg 2006) at 1 star — do not oversell. PG26 assigns rupture repair to therapist work and rates rupture-repair association LOW (r=.29).

### [ATT:ANGER-GRADED] Graded anger expression
**Move:** For the suppress-then-explode cycle, where chronic suppression is punctuated by outbursts that justify further suppression: link each surge to its interpersonal situation, validate the legitimate portion, then rehearse progressively more muted expressions — staying in the room without leaving, then voicing the feeling, then delivering a measured version inside the real relationship.
**APPLY WHEN:** The cycle is visibly wrecking relationships and the person is in ongoing treatment where the escalation can be monitored.
**AVOID WHEN:** Run solo in a context with self-harm risk, which requires close monitoring, or during an active crisis, where containment comes first.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** Case-level within an unfinished open trial (Markowitz, Skodol & Bleiberg 2006) plus one small unpublished trial (Angus & Gillies 1994); 1 star — the weakest evidence in this file.
