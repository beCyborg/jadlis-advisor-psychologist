# advisor-behavioral — core

The principles of the behavioral lens, ranked by how well each is actually evidenced rather than by how central it feels inside the tradition. Read top-down: the further you go, the more you are dealing with plausible mechanism and clinical convention rather than measured effect. Citation tags match `moves.md`.

---

## Tier 1 — Directly measured, large evidence mass

### The intention–behavior gap is the default, not a defect (d = .66 → .36; 47% vs 7%)
Wanting predicts acting far less than it looks. Correlationally intentions cover roughly 28% of behavioral variance across 422 studies (N = 82,107), but experimentally raising intention strength by d = .66 moves behavior only d = .36, and after past behavior is controlled the unique contribution falls to R² = .07. The gap is carried almost entirely by people who intend and do not act — a median of 47% of intenders — against 7% who act without intending. Practical consequence for this lens: "I understand it and still don't do it" is the documented normal output of intention-based self-regulation, so more insight, more reasons, and more commitment are the wrong prescription. [BA:MODEL-MAP] [BA:IFTHEN]

### If-then planning closes it (d = .65, k = 94, N = 8,461)
Adding "if situation Y, then I will response X" on top of an already-formed goal improves attainment by d = .65 [.60, .70] over the goal alone. The estimate is robust: 88% experimental designs, 49% of tests unpublished with equal effects, equal for objective and self-reported outcomes, present in all eight goal domains examined. Two components carry it — the if-part makes the cue highly accessible (d = .80: detection .72, discrimination .82, accessibility .95, attention .72, memory .87), the then-part gives the response immediacy (d = .77), efficiency under cognitive load (d = .85), and initiation without conscious intent at the moment (d = .72). This is the single hardest number in the whole lens. [BA:IFTHEN] [BA:CUESPEC]

### Planning substitutes for weak executive control, it does not require it (d = 1.10 vs .66)
Effects are LARGEST in samples with impaired self-regulation. That reverses the intuition that people who struggle to follow through need to build willpower before they can use structure — the structure is what compensates. Per-problem breakdown: getting started d = .61 (remembering .54, seizing brief windows .61, overcoming in-the-moment reluctance .65); shielding an ongoing pursuit ≈ .77 (attention .90, behavior .54, blocking bad self-states 1.10, blocking adverse contexts .93). [BA:SHIELD] [BA:REHEARSE]

### The effect is volitional, not motivational (+33% at 4.60/5 motivation)
Forming a plan raises neither commitment nor self-efficacy, and works at ceiling motivation — screening attendance rose by a third in a sample already near the top of the motivation scale. Conversely the automaticity stays goal-dependent: no activated goal, no effect. So this component is a tool for the "how", never a substitute for a wanted direction. [BA:VALUES] [BA:IFTHEN]

### Behavioral activation as a package works for depressive patterns (MODERATE)
Bona fide psychotherapy beats waitlist/usual care for depression at MODERATE certainty (d = −0.62…−0.92 vs waitlist), and behavioral activation and cognitive restructuring hold up as standalone components at MODERATE. Anchor trials: component analysis of ~150 adults where activation alone matched activation plus restructuring and matched full cognitive therapy acutely and at two-year follow-up; the 241-adult trial where activation matched an antidepressant acutely, with a severity subgroup where it outperformed cognitive therapy; Moradveisi 2013 (Iran, n = 100, therapists trained in 20 hours) 91.1% vs 68.6% remission at 13 weeks and 65.9% vs 27.9% at 49 weeks; Richards 2017 non-inferiority to cognitive therapy at lower cost with junior staff; a 6–8 session lay-counsellor programme in India that was clinically and cost-effective. [BA:OUTSIDE-IN] [BA:SCHEDULE] [BA:GRADE]

### Exposure with rituals withheld is the treatment-specific answer for ritual patterns (method base ~50 years)
Roughly 60–70% probability of at least some improvement; a full course associated with a 50–70% reduction in symptom load. This matters for routing: the finding that methods are broadly interchangeable holds for depression ONLY and explicitly does not extend to ritual-and-intrusion targets or trauma-shaped targets. A general-purpose lens is the wrong instrument there. And the graded, repeated, prolonged form of exposure from a clinician-built hierarchy is the standard the evidence describes (MODERATE). [BA:SITEXP] [BA:RP-COLDMOD] [BA:PROGRAM]

## Tier 2 — Well-supported mechanisms, bounded claims

### Function beats form
The same act can serve connection or escape; only its antecedents and consequences say which. Rumination is private behavior under the same laws as public behavior. This is the analytic engine of the lens, and it is foundational rather than trial-tested in isolation — the trials rest on it, no trial isolates it. [BA:ABC] [BA:TRAP-TRAC] [BA:RUM-ASSESS]

### Relief is the mechanism, not the evidence
Withdrawal, checking, reassurance-seeking, and numbing all pay out immediately, which is precisely why they persist and why the payoff should never be read as proof they work. Three parts of the loop are involuntary (the intrusion, the alarm response, the rebound after suppression) and two are levers: how the intrusion is interpreted, and whether you ritualize or avoid. [BA:TRAP-TRAC] [BA:OCD-GRID]

### Suppression fails and intensifies
Rebound after thought suppression is replicated across many studies. Intrusions themselves are near-universal — reported by 90–99% of people, with content indistinguishable from that of people with a ritual problem; what differs is frequency, distress, imputed importance, and how hard the fight is. [BA:UNIVLIST] [BA:THOUGHT-EXP]

### Safety learning, not habituation
The active ingredient in exposure is learning that the anxiety and the uncertainty are tolerable and the situation safer than predicted. Within-session anxiety decline is neither necessary nor sufficient; watching the clock for the drop converts the practice back into a control strategy and voids it. Success is staying and functioning while anxious. Practices therefore maximize surprise and vary setting and order. [BA:SITEXP] [BA:IMEXP]

### Outside-in: action first, mood second
The model of change in this tradition is deliberately inverted relative to cognitive work — where cognitive therapy has the head teach the hands, this lens has the hands teach the head. The claim is narrow and worth stating precisely: it is not that feelings do not matter, and not that they should be overridden by effort. It is that the feeling's veto over a scheduled action is removed while the feeling itself is acknowledged, and that repeated contact with reward is what shifts the state. This is why the tradition grades tasks and starts below current capacity instead of exhorting. [BA:OUTSIDE-IN] [BA:GRADE]

### Reward is idiosyncratic and has to be assessed, never assumed
What reinforces one person punishes another, and an objectively good event can be a net loss of reinforcement. The corollary is that a single trial settles nothing: a well-chosen activity may only pay off across repetitions, so the unit of evidence here is a series, not an attempt. Natural reinforcers — payoffs that follow logically from doing the thing — generalize; engineered ones are a bridge that has to be dismantled. [BA:NATURAL-REINFORCE] [BA:EXPERIMENT]

### Secondary problems first
Real losses — a job, a relationship, a health event — are not what treatment addresses first. The coping that grew around them is, because that is what keeps the remaining rewards out of reach and compounds the original problem. [BA:MODEL-MAP] [BA:TROUBLESHOOT-BA]

### Brooding and reflection are not one thing
Only the abstract, evaluative form predicts worsening; concrete process-focused reflection does not. The lens therefore targets the process of looping and drops the content entirely — no testing of whether the thoughts are true. [BA:RUM-ASSESS] [BA:2MIN]

## Tier 3 — Real but thin: single studies or k = 3

- **Link rehearsal over reminder notes.** 87% acted after rehearsing the cue–response link, 40% with a posted reminder, 20% with no plan — a striking result from ONE experiment (Milne & Sheeran 2002c). Use it, do not quote it as settled. [BA:REHEARSE]
- **Pre-committed disengagement rules** against sunk-cost escalation: d = .47 across k = 3 escalation-paradigm studies. Promising, not proven — and a hair-trigger rule on a noisy signal forces premature abandonment.
- **Capacity conservation** across back-to-back demanding tasks: d = 1.28 across k = 3. The surrounding ego-depletion literature predates the replication-crisis re-examination, so treat the mechanism story with more caution than the headline.
- **Exercise dosing.** Three to five days a week reduced symptoms against placebo and against a lower dose (Dunn 2005); reviews put exercise near medication (Carek 2011; Schuch 2019). One RCT found the same activity without an explicit rationale performed worse (Nyström 2017) — the rationale is part of the dose. [BA:NATURAL-REINFORCE]

## Tier 4 — Method-level or author-clinical: procedure transfers, effect sizes do not

- **Every mnemonic and worksheet in the tradition is untested as such.** The acronyms, the model chart, the avoidance-substitution sheet, the completeness checklist, the two-minute rule — all are packaging over trial-tested treatment, and the source books say so. The individualization rule is explicit: use what matters, drop the rest, replace acronyms with your own metaphors. [BA:TROUBLESHOOT-BA] [BA:2MIN]
- **The activation scale has no cutoffs**; read direction of change only. The rating and readiness forms in the ritual workbook are clinic conventions without cited psychometrics — usable for within-person tracking, not for severity claims. [BA:MEASURE] [BA:READINESS]
- **Values work is imported**, method-level in its own tradition, integrated here by clinical reasoning. [BA:VALUES]
- **Acceptance components, random practice ordering, the modified full-stop protocol, and the script rules** sit on top of the exposure base with more confidence than their component-level evidence supports. [BA:ACT-DEFUSE] [BA:RP-COLDMOD] [BA:IMEXP]
- **Approaching avoided loss material** rests on the authors' clinical experience and on theories the book itself calls untested in this population; the routing rule is measurement first, therapist-led only. [BA:GRIEF-APPROACH]
- **Cue-deconditioning and opposite-to-urge action** are principle-level extensions of the tradition, not findings of it.

## Tier 5 — Boundary conditions the lens must state out loud

- **No study has tested a clinician-free delivery of any of this.** Two indirectness layers cap everything: clinical to subclinical populations, and live therapist to self-guided or AI-mediated format. Procedures transfer; effect sizes do not.
- **Unguided self-help is weaker than every guided format** (HIGH) and no better than usual care, though not harmful in the aggregate (5.8% vs 9.1% deterioration). Lay guidance closes most of the gap — the guidance, not the content, is the lever.
- **Homework presence sits at d = 0.48 and was demoted to LOW**; quality beats quantity (g = 1.07 vs 0.51) and no reliable completion benchmark exists. Non-completion is a design failure of the task, not a character reading. [BA:TROUBLESHOOT-BA]
- **Adverse events run 5–20%, and 5–10% of people leave psychotherapy worse.** Temporary worsening is baseline, not a switch signal; a single hard week is not a trajectory.
- **Most people do not respond within about two months** (response 41% vs 17%). Review at 2–4 weeks; at zero response by 4–6 weeks run the checklist before changing anything.
- **The evidence base of both source books is Western and English-language.** There is no Russian translation of any book in this lens: every worksheet name, mnemonic, and instruction has to be built from scratch in Russian, and the acronyms will not survive translation — the canon itself licenses replacing them with metaphors.
