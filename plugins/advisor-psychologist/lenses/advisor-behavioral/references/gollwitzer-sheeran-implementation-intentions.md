# Implementation Intentions and Goal Achievement: A Meta-analysis of Effects and Processes — digest

## What this book is for the lens

This is the lens's hardest evidence for the exact complaint "понимаю, но не делаю": a meta-analysis of 94 experimental tests (N = 8,461) showing that adding an if-then plan on top of an already-formed goal intention improves goal attainment by d = .65 — over and above wanting, deciding, and knowing why. Its mechanism-level thesis: the intention–behavior gap is not a motivation problem, so motivational fixes (more insight, more commitment, more "why") cannot close it; what closes it is pre-deciding when/where/how and delegating action control to a situational cue ("strategic automaticity", or "instant habit"). It beats every BA workbook on this specific joint because BA books assume that a scheduled activity gets done; this paper explains why scheduled activities don't get done (forgetting, missed windows, in-the-moment reluctance, derailment) and supplies the one technique with meta-analytic backing for each of those failure points. Use it whenever an activation plan exists but execution keeps failing.

## Core model

The paper is a meta-analysis, so its "chapters" are argument sections; use these as navigation anchors: **The Intention–Behavior Gap** → **Four Self-Regulatory Problems of Goal Striving** (model of action phases) → **The Strategy: If-Then Plans and Their Mechanisms** → **Meta-Analytic Findings** → **Amplifiers / Moderators** → **Boundary Conditions and the Rigidity Question**.

**1. The gap is real and normal (The Intention–Behavior Gap).** Intentions look predictive in correlational work (~28% of behavioral variance across 422 studies, N = 82,107) but the causal picture is weaker: experimentally raising intention strength by d = .66 moves behavior only d = .36, and controlling past behavior drops intentions' unique contribution to R² = .07. The gap is driven almost entirely by *inclined abstainers* — people who intend and don't act (median 47% of intenders) — not by people acting without intending (7%). Diagnostic frame for the user: "understanding but not doing" is the documented default of intention-based self-regulation, not a character defect.

**2. Four ways goal striving breaks (Four Self-Regulatory Problems).** Goal pursuit is a sequence of tasks — deliberate → plan → act → evaluate/disengage — and it fails at four points: (a) **failing to get started**: forgetting (70% of breast-self-exam abstainers cited it), missing brief opportunity windows (mean 8-hour deviation from intended start; only 37% completed a short-window task), and head-vs-heart reluctance at the critical moment; (b) **getting derailed**: distraction, antagonistic habits, and — crucially — influences people cannot consciously anticipate: detrimental self-states (good mood → stereotyping, ego-depletion, self-definitional incompleteness) and adverse contexts (social loafing, loss frames, nonconsciously primed goals); (c) **not calling a halt**: escalation of commitment / sunk costs under self-justification; (d) **overextending**: spending self-control on task one and having none for task two (ego-depletion, ironic rebound of suppressed thoughts). Mere goal intentions demonstrably fail against all four.

**3. The mechanism (If-Then Plans and Their Mechanisms).** An implementation intention is a second act of willing layered on a goal intention: "If situation Y occurs, then I will initiate response X." Two components carry the effect. The **if-component** makes the chosen cue highly accessible — better detection, discrimination, attention, memory (overall d = .80). The **then-component** delegates control to the situation, giving the response automaticity features: immediacy (d = .77), efficiency under cognitive load (d = .85 — holds even during heroin withdrawal), and no need for conscious intent at the moment of action (d = .72). The effect is *not* mediated by motivation: commitment and self-efficacy do not rise after forming a plan, and effects appear even at ceiling motivation (cervical-screening attendance rose 33% in a sample already at 4.60/5 motivation). Automaticity stays goal-dependent — plans do not fire when the superordinate goal is weak or deactivated.

**4. What the numbers say (Meta-Analytic Findings).** d = .65 [.60, .70] overall, robust to publication bias (49% of tests unpublished, effects equal), equal for objective vs self-report outcomes, present in all eight goal domains, and *largest where self-regulation is most impaired* — clinical samples (schizophrenia, frontal-lobe injury, heroin addiction) show d = 1.10 vs .66 for everyone else: planning compensates for weak executive control rather than requiring strong executive control.

## Moves

### [BA:IFTHEN] Form an if-then plan on top of the goal
**Move:** Take an existing, genuinely wanted goal; pick one concrete goal-directed response and one specific anticipated occasion (external cue — place/time/person/object, or internal cue — a feeling), and write the plan verbatim as "If <situation Y>, then I will <response X>". The plan pre-decides when, where, and how, so the moment of action requires no deliberation.
**APPLY WHEN:** Strong goal but weak follow-through ("понимаю, но не делаю"); new or non-routine behaviors; deadlines and brief opportunity windows; known reluctance at the critical moment.
**AVOID WHEN:** The goal intention itself is weak or ambivalent — effects vanish without an activated strong goal, so fix commitment first; or the behavior is already trivially easy and routine (little added benefit).
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Direct — this meta-analysis: k = 94, N = 8,461, d = .65 [.60, .70] over and above goal intention alone.

### [BA:SHIELD] Shielding plan against a known obstacle
**Move:** Put the anticipated obstacle or temptation into the if-part and a protective response into the then-part: ignoring ("if I see the spider, then I ignore it"), self-instruction ("if I feel my anger rise, then I tell myself: stay calm"), or restating the focal striving. This defends an ongoing pursuit instead of initiating one.
**APPLY WHEN:** Ongoing pursuits threatened by distraction, cravings, habitual antagonistic responses, provocation, or known detrimental self-states.
**AVOID WHEN:** The real obstacle is unknown — then spell out the focal striving itself instead of guessing obstacles (or run [BA:CONTRAST] first); a plan aimed at an obstacle that doesn't actually hinder the goal is wasted.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Direct — meta-analytic subsets: suppressing attention responses d = .90 (k = 5), suppressing behavioral responses d = .54 (k = 5), blocking detrimental self-states d = 1.10, blocking adverse contextual influences d = .93.

### [BA:CONSERVE] Capacity-conserving plan for two-phase situations
**Move:** When a demanding task precedes another goal, automate the first task via if-then so self-control isn't spent before phase two — e.g. "If I have solved one anagram, then I immediately move to the next"; "If the amusing scene appears, then I tell myself: just silly jokes." The plan does the regulating, leaving capacity intact.
**APPLY WHEN:** Back-to-back demanding tasks; emotion control followed by performance; depletion-prone days where one effortful episode predictably wrecks the rest.
**AVOID WHEN:** No second task is at stake — plain [BA:IFTHEN] suffices and the extra structure adds nothing.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Direct but thin — k = 3, d = 1.28; planners showed no ego-depletion carry-over (d = .87) and no stereotype rebound (d = 1.81 vs controls).

### [BA:HALT] Disengagement plan against sunk costs
**Move:** Pre-commit a switch rule before escalation risk appears: "If I receive disappointing/negative feedback, then I will switch to a different strategy (or halt this course of action)." The halt decision is made in advance, when self-justification isn't yet active.
**APPLY WHEN:** Escalation risk — continued investment mainly justifying past investment; projects with periodic feedback checkpoints where a rational exit rule can be defined ahead of time.
**AVOID WHEN:** The feedback signal is noisy or ambiguous — a hair-trigger rule forces premature abandonment; define what counts as "disappointing" precisely or don't arm the rule.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Direct but thin — k = 3 escalation-paradigm studies, d = .47 (medium).

### [BA:REHEARSE] Rehearse the cue–response link, don't post reminders
**Move:** Write the if-then plan down twice; on the second write-through, deliberately concentrate on the *link* between situation and action — not on the note as an artifact. The strength of the if→then association, not the existence of the plan on paper, is what carries the effect over time.
**APPLY WHEN:** Plans that must survive days or weeks until the cue appears; prospective-memory-heavy goals; a previously formed plan that existed but never fired.
**AVOID WHEN:** Tempted to substitute a posted reminder note for rehearsal — the reminder condition performed barely above no-plan control (40% vs 20% acted), while link rehearsal reached 87%.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Single experiment (Milne & Sheeran 2002c): 87% acted with link rehearsal vs 40% reminder vs 20% control — strong result, but method-level, one study.

### [BA:CUESPEC] Quality-gate the plan's components
**Move:** Before adopting any plan, check both components: the cue must be *precise* (no deliberation left for the moment itself), *viable* (will actually be encountered; response actually performable there), and *instrumental* (the situation permits the action; the action really advances the goal). Reject "if tomorrow, then eat healthily"-grade plans and re-specify: "if it's lunchtime at my usual place, then I order the salad."
**APPLY WHEN:** Every plan formation; mandatory re-run when a previous plan silently failed — all documented failure modes of implementation intentions are specification errors.
**AVOID WHEN:** n/a — this is a validity gate; skipping it *is* the failure mode.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Authors' boundary-condition analysis within the meta-analysis; vague or non-instrumental plans predicted to confer no benefit (conceptual synthesis, not a separate RCT).

### [BA:CONTRAST] Mental contrasting to find the real obstacle
**Move:** Before forming shielding plans, run mental contrasting: vividly elaborate the desired future, then the present negative reality standing in its way; use the obstacles that emerge as the if-components of subsequent [BA:SHIELD] plans. This front-end selects targets; the if-then plan still does the work.
**APPLY WHEN:** Unclear which barrier actually blocks the goal; plans that keep firing but keep missing the real obstacle.
**AVOID WHEN:** The goal is judged infeasible — contrasting binds people to feasible goals only; and never as a substitute for the plan itself.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Method-level (Oettingen 2000; Oettingen et al. 2001) — recommended by the authors as a front-end to if-then planning; not tested inside this meta-analysis.

## Tier boundary

The paper genuinely contains no REQUIRES-THERAPIST technique: implementation intentions are self-administrable by design, and every move above was tested as a brief self-instruction. The boundary the lens must respect is elsewhere: (1) the *strongest* effects (d = 1.10) come from clinical samples — schizophrenia, frontal-lobe injury, heroin withdrawal — where plans were embedded in supervised research/clinical protocols; replicating "if-then for a psychiatric condition" (e.g. planning around craving, self-harm urges, or psychotic symptoms) is therapist territory, not a self-help extrapolation. (2) The authors themselves flag ADHD, depression, and pain/stress control as promising-but-untested — the lens must not present these applications as evidenced. (3) If the user's blocker is a weak or ambivalent goal intention (the "AVOID WHEN" of [BA:IFTHEN]), that is a values/motivation problem this paper explicitly does not solve — route to the live therapist or to the lens's motivation-side sources, not to more planning.

## What the book debunks

- **"If motivation is real, action follows."** Traditional goal theories (TPB, social cognitive theory, goal setting) overweight intention strength; a d = .66 experimental boost to intentions moves behavior only d = .36. The gap lives among inclined abstainers (median 47%), so "want it more" is the wrong prescription for "понимаю, но не делаю".
- **"Just try harder in the moment."** Willpower-in-the-moment cannot target influences people don't consciously register — social loafing, loss framing, primed antagonistic goals, ego-depletion. Ego-depleted people report equal effort and equal desire to persist yet perform worse; suppressing stereotypes by sheer intention backfires (rebound). Defenses must be installed *before* the situation.
- **"Implementation intentions are a motivation technique."** They are not: forming a plan raises neither commitment nor self-efficacy, and effects appear at ceiling motivation. Conversely, they cannot substitute for a missing goal — no strong activated goal, no effect.
- **"If-then plans make you a rigid robot."** Tested and rejected: automaticity is goal-dependent (plans don't fire against one's goals), automating one dimension cost nothing on others (speed didn't hurt accuracy; quantity *and* variety of creative solutions rose), and planners didn't ignore alternative good opportunities — conserved capacity let them process alternatives.
- **The reverse over-claim — "if-then plans always work."** Badly specified plans yield roughly nothing; all documented failure modes are specification errors (cues that rarely occur, actions that turn out impossible in situ, low-instrumentality responses — taking the stairs alone won't cause weight loss, vagueness).
- **"A reminder note is as good as a plan."** Reminder condition ≈ no-plan control (40% vs 20%); the active ingredient is the rehearsed cue–response link (87%).
- **Misquoting the headline number.** d = .65 is the increment *over an already-formed goal intention*, not over doing nothing — never quote it as "planning triples success".

## Numbers that matter

- **d = .65 [.60, .70]** — overall effect of implementation intentions on goal attainment beyond goal intentions; k = 94 tests, N = 8,461; 88% experimental designs; robust across published/unpublished (49% unpublished) and objective/self-report outcomes.
- **28% / R² = .07 / d = .66 → d = .36** — intentions' share of behavioral variance in correlational meta-meta (422 studies, N = 82,107); unique contribution after past behavior; experimental intention change vs resulting behavior change. Together: the intention–behavior gap quantified.
- **47% vs 7%** — median rate of inclined abstainers (intend, don't act) vs disinclined actors: the gap is a failure of enactment, not of intention.
- **70% / 8 hours / 37%** — abstainers citing forgetting (breast self-exam); mean deviation from intended start time; completion rate of a short-window task without a plan. The anatomy of "failing to get started".
- **d = .80 (if-component)** — cue-processing advantage: detection .72, discrimination .82, accessibility .95, attention .72, memory .87.
- **d = .77 / .85 / .72 (then-component)** — immediacy; efficiency under cognitive load (holds in heroin withdrawal); action without conscious intent in the moment. The "instant habit" signature.
- **d = 1.10 vs .66** — clinical samples (schizophrenia, frontal-lobe injury, heroin addiction) vs everyone else: planning substitutes for impaired executive control.
- **Per-problem effects** — initiation d = .61 (remembering .54, seizing opportunities .61, overcoming reluctance .65); shielding ≈ .77 (attention .90, behavior .54, self-states 1.10, contexts .93); disengagement .47 (k = 3); capacity conservation 1.28 (k = 3).
- **Domains** — environmental 1.12, prosocial 1.01, antiracist .87, academic .72, laboratory .70, health .59, personal goals .58, consumer .41; health evidence (k = 23) is mostly initiation of protective behaviors.
- **87% / 40% / 20%** — link rehearsal vs reminder note vs control (Milne & Sheeran 2002c).
- **+33% at 4.60/5** — cervical-screening attendance gain despite near-ceiling pre-intervention motivation: proof the effect is volitional, not motivational.

## Blind spots of this book

- **It starts where motivation ends.** The entire apparatus presupposes a strong, activated goal intention; the paper says nothing about building commitment, resolving ambivalence, or choosing goals worth wanting. For a user whose "понимаю" is actually shaky, this source is silent — pair with the lens's motivation/values sources.
- **Evidence mass is front-loaded on initiation.** 52 of 94 tests concern getting started; shielding has 21; disengagement and capacity conservation rest on k = 3 each. The lens should present [BA:HALT] and [BA:CONSERVE] as promising, not proven.
- **Stopping risk behaviors is under-tested.** Health evidence is mostly initiating protective behaviors (exercise, screening); smoking, alcohol, and diet cessation are thin here — don't transfer the d = .59 health estimate to quitting.
- **Mostly single, discrete behaviors over short horizons.** Lab tasks and one-shot health actions dominate; sustaining complex, multi-month behavior change (the typical BA course) is an extrapolation the meta-analysis doesn't directly license, beyond the note that plan-based repetition grows habit qualities (Orbell & Verplanken 2005).
- **What happens after a plan fails is unknown.** The authors flag it themselves: no data on whether people stubbornly re-run faulty plans, revise them, or abandon planning altogether — so the lens's "re-run [BA:CUESPEC] after silent failure" is sensible practice, not tested protocol.
- **Untested populations flagged by the authors.** ADHD, depression, pain/stress control, routine clinical settings — promising by mechanism (compensation for executive deficits), but not evidenced in this paper.
- **No individual-differences guidance.** People differ in ability to form strong if-then links; beyond "repetition strengthens links", the paper offers no way to predict or diagnose who needs more rehearsal.
- **Era caveat.** The synthesis is from 2006; it predates the replication-crisis re-examination of some neighboring literatures it cites (notably ego-depletion). The core d = .65 rests on 94 mostly-experimental tests and is the strongest claim; mechanism-adjacent citations deserve more caution than the headline effect.
