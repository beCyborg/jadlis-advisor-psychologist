# Behavioral Activation for Depression: A Clinician's Guide — digest

## What this book is for the lens

The mechanism-level canon of BA: depression is maintained by life context — lowered response-contingent positive reinforcement plus avoidance coping that buys short-term relief at long-term cost (Ferster/Lewinsohn lineage) — so treatment changes what the person does, not their insides. Its edge over other lens sources: the full clinician-grade protocol behind the strongest BA trial base (Jacobson 1996 component analysis; Dimidjian 2006 Seattle trial), with the complete worksheet toolkit (Appendices 1–7), the troubleshooting chapter, and the rumination-as-behavior chapter briefer sources omit. Use it as the authority on HOW to apply a move and when it is misused; it is not a self-help book — tier boundaries below matter.

## Core model

- **Model of depression (Principle 2, Ch. 2)**: life changes → reduced reward / increased punishment → dysphoria → secondary-problem coping (withdrawal, escape, avoidance) that keeps rewards unexperienced and compounds the primary problems (real losses: layoff, divorce). Secondary problems are targeted FIRST (Ch. 9). The model coexists with biological/cognitive models; it is a clinical tool for composing an individualized "behavioral antidepressant."
- **Model of change (Principle 1)**: act "outside-in" — action first, motivation follows. Shorthand: CT = "the head teaches the hands"; BA = "the hands teach the head."
- **10 core principles**: P1–P3 ≈ model + ABC assessment; P4–P10 ≈ conduct (schedule from plan not mood; start small; prefer natural reinforcement; coach stance; every assignment is an experiment; stay action-oriented; troubleshoot as a norm).
- **Function over form (P3, Ch. 4)**: identical behaviors can serve connection or avoidance; judge by ABC, never by appearance. Rumination is private behavior under the same laws (Ch. 7).
- **Course (Ch. 3, 9)**: early = model presentation (Fig. 3.1 / App. 1a), conceptualization, heavy monitoring; middle = cycle of monitoring → scheduling → problem solving → troubleshooting; late = consolidation, generalization, relapse prevention (programmed from session one; termination by progress data; brief boosters when self-applied strategies fail — therapist territory). Session skeleton: symptom measure, collaborative agenda, homework review + new assignment every time.
- **Style (ENLIVEN, Ch. 3)**: nonjudgmental matter-of-fact stance, validate before encouraging, orient (rationale for every procedure), reinforce every adaptive sign, genuine warmth; ambivalence gets motivational interviewing, not pressure.
- **Navigation anchors**: activity charts (App. 1b–1d), scheduling charts (1e–1f), TRAP–TRAC sheet (1g), ACTION sheet (1h), Notebook & Weekly Therapy Plan (App. 2, 11 prompts, self-help-usable), Posttherapy Plan (App. 3), ACTIVATE (App. 4), BADS (App. 5), Q-BAS (6a/6b), Fidelity Checklist (7). Brief formats: BATD weekly value-driven goal lists (Lejuez/Hopko); HAP 6–8 sessions via lay counselors.
- **Individualization rule (Ch. 10)**: use what matters, drop the rest — scheduling alone can suffice for an inactive non-avoider; skip rumination work for non-ruminators; acronyms are optional scaffolding, replaceable by metaphor.

## Moves

### [BA:OUTSIDE-IN] Act from the plan, not the mood
**Move:** Commit to acting on a goal/plan at a set time regardless of current mood, as an experiment in whether feelings follow action; acknowledge the feeling, remove only its veto over action; pre-frame expected awkwardness (broken-arm analogy: effortful and unnatural until healing).
**APPLY WHEN:** "I'll do it when I feel better / motivated"; activity collapsing with mood; waiting for motivation to arrive first.
**AVOID WHEN:** Misread as bare willpower ("just do it") or as faking happiness/suppressing feelings — pair with grading and reinforcement planning, never exhortation.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Core BA premise; method-level via full-package RCTs (Jacobson et al. 1996; Dimidjian et al. 2006 — BA acutely superior to cognitive therapy).

### [BA:ACTIVITY-CHART] Self-monitor activity–mood links
**Move:** Record activities + mood/emotion + intensity (1–10) close to real time; realistic default = block recording every 3–4 hours (hourly rarely survives real life); log internal behaviors like rumination as activities; alternative = Mastery and Pleasure 0–10 anchored by the person's own activities (teeth-brushing = 0, mowing the yard = 10); any medium counts. Review weekly with five questions: which activities link to which moods (probe same-activity/different-day gaps), what is the emotional range, is the sleep/meal/work routine disrupted (if yes, regularize routines early), what avoidance shows up, where to start (easiest-to-shift behaviors first).
**APPLY WHEN:** Start of any BA effort (baseline); ongoing to test plans; flat "depressed all the time" reports (walk back the last few hours to find subtle shifts).
**AVOID WHEN:** Entries so global they hide variability ("at work, bored—8" all day); if monitoring feels like a worsening microscope, grade the monitoring (activities only, fewer periods, other medium) rather than dropping it.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Monitoring is inside virtually all trials in the BA meta-analytic base (Ekers et al. 2014); M/P ratings adopted from CT (Beck et al. 1979).

### [BA:SCHEDULE] Activity scheduling and structuring — the backbone
**Move:** For each activity fix frequency (start BELOW pre-depression capacity), capped duration, specific written days/times (App. 1e–1f), place, companions, success criterion; pre-plan around obstacles ("rain or shine"); add environmental cues (object by the door, alarm, written plan) and public commitment (tell a friend, report back, do it together); minimal version = 1–2 activities with a specific-time commitment.
**APPLY WHEN:** Always — every other BA strategy hangs on this; good intentions repeatedly dissolving into mood-dependent inaction.
**AVOID WHEN:** Scheduling by mood; vague agreements nobody could restate; loading the week beyond what a depressed person can absorb — overloaded first weeks manufacture demoralization.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Method-level since Lewinsohn's hourly schedules; in the trial base (Ekers et al. 2014); goal research — specific beats vague, implementation intentions help (Locke & Latham 1990; Gollwitzer 1999).

### [BA:GRADE] Graded task assignment — start absurdly small
**Move:** Decompose the target into extremely small ranked steps and secure each before the next (ex-marathoner stepped back to "buy shoes and shorts"); start at the easy end with highest mood payoff; count partial completion as success to build on; expect payoff only over repetitions — mastery counts even without enjoyment.
**APPLY WHEN:** Overwhelm, hopelessness, freezing before a mountain of tasks, all-or-nothing behaving, self-chiding "just do it" talk.
**AVOID WHEN:** Starting where the person believes they SHOULD function instead of at measured baseline; using "partial counts" to never raise the bar; drastic avoidance cuts (5 h/day gaming → 30 min won't hold — shave 10-minute increments).
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Graded task assignment from CT/behavioral tradition; method-level via BA trials; case: job-loss client rebuilt via micro-steps to a dog-walking business.

### [BA:ABC] Functional assessment of the depression loop
**Move:** Map Antecedent (external or private — a memory, an emotion), concrete Behavior (public or private, incl. rumination), and Consequences (immediate and long-term); chain ABCs (a consequence becomes the next antecedent); simplified script: "What happened? How did you feel? What did you do? Then what happened?" (Fig. 3.2). Classify behaviors as increase-targets (contact positive reinforcement) or decrease-targets (avoidance under negative reinforcement).
**APPLY WHEN:** Any recurring mood–behavior pattern; choosing targets; a failed homework needing explanation; "productive-looking" activity yielding no reward.
**AVOID WHEN:** Treating the formulation as fact — it is always a hypothesis to test; labeling behavior good/bad by form alone.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Behavior-analytic foundation (Ferster 1973; Lewinsohn 1974); the BA trials rest on this analysis.

### [BA:TRAP-TRAC] Catch avoidance and substitute approach
**Move:** Detect avoidance by FUNCTION: ask what happens to the fatigue/anxiety/hopeless thoughts on retreat — if they temporarily fade, negative reinforcement is confirmed. Then TRAP = Trigger, Response (emotional reaction), Avoidance-Pattern; get back on TRAC(k): same Trigger and Response, substitute Alternative Coping — an approach behavior (Sheet App. 1g; also prospectively on feared opportunities). First validate that being stuck makes sense: short-run relief vs long-run cost, no blame.
**APPLY WHEN:** "I'm stuck and can't just do it"; isolation; subtle avoidance (busy but reward-less activity, numbing, subordinating one's needs); self-blame blocking collaboration.
**AVOID WHEN:** Treating relief as proof retreat "works" — relief is the trap's mechanism; alternative coping that is itself covert avoidance; triggers that are traumatic material — that is exposure work for a therapist.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** BA method canon (Jacobson et al. 2001); avoidance-modification component of the trials; validation-facilitates-help finding (Warwar et al. 2008).

### [BA:EXPERIMENT] Every assignment is an experiment (ACTION)
**Move:** Predict, try, observe outcome on mood/goal progress; repeat across occasions and vary activities before judging; failed experiments get functional analysis ("now we know something new"), not blame. Portable form = ACTION sheet (App. 1h): Assess the behavior's function (antidepressant or depressant? consistent with long-term goals?), Choose approach, Try with a specific plan, Integrate into routine, Observe results, Never give up.
**APPLY WHEN:** Uncertainty about what will reinforce this person; an assignment "didn't work"; demoralization after a flat result.
**AVOID WHEN:** Concluding from one trial — the model mandates repetition; promising that a given activity will lift mood — no such guarantee exists in principle.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Core BA stance (Principle 8); method-level via BA trials; ACTION adapted from Martell, Addis & Jacobson (2001).

### [BA:NATURAL-REINFORCE] Prefer natural reinforcers; engineer contingencies as a bridge
**Move:** Pick activities whose rewards follow logically from doing them — mine history with "What do you tend to do when you're not depressed?" When the environment won't pay off yet, bridge with engineered consequences: contingent self-reward ($20 in a jar per diet week), public commitment, report-back, Premack principle (TV contingent on doing the low-frequency task first), pairing tedious tasks with pleasant context, sequencing a quick win before a long task.
**APPLY WHEN:** Choosing among candidate activities; early activation being punished or ignored (rebuffed social attempts, unanswered résumés).
**AVOID WHEN:** Arbitrary rewards substituting long-term for natural reinforcers — behavior maintained that way doesn't generalize; contingencies turning into punishment-flavored surveillance.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Operant theory, natural vs arbitrary reinforcement (Sulzer-Azaroff & Mayer 1991; Premack 1959); Rehm 1977 self-reinforcement tradition; Principle 6.

### [BA:TROUBLESHOOT] Prospective and retrospective barrier work
**Move:** Prospective: before finalizing any assignment ask "what could get in the way?" — list external and internal barriers, prepare plan A/B, have the doer summarize the plan back concretely. Retrospective: on any failed assignment, assess the chain (context, decision point, consequences), then TWEAK — small modification plus persistence, almost never abandonment. Check seven failure points (Table 8.1): not understood; skills deficit (teach first); under-graded; no monitoring; monitoring lacks mood detail; no environmental cue; contingencies/conditioned responses against the plan — incl. secondary gains (disability claims, partner attention for staying in bed) and backfiring context (reschedule so consequences reward, not punish).
**APPLY WHEN:** Every assignment prospectively; every incomplete assignment or worsening week retrospectively — data, not verdict.
**AVOID WHEN:** Replaced by trait judgments ("lazy," "manipulative") — burnout-fueling analysis-killers; barrier-listing ballooning into pessimism rehearsal; framing secondary gain as intentional deception.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Principle 10; BA method canon; authors' clinical distillation (Table 8.1); tweaking after Watson & Tharp.

### [BA:MEASURE] Track outcomes with brief standardized scales
**Move:** Same brief measure at every check-in, graphed: PHQ-9 (9 items, free) or BDI-II; GAD-7 for comorbid anxiety; plus BA-specific BADS (25 items, 0–6, past week — weekly; subscales Activation, Avoidance/Rumination, Work/School Impairment, Social Impairment; reverse-score R-items for the total only). Reflect results back ("sleep looks worse — accurate?"); sustained nondepressed-range scores across several sessions = readiness-to-end signal. <!-- privacy-ok: GAD-7 — публичная шкала из книги-источника, не данные пользователя -->
**APPLY WHEN:** Any structured BA course; deciding whether the plan works or needs reconceptualizing; maintenance checks.
**AVOID WHEN:** Interpreting BADS against cutoffs — none exist, use direction of change; scores replacing functional review of actual life change; assessment stacking that burdens the person.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Validated instruments (PHQ-9 Kroenke et al. 2001; GAD-7 Spitzer et al. 2006; BADS Kanter et al. 2007; short form Manos et al. 2011); universal in BA trials. <!-- privacy-ok: GAD-7 — публичная шкала из книги-источника, не данные пользователя -->

### [BA:PROBSOLVE] Define concretely, then solve
**Move:** (1) Translate the vague complaint ("I can't get anything done") into behavioral terms — timing, frequency, duration, what exactly happens; (2) brainstorm many solutions withholding judgment; (3) weigh pros/cons, pick one; (4) run it for a predetermined window; (5) evaluate, modify or swap. The activity chart is the prime source of recurring problems. Also the exit route from rumination masking a real unsolved problem.
**APPLY WHEN:** A definable external/behavioral problem triggers or maintains low mood; rumination felt like "doing something" about a life problem.
**AVOID WHEN:** The problem is uncontrollable (awaiting medical results — shift to tolerating/soothing/distraction); the definition is still vague — solving before defining fails.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Problem-solving therapy has stand-alone trial literature (D'Zurilla & Nezu; Mynors-Wallis); within BA — method-level; BA solves in vivo rather than teaching steps didactically.

### [BA:MODEL-MAP] Personal case formulation on the BA model
**Move:** Fill the BA Depression Model chart (Fig. 3.1 / App. 1a) with the person's own details: (1) what changed in life → (2) how rewarding/stressful life became → (3) how I react → (4) what I do to cope → (5) what that coping does to the rewards and stressors. Presenting it well is itself validating: "feeling depressed makes sense given your context; it is not your fault." Fold biology into "life events" — internal and external context both count.
**APPLY WHEN:** Starting BA work; re-orienting after a setback; self-blame for symptoms.
**AVOID WHEN:** Presented as fact rather than hypothesis; filled abstractly without concrete events; head-on collision with the person's biological beliefs instead of folding biology into context.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** The model is what the BA trials tested (method-level); the worksheet itself untested.

### [BA:VALUES] From feeling-goals to value-directed behavior
**Move:** Convert "I want to feel better" into behavior-goals and near-term steps ("What will be different when this is done?"); when goals won't come, elicit values across four domains — family, interpersonal relationships, work, leisure — with plain questions ("What has been important to you in your life?", "What kind of person do you want to be?"); use values to pick targets and arbitrate mood-vs-commitment conflicts (depressed Saturday vs promised help → the friendship value decides). Value-consistent activities are likelier naturally reinforced — which makes them doable against the pull to avoid.
**APPLY WHEN:** Choosing activation direction; stuck points, competing goals; demoralization after long episodes; assignments feel pointless.
**AVOID WHEN:** Values treated as morals/"shoulds" or imposed — low-social-desirability values (power, pleasure, solitude) are legitimate; values talk displacing actual activation; expecting fast mood payoff — purpose is the nearer-term return.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Imported from ACT (Hayes 2011; Dahl et al. 2009 — method-level there); authors' clinical integration; values tools from Hopko & Lejuez (2007).

### [BA:RUM-ASSESS] Treat rumination as behavior: chain-analyze the episode
**Move:** Chain-analyze backward: where, doing what, when exactly did looping start, what preceded it (trigger context), what followed (function: felt "working on problems," avoided acute sadness, habit, no competing behavior); then modify the triggering context (drop daytime talk shows; move email out of low-mood windows) and make consequences visible — map the episode to its results (mood down, plan never executed), ask in the moment "Is this way of thinking useful right now?" Target the PROCESS, never the content or validity of thoughts.
**APPLY WHEN:** Activities done but no enjoyment — "going through the motions" (public behavior activated, private behavior ruminating); before choosing among the five counter-rumination strategies (Table 7.1).
**AVOID WHEN:** The analysis becomes another rumination loop — keep it brief and action-bound; extensively narrating rumination content (that is public rumination and can reinforce it); insight alone doesn't unstick — add an active replacement strategy.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Functional-analysis core of the BA canon; method-level trials; consistent with rumination research (Nolen-Hoeksema; brooding predicts worsening — Treynor et al. 2003).

### [BA:2MIN] The 2-minute rule for thinking vs brooding
**Move:** After ~2 minutes on a topic ask: progress toward solving a problem? understood something new? less self-critical/depressed than before? All "no" = ruminating, not thinking — interrupt and switch strategy; companion mnemonic RCA = Rumination Cues Action: catching rumination is itself the cue to start another engaging activity.
**APPLY WHEN:** Unsure whether inward thought is productive reflection or brooding; needing a portable detection→response link.
**AVOID WHEN:** Pathologizing all inward thought — concrete, process-focused reflection is adaptive (abstract-evaluative is the maladaptive form, Watkins 2008); the "action" chosen is itself avoidance of a needed task.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Heuristic from Addis & Martell (2004) self-help companion (that family carries the bibliotherapy RCT — reserve shelf); no direct trial of the rule itself.

### [BA:ATTEXP] Attention to experience / task focus (with distraction fallback)
**Move:** During an activity, repeatedly redirect attention to present sensory input (sights, sounds, smells, touch) or — for decomposable tasks — to the concrete next step; train in tiny doses (60-second drill; a few minutes ~3×/week scheduled into the chart; 10-minute blocks 4×/workday in the work case), expect constant drift, count each noticing-and-return as the rep. Fallback when sensory attention is unavailable or aversive (bedtime rumination in the dark): distraction — ADD an unrelated stimulus or mental task (radio, lyric-effortful song, alphabet game animals A→Z) as a circuit-breaker.
**APPLY WHEN:** Rumination during otherwise well-chosen activities; "present but absent" pattern; performance eroded by looping; distraction variant — when a brief interrupt is needed before other strategies can land.
**AVOID WHEN:** Formal seated meditation for an acutely depressed person — the book de-emphasizes it (high concentration demand, sedentary, solitary; MBCT evidence is relapse prevention in RECOVERED patients, not acute treatment); distraction becoming the standing substitute for solving solvable problems or approaching avoided emotion.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Authors' adaptation (Martell et al. 2001) of mindfulness work; MBCT base (Segal et al. 2013) with the book's own scope caveat; distraction — clinical practice, case-level support.

### [BA:ACTIONURGE] Act against the maintaining action urge (transdiagnostic)
**Move:** Identify the presenting emotion's action urge (flee when anxious, lash out when angry, withdraw when sad), then schedule specific behavior counter to the urge when urge-following demonstrably maintains the problem — approach instead of avoid, kindness instead of hostility (kin to DBT opposite action; Barlow's unified-protocol logic).
**APPLY WHEN:** Non-traumatic anxiety, irritability/anger, shame, low mood where short-term urge-following worsens the long-term pattern.
**AVOID WHEN:** The urge signals a real present threat (short-term avoidance can be sensible); trauma-cued fear — countering that urge is exposure work for a therapist.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Opposite-action logic from DBT (Rizvi & Linehan 2005) and Barlow et al. (2017); the BA extension is the authors' reasoning, not a BA trial. Related: exercise for mood works only when paired with an explicit rationale (Nyström et al. 2017 RCT — same activity without rationale inferior).

### [BA:POSTPLAN] Posttherapy plan, high-risk map, generalization
**Move:** Near the end of an active phase, write a posttherapy plan (App. 3; Fig. 9.1): which contexts raise my vulnerability, which behaviors keep the cycle going, which antidepressant behaviors to maintain, what commitments raise follow-through. Map high-risk situations with three questions (after Wilson 1992): when is this most likely, what can prevent it, how to reduce impact — seeded from precipitants of past episodes (loss of status, criticism, reduced access to effort-based rewards). Mine accumulated charts and the score graph for vulnerability patterns and working counter-strategies; distill into a one-sheet "ingredients of my behavioral antidepressant" handbook. Program durability throughout: practice new behaviors across times/settings/people, fold into routines, optionally enlist family to reinforce antidepressant behavior; after symptom relief, extend activation to larger domains (work, housing, relationships, finances).
**APPLY WHEN:** Symptom relief achieved and an active phase winds down; a foreseeable stressor; a skill that "works" only in one narrow context; periodic self-review.
**AVOID WHEN:** Generic entries ("be more social") without behavior-level specifics; rehearsing every imaginable catastrophe — plan for reasonably likely events only; enlisting network members who contribute to the problem; using big life goals to skip the small-steps phase while still severely depressed.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Authors' clinical practice on the relapse-prevention framework (Marlatt tradition via Wilson 1992); generalization literature (Stokes & Baer 1977); BA trials showed durable gains (method-level), but no trial of these worksheets.

### [BA:ACTIVATE] ACTIVATE as a completeness checklist
**Move:** Check any BA effort against ACTIVATE (App. 4): Assess contributing factors and behavior (charts + ABC); Counter avoidance with structured activation and problem solving; Take time to get specific (which friends, seen how, what counts as "more"); Include monitoring throughout; Validate that current behavior makes sense in context while insisting change requires action; Assign activities (graded, scheduled, naturally reinforced); Troubleshoot barriers before and after; Encourage every sign of progress. Elements in any order — a fluid toolkit, not a sequence. Weekly consolidation: Notebook & Weekly Therapy Plan (App. 2, 11 prompts, explicitly self-help-usable).
**APPLY WHEN:** Structuring self-guided BA work; checking that an effort hasn't silently dropped a component (most commonly monitoring, specificity, or troubleshooting).
**AVOID WHEN:** Applied rigidly as a script; the sheet is written for therapists — Validate/Encourage describe a stance toward oneself when used solo.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Authors' synthesis of the trial-tested treatment (method-level); the acronym itself untested.

### [BA:RISK-ASSESS] Suicide risk assessment
**Move:** Routinely and directly ask a depressed person about suicidal thoughts; if present, distinguish active wish to die from passive escape ideation, and assess plans, intent, access to means, deterrents, and reasons to live.
**APPLY WHEN:** Always at the start of work with depression; whenever outcome measures flag critical items (OQ self-harm items; PHQ-9 item 9).
**AVOID WHEN:** Never skip; never treat passive ideation as trivially safe.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** Clinical standard of care (Bongar 2002; Jobes 2006), not BA-specific; book cites substantial suicide mortality in severe depression (~15% figure) as the rationale.

### [BA:PTSD-ACT] BA for PTSD avoidance (without trauma memory work)
**Move:** Apply activation and avoidance modification to re-engage avoided activities and life areas in PTSD, explicitly without working on traumatic memories.
**APPLY WHEN:** PTSD with broad behavioral avoidance, especially when front-line options (CPT, prolonged exposure) are unavailable, refused, or being sequenced.
**AVOID WHEN:** Presented as an equal-evidence substitute for CPT/PE — the book says BA's PTSD data are less robust; any move toward trauma-memory content exits this protocol.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** Wagner et al. (2019) randomized trial in veterans; Etherton & Farley (2020) meta-analysis of 8 studies (3 controlled) — improvement, no significant difference vs CPT/PE, weaker evidence base.

### [BA:GRIEF-APPROACH] Graded approach to avoided grief
**Move:** When rumination or rigid "coping" activity functions to blunt grief (inhibited grieving), schedule graded activities that approach the emotion — reviewing photos of the deceased, talking through the dying process, reinitiating old ties, doing formerly shared activities — expecting a temporary rise in sadness followed by falling rumination and depressive symptoms.
**APPLY WHEN:** Loss-focused rumination with flattened emotion; ruminating or performing "instead of" grieving; avoidance of loss material maintaining withdrawal.
**AVOID WHEN:** Solo use — this deliberately opens loss material and needs titration and containment; raw acute loss needing stabilization first.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** Authors' clinical experience; supporting theories (Borkovec worry-as-avoidance; Linehan inhibited grieving) explicitly untested in depressed clients per the book itself.

### [BA:COND-CUE] Deconditioning cue-triggered responses
**Move:** When a behavior is a classically conditioned automatic response to a cue (neighborhood tied to a painful breakup triggers overwhelm; a specific bar-and-coworkers combo triggers drinking urges), either reduce the cue's power via desensitization or route plans around the cue. Mapping cues and rerouting is self-doable; the desensitization arm is not.
**APPLY WHEN:** Sudden "irrational" emotional or urge responses tied to specific people/places/situations, especially post-loss or post-trauma.
**AVOID WHEN:** Solo desensitization of trauma-linked cues — that is exposure to traumatic material.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** Classical-conditioning principles; authors' clinical experience; no dedicated trial.

## What the book debunks

- **"Severe depression needs medication; psychotherapy is inadequate" (APA workgroup 2000)** — Seattle data contradict it: BA matched paroxetine acutely even in the severe subgroup, retained patients better; drug withdrawal caused more relapse than completed BA/CT.
- **"Just do it" willpower framing** — rejected; force-yourself does not work with depression; BA grades tasks instead of exhorting.
- **"BA is just doing more activities"** — caricature; "simple but not easy." Reward is idiosyncratic (a promotion can be a net reinforcement LOSS) — never assume, assess.
- **"Activation is fake"** — acting-as-if ≠ pretending to feel; feelings are acknowledged, only their veto is removed.
- **Chemical-imbalance vs behavior dichotomy** — arbitrary: changing action changes biology (behavior-therapy neuroimaging; exercise trials); BA needn't discredit medication.
- **Relief as proof avoidance "works"** — relief IS the negative-reinforcement mechanism that maintains the trap.
- **All-or-nothing change, romanticized pre-depression capacity** — overloaded first weeks and drastic avoidance cuts both fail; clients overestimate early capacity.
- **One-trial verdicts** — a well-chosen activity may pay off only over repetitions.
- **Trait labels ("lazy," "manipulative," "Axis II") kill analysis** — there is a learning- or biology-based explanation for all behavior.
- **"A new job / life fix will cure it"** — reemployment doesn't restore life-satisfaction baseline by itself (Lucas et al. 2004).
- **Rumination is not unitary** — reflection vs brooding; only brooding predicts worsening (Treynor et al. 2003); BA targets the process, dropping content-testing.
- **Over-claiming adjacent evidence** — MBCT is relapse prevention for recovered patients, not acute treatment; BA for anxiety beats inactive controls but NOT established active treatments (Stein et al. 2020); no BA data for maintenance sessions (borrowed from other CBT); BADS has no cutoffs; exercise without a stated rationale underperforms (Nyström et al. 2017).
- **Therapist-side failure modes** — expecting change too quickly, delivering BA mechanically, giving up too soon, persisting too long on a non-responder; unreviewed homework extinguishes compliance.

## Numbers that matter

- Component analysis (Jacobson et al. 1996): ~150 depressed adults; BA alone ≈ BA+restructuring ≈ full CT, acutely and at 2-year follow-up (Gortner et al. 1998).
- Seattle trial (Dimidjian et al. 2006; Dobson et al. 2008): 241 adults; BA ≈ paroxetine acutely (in the severe subgroup BA > CT acutely); relapse protection ≈ CT; paroxetine discontinuation → more relapse.
- Moradveisi et al. 2013 (Iran, n=100, therapists trained 20 h): 13 weeks — BA 91.1% remission vs 68.6% medication; 49 weeks — 65.9% vs 27.9%.
- Richards et al. 2017 (UK): BA noninferior to CT, cheaper, junior workers. HAP India (Patel et al. 2017): 6–8 sessions, lay counselors, clinically and cost-effective. Hopko et al. 2011: ~75% of medically ill depressed improved (BATD and problem solving alike).
- Dose: original BA research ≤24 sessions; other BA models 8–15 sessions (Hopko et al. 2003).
- Instruments: PHQ-9 = 9 items; GAD-7 = 7 items; BADS = 25 items × 0–6, 4 subscales, weekly, NO cutoffs; Q-BAS = 14 items × 0–6 (fidelity, expert-rated only); PES = 320 items (historical Lewinsohn instrument). <!-- privacy-ok: GAD-7 — публичная шкала из книги-источника, не данные пользователя -->
- Rumination dosing: 2-minute rule; 60-second in-session drill; a few minutes ~3×/week initial homework; 10-minute task-focus blocks 4×/workday (case); five counter-rumination strategies (Table 7.1).
- Exercise: ACSM dose 3–5 days/week reduced symptoms vs placebo and lower dose (Dunn et al. 2005); reviews find exercise ≈ medication (Carek 2011; Schuch 2019).
- Structure: 10 core principles; ACTIVATE = 8 components; 4 value domains; monitoring blocks every 3–4 hours; ~15% suicide-mortality figure cited for severe depression (book's claim).

## Blind spots of this book

- **No direct self-help evidence for THIS book** — clinician-facing; the BA family's bibliotherapy RCT belongs to the reserve Addis & Martell line (Moss 2012). Solo application of its moves is an extrapolation the book doesn't test.
- **The therapist is a load-bearing component with no validated solo substitute** — validation, spot-reinforcement, MI for ambivalence, homework review that prevents extinction, boosters; the Appendix 2 notebook is untested standalone.
- **Cognitive content deliberately ignored** — justified by equal-outcome trials, not superiority; cases where belief-level work matters get no guidance beyond "refer to CT."
- **Thin pharmacotherapy interface** — biology folded into "context"; no guidance on combining or sequencing BA with medication.
- **Rumination chapter rests heavily on author clinical method** — the five strategies, 2-minute rule, and RCA have no direct trials; grief-approach theory explicitly untested in depressed clients.
- **Little on who fails BA** — moderators of non-response barely covered; the advice for a non-responder is "don't persist too long," without decision rules.
- **Cultural portability shown at program level (India, Nepal, perinatal US/UK), not at self-guided individual level**; family-involvement defaults vary by culture — flagged but unresolved.
