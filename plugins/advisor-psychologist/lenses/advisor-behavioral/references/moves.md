# advisor-behavioral — moves

Curated move set of the behavioral lens (citation prefix `[BA:]`), deduplicated across three sources: Martell/Dimidjian/Herman-Dunn *Behavioral Activation for Depression* (canon), Abramowitz *Getting Over OCD* (workbook), Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 implementation-intentions meta-analysis (evidence).

Tier is assigned per OPERATION (safety-protocol §3), never per school. Inside the OCD workbook the split is fixed: ch. 1–6 material — understanding the loop, mapping triggers and avoidances, cognitive and acceptance preparation — is SELF; anything that designs-and-launches exposure with rituals withheld (ch. 7+) is REQUIRES-THERAPIST, without exception. Codes are unique within this file; where two sources gave one shortcode to different operations the code carries a source suffix (`TROUBLESHOOT-BA` vs `TROUBLESHOOT-ERP`).

## Formulation and baseline

### [BA:MODEL-MAP] Put the pattern on the context map
**Move:** Fill the behavioral model chart with concrete personal detail: what changed in life → how rewarding or punishing daily life became → how you react → what you do to cope → what that coping does back to the rewards and stressors. Fold biology into "life context" rather than arguing against it. The point is that the state makes sense given the context — it is not a character verdict.
**APPLY WHEN:** starting behavioral work; re-orienting after a setback; self-blame for symptoms themselves. / **AVOID WHEN:** the map is presented as fact instead of a hypothesis to test; filled abstractly with no dated events; used to overwrite the person's own causal beliefs head-on.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** the model is what the trials tested (method-level, MODERATE for the package); the worksheet itself untested (Martell App. 1a).

### [BA:ABC] Functional analysis of one episode
**Move:** Take one recent episode and split it: Antecedent (external event, or private — a memory, a body state), concrete Behavior (public or private, rumination included), Consequences immediate and long-term. Chain them — one consequence becomes the next antecedent. Plain script: what happened, how did you feel, what did you do, what happened then. Then sort behaviors into increase-targets (contact real reward) and decrease-targets (escape maintained by relief).
**APPLY WHEN:** any recurring mood–behavior loop; choosing targets; a plan that failed and needs explaining; busy-looking activity that yields nothing. / **AVOID WHEN:** the formulation gets treated as established fact; behavior is judged good or bad by its appearance rather than its function.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** behavior-analytic foundation (Ferster 1973; Lewinsohn 1974); the trial base rests on this analysis (method-level).

### [BA:ACTIVITY-CHART] Log activity against mood before changing anything
**Move:** Record what you did plus mood and intensity 1–10, close to real time; realistic default is a block every 3–4 hours, not hourly. Log internal behavior (looping, replaying) as an activity. Alternative rating: mastery and pleasure 0–10 anchored to your own life, not to an imagined norm. Review weekly on five questions: which activities track which moods, what the actual emotional range is, whether sleep/meal/work routines are disrupted, where avoidance shows, which behavior is easiest to shift first.
**APPLY WHEN:** baseline at the start of any behavioral effort; testing whether a plan changed anything; flat "it's the same all the time" reports — walk back the last few hours and find the variation. / **AVOID WHEN:** entries so global they erase variability; if monitoring itself feels like a worsening microscope, grade the monitoring down (fewer blocks, activities only) rather than abandoning it.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** monitoring sits inside virtually every trial in the behavioral-activation base (Ekers 2014); mastery/pleasure ratings borrowed from cognitive therapy (Beck 1979).

### [BA:MEASURE] Same brief scale every week, graphed
**Move:** Re-run one short standardized measure at each check-in and plot it: PHQ-9 for mood, GAD-7 where worry is prominent, plus the behavioral-activation scale (25 items, 0–6, past week; subscales activation, avoidance/rumination, work and social impairment) read by direction of change. Reflect the numbers back as a question, not a verdict. Sustained non-elevated scores across several check-ins are a readiness signal, not proof. <!-- privacy-ok: GAD-7 — публичная шкала из книги-источника, не данные пользователя -->
**APPLY WHEN:** any structured self-guided course; deciding whether a plan works or needs reformulating; periodic maintenance checks. / **AVOID WHEN:** reading the activation scale against cutoffs — it has none; letting scores replace a functional look at what actually changed in the week; stacking so many instruments that measurement becomes the work.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** validated instruments (PHQ-9 Kroenke 2001; GAD-7 Spitzer 2006; BADS Kanter 2007); routing gate PHQ-9>9 requires a human in the loop (PG26, MODERATE). <!-- privacy-ok: GAD-7 — публичная шкала из книги-источника, не данные пользователя -->

### [BA:OCD-GRID] Map the intrusion→ritual loop as it actually runs
**Move:** Replay one recent episode end to end — trigger, intrusion, what you felt, what you did, what relief followed, why it seemed to help. Then build the grid: rows are the top recurring intrusions, columns are triggers, feared consequences, avoidances, and rituals — including mini-rituals, mental ones, and asking others for reassurance. Be specific (which room, which road, which phrasing). Where no discrete catastrophe exists, name the feared consequence anyway; intolerance of uncertainty counts as one. Budget a few hours; keep it a living document.
**APPLY WHEN:** repetitive checking/washing/reviewing patterns; presentations that look "purely mental" — hunt for the covert rituals; before any treatment planning. / **AVOID WHEN:** it slides into figuring-out-the-meaning (that is itself the ritual); discomfort at naming an intrusion is a reason to list it, not to skip it.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** functional assessment is the backbone of exposure-based case formulation (method-level); the worksheets are the author's clinic procedure (Abramowitz Step 2).

## The gap between understanding and doing

### [BA:IFTHEN] Put an if-then plan on top of the goal
**Move:** Take a goal you already genuinely want, pick ONE concrete goal-directed response and ONE specific anticipated occasion — a place, time, person, object, or an internal cue such as a felt state — and write it verbatim: "If <situation Y>, then I will <response X>." The moment of action then requires no deliberation, because when/where/how were decided in advance and control was handed to the cue.
**APPLY WHEN:** the complaint is "I understand it but I don't do it"; new or non-routine behavior; short opportunity windows and deadlines; known reluctance at the exact moment of action. / **AVOID WHEN:** the goal itself is weak or ambivalent — the effect vanishes without an activated goal, so commitment work comes first; the behavior is already trivially routine.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** direct and strongest in the lens — meta-analysis k=94, N=8,461, d=.65 [.60, .70] over and above an already-formed goal intention; largest where self-regulation is impaired (clinical samples d=1.10 vs .66).

### [BA:CUESPEC] Quality-gate the plan before adopting it
**Move:** Check both halves before you commit. The cue must be precise (no decision left for the moment itself), viable (you will actually meet it, and the response is performable there), and instrumental (the situation permits the action and the action really advances the goal). Reject "if tomorrow, then eat better"-grade plans and re-specify to the level of "if it is lunchtime at my usual place, then I order X."
**APPLY WHEN:** every plan formation; mandatory re-run whenever a previous plan silently failed. / **AVOID WHEN:** never skipped — skipping this gate IS the documented failure mode; every recorded null result traces to a specification error, not to the technique.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** authors' boundary-condition analysis inside the meta-analysis (conceptual synthesis, not a separate trial).

### [BA:REHEARSE] Rehearse the cue–response link, don't rely on the note
**Move:** Write the if-then plan out twice; on the second pass concentrate deliberately on the LINK between the situation and the action, not on the paper. What carries the effect over days and weeks is the strength of the if→then association, not the existence of a written artifact.
**APPLY WHEN:** plans that must survive until a cue appears days later; goals heavy on remembering; a plan that existed on paper and never fired. / **AVOID WHEN:** substituting a posted reminder for rehearsal — the reminder condition performed barely above no-plan control.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** single experiment (Milne & Sheeran 2002c): 87% acted after link rehearsal vs 40% with a reminder vs 20% control — strong but one study, method-level.

### [BA:SHIELD] Plan against the obstacle, not just for the action
**Move:** Put the anticipated obstacle, temptation, or self-state into the if-part and a protective response into the then-part: ignoring it, a self-instruction ("...then I tell myself: stay calm"), or a restatement of what you are actually pursuing. This defends a pursuit already under way instead of starting one.
**APPLY WHEN:** ongoing efforts wrecked by distraction, craving, an antagonistic habit, provocation, or a predictable bad state. / **AVOID WHEN:** the real obstacle is unknown — then either spell out the focal striving itself or run [BA:CONTRAST] first; a shield aimed at a barrier that is not actually blocking you is wasted.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** direct meta-analytic subsets — suppressing attention responses d=.90 (k=5), behavioral responses d=.54 (k=5), blocking detrimental self-states d=1.10, blocking adverse contexts d=.93.

### [BA:CONTRAST] Find the obstacle worth shielding against
**Move:** Before writing shielding plans, elaborate the wanted future vividly, then the present reality standing in its way; take the obstacles that surface and use them as the if-components of the next [BA:SHIELD] plans. This is a front-end that selects targets — the if-then plan still does the work.
**APPLY WHEN:** it is unclear which barrier actually blocks the goal; plans keep firing but keep missing the real obstruction. / **AVOID WHEN:** the goal is judged infeasible — contrasting binds people only to feasible goals; and never as a replacement for the plan itself.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** method-level (Oettingen 2000; Oettingen et al. 2001), recommended by the authors as a front-end; not tested inside the meta-analysis.

## Activation

### [BA:OUTSIDE-IN] Act from the plan, not from the mood
**Move:** Commit to acting on a written plan at a set time regardless of how you feel, and treat it as an experiment in whether the feeling follows the action rather than preceding it. The feeling is acknowledged; only its veto over the action is removed. Pre-frame the awkwardness: an arm out of a cast moves badly before it moves well.
**APPLY WHEN:** "I'll do it when I feel better / when motivation comes"; activity collapsing in step with mood. / **AVOID WHEN:** it gets heard as bare willpower or as faking cheerfulness — always paired with grading and reinforcement planning, never with exhortation.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** core premise; method-level via full-package trials (Jacobson 1996; Dimidjian 2006), MODERATE for behavioral activation as a standalone component.

### [BA:SCHEDULE] Schedule specifically, below current capacity
**Move:** For each activity fix frequency (starting BELOW pre-episode capacity), a capped duration, written days and times, place, who is present, and what counts as done; pre-decide the weather/obstacle branch; add an environmental cue (object by the door, alarm, the plan itself in sight) and a public commitment (tell someone, report back, do it together). Minimal viable version: one or two activities with a specific time attached.
**APPLY WHEN:** always — every other move in this lens hangs off it; intentions repeatedly dissolving into mood-dependent inaction. / **AVOID WHEN:** scheduling by mood; agreements too vague to restate from memory; loading a week beyond what the person can absorb — an overloaded first week manufactures demoralization.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** in the trial base (Ekers 2014, method-level); specificity effects from goal research (Locke & Latham 1990); planning increment d=.65 (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006).

### [BA:GRADE] Start absurdly small and secure each step
**Move:** Decompose the target into very small ranked steps and secure one before adding the next; start at the easy end where the mood payoff is highest; count partial completion as data to build on. Expect the payoff across repetitions, not on the first attempt — mastery counts even when nothing was enjoyed.
**APPLY WHEN:** overwhelm, freezing in front of a pile of tasks, all-or-nothing behaving, self-chiding "just do it" talk. / **AVOID WHEN:** starting where you believe you SHOULD function instead of at measured baseline; using "partial counts" to never raise the bar; drastic cuts to an avoidance behavior (a five-hour habit will not become thirty minutes — shave in ten-minute increments).
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** graded task assignment from the behavioral/cognitive tradition; method-level via the trial base; case-level illustration only for specific ladders.

### [BA:VALUES] Convert feeling-goals into direction
**Move:** Turn "I want to feel better" into behavior-goals and a near-term step by asking what will be observably different when it is done. Where goals will not come, elicit values across four domains — family, other relationships, work, leisure — with plain questions about what has mattered and what kind of person you want to be. Use values to pick targets and to arbitrate mood-versus-commitment conflicts.
**APPLY WHEN:** choosing a direction for activation; competing goals; assignments that feel pointless. / **AVOID WHEN:** values get treated as moral obligations or imported from outside — low-social-desirability values (solitude, pleasure, power) are legitimate; values talk displacing actual activation; expecting a fast mood payoff.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** imported from acceptance-and-commitment work (Hayes 2011; Dahl 2009 — method-level there); integration into this lens is the authors' clinical reasoning, not a trial result.

### [BA:NATURAL-REINFORCE] Prefer rewards that follow from the act itself
**Move:** Choose activities whose payoff follows logically from doing them; mine personal history with "what did you tend to do when this wasn't happening?" Where the environment will not pay off yet, bridge with engineered consequences: a contingent self-reward, a public commitment, a report-back, doing the low-frequency task before the high-frequency one, pairing a tedious task with pleasant context, sequencing a quick win before a long haul.
**APPLY WHEN:** choosing among candidate activities; early activation being ignored or punished by the environment (unanswered messages, rebuffed attempts). / **AVOID WHEN:** arbitrary rewards permanently substituting for natural ones — behavior maintained that way does not generalize; contingencies drifting into self-surveillance with a punitive flavor.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** operant theory, natural vs arbitrary reinforcement (Premack 1959; Sulzer-Azaroff & Mayer 1991); method-level within the lens.

### [BA:EXPERIMENT] Every assignment is an experiment
**Move:** Predict the outcome, run it, observe what happened to mood and to goal progress; repeat across occasions and vary the activity before judging anything. A failed attempt gets a functional analysis, not a verdict. Portable form: assess whether the behavior works for or against you and against your longer-term direction, choose an approach, try it with a specific plan, integrate it into routine, observe, and keep going.
**APPLY WHEN:** uncertainty about what will actually reinforce this person; an assignment that "didn't work"; demoralization after a flat result. / **AVOID WHEN:** concluding anything from a single trial; promising that a chosen activity will lift mood — no such guarantee exists in this model.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** core stance of the method (method-level via the trial base); the mnemonic packaging is untested.

### [BA:TROUBLESHOOT-BA] Barrier work before and after an assignment
**Move:** Before finalizing any plan, ask what could get in the way, list external and internal barriers, prepare a plan B, and restate the plan concretely in your own words. After any incomplete assignment, walk the chain — context, decision point, consequences — and TWEAK rather than abandon. Seven standard failure points: not understood; a missing skill; graded too steeply; not monitored; monitored without mood detail; no environmental cue; contingencies working against the plan, including payoffs for staying stuck and a context that punishes the very behavior you scheduled.
**APPLY WHEN:** every assignment prospectively; every missed assignment or worse week retrospectively — as data, not as a judgment. / **AVOID WHEN:** replaced by trait labels ("lazy", "manipulative"), which end the analysis; the barrier list ballooning into a rehearsal of pessimism; framing an unintended payoff as deliberate deception.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** authors' clinical distillation on method canon (Martell Table 8.1); non-completion as a design failure is the PG26 normative line (LOW-MODERATE).

## Avoidance and rumination

### [BA:TRAP-TRAC] Catch avoidance by its function and substitute an approach
**Move:** Test for avoidance by function, not appearance: what happens to the fatigue, dread, or hopeless thinking when you retreat? If they briefly ease, negative reinforcement is confirmed. Then name Trigger, Response (the emotional reaction), and Avoidance-Pattern, and get back on track by keeping the same trigger and response but substituting an alternative approach behavior. Validate first: the short-run relief is real, the long-run cost is the problem — no blame in the analysis.
**APPLY WHEN:** "I'm stuck and can't just do it"; withdrawal; subtle avoidance — busy but reward-free activity, numbing, deferring your own needs; self-blame blocking any planning. / **AVOID WHEN:** relief is read as evidence that retreating works — the relief IS the mechanism; the substitute is itself covert avoidance; the trigger is traumatic material, which leaves this protocol entirely.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** method canon (Jacobson 2001); the avoidance-modification component inside the trials; validation-facilitates-change finding (Warwar 2008).

### [BA:RUM-ASSESS] Chain-analyze a looping episode as behavior
**Move:** Work backward through one episode: where you were, what you were doing, when the looping began, what preceded it, and what followed — did it feel like working on a problem, did it push away a sharper feeling, was it simply habit with nothing competing? Then change the triggering context (move the trigger activity out of the vulnerable window) and make the consequences visible by mapping the episode to its actual results. Target the PROCESS, never the content or truth of the thoughts.
**APPLY WHEN:** activities completed with no engagement — going through the motions while looping; before choosing a counter-looping strategy. / **AVOID WHEN:** the analysis becomes another loop — keep it brief and tied to a next action; narrating the content at length, which can reinforce it; expecting insight alone to unstick anything.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** functional-analysis core of the canon (method-level); consistent with the reflection-vs-brooding distinction (Treynor 2003).

### [BA:2MIN] Two-minute test, then switch the activity
**Move:** After roughly two minutes on an inward topic ask three questions: any progress toward solving something, anything newly understood, less self-critical than when you started? Three "no" means looping, not thinking — interrupt. Treat catching the loop as the cue to start another engaging activity: redirect attention to present sensory input or to the concrete next step of a task, in small doses, counting each notice-and-return as one repetition; if sensory attention is unavailable or aversive, add a competing stimulus or mental task as a circuit-breaker.
**APPLY WHEN:** unsure whether reflection is productive; a portable detect-then-respond link is needed; engagement eroded by looping during otherwise well-chosen activity. / **AVOID WHEN:** all inward thinking gets pathologized — concrete, process-focused reflection is adaptive; the chosen "action" is itself the avoidance; a distracting circuit-breaker becomes the standing substitute for solving a solvable problem.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** heuristic from the authors' self-help companion, no direct trial of the rule; the attention component is their adaptation of mindfulness work — note the scope caveat that mindfulness-based relapse work was tested in recovered, not acutely depressed, people.

### [BA:PROBSOLVE] Define behaviorally, then solve on a deadline
**Move:** Translate the vague complaint into behavioral terms — when, how often, how long, what exactly happens; brainstorm many candidate solutions with judgment withheld; weigh costs and benefits and pick one; run it for a predetermined window; then evaluate, modify, or swap. The activity chart is the best source of recurring problems, and this is the exit route when looping is standing in for an unsolved real problem.
**APPLY WHEN:** a definable external problem triggers or maintains the state; looping that feels like "dealing with" a life problem. / **AVOID WHEN:** the problem is genuinely uncontrollable (awaiting a result) — shift to tolerating and soothing instead; the definition is still vague, since solving before defining reliably fails.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** problem-solving therapy has its own trial literature (D'Zurilla & Nezu; Mynors-Wallis); within this lens it is method-level and delivered in vivo rather than taught as steps.

## Intrusions and rituals — understanding and planning only

### [BA:UNIVLIST] Normalize the content against the universal list
**Move:** Read through the documented list of intrusions reported by people with no ritual problem — harm, contamination, inappropriate acts, morality and religion, sexual, symmetry — and match your own to those categories. The content is ordinary mental noise; what differs is frequency, distress, the importance attributed to it, and how hard it is fought.
**APPLY WHEN:** shame, or the appraisal "this thought means I am dangerous / a pervert / not who I thought"; the belief that nobody else has such thoughts. / **AVOID WHEN:** the list gets re-read to re-establish certainty of being normal — that reconstructs the compulsion in a new place.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** population studies of intrusive thoughts since the 1970s (Rachman & de Silva line); intrusions reported by 90–99% of people.

### [BA:EVIDENCE] Weigh the belief, aiming at acceptably low, never at certain
**Move:** Name the mistaken belief behind the fear, then run the standard questions: what does past experience say, is a memorable event being over-weighted, when did the feared outcome fail to happen, is this probability or mere possibility, is this a feeling or a circumstance. Weigh the evidence for the old belief and for an alternative, and write a realistic belief that supports acting under residual risk. Variants: write the advice you would give a friend with the identical fear and then apply it to yourself; hunt within-self inconsistencies (risks you accept daily without ritual); for feared harm you feel responsible for, list every other contributing factor, assign percentages summing to 100, and re-estimate your own slice.
**APPLY WHEN:** any cognitive-error type; preparation before a therapist-agreed exposure plan; demands for an absolute guarantee before acting. / **AVOID WHEN:** it slides into reassurance — the target is acceptably low probability, never proof the fear is false; the worksheet gets recomputed until a "safe" number appears, which is the ritual wearing a cognitive costume.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** core cognitive technique, method-level in the cognitive-for-rituals tradition (Wilhelm, Freeston, Steketee, Whittal, D. A. Clark); the specific worksheets are the author's clinic procedure.

### [BA:THOUGHT-EXP] Test the power of thoughts by experiment
**Move:** Three short experiments. Power of thoughts: deliberately wish a trivial mishap on an unaware person, hold the thought all day, check what happened. Predictions: generate deliberate premonitions and log hits against misses to expose how memorable hits distort the count. Suppression: try for thirty seconds not to think of one specific image and tick every intrusion — then generalize, since suppression fails and intensifies, which makes the opposite the workable strategy.
**APPLY WHEN:** thoughts read as morally equivalent to acts; magical causality; the belief that thoughts can and should be controlled. / **AVOID WHEN:** the chosen target is serious harm — use a positive event instead; the logging becomes a checking ritual.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** suppression rebound replicated across many studies (Wegner line); standard cognitive behavioral experiments (method-level).

### [BA:ACT-DEFUSE] Rehearse openness metaphors outside the practice
**Move:** Rehearse the acceptance metaphor set daily and away from any exposure practice — the rigged tug-of-war you can simply stop pulling, the ditch that digging enlarges, quicksand, the swamp crossed for what is beyond it, unwelcome visitors at the door, the passengers who shout while you keep driving toward what matters, thoughts placed on passing leaves. Write out how each maps onto your own rituals, and deploy them to make room for anxiety rather than to end it.
**APPLY WHEN:** explaining to yourself why control strategies failed; preparing for the possibility of resisting a ritual; entanglement between practices. / **AVOID WHEN:** metaphors get recited as anxiety-suppression mantras, or openness is adopted as a smarter control tactic — "I'll accept it so it goes away" reinstalls the loop.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** acceptance-and-commitment components (Hayes) integrated into exposure work; method-level for the components, author's adaptation for the packaging.

### [BA:READINESS] Ambivalence work and an explicit go/no-go gate
**Move:** Do not start until reasons-for outweigh reasons-against, and make that explicit. Package: a workability check (has controlling the intrusions worked long-term, has it reduced them, has it opened life?); a payoff audit contrasting rituals that relieve for minutes with rituals that keep you free for months — the second list stays empty, which indicts the strategy class, not your effort; a readiness survey; a pros-and-cons ledger; a cost inventory including who else carries your rituals; a projection of life without them; and a short-term-cost versus long-term-benefit chart. Close on four questions: motivated, willing to change the relationship to anxiety rather than control it, competing stressors parked, daily practice time actually blocked.
**APPLY WHEN:** any hesitation; the cure sounding worse than the problem; symptoms feeling cheap because others absorb them. / **AVOID WHEN:** the person is unambivalently ready (skip it); motivation is built out of guilt or shame — explicitly banned; anyone is being argued into exposure.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** motivational-interviewing-style practice; the survey is adapted from a stages-of-change instrument with no target-specific validation.

### [BA:EXP-MENUS] Draft the candidate exposure menus — as material for the session
**Move:** From the mapping worksheets draft two lists: 10–20 representative feared or avoided situations, written specifically (place, circumstances, who is present), each rated 0–100 for how anxious it would be WITHOUT ritualizing; and 5–10 intrusions or feared consequences rated by the distress of letting the thought sit. Pure uncertainty can be an item. The worst and most shameful fears belong on the list even if unthinkable now — writing them down adds nothing that is not already intruding. Separate lists per theme are fine.
**APPLY WHEN:** the loop has been mapped and a therapist-guided program is being prepared; there is an existing agreed program and the list needs extending with missed avoidances. / **AVOID WHEN:** anything on the list gets attempted from this step — ordering, difficulty design, and every escalation are therapist operations, and a failed solo exposure strengthens the fear; sanitized or over-general items; a helper who drifts into reassurance while reviewing it.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** hierarchy construction is standard in the method base (method-level); the design-and-escalate operation is fenced to the therapist by PG26 (MODERATE) — this move produces a draft, not a plan of action.

### [BA:RIT-MON] Baseline the rituals; write the targets; change nothing yet
**Move:** For every ritual log the date, start time, triggering situation or thought, distress rating, a description, and the duration timed rather than guessed; fill it in immediately and review at night. Collect one to two days of baseline. In parallel write out every ritual that would eventually be targeted — explicitly including the ones that feel realistic or necessary, and the ones that have decoupled from any obsession and now run as fixed daily habits. Stop nothing at this stage; monitoring alone is reactive and usually reveals far more ritualizing than expected.
**APPLY WHEN:** automatic or underestimated rituals; preparing the material a clinician will need. / **AVOID WHEN:** read as an instruction to quit cold now — unplanned abrupt stopping is precisely what the protocol defers; the log itself turns into counting or checking.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** the author's clinical protocol; monitoring reactivity is his clinical observation, not a trial finding.

## Maintenance and relapse

### [BA:RELAPSE-WATCH] Standing lapse watch with a calibrated response
**Move:** Separate a lapse — a temporary rise in avoidance or rituals after real progress, which is a matter of when, not if — from a relapse, where lapses become the rule. Re-run the rating forms every few months and after stress. Pre-list high-risk situations including positive changes (a move, a new job, a new baby) and coincidences that echo the old theme. Know the warning signs: less openness, rising checking or reassurance, irritability, routines being interfered with. Meet a lapse with a plain coping statement; the panic and self-criticism are what add the load. Fill reclaimed hours with rewarding activity.
**APPLY WHEN:** from the beginning of maintenance, indefinitely; after a first slip. / **AVOID WHEN:** the monitoring drifts into obsessive self-scrutiny; the chosen filler activities double as reassurance sources; an absolute standard ("never again") is set, since one slip then flips the whole plan.
**Tier:** SELF / **Evidence:** relapse-prevention framework including the abstinence-violation-effect concept (method-level) plus the author's sign list and cases.

### [BA:MAINT-TAPER] Taper the practice instead of stopping it
**Move:** Never stop like a diet. Taper planned practice — every other day, then twice weekly, then weekly, then unscheduled after several good months — and keep informal practice permanently in new settings. Write about five standing mini-rules and post them where they are seen, without distress bookkeeping. Replace response prevention with normal-behavior rules decided by the necessity test (why am I doing this; do other people take it for granted): bounded washing and one short shower a day; a single visual check, never delegated, never turning back; reassurance once per topic and none from the internet; each rite performed once. Any action performed to obtain certainty or remove a thought is still a ritual, whatever it looks like.
**APPLY WHEN:** an active therapist-led program has been completed and is winding down; symptoms interfering again, which means re-escalating frequency with the clinician. / **AVOID WHEN:** a cliff-edge stop as soon as things feel better; routine inspection turning into poring over; standing mini-rules adopted without a completed program behind them.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST / **Evidence:** the author's maintenance protocol and clinic norms; overlearning and variation rationale from method-level exposure research; no direct trial.

## Therapist-tier operations — route, do not run

### [BA:SITEXP] Planned situational exposure under safety-learning rules
**Move:** Deliberate, prolonged confrontation with a menu item while rituals are withheld — roughly twice daily, an hour or more, at least a week per item, inside a supervised program. Rules that define it: match the real trigger closely; write and test a specific prediction (for untestable or distant fears the prediction becomes whether you can function with the uncertainty now); continue until something is learned; practise openness rather than anxiety reduction; response prevention throughout; no distraction; maximize surprise by not pre-reassuring; vary intensity, order, and setting; record distress periodically; debrief in writing what surprised you.
**APPLY WHEN:** menus drafted, cognitive and acceptance preparation done, response-prevention plan agreed — with a clinician holding the hierarchy. / **AVOID WHEN:** launched solo; accidental brief contact mistaken for exposure; under substances that block the learning (alcohol, cannabis, benzodiazepine-type medication; SSRIs are compatible); safety behaviors or reassurance still running; a fall in distress used as the success criterion.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST / **Evidence:** the method base is decades of trials and is the treatment-specific answer for ritual patterns; variability and expectancy-violation refinements come from newer inhibitory-learning work; no trial of the workbook itself.

### [BA:IMEXP] Imaginal exposure: script, loop, pairing
**Move:** A one-to-three-minute script read aloud that matches the actual intrusion and never exceeds it, starting abruptly, centred on personal responsibility and on uncertainty, ending unresolved, with no rituals, no reassurance, and no commentary that it is senseless. Recorded plainly, played on a loop while welcoming the scene, until a prediction has been tested; loss of charge is the marker. Repeated daily for about a week across settings, and chained after the matching situational practice where a real trigger exists.
**APPLY WHEN:** fears that cannot or must not be staged in reality; doubts surfacing mid-programme; attention drifting off the actual fear during practice. / **AVOID WHEN:** the script is sanitized or padded with reassurance; the recording is made while crying or joking; listening becomes a reassurance check; run without response prevention; run solo.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST / **Evidence:** method-level exposure trials; the script rules and worksheet are the author's clinical protocol.

### [BA:RP-COLDMOD] Response prevention: default full stop, graded fallbacks
**Move:** Default is zero rituals from the first exposure onward; an unavoidable slip is answered by immediately re-exposing to the trigger or a mastered item, and mini-rituals (a wipe, a pocket check) are undone or re-exposed so they lose their function. If a full stop repeatedly fails, grade it while preserving the unfinished feeling: perform the ritual wrongly, restrict it with a timer on a descending schedule, or delay it in increments while re-engaging with something else. Domain rules follow the ritual class — staying contaminated rather than washing, taping over checked objects and dropping all reassurance queries, leaving things deliberately out of order, and containing an entrenched expert-reassurance loop to one pre-planned short meeting with broad written questions.
**APPLY WHEN:** entry strategy for a ritual class once a supervised programme has begun; rule-bound, lengthy, or seemingly irresistible rituals. / **AVOID WHEN:** slips are silently ignored, which undoes the exposure; a modification stabilizes into a new tolerable ritual; the delay is spent white-knuckling on the urge; sequencing is used to postpone the hardest rituals indefinitely; applied outside an active programme.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST / **Evidence:** method-level trials, with stricter ritual-stopping associated with better outcomes; the modified-cold-turkey framing and domain guidelines are the author's clinic protocol.

### [BA:PROGRAM] The integrated schedule and advancement criteria
**Move:** A staged programme: a first week of cognitive practice around three quarters of an hour daily; a second week continuing that, reading the whole execution section before touching any exposure, revising the menus, filling a weekly practice schedule that pairs one situational item with its matching imaginal item and the rituals that item will provoke, and starting ritual monitoring; from the third week, two hour-long practices daily opened by a short cognitive and acceptance warm-up, response prevention around the clock, an end-of-day review, one menu item per week, and treating unplanned triggers as practice. Advance on six criteria: no longer expecting the harm, able to have the anxiety without fighting it, residual uncertainty acceptable, functioning while exposed, distress consistently low, ritual urges substantially reduced. Whole programme five to ten-plus weeks.
**APPLY WHEN:** preparation is complete and a clinician is converting plans into a schedule. / **AVOID WHEN:** launched solo; the preparation weeks are skipped; anxiety reduction alone is used as the advancement criterion; the sequence is reshuffled to dodge the hardest items.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST / **Evidence:** mirrors the author's clinic protocol (about fifteen 90-minute sessions plus two or more hours of daily homework); parameters are clinic convention with no dismantling data.

### [BA:TROUBLESHOOT-ERP] Diagnose flat, overwhelming, or mutating practice
**Move:** No anxiety during practice means suspect a flawed exposure before concluding recovery — audit for precautions, control tactics, and covert safety manoeuvres such as pre-warning someone, then re-run without them. Overwhelm calls for a stepping-stone item, a temporary modification, and techniques for making room for the anxiety, ranked and tried in that order, with a legitimate pause when life stressors have genuinely stacked. Unnoticed rituals get a fresh functional analysis, mechanical counting, spoiling mid-stream, and progressive delay. New rituals appearing as old ones stop must be diagnosed: previously invisible rituals surfacing is progress and gets folded in, while invented substitutes mean the plan was too narrow and must be extended to all escape behaviors.
**APPLY WHEN:** distress suspiciously low, practices repeatedly aborted, symptom whack-a-mole, suspiciously fast recovery — inside an active supervised programme. / **AVOID WHEN:** low anxiety genuinely reflects a resolved fear; modification becomes a standing excuse; the monitoring itself turns compulsive.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST / **Evidence:** author's clinical protocol resting on the method-level rationale; case illustrations only.

### [BA:FAM-END] Dismantle accommodation without turning anyone into police
**Move:** Identify each person's role in the rituals, avoidance, and reassurance; explain that anxiety itself is not dangerous; agree scripted replies so requests for reassurance can be declined warmly and consistently. Helpers act as consultants on request. Accommodation and reassurance are the same ritual performed by proxy, which is why the arrangement keeps the pattern cheap for the person who has it.
**APPLY WHEN:** relatives check, clean, avoid, or reassure on the person's behalf; life has been rearranged around the symptoms. / **AVOID WHEN:** it becomes policing — never physically preventing rituals, arguing, threatening, or name-calling, since the added strain worsens the pattern; the decision to ritualize stays with the person.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST / **Evidence:** accommodation-reduction rationale consistent with the model; the procedure is the author's clinical protocol.

### [BA:GRIEF-APPROACH] Graded approach to avoided loss material
**Move:** Where looping or rigid activity functions to keep loss material at a distance, schedule graded activities that approach it — reviewing photographs, talking through what happened, re-initiating old ties, doing things once done together — with the explicit expectation that sadness rises before looping and withdrawal fall. Entry is gated by measurement first, not by theme.
**APPLY WHEN:** loss-focused looping with flattened feeling, and prolonged-grief measures plus a mood measure are elevated — the measurement step itself is self-administrable. / **AVOID WHEN:** used solo in any form — no solo format for this exists; scores are unremarkable, since for uncomplicated grief these protocols show near-zero effect and carry potential for harm; raw recent loss needing stabilization first.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST / **Evidence:** authors' clinical experience with supporting theories the book itself calls untested in this population; PG26 gate: measure first, therapist-led loss exposure only, no brand superiority (Hao 2024).

### [BA:RISK-ASSESS] Direct risk assessment and the crisis circuit
**Move:** Ask directly and routinely about suicidal thoughts. If present, distinguish an active wish to die from passive escape thinking, and assess plans, intent, access to means, deterrents, and reasons to live. The trigger for the circuit is suspicion, not stated intent, and it does not reopen if the person walks the statement back.
**APPLY WHEN:** always at the start of work in this territory; whenever a measure flags a critical item. / **AVOID WHEN:** never skipped; passive thinking treated as trivially safe; assessment continued inside the self-help frame instead of routing to a person.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST / **Evidence:** clinical standard of care (Bongar 2002; Jobes 2006), not specific to this lens; crisis routing is the mandatory separate circuit (PG26 safety block).
