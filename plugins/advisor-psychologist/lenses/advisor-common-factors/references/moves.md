# advisor-common-factors — moves

Curated move set for the common-factors lens (citation prefix `[CF:]`), distilled from the
motivational-interviewing canon (Miller & Rollnick, 4th ed., 2023). Not therapy: this sits
next to a live therapist as structured self-help and psychoeducation.
Tier is assigned per OPERATION per safety-protocol §3, not per school. Codes are unique;
cross-references to a code inside another entry are intentional.

## 1. Stance and the standing failure mode

### [CF:GUIDE] Take the guiding stance on purpose
**Move:** Name which of the three styles you are using — directing, following, guiding — and default to guiding: listen first, offer direction only where it fits, keep beginner's-mind curiosity, hold back the urge to solve, stay the calm centre when anger points at you.
**APPLY WHEN:** any conversation about a change that is someone else's to make; you feel the pull to tell them what to do.
**AVOID WHEN:** acute risk, where directing is the correct style; they explicitly asked for instruction; you would fake calm while denying what you feel — the phoniness is detected.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Method-level only — the guiding style is the shared stance across the MI trial base, never isolated in a trial of its own.
### [CF:FIXREFLEX] Catch the fixing reflex and audit the four traps
**Move:** Notice the urge to convince, warn or prescribe; when you hear "don't you see…" or "yes, but…", stop arguing and ask instead of telling. Audit yourself against the four traps — expert (solving for them), persuasion (you take the pro-change side), time (rushing, which slows everything), wandering (following with no direction) — and build one replacement response.
**APPLY WHEN:** you feel urgency about what another person should do; they counterargue you; the exchange left them defensive or you did most of the talking.
**AVOID WHEN:** genuine emergencies where directive action is the job; the audit turns into self-flagellation rather than one concrete alternative.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** MI process research on helper→client language plus the authors' training experience; the traps themselves are taxonomy, not a measured construct.
### [CF:LAYDOWN] Keep responsibility for your conduct, drop it for the outcome
**Move:** Restate your job before the conversation: create conditions under which change becomes possible, not produce the change. Nobody can force another person's choice, and carrying that assumption both burns you out and reads as coercive intent. Pair with setting aside what you already "know" about them.
**APPLY WHEN:** you catch the thought "I am doing everything and they still will not change"; you notice yourself rehearsing remedies while they are still speaking.
**AVOID WHEN:** it becomes an excuse for passivity or lets you skip a real duty of care; risk assessment cannot be postponed this way.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Authors' experience plus early studies linking the mindset shift to lower burnout; no outcome trial.

## 2. Engaging and listening

### [CF:RAPID-ENGAGE] Spend the first fifth of the time engaging only
**Move:** For roughly 20% of the available time do nothing but engage — one open opener, then listening and reflection, no investigative questions, no problem-hunting, notes written afterwards. Under time pressure this makes the remainder faster; all four tasks can fit inside a 5–10 minute contact.
**APPLY WHEN:** the opening of any consultation or hard conversation, especially when it feels like there is no time for it.
**AVOID WHEN:** it extends into following-only for the whole conversation (the wandering trap); triage that genuinely needs fast information first.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Authors' training experience; the surrounding claim — alliance is among the most consistent outcome predictors across orientations — is correlational (see core.md Tier C).
### [CF:MIRROR] Reflect as a statement, not as a question
**Move:** Strip the question markers and let the voice fall at the end. A simple reflection stays close to what was said; a complex one guesses at the meaning underneath. Keep the reflection no longer than their utterance and shift simple→complex as understanding builds. A wrong guess costs nothing — being corrected teaches as much as being confirmed.
**APPLY WHEN:** any stage, any moment you want the person to keep exploring and to hear themselves; whenever you were about to ask yet another question.
**AVOID WHEN:** it degrades into parroting or light paraphrase; the voice rises at the end, which reads as doubting their right to feel that; you are performing technique with no real intent to understand.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Method-level core skill, operationalized in the MISC/MITI coding systems; the empathy→outcome link is correlational but stable.
### [CF:OPENQ] Open questions, roughly two reflections per question
**Move:** Ask questions that leave room to choose what to say ("in what ways does this matter to you?"), hold about two reflections per question and never fewer than one, and speak less than half of the time.
**APPLY WHEN:** opening a conversation, building momentum, or learning how the person frames their own situation.
**AVOID WHEN:** you are running a chain of closed intake questions — they make the person passive and imply your questions will produce the answer; closed chains read as cross-examination.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Method-level (OARS; question and reflection counts are MITI categories); the 2:1 ratio is an author guideline, not a tested threshold.
### [CF:AFFIRM] Affirm something that is actually true
**Move:** Comment appreciatively on something real. A simple affirmation names a specific act ("you saw the warning signs and acted"); a complex one names an enduring strength. Leave "I" out of it. When you notice yourself turning into a deficit detective, switch deliberately to looking for strengths.
**APPLY WHEN:** defensiveness is present — affirmation lowers it and raises openness to threatening information; a strength is visible and has gone unremarked.
**AVOID WHEN:** it would be insincere or is already overused; you would be affirming content that leads somewhere worse, since people produce more of whatever gets affirmed; it is premature reassurance.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Method-level — positive regard sits among the skills distinguishing more effective therapists, and affirmation raises change talk in process studies.
### [CF:INTENSITY-DIAL] Choose the intensity of a reflection deliberately
**Move:** Reflect one notch below or above what you heard. Understating ("a little annoyed" for "furious") invites elaboration; overstating — the amplified reflection — states an absolute claim fully enough that the person walks it back themselves. Track which way they correct you and adjust.
**APPLY WHEN:** you want more elaboration (understate); an all-or-nothing claim would soften if it were said out loud in full (overstate).
**AVOID WHEN:** any sarcasm is present — amplification collapses into mockery instantly; rapport is thin; the person is flooded; you are aiming at neutrality, since intensity is directional.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Authors' clinical experience inside a method-level base; no trial isolates over- or understating.

## 3. Focusing: deciding what is actually being worked on

### [CF:AGENDA-MAP] Lay the agenda out and treat the focus as renegotiable
**Move:** Put candidate topics out as a menu — spoken, or as a sheet of circles with at least one blank "?" (Fig. 5.1 uses 12 diabetes topics plus a blank) — and let the person pick two or three. Then keep re-clarifying: hold the agreed horizon, attend to the immediate concern, steer back, and let a genuinely new horizon replace the old one.
**APPLY WHEN:** several change targets compete; a broad new topic has just landed; time is short; the conversation keeps drifting.
**AVOID WHEN:** the bubbles are pre-filled with your priorities — a fake menu costs trust; they have already stated a specific goal; no shared focus exists yet, so steering is imposition rather than guiding.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Rollnick's primary-care development work from the early 1990s; the bubble sheet comes from Steinberg & Miller (2015). Procedure, not trial.
### [CF:VALUES] Explore values as the person's own frame of reference
**Move:** Ask open questions about guiding values and follow each answer with a reflection rather than another question: what matters most, how they hope life differs in a few years, the rules they live by, five values in one word each, what a close friend would say they live for. The structured version is the Values Card Sort (Box 9.1, 100 public-domain items): sort into five piles, allow blanks, rank the top 5 or 10, then elaborate each one.
**APPLY WHEN:** at the engaging stage; the goal is unclear; motivation appears absent; you need leverage that belongs to them rather than to you.
**AVOID WHEN:** you fire questions without reflections; you score the card sort as if it were an instrument; you stop at slogan labels; you confuse understanding a value with endorsing it.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Method-level MI research plus the self-affirmation literature; no trial of the card sort itself.
### [CF:VALUE-GAP] Let them notice the gap between value and behaviour
**Move:** Put a stated long-term value and the current behaviour into one frame with curiosity rather than challenge — for each top value ask whether the behaviour helps it, is neutral, or works against it — and leave the discrepancy for them to see. Do not announce it on their behalf.
**APPLY WHEN:** values are already articulated in their own words and ambivalence is live, or you are trying to develop ambivalence where there is none.
**AVOID WHEN:** it becomes a gotcha designed to prove hypocrisy — that converts discrepancy into discord and is the main documented misuse of values work.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Discrepancy research cited by the authors; one single-session values-based MI trial cut alcohol use at 3 and 6 months, mediated by self-esteem.
### [CF:WILLING-STEP] Ask what they are willing to do, not what they refuse
**Move:** Stop cataloguing refusals. Start from a broad life goal the person actually holds — health, staying out of trouble, a relationship that works — list steps toward it, and pick the first one they would genuinely try. Keep their interest at the centre rather than an institution's, and state your own hopes openly without imposing them.
**APPLY WHEN:** entry is mandated or pressured (courts, probation, family); you and they want different things; they are described as "unmotivated", which usually means argued at.
**AVOID WHEN:** it is a disguised route back to your agenda — relabelling an institutional goal as "theirs" is the named misuse; no part of them wants the change at all, in which case go to [CF:SEEDFRAME].
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Method-level MI trials in addiction and mandated settings; matching data suggest larger benefit at lower initial motivation or higher anger.

## 4. Evoking: whose voice says the reasons

### [CF:EAR] Train the ear for change talk, including hedged and embedded
**Move:** Attend to, recognize and remember every fragment of change talk, always relative to the current focus, sorted by type: preparatory (desire, ability, reasons, need) and mobilizing (commitment, activation, taking steps). Mark fragments buried inside a mostly-sustain-talk paragraph, and count hedged forms — "maybe", "I guess" — as real. Self-use: label your own journal entry sentence by sentence and count what is there.
**APPLY WHEN:** always, once a focus exists; when a stance feels uniformly stuck; before concluding that you are "not ready".
**AVOID WHEN:** you count talk about a different goal as change talk for this one; the target is grief or trauma rather than a behaviour choice — coding then substitutes for feeling.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Method-level — change-talk coding is the measurement backbone of forty years of MI process research.
### [CF:DIRQ] Ask questions whose natural answer is change talk
**Move:** Build questions directly from the preparatory categories: why would you want this, how could you succeed if you decided, what are the best reasons for you, how important is it. The mechanism is that they hear their own answer, not that you hear it.
**APPLY WHEN:** the focus is set and you are evoking the why; you want depth and momentum rather than more balance.
**AVOID WHEN:** the phrasing invites the opposite ("why haven't you changed?", "what stops you?") — too early for obstacles while they are still weighing whether; no direction has been agreed; you hold a conflict of interest, which makes the same question manipulation.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Method-level trials — evoking skills raise the change-talk ratio, and the ratio predicts subsequent change.
### [CF:RULER] Importance and confidence rulers, asked upward
**Move:** Ask two separate 0–10 questions: how important is this, and how confident are you that you could do it if you decided. Then the follow-up that does the work: "why a 4 and not a 0?" — the answer is change talk. Optionally "what would move it one point up?"
**APPLY WHEN:** you need to tell a motivation problem apart from a self-efficacy problem; as step 3 of brief action planning ([CF:BAP]).
**AVOID WHEN:** you ask "why not an 8?", which elicits the opposite; the ruler becomes a ritual; you treat the number as a measurement rather than a prompt for speech.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Standard across MI and MET protocols; the upward direction of the follow-up comes from change-talk research, not from a trial of the ruler.
### [CF:OARS-CT] When change talk appears, do something with it
**Move:** Decide in advance what you will do the moment change talk shows up, then do it: ask for elaboration or a concrete example, affirm the strength it implies, reflect it, summarize it later — and ask "what else?" until the theme runs out. Then watch how they respond to your response.
**APPLY WHEN:** any change talk or confidence talk surfaces, including the first thin reason.
**AVOID WHEN:** it turns into interrogation; fatigue or discord appears, in which case reflect instead; the content being affirmed leads somewhere worse; the repetition becomes audibly mechanical.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Method-level — the helper's response to change talk influences its later frequency; walking past offered change talk is the commonest trainee failure.
### [CF:DIRREF] Reflect selectively toward the change side
**Move:** In a mixed statement ("I don't want to quit… I know I should… it's hard"), mirror the change strand back. What you reflect is what the next utterance continues. Every candidate must be a legitimate reflection of something actually said — this is selection, not distortion.
**APPLY WHEN:** change talk and sustain talk are tangled together and a direction has been agreed.
**AVOID WHEN:** deliberate neutrality is the aim; the reflection distorts what was said; you reflect sustain talk by default — legitimate early for engagement, corrosive as a standing habit.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Method-level sequential studies — what gets reflected shapes subsequent change and sustain talk.
### [CF:DOUBLESIDE] Double-sided reflection, change side last, joined by "and"
**Move:** Hold both horns in one sentence: sustain talk first, their own change talk last, connected by "and" or "at the same time". "But" cancels whatever precedes it, and people elaborate whatever was said last. A softer variant opens with "there's a part of you that…".
**APPLY WHEN:** both sides have been voiced; single-sided reflections keep producing "yes, but"; you want the whole picture held without taking a side.
**AVOID WHEN:** the change side is your invention rather than theirs; the decision is already made and needs consolidating; it becomes a trick for burying the sustain side, which is heard as manipulation.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Method-level research on change/sustain-talk sequencing; the ordering effect is illustrated by the authors, not measured.
### [CF:BOUQUET] Collect their change talk, hand it back, then ask the key question
**Move:** Gather the accumulated change talk — without the sustain-talk weeds — into one summary so they hear their own arguments a third time; two or three statements make a first posy. Use mini-summaries mid-conversation ("so far you've mentioned X and Y — what else?") and an end summary that invites correction. Then ask what they think they will do.
**APPLY WHEN:** change talk has accumulated; at transitions and at the close; readiness signs are present but the shift to planning has not been made explicit.
**AVOID WHEN:** you are in prosecutor mode assembling admissions; the summary ends on the status quo, since the last item lingers; the summary is all problems, which demoralizes; you ask "are you ready?" head-on.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Method-level change-talk research plus OARS trials; the four contrasting summaries of one interview are an author demonstration.
### [CF:SEEDFRAME] With no change talk at all, switch the goal to developing ambivalence
**Move:** Stop evoking motivation that is not there. Change the aim to creating ambivalence over weeks or months while protecting the relationship: keep engaging, hold off on any plan, and treat one raised doubt as a complete success. Motivation is not a fixed internal quantity — it is generated inside a relationship and is specific to one concrete action.
**APPLY WHEN:** repeated evocation returns only sustain talk; the person has not considered the change at all, or has given up on it.
**AVOID WHEN:** they are in fact ambivalent and simply have not been listened to yet — most people who look unmotivated are defending an ambivalence against people who lecture them.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Method-level MI trials plus the authors' experience; no trial of seed-planting as an isolated procedure.

## 5. Information, advice and autonomy

### [CF:ASK-PERMISSION] Gate every piece of advice on permission
**Move:** Before offering information, an opinion or a suggestion, secure one of three conditions: they asked; you asked and they agreed; or you prefaced it by emphasizing their freedom to discount it. Accept a no. Without one of the three, the behaviour is coded as inconsistent with the method.
**APPLY WHEN:** you hold relevant knowledge and no request has been made; any power gradient is present (clinician/patient, teacher/student, parent/adult child).
**AVOID WHEN:** you will say it regardless — permission as a ritual prefix to pre-decided persuasion is the named misuse; safety-critical information that cannot wait.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** MITI coding — persuasion without permission and confrontation are the two explicitly MI-inconsistent behaviours; supported by the reactance literature.
### [CF:AOA] Ask–offer–ask around every piece of information
**Move:** Ask first (permission, plus what they already know, want to know or have tried), offer one small chunk framed by their answer, then ask again for their reaction and what it means for them going forward. Repeat in cycles. Give just enough to make it intelligible and then stop, instead of finishing the lecture you already have in your head.
**APPLY WHEN:** any psychoeducation, feedback, test result or advice; whenever you feel the pull to explain, warn or educate about a risk.
**AVOID WHEN:** a full emergency instruction is needed; the chunking turns into a quiz or an interrogation; you are walking them to a pre-scripted conclusion; you skip the closing ask, which turns the whole thing back into lecturing.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Method-level trials on information exchange plus group-MI data; the prenatal-clinic case is an author demonstration, not an experiment.
### [CF:AUTONOMY-EMPHASIS] Say the true thing: the choice is theirs
**Move:** Hand the decision back explicitly — it is up to you, nobody can decide this for you — and preface content with autonomy language ("you may already know this", "I wonder what you'll make of it"). If you must speak unbidden: you don't have to agree, and that's fine, but I wouldn't feel right saying nothing. Drill it by saying "it's your choice" warm, flat, cynical and threatening, and hearing the difference.
**APPLY WHEN:** any advice-giving, especially where you hold authority or a stake; the person bristles at pressure; they are under real constraint and still have a choice inside it.
**AVOID WHEN:** it is a fig leaf while the tone coerces — the mismatch is heard; it lets you dodge honesty about consequences; you would make false control claims ("you can't drink"), which invite the very test you fear.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Method-level, supported by reactance research and freedom-of-choice framing studies; the tone effect is asserted from clinical experience.
### [CF:OPTION-MENU] Offer several options in one pass, after eliciting theirs
**Move:** Never prescribe a single route. Say that several exist, ask what they have already considered or tried, brainstorm without evaluating (welcoming unrealistic ideas to unfreeze thinking), ask permission before adding yours, lay the whole menu out in one continuous pass, then ask which ones seem possible — with an explicit veto over any of them.
**APPLY WHEN:** the goal is agreed and only the method is open; they produce a reason each single suggestion will not work; the option space feels closed.
**AVOID WHEN:** only one option is genuinely appropriate, so padding the menu would be dishonest; you ask "what about this one?" after each item, turning choosing into serial critiquing; they are overwhelmed and need one small step instead.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Method-level practice plus choice/commitment research; rapid-fire single suggestions are documented to manufacture sustain talk.

## 6. Planning, persistence and setbacks

### [CF:READY-SIGNS] Read the six readiness signs instead of scheduling the shift
**Move:** Before moving from why to how, listen for: more change talk; less sustain talk; resolve or a quiet settling; envisioning aloud what the change would be like including its difficulties; questions about the change; small steps already taken. The single commonest sign is the person raising the topic of how.
**APPLY WHEN:** you are deciding whether to shift from evoking to planning.
**AVOID WHEN:** one cue is treated as a green light to prescribe; planning is pushed on a schedule rather than on signal; imagined obstacles are misread as sustain talk — that is trying the change on.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Authors' clinical observation, consistent with process research on change/sustain ratios; not separately validated.
### [CF:SKIPEVOKE] With high readiness, skip evoking and go to the how
**Move:** When readiness and confidence are already high, stop building motivation and move to method. Do not allocate fixed time per task in a structured delivery — let observed readiness decide how much evoking happens versus how much planning.
**APPLY WHEN:** the person is asking for method, not motivation.
**AVOID WHEN:** readiness is stated but not demonstrated, or ambivalence is still audible in sustain talk; a protocol requires standardization, in which case measure what was actually delivered.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Several reports of adverse effects at high initial readiness — the strongest contraindication signal in the book; causal direction inferred, not tested.
### [CF:BAP] Brief action planning
**Move:** Five steps in a few minutes: ask whether there is anything they would like to do in the next week or two; if yes, shape it into a specific plan; have them restate it in their own words; rate confidence 0–10 and, if it is under 7, ask what would raise it; offer some form of accountability. Frame the result as an experiment — one option to try, with the message that you will keep working until something fits.
**APPLY WHEN:** they sound engaged and action-ready; the contact is brief and medical-style; a small near-term step is on the table; they are afraid of another "failure".
**AVOID WHEN:** ambivalence or low readiness is present — evoke the why first; the person is restless with step-by-step structure; "experiment" is being used to dodge specificity about what, when and how.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** MI-consistent five-step method taught in medical settings; method-level support only, no book-specific trial.
### [CF:COMMIT-LISTEN] Track the verb ladder, never press for "I will"
**Move:** Listen for the modal actually used — want, could, need, willing, will — and treat movement up that ladder as strengthening commitment. Check whether words and body agree. Support specific stated intentions and let the language strengthen on its own.
**APPLY WHEN:** a plan is forming and mobilizing change talk has appeared; you are reviewing your own stated intentions.
**AVOID WHEN:** you press ("so are you going to do it or not?") — pressure can undo motivational progress; you use the ladder to score or catch someone out. A declared commitment is not required: a favourable balance predicts action without one.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Psycholinguistic MI process research plus implementation-intention findings; directly debunks "no commitment language, no change".
### [CF:SETBACK-NORMALIZE] Bound the setback before it eats the plan
**Move:** Catch a lapse early, name it as an ordinary event rather than a failure, and keep it explicitly bounded. Replace absolute perfection rules ("I will never eat sweets") with non-binary targets, treat imperfection as partial progress, and affirm the ground already held.
**APPLY WHEN:** a black-or-white rule has just broken and the "nothing left to lose" logic is starting up.
**AVOID WHEN:** the lapse involves acute risk — overdose, self-harm, a medical emergency. Normalizing is not the response there; safety-protocol §1 is.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Cited work on rule-violation breakdown of self-control and on early support maintaining change; no isolated trial of the move.

## 7. Pushback, discord and repair

### [CF:DISCORD-SCAN] Classify sustain talk versus discord; treat any rise as a stop signal
**Move:** Ask what you just heard: is it about the topic (sustain talk) or about you (discord — it usually contains the word "you")? Discord markers: being challenged or discounted, defending their own worth, new interrupting, disengagement. Any rise means stop what you were doing, back off, reflect, reaffirm autonomy and leave the door open — pushing trains them to defend the status quo.
**APPLY WHEN:** a change conversation turns adversarial, or the tone shifts with no content reason; whenever concern is being raised about behaviour someone else wants changed.
**AVOID WHEN:** it slides into silent diagnosis of their character — the framing is interactional, not a trait rating.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** MI session-coding research (Moyers); the amount of sustain talk predicts outcome better than the amount of change talk.
### [CF:REFLECT-SUSTAIN] Answer pushback with a reflection, not a counterargument
**Move:** Meet sustain talk and discord with a plain reflection of content or feeling — it just doesn't feel right to you; you really want me to hear you — with no stock preamble. Reflection de-escalates because the person registers being heard. Track intensity across exchanges: if negativity is climbing, change the approach rather than the volume.
**APPLY WHEN:** they argue for the status quo, are heated, or have just accused you of not listening.
**AVOID WHEN:** the reflection would carry sarcasm; the discord is about a real grievance that needs an actual remedy rather than an accurate mirror.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Method-level trials plus coding research: softening reduces resistance, refuting strengthens it.
### [CF:REPAIR-DISCORD] Apologize, or step off the argument
**Move:** When the friction is about the two of you, take partial responsibility out loud — I'm sorry, let me try again; tell me what you want me to understand. When the fight is over a label or over who is at fault, step off it and name what you actually care about instead of the label.
**APPLY WHEN:** the signal is discord rather than sustain talk; winning the sub-argument would change nothing.
**AVOID WHEN:** you would be apologizing for someone's ambivalence, which is sustain talk and needs no apology; the topic you sidestep is their actual agenda, in which case sidestepping reads as evasion and produces more discord.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Authors' clinical experience; the repairs are defined without effect sizes.
### [CF:COMING-ALONGSIDE] Voice their own position fully instead of disputing it
**Move:** State the extreme or self-deprecating position back to them without endorsing it and without sarcasm — perhaps you will just have to keep going regardless of the consequences — and let them supply the counterargument. Variant: summarize the whole list of reasons-it-won't-work, count them out neutrally, then say you suspect there is another list and ask what is on it.
**APPLY WHEN:** direct reassurance keeps producing more self-discounting; other softening responses have failed and the fixing reflex is pulling you toward persuasion.
**AVOID WHEN:** it would be delivered as a dare, a bluff or reverse psychology — manipulation is sensed; the belief concerns safety or self-harm, where amplification reads as agreeing with a dangerous conclusion.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Long-standing MI technique with method-level backing; the specific wordings are the authors' clinical experience, with no isolating trial.

## 8. Practising the skill on yourself

### [CF:PRACTICE-LOOP] Drill one component, then count instead of judging
**Move:** Drill: practise one OARS component at a time in ordinary conversations; substitute a reflection every time you were about to ask a question; ask permission to just listen for a long stretch on a topic where you disagree. Measure: with consent, record real practice, review a 10-minute segment and count — who talked more, questions versus simple versus complex reflections, what preceded each piece of change talk or discord, and how you responded.
**APPLY WHEN:** you want data rather than recollection; skill feels stuck; a peer learning group exists, since real practice beats role play.
**AVOID WHEN:** consent is absent or recordings are unprotected; monitoring becomes so absorbing that you stop listening; self-rating substitutes for counting, since self-reports err upward.
**Tier:** SELF · **Evidence:** Training research — workshops alone left about one extra reflection per 10 minutes at 4 months with no change in client responses; only feedback plus coaching shifted client language.

## 9. Operations that require a live session

### [CF:ADMITLIMIT] Name where your competence ends and route out
**Move:** State plainly what you are not qualified to judge, pair it with a reflection of what the person actually wants, and offer a concrete next step — making an enquiry, arranging a contact, meeting again — instead of an answer you cannot back. Hand over the format, not just the topic: what to bring, why it belongs in session, what question to ask.
**APPLY WHEN:** you are asked whether to open trauma-focused work or pick a specific therapy; wrong-door situations; any request beyond scope.
**AVOID WHEN:** the mismatch is really ambivalence about a goal you can work on, so referral becomes avoidance; you would hand off someone in acute distress without arranging a real contact.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST · **Evidence:** Professional-ethics norm asserted by the authors; naming the limit is the part a non-clinician can do — the decision to open traumatic material is not.
### [CF:VERTICAL-AMB] Name a motive outside awareness — once, then wait
**Move:** When behaviour persistently contradicts a stated motive, name the inconsistency a single time as a question, then give patient empathic support and double-sided reflections while the person integrates it. Do not press for insight on a schedule.
**APPLY WHEN:** one pole is clearly outside awareness, the person is puzzled by their own repeated behaviour, and a therapist is already in the picture.
**AVOID WHEN:** the hidden material is likely grief or trauma — the authors' own example resolves into a childhood bereavement, and the timing of an interpretation is a clinical judgment; regulation is shaky.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST · **Evidence:** Authors' clinical experience framed as psychodynamic timing of interpretation; no trial, and they concede they have nothing better than patient support to offer.
### [CF:EXPERIENCING-SHIFT] Deepen from abstract account to present-tense felt experience
**Move:** Notice when an account is abstract, past-tense and impersonal, and steer it toward first-person present-tense description — what is this like right now, rather than why did that happen. General rule attached: name which in-session response your method treats as the healing signal, and check that it predicts outcome before you start evoking it.
**APPLY WHEN:** self-reflection has gone theoretical and detached and the content is ordinary present-life material, with a therapist available.
**AVOID WHEN:** the material is traumatic or grief-laden — deepening immediacy there is exposure work; regulation is already poor; the marker is one you like rather than one with predictive evidence.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST · **Evidence:** Strong observational evidence that in-session experiencing predicts outcome across orientations; not MI-specific and not a controlled manipulation.
