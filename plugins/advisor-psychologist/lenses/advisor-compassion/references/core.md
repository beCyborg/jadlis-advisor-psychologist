# advisor-compassion — core

Principles of the CFT/CMT lens, ranked by what actually supports them. The organizing honesty of this lens: almost everything with a decent effect size in it was borrowed from another tradition, and almost everything distinctive to it is mechanism, model or clinical report. Read the tier before quoting the principle. All numbers here are in their post-correction form per the 2026 guidelines layer; where a figure was demoted, the demotion is stated inline.

## Tier 1 — Borrowed components with method-level trial support

These carry real evidence, but the evidence belongs to the *technique*, not to the compassion frame wrapped around it. The lens never claims the wrapper adds the effect; that increment has never been measured.

### Graded exposure — the strongest base in the lens
Approach in small repeated steps until anxiety eases is the best-supported operation the tradition uses ([CFT:APPROACH], [CFT:EXPOSURE-PROG]). CFT contributes a tolerable stance, not the mechanism. Operationally this splits the tier boundary: executing an ordinary non-traumatic step is self-work; designing or escalating a hierarchy, and anything trauma-linked, is a session operation — failed solo exposure reinforces the fear it targeted.

### Mindfulness — method-level, no CFT-specific increment
Anchored attention and open monitoring have trial literature of their own ([CFT:MINDFUL]). The lens adds threat-mind monitoring as a clinical extension. Boundary from the guidelines layer: where a trauma history is present and practice makes things worse, the instruction is to stop the practice, not to try harder.

### Cognitive restructuring and behavioural activation — MODERATE as standalone components
Thought records and activity/values work import their support from CBT and BA ([CFT:THREC], [CFT:DRIVE-VALUES]). The compassionate variants are adaptations with no direct trial. Between-method differences in depression are approximately zero, so the lens should not argue it beats CBT on these operations — it argues it makes them land in people for whom cold versions bounce off.

### Slow-paced breathing — physiology, not outcome
The 5–6 breaths-per-minute protocol has method-level physiological support ([CFT:SBR]). The deliverable is measurable slowing, not a soothed feeling. The embodiment layers are cheap add-ons; the facial-expression layer rests on weakly replicated facial-feedback findings and should never be presented as an established mechanism.

### Expressive writing — direction only
Letter work rides on Pennebaker's paradigm ([CFT:LETTER]) — small effects, no effect sizes carried into these books. The tone-steering and the eight-quality audit are the authors' own. Bounded correction from the guidelines layer: writing is not unsafe by default; what is contraindicated is unstructured venting during active rumination-driven distress.

## Tier 2 — Direct outcome evidence for compassion training (LOW, and passive-control-inflated)

### Self-compassion interventions for self-criticism — g=0.51, demoted MODERATE → LOW
The pooled self-compassion figure (Wakelin 2021, g=0.51) is driven by passive comparators. Correct phrasing: better than nothing; **not shown superior to active treatment**. This single line governs how strongly any Tier 2 claim may be stated.

### Loving-kindness — 67 vs 72, waiting-list controlled
Fredrickson et al. 2008 (six weekly 60-minute sessions), Lutz et al. 2008, and Pace et al. 2008 (six weeks of compassion meditation improving immune and neuroendocrine stress responses) are the best quantitative results in the canon and are all against passive comparators ([CFT:FLOW-OUT]).

### Mirror-based self-directed practice — one three-arm study
Petrocchi et al. found the phrases-plus-mirror arm produced the highest positive affect and significantly lower self-criticism ([CFT:SELF-LKM]). Best-evidenced single exercise in the lens, and still: one study, not of these books, no active comparator.

### Uncontrolled CFT programme data
Laithwaite et al. 2009 (group CFT, 19 clients, high-security setting: large changes in depression and self-esteem, moderate in social comparison, small in shame, held at 6-week follow-up) and Gilbert & Procter 2006 (small uncontrolled pilot: reduced shame, self-criticism, depression, anxiety). Both uncontrolled. As the canon went to press in 2010, its author stated RCTs were only being planned.

### Fears of compassion — correlational only
Fears of giving, receiving and self-directed compassion all correlate with stress, anxiety and depression, self-directed most strongly, and with fear of happiness ([CFT:FBR-WORK]). The instrument is validated; there are no clinical cut-offs, and correlation carries no direction.

## Tier 3 — Mechanism and physiology: direction, never dosage

### The high-self-critic threat response — the lens's most application-relevant finding
Rockliff et al. 2008: compassion imagery raised heart-rate variability and lowered cortisol in *low* self-critics, but *lowered* HRV — a threat signature — in high self-critics, with no cortisol change. This is why more practice is the wrong prescription for someone recoiling from compassion, and why [CFT:FBR-WORK] precedes added practice hours rather than following failure.

### Self-criticism and self-reassurance are not one system
Longe et al. 2010 (fMRI): distinct neural patterns for the two, with threat-like activation in high self-critics attempting to reassure themselves. Supports the claim that self-attack recruits threat machinery ([CFT:OLDNEW], [CFT:SELFCORR]) — an imaging correlate, not an outcome.

### Felt care down-regulates threat
Cited generally: hand-holding by a spouse reduced threat-area activation under shock, more so at higher relationship quality ([CFT:IDEAL-OTHER]). Analogical support for the soothing-system claim; the leap from partner's hand to imagined figure is theory.

### Attention is threat-biased by design
Negativity-bias research (Baumeister et al. 2001; Öhman et al. 2001 — hostile faces found faster than friendly ones; one frowning face among ninety-nine smiling found faster than the reverse) grounds the rebalancing rationale ([CFT:ATT-FUNC], [CFT:IDEAL-OTHER]). It justifies correcting a bias; it does not license positive thinking.

### Emotional tone, not content, does the damage
Whelton & Greenberg 2005: the affect carried in self-criticism predicts its impact. This is the empirical anchor under the lens's signature distinction between shaming self-attack and compassionate self-correction ([CFT:SELFCORR], [CFT:WARMREAD]).

## Tier 4 — Clinical model: coherent, teachable, untested

Everything here is a map. It earns its place by organizing observations and de-shaming them, not by having outperformed anything.

- **Three affect-regulation systems** — threat, drive, soothing, with pathology framed as organization around threat while soothing starves ([CFT:FORMULATION]). Partial affective-neuroscience grounding; the three-circles exercise is practice.
- **Safeness is not low arousal** — isolation calms while cutting off the only system that regulates threat, so "fine alone" can be safety-seeking ([CFT:FORMULATION], safeness check).
- **Social mentalities and old-brain/new-brain loops** — care-giving, care-seeking, competing, co-operating as whole-mind configurations; threat switches mentalizing off ([CFT:OLDNEW]). Teaching simplification, explicitly not established neuroscience.
- **Functional, non-diagnostic formulation** — history → fears → protective strategies → unintended consequences → feedback ([CFT:FORMULATION]). No trial of self-administered use; its de-shaming effect is the claim, not symptom change.
- **Compassion as motivation with two psychologies** — engagement with suffering plus commitment to relieve it, six attributes and six trainable skills; therefore compassion includes boundaries, refusal and courage ([CFT:FLOW-OUT], [CFT:ASSERT]).
- **Three flows are not interchangeable** — to others, from others, to self, each with its own blocks; the from-others flow is the one people skip ([CFT:IDEAL-OTHER]).
- **Compassionate identity built by acting** — imagining having the qualities is the practice; feeling them is not required ([CFT:COMP-SELF]). Correlational identity-goal research only.
- **The mind is multiple** — bullying, perfectionist and frightened patterns coexist and the unified self is an achievement ([CFT:MULTISELF]). Roughly ten hypothesized mentalizing mechanisms that the author states await research; the parts framing must not be literalized, and is contraindicated where reality testing or sense of self is unstable.
- **Sequencing: base before aversive material** — labelled by its author as a clinical claim, never trial-tested ([CFT:BASEFIRST]); the readiness gate underneath it cannot be self-administered.
- **Shame as social threat, two forms of self-criticism** — external vs internal shame has psychometric support and the inadequacy/self-hatred distinction is published questionnaire work; the intervention protocols on top are practice ([CFT:SHAME-WORK], [CFT:CRIT-WORK]).

## Tier 5 — Weak by the authors' own account

- **Grief work** ([CFT:GRIEF-WORK]): the canon states outright that good evidence is lacking and offers clinical report. The guidelines layer adds hard boundaries — entry only at measured elevated prolonged-grief symptoms, therapist-led, no solo format, and no modality has shown superiority (Hao 2024: 55 studies, N=10,330, five modalities with overlapping intervals). At ordinary grief these protocols run near zero and can harm.
- **Guilt tolerance** — named as almost unresearched, while being asked to carry the shame-to-guilt conversion.
- **Dosage, duration, selection** — nothing beyond "months", "5–10 minutes a day", "about a week per exercise", "grade it". No minimal effective subset has been identified for a programme running to dozens of practices.
- **Practice-ladder ordering** ([CFT:PRACTICE-LADDER]) — a clinical heuristic with no trial, doing real safety work.
- **Borrowed physiology stated too confidently** — facial feedback, a mother's-voice oxytocin study used analogically, the claim that imagery beats words for emotion.
- **Societal critique** — competitive-culture-as-pathogen is argument, not data, and does not belong in a per-person verdict.

## Operating principles that do not depend on effect size

These are how the lens reads a situation, and they hold whether or not the outcome literature ever firms up.

1. **Felt tone outranks accuracy.** An alternative thought only works if it can be felt as reassuring; correctness is insufficient ([CFT:WARMREAD]).
2. **Not your fault, still your responsibility.** Both halves or neither — the first alone becomes an excuse, the second alone is the critic in new clothing ([CFT:NOTFAULT]).
3. **Self-attack is a safety strategy, not a character flaw** — so it is mapped and out-competed, never argued with or deleted ([CFT:CRIT-WORK]).
4. **Recoil from compassion is the work, not resistance to overcome** ([CFT:FBR-WORK]) — with the Rockliff finding as the reason.
5. **The trauma boundary is enforced by the lens, not by the reader.** Both books route trauma material out and then leave detection of flooding to the person mid-exercise, which is exactly when self-assessment fails. Anything that opens traumatic material, loss, or vulnerable-part contact is routed as a format: theme, why in session, what to prepare, what to ask.
