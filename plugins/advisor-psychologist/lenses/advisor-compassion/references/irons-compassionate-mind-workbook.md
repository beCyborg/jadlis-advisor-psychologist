# The Compassionate Mind Workbook — digest

## What this book is for the lens

The lens's structured workbook: a full CFT/CMT self-training curriculum from psychoeducation (tricky brain, three systems, formulation) through capacity building (breathing, imagery, compassionate self) to applied skills (thinking, emotion, behaviour, letters) and the hard cases (shame, self-criticism, fears of compassion). Its edge: it treats compassion as a trainable *motive* requiring courage and skill — not a feeling — and it is the lens's only source with a worked taxonomy of fears/blocks/resistances (FBRs) and a shame/self-criticism protocol. Use it for concrete exercises, pacing decisions, and explaining why compassion practice itself is backfiring.

## Core model

- **Tricky brain (Ch. 1):** old brain (threat emotions, defensive behaviours) + new brain (imagination, rumination) create self-sustaining "loops" — worry/self-criticism re-triggers threat with no external stimulus. Design flaw, not personal fault; "not your fault" coexists with responsibility for training the mind.
- **Shaped, not chosen (Ch. 2):** the current self is one of many possible versions, shaped *for* us by history. Change is forward-looking practice, not past-rewriting or positive thinking.
- **Three systems (Ch. 3):** threat (protective, over-fires by design), drive (wanting/achieving), soothing (parasympathetic safeness, wired to care signals; down-regulates threat and *enables* action). Typical presentation: overactive threat, blocked soothing, drive flat or threat-fused (striving to avoid inferiority). CMT rebalances by building soothing — no system is deleted.
- **Formulation (Ch. 4):** history → key fears → protective strategies → unintended consequences → feedback loop. Strategies were never chosen, consequences never intended — which is what makes compassion toward the pattern possible.
- **Compassion architecture (Ch. 5–7):** sensitivity to suffering + commitment to relieve it. Two psychologies (engagement; alleviation), six attributes (care, sensitivity, sympathy, distress tolerance, empathy, non-judgement), six trainable skills (attention, imagery, reasoning, behaviour, sensory focusing, feeling). Three flows — to others, from others, to self — not interchangeable, each with its own blocks (FBRs).
- **Capacity → identity → application (Ch. 8–26):** mindfulness and soothing-rhythm breathing (8–9), imagery/safe place (10), drive entered "counter-clockwise" from soothing (11), compassionate self/other/team via actor-training (12–14), the three flows (15–17), application to attention (18), thinking (19), emotion (20), behaviour incl. exposure and assertiveness (21), letters (22), multiple selves (23), shame/self-criticism (24), FBRs (25), maintenance (26).
- **Working method:** every skill chapter opens with ~1 week of brief self-monitoring before intervening (loop log 1.1, practice record 8.1, attention log 18.1, thought log 19.1, emotion/body logs 20.1–20.2, critic log 24.1) — entries brief, timeboxed, never a stick for self-criticism.

## Moves

### [CFT:SELF-LKM] Self-directed loving-kindness, mirror variant
**Move:** From the embodied compassionate self, visualize your everyday self (or a mildly distressed version — ordinary stressors only) and offer warm-toned phrases by name: may you be well / be happy / be safe / find the strength and courage to tolerate your difficulties. Strongest variant (Ch. 17): say the phrases at a mirror with a gentle smile; step-down to a photo/selfie camera; extend into brief daily mirror pauses.
**APPLY WHEN:** Self-criticism, self-invalidation, "kinder to everyone but myself"; phrase-only practice feels flat.
**AVOID WHEN:** Aiming the distressed-self variant at trauma-linked material; mirror overwhelms — take the step-down. Initial awkwardness is expected, not a stop signal.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Best-evidenced exercise in the book: Petrocchi et al. three-arm study — phrases-while-mirror-gazing gave highest positive affect and significantly lower self-criticism (single study, not of this book).

### [CFT:FOC-SCALE] Fears of Compassion baseline
**Move:** Complete the Fears of Compassion questionnaire (Gilbert et al., compassionatemind.co.uk); its three subscales mirror the three flows — find the trickiest, inspect highest-endorsed items for the specific fear; re-take to track change.
**APPLY WHEN:** Wanting a structured FBR baseline; ambiguity about which flow to target first.
**AVOID WHEN:** Over-reading scores as diagnosis — no clinical cut-offs; high scores are starting points, not verdicts.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Validated instrument; high scores correlate with more mental-health symptoms and fear of happiness (correlational).

### [CFT:SRB] Soothing Rhythm Breathing with embodiment layers
**Move:** Grounded posture (feet flat, spine lengthened, chest open) + warm facial expression; breathe slower/deeper/smoother ~5 min daily (1–2 min start). Layers as each stabilizes: pace to ~5–6 breaths/min (in-5s, hold-1, out-5s, hold-1; apps allowed); a stretched out-breath word ("calm") in a warm inner voice; if body-focus is uncomfortable, keep the rhythm but anchor externally (textured object, app visual); pair with a tested smell or sound. Transfer: beside a comfortable person → safe conversation → live threat.
**APPLY WHEN:** Core daily regulation; bridging threat → soothing; opener for nearly every other exercise.
**AVOID WHEN:** Chasing a "soothed feeling" — the deliverable is physiological slowing/groundedness; breathing-related health conditions — only the comfortable parts; slowing spikes anxiety — grade it as informal exposure.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Method-level slow-paced-breathing physiology; CFT's signature practice, no book trial. The facial-expression layer rests on weakly-replicated facial-feedback findings — low-cost add-on, not established mechanism.

### [CFT:MINDFUL] Anchored mindfulness, then open monitoring
**Move:** Ch. 8 sequence: anchored practices first, ~5 min — breath at one focal point, body scan (~30s/region), or receptive sound; return kindly on wandering — the noticing IS the rep. After weeks, open monitoring: label whatever arises ("worrying", "anxiety", "memory") for distance without suppression. Generalize via one fully-attended routine activity daily or mindful walking.
**APPLY WHEN:** Worry/rumination loops; over-identification with thoughts; foundation before all imagery work.
**AVOID WHEN:** Breath/body focus activates threat — switch to sound/external anchor; open monitoring as a first practice; labelling as covert suppression.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Method-level mindfulness trial literature; sequencing is the authors' clinical practice.

### [CFT:APPROACH] Avoidance mapping and graded approach ladder
**Move:** Map avoidances, safety strategies, feared outcomes (Worksheet 21.1); from the compassionate self validate both, then ask what would help tolerate rather than avoid (21.2); build a least→most ladder of feared situations (21.3; e.g. 7 steps from writing a talk to delivering to managers); climb in small repeated steps until anxiety eases, crediting attempts. For predictable challenges, the Compassion PDA (21.4): Pre (skills, information, bias-watch) / During (breathing, embodied posture, slow down) / After (debrief with a friend, savour or tolerate, extract learning, block rumination).
**APPLY WHEN:** Life narrowed by avoidance of ordinary, non-traumatic situations; procrastination on known-helpful change; shame about avoidance blocking change.
**AVOID WHEN:** Avoidance is trauma-rooted, panic-level, or keeps flooding — that is [CFT:EXPOSURE-PROG] with the therapist; validation stopping at acceptance-of-avoidance; PDA becoming perfectionist over-preparation.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Graded exposure has the strongest method-level base in the book; the CFT wrapper and PDA are clinical adaptation.

### [CFT:THREC] Compassionate Thought Record
**Move:** Worksheet 19.2: (1) trigger event, upsetting thoughts/images, emotions; (2) FIRST embody the compassionate self, then write the response in four sub-blocks — empathy/validation ("understandable… not my fault"), compassionate attention (balancing facts), compassionate thinking (what you'd say to a friend; common humanity; "threat system active → thinking biased"; thoughts are events, not facts), compassionate behaviour (one supported action); (3) re-read in a warm voice tone, record the felt shift. Portable: flash cards / phone notes by bed, fridge, mirror.
**APPLY WHEN:** A specific recent difficulty keeps generating threat thoughts; best filled when an emotion shifts.
**AVOID WHEN:** Skipping the embodiment step — without it this degrades to cold disputation; expecting it to erase sadness (the worked example stays sad but stops self-attacking).
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Thought records are core CBT (method-level); the compassionate variant is a CFT adaptation, no direct trial.

### [CFT:LETTER] Compassionate letter writing
**Move:** Ten-step letter FROM the embodied compassionate self (Ch. 22): embody → hold intention → greet yourself by name, name the difficulty → validate/empathize incl. learning-history roots → frame coping strategies and their costs as understandable, not your fault → shift to responsibility → compassionate thoughts and concrete actions → anticipate blocks/setbacks → close with commitment and companionship → re-read slowly in a warm voice (or audio-record and listen). Steps are a guide; "you"-form if "I" is harder.
**APPLY WHEN:** Integrating all skill channels around one persistent struggle; post-event processing; setbacks in behaviour or assertiveness work.
**AVOID WHEN:** Critical voice hijacks mid-letter ("battery gone flat") — stop, recharge, resume; perfectionism about wording; monitor WHICH part of you is writing.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Pennebaker expressive-writing paradigm (method-level, direction only); the compassionate variant is untested.

### [CFT:DRIVE-VALUES] Rebuilding drive counter-clockwise
**Move:** Enter drive from soothing, not threat (Ch. 11). Three tools: Worksheet 11.1 values — for 5 domains (work, family, friends, personal development, self-care) state what you value, rate 0–10, list short/long-term moves; values are a compass, goals are strike-off items; value-congruent action works even during low mood. Best Possible Self — ~5 min imagining a realistic good future + ≥10 min writing. Three Good Things — nightly, 3 events + what led to each, relived in detail, ≥1 week.
**APPLY WHEN:** Underactive drive, anhedonic narrowing, negativity bias dominating; fuzzy direction after low mood.
**AVOID WHEN:** Already over-striving — "doing more" is counter-indicated; build soothing first, then audit fear-driven vs intrinsic activities; gratitude used to paper over real pain.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** ACT-style values, best-possible-self and gratitude interventions have method-level support (modest effects); threat-fused striving linked to higher depression/anxiety/stress in the authors' correlational research.

### [CFT:FLOW-OUT] Compassion to others — imagery plus acts
**Move:** From the compassionate self, direct loving-kindness at a being you care about, by name, warm tone: may you be well / be happy / be content / tolerate the difficulties you face. Target ladder, advanced only when the current rung is easy: loved one → friend/family → stranger → groups → someone you dislike. Behavioural arm (Worksheet 15.1): pick from the 11-item kindness menu (greet, ask about someone's day, card to someone struggling, text a lapsed contact, praise), plan in writing (raises follow-through), execute from the compassionate-self state, consolidate by replaying or logging.
**APPLY WHEN:** Cultivating outward warmth; moving compassion from imagery into behaviour — giving also benefits the giver.
**AVOID WHEN:** Kindness driven by approval-seeking ("submissive compassion"); targets you hold live anger toward or whose suffering is severe (both hijack into threat); jumping ladder rungs.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Secularized loving-kindness meditation and kindness-intervention literature (method-level); worksheets untested.

### [CFT:ASSERT] Compassionate assertiveness and emotion expression
**Move:** Six steps (Ch. 21): identify where you can't assert → find what sits behind it (fears of selfishness, rejection) → bring compassion to the struggle (validate; spot learned patterns, e.g. copying a parent's submissiveness) → script from the compassionate self: "I need/feel…", "This matters because…", "I would like you to…", invite discussion → plan when/with whom → manage setbacks via thought record or letter. Rehearse aloud, in a mirror, in imagery, or with a safe friend (Ch. 20).
**APPLY WHEN:** Chronic yes-saying, needs unrecognized, resentment accumulating, hoarding-then-erupting pattern.
**AVOID WHEN:** Tipping into the aggressive pole (demands, blame, threats); expecting instant acceptance — recipients unused to your expressing may react with confusion or threat.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Assertiveness training has an old method-level base; the CFT protocol is clinical adaptation.

### [CFT:CRIT-WORK] Mapping and converting the self-critic
**Move:** Ch. 24. (1) Log self-critical episodes first — most underestimate ("like breathing"). (2) Profile the critic on five attributes: Focus, Content (its actual words), Form (inadequacy put-down vs disgust-based self-persecution), Function ("what do I fear would happen if I could never self-criticize again?" + FSCS: keep-standards vs punish), Origin. (3) Externalize in ~5-min imagery — size, face, voice, the emotion it directs at you — then engage from the compassionate self: wisdom (what is it trying to do for me?), strength (step the image back if overwhelmed), caring (how could its concerns be met more wisely?). (4) Convert: two-teachers test (your critic teaching a struggling child you love; list the teacher qualities you'd actually want), then set an intention of compassionate self-correction — same improvement aims, delivered by encouragement instead of threat.
**APPLY WHEN:** Recurring self-attack after mistakes; belief "self-criticism keeps my standards up"; fear of losing the critic (laziness, arrogance).
**AVOID WHEN:** Critic is trauma-derived (an internalized abuser's face appears; flooding/dissociation) — therapist territory; the Origin question dragging into trauma review — map, don't relive.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** FSCS is a published Gilbert-et-al measure; the two-forms/two-functions distinction is published research (Gilbert & Irons); the engagement/conversion protocol is clinical practice.

### [CFT:FORMULATION] Formulation through the three CFT lenses
**Move:** Three layers. (1) Three-systems profile (Worksheet 3.1): per system — triggers, frequency, duration, intensity 1–10, thoughts, urged behaviours, shaping experiences; draw the three circles sized proportionally. (2) Loop map (1.1): for one recent episode, which new-brain content fed which old-brain emotion and back. (3) Five-column formulation (4.1–4.5): background → key external fears (rejection, criticism, harm) and internal fears (unworthiness, overwhelming emotions) → protective strategies (avoidance, submissiveness, suppression, striving, self-criticism-as-improvement) → unintended consequences → feedback arrows. Close with the compassion re-read: would I blame a friend who lived this history?
**APPLY WHEN:** Entry-point assessment; recurring difficulties with unclear origin; self-blame for one's personality blocking change; the map before all skills chapters.
**AVOID WHEN:** History includes significant trauma/abuse and recalling floods or dissociates — the book itself says pause, stage it, involve a safe person, do it alongside the therapist; the compassion re-read feels forced — park it until skills are built.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Standard CFT assessment and formulation on Gilbert's model; no trial of self-administered use.

### [CFT:COMP-SELF] Building the compassionate self (actor method)
**Move:** Ch. 12: ground in SRB + posture, then like an actor imagine BEING a deeply compassionate person, ~60s per quality — wisdom (tricky brain, not-your-fault, responsibility), strength (upright body, confident voice), caring-commitment (face, tone, devoted stance) — then integrate; repeat over weeks. Entry ramps: write why becoming compassionate matters to you; recall being compassionate to someone NOT in much distress and reconstruct your posture/voice/actions. Variants: visualize the compassionate self in front of you, then merge into its body; daily micro-embodiment of its voice, expression, posture in found moments.
**APPLY WHEN:** Daily base practice from Section IV on; prerequisite state for nearly every applied move; doubting you possess the qualities — the acting frame removes that requirement.
**AVOID WHEN:** All three qualities at once overwhelms — one per session; entry memory carries high recipient distress; strong resistance — read the FBR material (Ch. 25) first.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** CMT method-level; authors cite a growing literature linking compassionate identity to lower self-criticism, low mood, stress (general claims, no component trial).

### [CFT:IDEAL-OTHER] Ideal compassionate other, inner team, and receiving care
**Move:** Ch. 13–14, 16. Build: written spec of a perfect supporter — form (human/animal/nature), voice, expression, optional backstory (often compassionate through its own suffering); then imagery around wisdom (understands you and tricky brains), strength (tolerates your distress no matter what), caring-commitment (non-critical, wants you to flourish). Team: as your compassionate self, meet it in your safe place and stand shoulder to shoulder — a secure-base primer. Receiving: stay open ~60s to its incoming care and hear it say, by name, warm voice, may you be well / be happy / find the strength and courage to tolerate your difficulties; also recall one memory of receiving kindness (from a time you were NOT in high distress), dwelling ~60s on the felt safeness; and scan the last day for kindness you barely registered, setting a daily noticing intention. Asking others for help is itself self-compassionate behaviour.
**APPLY WHEN:** Little lived experience of feeling cared for; low felt connection; threat-busy mind missing care signals; priming support before hard tasks — "from others" is the flow people most often skip.
**AVOID WHEN:** Receiving feels strongly aversive — FBR territory, slow down rather than force; care memory from a period of struggle; grief for a lost carer surfaces — ground, try another memory; recurrent overwhelming grief or care-linked trauma belongs with the therapist; treating the image as a substitute for real relationships — it is a bridge toward them.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Contemplative lineage, secularized; felt-care-regulates-threat research cited generally (spousal hand-holding reduced threat-area activation under shock); clinical method, no trial.

### [CFT:SAFE-PLACE] Safe-place and soothing imagery
**Move:** Ch. 10: after SRB, construct a safe/peaceful place (real or fantasy), walking the senses ~30s each; key detail: the place is AWARE of you, welcomes you, wants you at ease. Gentler first step: a calming colour as light/mist with caring awareness. Amplify with real props: matching photo, nature sounds, tested smell, grounding object, self-touch (hands over heart).
**APPLY WHEN:** Building on-demand access to safeness/calm; a lower practice-ladder rung before compassionate-self/other work.
**AVOID WHEN:** Expecting vividness — fleeting, hazy, non-visual imagery is normal and sufficient; the word "safe" triggers bad memories — rename "peaceful place", ask what the place would need for you to feel safe.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Imagery-drives-physiology rationale; CFT clinical practice, method-level imagery research for the mechanism only.

### [CFT:EMO-ENGAGE] Validating and tolerating difficult emotions
**Move:** Ch. 20, on four regulation skills: detect, name, understand, tolerate/use. Validate first: from the compassionate self, see the feeling as understandable given situation and learning history; if you can't, "validate the invalidation" (it makes sense you dismiss feelings, given how you were taught) or route via how you'd validate a friend. Then tolerate: SRB + friendly expression ~60s → embody the compassionate self stressing strength → bring up the avoided emotion and its last trigger → stay in its presence from groundedness; if overwhelmed, return to breath, re-approach; optionally imagine the struggling-you and offer care.
**APPLY WHEN:** "I shouldn't feel this" self-talk; emotions avoided out of fear of overwhelm; absent emotions (no access to anger → can't assert) — absence is diagnostic, not automatically healthy.
**AVOID WHEN:** The emotion is welded to trauma memories, dissociation shows, or overwhelm doesn't settle with grounding — therapist territory; validation used to justify harmful acting-out — validate the feeling, not every behaviour.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Clinical practice; graded affect-tolerance logic shared with method-level exposure/emotion-focused work; no direct trial.

### [CFT:ATT-FUNC] Rebalancing attention and reading its function
**Move:** Ch. 18. Rebalance: after a threat-captured episode, upright posture + ~30s SRB, connect to the compassionate self, then deliberately hold in detailed attention what went well, positive feedback, past coping, times of being valued — resting on the feelings. Function-read: if the same content keeps returning, ask from the compassionate self what the mind is trying to resolve — which threat emotion, fear or unmet need sits behind the pull (worked case: a recurring humiliation memory signalled unexpressed protest → assertiveness practice → addressed the boss).
**APPLY WHEN:** Post-event rumination after talks, meetings, social nights; redirection only partly works and content recurs; suspected avoidance-by-distraction (phone/social media).
**AVOID WHEN:** Used to deny a real problem needing action — this is bias-correction, explicitly not positive thinking; the recurring content is a trauma memory that floods — take it to the therapist.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Clinical practice; consistent with method-level CBT attention training; no trials cited.

### [CFT:MULTISELF] Multiple selves — parts work from the compassionate stance
**Move:** Worksheet 23.1: pick a recent, deliberately LOW-activation argument; step into the angry, anxious, then sad part; per part note thoughts / body / behaviour-if-in-control / memories / desired outcome, breathing between parts; answer the cross-questions on how each part views the others. Then embody the compassionate self, run the same prompts from that stance, and address each part — what it understands, what it would say, how it would help — so each part feels heard while its grip loosens. Other parts (shame, guilt, jealousy) also usable.
**APPLY WHEN:** Mixed/confusing reactions to one event; one emotion chronically runs the show; a blocked part (often sadness) — hard access is learning-history data, not failure.
**AVOID WHEN:** The example is still highly activating (threat blocks the exercise — learn in the shallow end); trauma memories surface via the Memories prompt — switch example or take to the therapist; watch "leakage" (angry content spilling into the anxious part).
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Core CFT clinical exercise; no component trial.

### [CFT:SHAME-WORK] Bringing compassion to shame
**Move:** Ch. 24: pick something you feel ashamed of; embody the compassionate self; first ONLY tolerate the feeling from strength and groundedness; then apply wisdom to name shame as an evolved social-threat response tied to rejection fear — it tracks how we exist in others' minds; its urge to conceal is design, not verdict. Follow with a letter or thought record. Counter the withdrawal pull: hiding cuts you off from exactly the care that would resolve shame.
**APPLY WHEN:** Identifiable shame about body, failure, role or a past event; shame-driven avoidance and concealment.
**AVOID WHEN:** Shame rooted in abuse/relational trauma — routed to specialist resources (Deborah Lee's trauma-focused work) and the therapist; tolerance collapses — step down the practice ladder instead of pushing.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Clinical protocol; shame–trauma link cited as considerable research evidence (method level); no component trial.

### [CFT:FBR-WORK] Working the fears, blocks and resistances
**Move:** Ch. 7, 25. Identify your FBRs (start with your associations to the word "compassion"); classify: fear (wanting it but dreading what it opens — grief, rage), block (circumstances — time, workload), resistance ("I don't deserve it" — often a masked fear). For each: validate; trace its learning origin; recheck against the CMT definition (sensitivity + commitment = strength and courage, not weakness); ask how you'd respond to a loved one holding the same belief; for "undeserving", identify which system speaks (threat fear vs drive's "earn it first") and run a sceptical-scientist experiment — practise anyway, judge by results. Micro-dose entry: one act of kindness or 1–2 min practice daily.
**APPLY WHEN:** Resistance, flatness, scorn or dread during any compassion practice; before Sections III–V to predict stalls; compassion feels alien (learnable like a foreign language).
**AVOID WHEN:** Tracing pulls into reliving relational-trauma memories rather than naming the pattern — processing is therapist territory; grief/trauma-loaded blocks that keep flooding despite self-paced steps — escalate.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** FBR framework is established CFT theory with method-level standing; case material is clinical experience.

### [CFT:PRACTICE-LADDER] Graded practice ladder for pacing
**Move:** Ch. 25: order the book's practices from least to most threat-activating for YOU (illustrative: mindfulness → SRB → safe place → compassionate self → compassionate other); when an exercise stirs too much (e.g. compassionate-other imagery evokes grief for a dead grandmother), step DOWN until regulated, then climb back; never leave the ladder entirely; redraw rung order to fit your experience.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any exercise triggers overwhelm, grief or a block; all pacing decisions across the workbook.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used to permanently avoid harder rungs; forcing instead of grading — fears lose power through small tolerable steps, not forced exposure (Kolts' frozen-shrimp point: force-thawing ruins the shrimp).
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Authors' clinical heuristic; no trial.

### [CFT:SETBACK-PLAN] Setback protocol and maintenance plan
**Move:** In the moment (Ch. 21): SRB → connect to compassionate self/other → name the feeling → validate ("not my fault; this is hard; others struggle too") → extract the lesson. Forward plan (Worksheet 26.3, from the embodied compassionate self): likely setback situations, early-warning signs, prevention and recovery steps — key points on a flashcard/phone. Prepare a "soothing kitbag": ~6 personal soothing triggers in one accessible place. Skills decondition like muscles — plan ongoing practice separately for good vs tough periods (26.2).
**APPLY WHEN:** After a failed/aborted attempt; self-criticism follows a try; finishing the programme; entering a known high-risk period.
**AVOID WHEN:** Relabelling repeated avoidance as "setbacks" without adjusting the plan — setbacks are practice material, not exemption.
**Tier:** SELF
**Evidence:** Relapse-prevention analogue; clinical experience, no trial.

### [CFT:EXPOSURE-PROG] Formal graded exposure programme
**Move:** The full Ch. 21 sequence run as a programme on hard targets: imaginal rehearsal — embody the compassionate self, imagine engaging the easiest ladder item carrying wisdom/strength/commitment, re-ground on threat spikes, end in safe place; then in-vivo — repeat each real-life step until anxiety eases, advance rung by rung over weeks, compassionate mind online before each step.
**APPLY WHEN:** Avoidance is trauma-rooted (e.g. relational fears after domestic violence), panic-level, or unsupported — run WITH the therapist, using the book as shared protocol; the compassionate-self wrapper supplies the tolerable stance.
**AVOID WHEN:** The target is a trauma memory itself (specialist work); imaginal rehearsal replacing real-life action indefinitely; running solo what keeps flooding — small everyday experiments belong to [CFT:APPROACH] instead.
**Tier:** REQUIRES-THERAPIST
**Evidence:** Graded imaginal and in-vivo exposure carry the strongest method-level trial support in the book; the tier reflects the book's own routing of trauma-linked execution to therapy.

## What the book debunks

- **Compassion = weakness/softness/self-indulgence.** Framed as courageous, effortful motivation (firefighters, medics); strength and wisdom exist precisely to detect and refuse exploitation.
- **Self-compassion = letting yourself off the hook.** A coach sharing the critic's improvement goals minus the threat delivery; "not your fault" coexists with responsibility.
- **Compassionate behaviour = being nice.** NOT people-pleasing, hiding feelings, submission, apologising for everything, or taking undue responsibility. "Submissive compassion" (kindness to win approval) is a different motivation that predicts feeling unappreciated.
- **Soothing avoidance ≠ self-compassion.** Bath-wine-film instead of the feared party maintains the difficulty; avoidance is kept alive by the relief cycle — "the world's best avoider of dogs" never loses the dog fear.
- **Compassionate thinking ≠ positive thinking.** The aim is validation plus balance connected to felt warmth; logic alone is insufficient. Thoughts are events, not facts.
- **Mindfulness ≠ clearing the mind.** Noticing the wander IS the rep; frequent falling asleep usually means you need sleep.
- **The "soothed feeling" trap.** SRB's deliverable is physiological slowing/groundedness — chasing a feeling backfires.
- **Imagery must be vivid.** Fleeting, hazy, non-visual imagery is normal and sufficient; intention beats picture quality.
- **Deleting the self-critic / becoming shameless.** Both wrong targets: the critic has protective aims (attacking it triggers threat), brief shame has prosocial functions; the target is conversion to compassionate self-correction.
- **"Nothing bad enough happened to me."** Difficulties are valid without dramatic trauma; conversely, suppression rebounds and emotional "hoarding" erupts.
- **Compassion must feel natural.** Without lived models it feels alien and is still learnable; if you believe compassion is weak, more practice hours won't help until the belief itself is worked.

## Numbers that matter

- **Dose:** 5–10 min/day; ~1 week per exercise before adding the next; 1–2 min/day micro-dose for blocked starters; ~1 self-compassion exercise per day in Section V.
- **SRB pacing:** ~5–6 breaths/min; count 5s in – 1 hold – 5s out – 1 hold, 2–3 min.
- **Model scaffolding:** 3 emotion systems; 2 psychologies; 6 attributes + 6 skills; 3 flows; 3 FBR categories; 3 emotional selves + compassionate self; 5 critic attributes; 2 forms and 2 functions of self-criticism; 4 emotion-regulation skills; 9 emotions on the body-mapping worksheet.
- **Exercise counts:** 10 letter steps; 11-item kindness menu; 4 loving-kindness phrases; 3-phase PDA; 5 value domains rated 0–10; 3 good things × ≥7 days; ~30s per sensory channel in safe-place; ~60s per quality in compassionate-self practice.
- **Cited findings (directional, method-level):** Petrocchi 3-arm mirror study (highest positive affect, significantly lower self-criticism in the phrases+mirror arm); spousal hand-holding under shock reduced threat-area brain activation, more with higher relationship quality; striving-to-avoid-inferiority correlated with higher depression/anxiety/stress (authors' own research); 12,000–50,000 thoughts/day (estimate as cited); 1 frowning face among 99 smiling is found faster than the reverse; expressive writing benefits health over time (Pennebaker, no effect sizes).
- **Evolution frame:** old-brain structures >200M years; new-brain competencies ~2M years.

## Blind spots of this book

- **No efficacy evidence for the book itself.** Every dose, sequence and worksheet rests on clinical practice plus method-level CFT/CMT and borrowed-paradigm research; the self-administered format is untested — evidence lines must not be upgraded in retrieval.
- **Trauma boundary is signposted but self-policed.** The book repeatedly routes trauma material out, yet leaves detection of flooding/dissociation to the reader mid-exercise — exactly when self-assessment is least reliable. The lens should enforce the routing, not assume the reader will.
- **No severity guidance.** Nothing on when a workbook is insufficient (suicidality, severe depression, dissociative presentations); no clinical cut-offs even for its own questionnaire.
- **Mechanism claims outrun citations.** Facial feedback (weak replications), a mother's-voice oxytocin study used analogically, "imagery beats words for emotion" — plausible physiology stated more confidently than the sources support.
- **Individual-centric frame.** Difficulties are located in the person's system balance; ongoing abusive relationships, poverty, discrimination or workplaces that *keep* the threat system accurate get one assertiveness protocol and little else. Sometimes the environment, not the attention bias, is the problem.
- **Load and drop-out.** Twelve competencies, dozens of practices, weeks of sequenced work; the authors acknowledge the overwhelm risk but offer only "small steps" — no minimal effective subset is identified.
- **Flat cultural range.** Secularized Western framing; contemplative sources borrowed without discussion of users for whom the practices carry religious or cultural charge.
