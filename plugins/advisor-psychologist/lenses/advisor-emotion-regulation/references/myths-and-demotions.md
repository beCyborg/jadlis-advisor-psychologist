# advisor-emotion-regulation — myths, demotions, failure modes

## 1. What the tradition itself debunks

- **Beliefs about emotions** (the 20-item list behind ER Worksheet 3): that there is a correct way to feel in a situation; that showing feelings is weakness; that being emotional means being out of control; that extreme emotion gets you further; that changing an emotion is inauthentic; that emotions can be trusted without checking; that emotions arrive for no reason. Correction the lens uses: emotions are always caused, they are signals rather than reports about the world, and intensity is not evidence.
- **Beliefs about asking** (the 24-item interpersonal list): that asking proves weakness; that people should know without being told; that saying no is selfish; that revenge will be worth it. Also rejected: that maximal assertiveness is the right default, and that skills can manufacture reciprocity or make someone care.
- **"Skills always work if you do them right."** Explicitly denied. An environment more powerful than the skill defeats correct use, and the remedy is allies and acceptance, not more drilling. Many apparent skill failures are motivational — the emotion is being reinforced by something [ER:TROUBLESHOOT].
- **Acceptance is not approval, compassion, passivity, or the opposite of change.** The belief that accepting means condoning is named as the main thing that blocks the skill [ER:RADACC].
- **Suppression.** Pushing an emotion away increases suffering and blocks nearly every other skill; criticizing your own emotion does not regulate it. This is load-bearing, not a footnote — several moves fail silently when they are performed as suppression [ER:WAVE], [ER:MOT], [ER:MINDCORE].
- **Punishment folklore.** Punishment teaches no new behavior, works only while the punisher is present, and drives avoidance of the punisher. Intermittent giving-in during an extinction burst trains escalation.
- **Relapse folklore.** "I have learned my lesson", "I can control it now", "willpower is enough", "I can do this alone" are documented precursors, and one slip does not doom the attempt — the shame spiral after a lapse is countered directly [ER:ADDICT].
- **Crisis-skill overuse.** Distraction and self-soothing are for short crises. Using them as the everyday operating mode is a named misuse, not a sign of mastery [ER:CRISISMENU].
- **Sleep catastrophizing.** A sleepless stretch is uncomfortable, not a catastrophe; the response is stimulus control, not forcing sleep [ER:SLEEP-HYGIENE].
- **Its own self-help framing.** The manual states there is no research on it as self-study, and the accompanying skills videos have no outcome data. It also says not every skill needs to be learned.

## 2. Demotions this lens must carry (align with PG26 verification corrections)

- **DBT skills standalone: LOW.** Not "unproven", not "shown". Phrase as: weaker evidence once the skills are separated from the program that delivered them.
- **The self-harm scare number is refuted.** The claim that online DBT skills raised suicide-attempt risk (hazard ratio 1.29) was an outcome-substitution error. Never reuse it. The correct statement: solo skill formats have weaker evidence, and harm has not been shown either.
- **The head-to-head with schema therapy is not an equivalence result.** n=204, p=0.27, d=0.15, dropout 52% versus 46%, superiority design, motivated cohort. Say "no difference demonstrated", never "as good as".
- **"Difficulty tolerating and leaving situations → DBT skills" is a routing hypothesis**, not a treatment assignment. About half of all such target→method arrows rest on LOW evidence.
- **Homework numbers are demoted.** Presence d=0.48 is LOW; the widely quoted completion percentages (a "41%" figure traced to a 1998 non-CBT study, and a "51% versus 72%" survey that does not exist) are refuted. There is no completion benchmark, so never set one — review the quality of one practice instead.
- **Written practice is not unsafe by default, and not safe by default either.** The blanket claim against expressive writing was bounded: what is contraindicated is unstructured venting during active rumination-driven distress. Structured worksheets with a defined target are a different operation.
- **Alliance and warmth do not substitute for the operation.** The alliance correlation (r=.278) is LOW and cannot be used to argue that which skill you run does not matter.
- **Grief.** Superiority of any brand for grief was retracted to LOW; grief work has support only above elevated prolonged-grief symptoms; for ordinary grief the effects are near zero and intervention can do harm. Measure before doing anything [ER:LOSS-ACCEPT].
- **No detailed retelling of fresh trauma**, in any worksheet form. Debriefing does not prevent post-traumatic problems and carries a harm signal (odds ratio around 2.5). This bounds the nightmare write-out and every trauma-targeted variant [ER:NIGHTMARE-RESCRIPT], [ER:GRIEFINVAL].
- **Effect sizes do not transfer across delivery modes.** No study tested a clinician-free format. Procedures transfer; magnitudes do not.

## 3. Anti-recommendations — framings to avoid inside this tradition

- **Do not present the manual as a self-help book that happens to be used in clinics.** The relationship is the reverse, and the difference is exactly the untested part.
- **Do not hand someone the full skill inventory.** 225 pleasant events, 58 values, dozens of worksheets, hundreds of techniques with no ranking. Handing over the catalog reproduces the manual's worst property — the selection burden — and predicts abandonment.
- **Do not use the biosocial story as an explanation of the person.** It is an etiological sketch, and in solo use it converts easily into "my family made me this way", which is a stopping point rather than a move.
- **Do not import the US practicalities** — hotline numbers, group norms, public-disclosure framing. Crisis contacts come from safety-protocol §4 and are local.
- **Do not use the acronyms as live prompts.** The tested workflow is script-first, rehearsed in advance; improvising an acronym mid-conflict is a different thing wearing the same name [ER:DEARMAN].
- **Do not treat the 2015 edition as current.** It predates a decade of work on app-delivered skills, dismantling studies and online formats, and it contains no effect sizes at all.

## 4. Documented and predictable failure modes

- **Opposite action on a justified emotion.** Running the skill against an emotion that fits the facts is self-invalidation performed with a technique, and it teaches the person that their reading of reality is the problem [ER:OPPACT].
- **Self-run exposure.** An approach task chosen and escalated alone, ended at peak fear, reinforces the fear. This is the specific mechanism behind the therapist tier on [ER:OPPACT-FEAR].
- **Chain analysis as self-prosecution.** The eight steps convert smoothly into an inventory of evidence for being defective. The manual warns about this briefly; solo, the warning is too small [ER:CHAIN].
- **Mindfulness pushed through worsening.** In a history of trauma, worsening during practice is a stop signal, not a signal to practice harder.
- **Acceptance as tolerance of harm.** "Radically accepting" an ongoing abusive or fixable situation is the most consequential misuse in the book, and it is the one that looks most like progress from outside [ER:RADACC], [ER:SAFEEXIT].
- **Crisis skills as a life.** Distraction and soothing running continuously means the solvable problem never gets solved, and the person reports "I'm using my skills" the whole time.
- **The breakdown point as a permanent address.** "I'm past the point where skills work" becomes an argument for doing nothing; the fact-check step exists for exactly this [ER:BRKPOINT].
- **Worksheet perfectionism, then abandonment.** The tracking apparatus assumes group accountability. Without it the two observed endpoints are meticulous logging that replaces change, and a folder no one opens after week two.
- **What surfaces has nowhere to go.** These skills operate on the response system and outsource history, attachment and trauma processing to therapy — correctly, but with no guidance about material that surfaces mid-worksheet. That handoff is this council's job, not the book's [ER:GRIEFINVAL].
- **Reassurance loops.** Repeated returns with the same anxious question are not information requests; answering them again reinforces the loop. The loop gets named out loud instead.
- **The council as the emotional first responder.** If this layer becomes the first place distress goes, that is a finding to state plainly and route, not a sign the skills are working.

## 5. Substitutions — what to say instead

- Instead of "DBT skills work for emotion dysregulation" → "the skills bundle has package-level support in facilitated groups; standalone it is LOW; solo it is untested".
- Instead of "this is evidence-based" → name the operation and its own evidence: physiology for [ER:TIP], behavioral activation for [ER:ABCPLEASE], exposure for [ER:OPPACT-FEAR], functional analysis for [ER:CHAIN].
- Instead of "just accept it" → "which part of this is actually unchangeable" and then the acceptance sequence on that part only.
- Instead of "you're not doing the skill right" → the six-checkpoint failure list, with "the environment is stronger than the skill" as a permitted answer.
- Instead of "do the exposure" → "what did you and your therapist agree on, and what happened when you ran it".
- Instead of "try to feel less sad about the loss" → nothing of the kind: measure, route, and let the session hold it.
- Instead of "here are ten skills to try this week" → one practice, one step, five minutes, reviewed on quality.
- Instead of a completion percentage → the quality of the single practice and what specifically got in the way.
- Instead of "you sound overwhelmed" → a hypothesis the person confirms or rejects; no state attribution, no labels.
- Instead of continuing when a risk marker appears → stop, ground, contacts, route, and no resumption in that conversation [ER:RISK-HANDOFF].
