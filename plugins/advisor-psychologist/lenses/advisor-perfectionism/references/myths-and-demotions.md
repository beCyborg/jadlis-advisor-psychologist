# advisor-perfectionism — myths and demotions

What this tradition itself debunks, what the calibration layer demoted inside it, and the failure modes documented for this exact protocol. Numbers here are already in post-correction form; do not reuse earlier versions found in the book digest's own prose.

## Myths the method contradicts

- **"Perfectionism just means having high standards."** The target is worth-contingency plus persistence despite cost. High standards with intact self-worth and normal recovery from setbacks are not treated and are not a problem to solve [PERF:SCREEN9].
- **"It's genetic, so it can't change."** Twin studies put heritability at 24–49%; the model runs entirely on present-tense maintainers.
- **"You have to understand the childhood causes first."** The authors state plainly that knowing origins does not drive change; the single aetiological study cited is retrospective and memory-biased.
- **"Self-criticism is what keeps me sharp."** Directly contradicted: criticism lowers motivation, drives self-punishment and giving up. Shame or flatness after a self-critical thought is the signal that it is unproductive, not that determination is about to arrive [PERF:VALUES-MIRROR].
- **"Silence the critical voice."** A no-criticism goal manufactures new failures. The stated aim is volume down, never off [PERF:OBSERVE-NOT-REACT].
- **"Harder work means better results."** Most perfectionists sit past the peak of the effort curve, where effort can be cut with no loss of outcome and a large loss of stress. Related corrections the book makes: successful people do not necessarily work harder; there is no cleverness-versus-hard-work law; feeling a fraud is common and does not mean being one; other people notice far less than you assume; and "a job worth doing is worth doing well" has exceptions [PERF:FACTCHECK].
- **"Checking prevents mistakes."** Repeated checking *decreases* confidence in your own memory — the more you check, the less certain you feel. Related: "not feeling right" is a fluctuating internal state, not information about quality [PERF:CONTRAST].
- **"Motivation comes first, then action."** It runs the other way; waiting to feel ready guarantees delay, and procrastination's relief is brief with the anxiety returning amplified [PERF:CHUNK], [PERF:PROC-AUDIT].
- **"Meeting the standard will settle it."** Success gets discounted and the bar re-set — the formulation worksheet exists specifically to make you watch that happen [PERF:CYCLE-MAP].
- **"Perfectionists keep immaculate homes."** Many live in mess precisely because the standard made the task impossible, so it was abandoned.
- **"Leaving it late protects me — then it wasn't really my best."** The self-protective excuse backfires through rushed errors, and the delay itself raises belief in the untested prediction [PERF:PROC-AUDIT].
- **"Avoidance settles it."** Avoidance rarely resolves anything; it removes the occasion on which the prediction could have been disconfirmed.
- **"Success is what makes people happy."** Not a relationship the method accepts, and the one-year projection exercise exists partly to make that visible [PERF:MOTIV-AUDIT].
- **"A lifelong rule-bound, control-oriented style has to be treated separately."** Where such a style coexists with perfectionism, the book's guidance is to work on the perfectionism, because moving it moves the pattern — its sequencing image is a house of cards where the supporting card is pulled first [PERF:COMORBID-TRIAGE].
- **"Rituals over checking and rituals driven by not-feeling-right are the same thing as perfectionism."** They resemble each other closely enough that qualified clinicians struggle to separate them, and research has not settled which treatment is better — which is an argument for routing, not for confident sorting [PERF:COMORBID-TRIAGE].

## Demotions from the calibration layer

Where the 2026 verification pass corrected a number or a claim, the corrected form is binding for this lens.

- **Perfectionism → this protocol is MODERATE but was never tested against generic CBT.** Read the routing arrow as a working hypothesis about lens choice, not as a treatment assignment.
- **The meta-analytic base is 15 RCTs, N = 912** (Galloway). An earlier "two papers" figure was a corpus counter, not the evidence base. Self-help ≈ face-to-face is an average across that base.
- **Compassion-focused work for self-criticism: demoted MODERATE → LOW.** The pooled self-compassion effect (g = 0.51) is driven by passive controls; superiority over active treatment is not shown. Say "better than nothing, not shown better than an active alternative" — including for [PERF:CRITIC-COMPASSION-DIARY] and [PERF:VALUES-MIRROR], whose own support comes from this programme's secondary outcomes, not from the compassion literature.
- **Intolerance-of-uncertainty findings are LOW, and transdiagnostic uncertainty work is VERY LOW.** Kothari's d = −0.9 for uncertainty is a secondary outcome of this package and does not license using this lens as an uncertainty protocol.
- **Allegiance inflation ~30%.** The trial base is largely the authors' own group — Egan, Wade, Shafran, Rees and Kane recur across the RCTs — and developer-affiliated trials inflate effects by roughly a third. Independent replication is thin.
- **"Several meta-analyses agree" carries less than it sounds** wherever authorship overlaps across those analyses.
- **Delivery indirectness is unresolved.** No study tested a clinician-free format. Procedures transfer to solo and AI-mediated use; effect sizes do not.
- **No completion benchmark for between-session practice exists.** The commonly quoted compliance percentages were traced to a non-CBT study and to a survey that does not exist. Judge practice by quality and by whether the episode changed, never by a completion rate.
- **Trauma-memory rescripting is therapist-performed even inside therapy.** This bounds the book's own imagery section, which offers the technique in a few paragraphs with no worksheet and no containment [PERF:IMG-RESCRIPT-MEM].
- **Grief work has an entry condition that must be measured first,** and no solo format exists. In uncomplicated grief the interventions show near-zero effect and can do harm. Nothing in this lens is applied to a bereavement, and no loss is reframed as an opportunity [PERF:COST-BENEFIT-DREAM].
- **Unguided self-help is not shown to be harmful, and that is a weaker statement than it looks.** Pooled individual-participant data give deterioration of 5.8% under unguided self-help against 9.1% in comparison conditions — but active adverse-event collection happened in only a small minority of trials, so "no harm reported" is not "no harm".
- **Structure, not school, is the retention lever.** Dropout runs near a fifth on average and is markedly lower with time-limited, manualised formats. For a solo user that argues for a fixed weekly slot and a defined end, not for a different method.
- **Expressive writing is not unsafe by default** — the contraindication is narrower than it was stated: unstructured venting during active rumination-driven distress. Structured written exercises with a defined output are not the problem.

## Anti-recommendations — framings to avoid, and why

- **Do not deliver this as a "perfectionism course to complete."** The single documented failure mode of this exact book is applying perfectionism to it: hours of homework, hunting for the correct diagram, or avoiding the sheets because they cannot be done well. The pace guidance — roughly one chapter a week, a fixed slot, an approximate time cap — is explicitly a guideline, and holding it as a standard reproduces the problem.
- **Do not promise better performance.** The method refuses that claim, and offering it recruits exactly the worth-contingency being treated.
- **Do not use anxiety reduction as the proof it is working.** Short-term increases are expected — loosening a protective standard made the book's own case *more* anxious before decisions got easier.
- **Do not read the compassion exercises as soothing.** The book prints a worked example where mood worsened and the kind statement was believed at 30%, and keeps it in the blank-worksheet section deliberately.
- **Do not treat self-help book advice as generically safe.** Negative effects are reported in a wide band across self-help interventions and roughly 18% of self-help books carry advice judged harmful. This particular book is one of the few with its own trials — which is a reason to prefer it, not a reason to trust the category.
- **Do not run this book instead of routing a comorbidity.** Where mood, rituals or eating symptoms are primary, the book routes outward and expects parallel treatment; it will not carry that load [PERF:COMORBID-TRIAGE].
- **Do not treat procrastination without perfectionism with this lens.** The authors put it explicitly out of scope and defer to general CBT methods for it.
- **Do not use this lens for a pause in contact, an unanswered message, or relationship ambiguity.** There is no protocol; the book refers those out. Answering anyway is the lens overreaching, and repeated reassurance on the same worry is itself the pattern being maintained.

## Documented failure modes and iatrogenic patterns

- **Monotonic improvement expected.** Both "I must improve every week" and "I must do this without help" are named as the all-or-nothing pattern plus the critical voice, arriving inside the treatment.
- **The exercise becomes the new arena.** New life areas turned into achievement domains with standards and checking attached [PERF:BROADBASE]; the pleasant-activities grid rated for how well the activities were done [PERF:BALANCE-SCHEDULE]; the relapse blueprint postponed until it can be written perfectly [PERF:RELAPSE-BLUEPRINT].
- **Quitting before starting**, out of fear that reducing perfectionism means becoming second-rate — a fear that survives even while the perfectionism is actively producing failure.
- **Retrospective logging.** Records written days later lose the prediction made at the moment of deciding, which is the only part that can be tested [PERF:PROC-AUDIT].
- **Ungraded first experiments.** Starting at high stakes, or so trivially that the belief never engages, both waste the method [PERF:BEHEXP].
- **Solo entry into autobiography.** The historical review prints six childhood age bands with no containment and is the item most likely to surface painful material alone [PERF:HISTORICAL-REVIEW].
- **Baseline distress that is not a failure signal.** Temporary worsening is the baseline in psychological work, adverse events run 5–20%, and 5–10% of people in therapy generally end up worse. One hard week is not a reason to switch method; a multi-point downward trajectory, functional decline, flooding lasting over a day, or worsening sleep are stop-and-route signals.
- **Reassurance loops.** Where the query is the same worry returning, giving another answer reinforces it. Name the loop instead.
- **Verbal rewrites that never get tested.** A rule reworded as a preference while the underlying rule stands untouched is a cosmetic change; the relaxation has to be verified behaviourally [PERF:RULE-LIST].
- **Portable cards written before the evidence exists.** Cards built from generic affirmations rather than from your own experiment results are the toolkit's weakest material used at its weakest [PERF:PORTABLE-COUNTERS].
- **Positive-evidence logs pre-filtered for worthy entries**, or stopped after a week, reproduce the discounting bias inside the exercise designed to correct it [PERF:POS-DIARY].
- **Overload.** More than a couple of actions at once reproduces the demanding-parent pattern; an unfinished task is a design failure of the task, not evidence about the person.
