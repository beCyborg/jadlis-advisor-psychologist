# advisor-schema — myths, demotions and documented failure modes

What this tradition has retracted about itself, what the calibration layer has taken away from it, and the ways it is known to hurt people.

---

## Refuted or bounded numbers — use only the corrected form

- **"Schema therapy has a large effect (d ≈ 1.46)."** Refuted at the source: that figure is a pre–post psychodynamic result (Leichsenring 2003) and cannot support this method at all. Say instead: Bamelis 2014 plus Zhang 2023, always with magnitudes.
- **"Group format works, individual format doesn't."** Over-reading. Group g = 0.859 and individual g = 0.163 are **both** non-significant; pooled g = 0.359 with CI 0.006–0.711 barely clears zero, and the corpus is not purely cluster-C. Say instead: a small fragile pooled effect, with the format story unproven.
- **"Schema therapy is equivalent to CBT because all bona fide therapies are equivalent."** The interchangeability network does not contain this method, and the finding does not extend beyond depression. Superiority over CBT sits at LOW.
- **"It beat dialectical behaviour therapy / it was shown equivalent to it."** Neither. That trial was a superiority design with a motivated cohort; p = 0.27 is a failure to find a difference, not equivalence.
- **"It works for everyday problems."** Subclinical use is VERY LOW — no direct trials exist. Downward transfer from personality-disorder samples is untested.
- **"There is a trialled schema self-help book, so self-help works."** One title, tested as a standalone course (n = 110), not as the paced adjunct these worksheets assume. The workbook this lens draws its worksheets from has no trial and no effect size for any exercise.
- **"More than two people saying the same thing means something" / "people overestimate their own contribution by about 50%."** Both are clinical impressions or uncited social-psychology folklore presented as thresholds. Usable as prompts, never as evidence [ST:CM-DETECT].
- **Screening item sets and 0–100 ratings are not instruments.** No psychometrics, no cut-offs, no norms; the authors themselves downgrade them to hints. Every number produced by [ST:PM-COGID] is a self-report artefact.

## Myths the tradition itself debunks

- **"Vanquish the schema."** Named an unrealisable ideal — most of these patterns never fully clear. Resistance to giving one up is self-preservation: it means giving up a stable account of who you are.
- **"Insight is change."** Necessary, not sufficient, and it carries less the more severe the picture. Clear self-knowledge with no distress is documented and must not be read as progress. "I can say the healthy side but don't believe it" is the expected intermediate state, not failure.
- **"Fix the flaw and the feeling goes."** Falsified inside the tradition's own material: the shame persisted after the physical change it had been blamed on.
- **"Coping behaviour identifies the pattern."** Opposite behaviours serve the same theme. This is also the tradition's own critique of criterion-based classification: criteria that list coping responses shift under you, because coping drifts and the theme does not.
- **"Overcompensation looks like health."** It is socially rewarded and common among high performers. Its failure mode is collapse into depression when a large enough setback breaks it. A sudden "flight to health" is confronted, not celebrated.
- **"Calm detachment is progress."** A punctual, compliant, homework-doing presentation is compatible with the switched-off state running throughout. The test is whether needs and feelings are experienced and expressed, not whether behaviour is appropriate. A polished, agreeable false self is the presentation most often mistaken for health.
- **"Strong feelings mean a pattern."** The criterion is disproportion **plus** uncontrollability. Irritation when hungry, tired or genuinely wronged is not a pattern; ordinary procrastination is not a pattern; genuine rest and pleasure are not symptoms.
- **"The parents did it."** Grandparents, siblings, teachers, coaches or classmates can install a demanding or guilt-loading voice, and a parent may have been loving in most respects and damaging in one narrow area. Demanding intentions were frequently good — what failed was the praise-to-demand balance.
- **"Feeling fine means the mode is functional."** The fight-back and impulsive styles feel good from the inside while compounding quiet costs — debt, abandoned courses, eroded relationships — with subjective distress staying low.
- **"All coping is pathological."** These are defence mechanisms; every functioning person needs some. Switching off during someone else's tantrum is the healthy version. The line is blocked needs or blocked obligations.
- **"Non-compliance means laziness."** A refused exercise, forgotten homework or blank imagery is data about the pattern, and forcing it backfires. Persistent inability to produce an image is a severity signal, not defiance.
- **"Recovered memories are historical fact."** Images carry emotional truth, which is not a claim about what happened. Suggesting content is prohibited outright; if someone later says they made it up, the theme is worked without adjudicating accuracy either way.

## Anti-recommendations — framings and sources to refuse

- **Do not open with cognitive argument** where the presentation is emotionally inhibited or switched-off. It is documented as counterproductive there: rationality is exactly what that state already does, and pressing it reinforces the problem. In fragile presentations it has also been experienced as mechanical, rejecting, or as abandonment.
- **Do not argue a pessimistic account out of someone.** It turns the exchange adversarial, and where the complaining is driven by unmet need, counter-evidence does not land (the "help-rejecting complainer" pattern, Frank et al. 1952). The move is asking for care, not supplying facts.
- **Do not advise trying harder to please a devaluing partner.** Documented to accelerate the devaluation.
- **Do not treat the tradition's own comparison chapter as a review.** It was written by the model's authors; its verdicts on rival models are advocacy.
- **Do not treat case vignettes as findings.** The strongest rhetorical claims — that "I've already forgiven them" is usually inaccurate, that "I can't visualise" is almost always avoidance, that one particular theme has a high success rate — rest on impression and are easily converted into pressure on the person.
- **Do not import the therapist role.** The donor material this lens was built from is written for a clinician managing a case. Anything that positions this lens as therapy, as a session, or as "your therapist" is out of scope by design.
- **Do not use this frame where the cause is structural.** The model routes everything to childhood need histories and internalised voices. Poverty, discrimination, workload, illness, medication and neurodivergence appear only as background. Someone burning out under an objectively impossible job gets handed a demanding-parent frame — and that is a misread, not a formulation.
- **Do not apply it to grief.** The affective-bridge and play-restoration exercises explicitly exclude memories that sit inside a loss, and nothing here is licensed to reframe a loss as an opportunity.

## Documented failure modes and iatrogenic patterns

- **Deep imagery on scenes of belittlement, punishment or abuse can trigger a mood collapse the person cannot exit unaided.** The workbook reverses its own earlier imagery permission for exactly this reason. Detailed retelling of fresh trauma carries a harm signal in its own right (OR ≈ 2.5); debriefing does not prevent later symptoms.
- **A strong punishing state may mock, escalate against, or absorb the exercises.** That is an indication for professional support, not for more effort. The same applies when an imagined supportive figure keeps answering in the punishing voice.
- **Chair dialogue fails at peak arousal** — and in this lens it is clinician-led regardless, because it works vulnerable material.
- **Dropping avoidance costs high distress up front; dropping the fight-back styles is worse** — the person meets the underlying inferiority directly and may become depressed or anxious first.
- **Reducing compliant behaviour irritates the people who benefited from it.** The fear of criticism is *initially realistic*; the loop only breaks if that phase is expected rather than read as proof the change was wrong.
- **Confronting someone mid-mode reliably produces more of the same** — so people go quiet and withdraw instead, which re-confirms the underlying loneliness and re-fuels the pattern. Naming a colleague's or a group's mistreatment can also offend the perpetrators and worsen the target's position.
- **Misidentification flips the intervention.** Reading deprivation as incompetence turns care into withheld guidance; what heals one theme perpetuates another. The formulation is not a low-stakes guess.
- **The helping relationship reproduces the pattern.** Permanent dependence where the theme is abandonment; new fusion where it is enmeshment; rewarded compliance where it is submission; a rewarded false self where it is approval-seeking; mirrored self-denial and mirrored perfectionism for their respective themes. Any self-help or conversational substitute should assume the same trap and check for it, not assume immunity.
- **The helper is a documented failure source.** Ten enumerated failure scenarios driven by the helper's own patterns — withdrawal under intense affect, retaliation, scolding from a demanding stance, meeting one's own needs through the other person, blame when progress stalls, envy. Treating this material is itself potentially harmful to the treater (Pearlman & MacIan 1995).
- **The model is partly unfalsifiable.** It explains any behaviour after the fact, and there is no procedure for concluding that a candidate state is *absent*. This is why [ST:DISPROPORTION] exists as a deliberate false-positive guard, and why any reading this lens offers has to be offered as a hypothesis the person can reject.
- **The self-help boundary is asserted, not operationalised.** Neither source book has a front-end screen that catches the reader who should not run the deep exercises alone; the boundary is discovered by hitting it. That is the gap the operation-level tier table exists to close.
- **Nothing is known about dose, duration, plateau, drop-out or adverse-event rates for solo use.** Absence of complaints is not safety evidence.
